Hard-Boiled Eggs
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Eggs simmered in water until the whites and yolks are fully set, then cooled in ice water so they are easier to peel. These are useful for breakfast, snacks, egg salad, and deviled eggs.

SERVINGS
6 eggs, about 3 to 6 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes, including cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 6 large eggs
- Water, to cover the eggs by about 1 inch
- Ice, for an ice-water bowl

TACTILE TIPS
Raw eggs feel heavy and smooth. After cooking they feel a little firmer in the shell. The ice bath should feel very cold.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Place the eggs in a single layer in a pot.

2. Add cold water to cover them by about 1 inch.

3. Feel the water line before you turn on the heat, or use a liquid level indicator.

4. Bring the water to a boil over high heat.

5. As soon as it boils, cover the pot and turn the heat to low.

6. Cook 10 to 12 minutes for large eggs.

7. Lift the eggs into a bowl of ice water.

8. Let them cool 5 to 10 minutes.

9. Tap each egg and peel it under a little running water, or store them unpeeled in the refrigerator.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The egg should feel firm when you roll it on the counter. After peeling, the white should feel set, and the yolk should feel dry and crumbly, not wet. You should not smell sulfur in a strong way if you avoided overcooking.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: A peeled egg should look opaque all the way through. The yolk should be pale and dry, not glossy and raw in the center. A green ring around the yolk means the eggs cooked a bit long, but they are still safe to eat.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. A peeled egg should look opaque all the way through. The yolk should be pale and dry, not glossy and raw in the center. A green ring around the yolk means the eggs cooked a bit long, but they are still safe to eat.

SAFETY
Use eggs that are not cracked. Do not eat a raw or undercooked yolk. Cool the eggs quickly. Wash your hands after peeling. Cook eggs until the yolk and white are firm. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Older eggs often peel more easily than very fresh ones. For an egg salad, see the Lunch chapter. Store hard-boiled eggs in the refrigerator and eat them within 1 week.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
