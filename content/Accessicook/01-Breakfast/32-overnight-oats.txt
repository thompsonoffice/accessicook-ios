ACCESSICOOK
Overnight Oats
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Rolled oats soaked in milk in the refrigerator so they soften with no cooking. This is a cold breakfast you make the night before.

SERVINGS
1

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes of work, plus overnight in the refrigerator

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) rolled oats
- 1/2 cup milk, or a milk alternative
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) yogurt, optional
- 1 teaspoon chia seeds or 1 teaspoon honey, optional
- Fruit, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
In the morning the oats should feel plump and tender, not hard, and the mix should be spoonable, not dry and stiff.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the oats, milk, and optional yogurt, chia, or honey in a jar or bowl.

2. Cover.

3. Refrigerate at least 6 hours, or overnight.

4. In the morning, stir.

5. Add a splash of milk if it is too thick.

6. Add fruit and eat cold, or warm it in the microwave until it is steaming if you want it hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The oats should feel soft all the way through. The mix should smell fresh and milky, not sour. If it smells sour or yeasty, throw it out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The oats should look swollen and creamy, with no dry pockets of unsoaked flakes.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The oats should look swollen and creamy, with no dry pockets of unsoaked flakes.

SAFETY
Keep the jar refrigerated. Use it within 2 days. If you warm it, heat it until it is steaming hot. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A mason jar travels well. Peanut butter or jam can be stirred in just before you eat.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
