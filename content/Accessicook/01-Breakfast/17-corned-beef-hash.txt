Corned Beef Hash
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chopped cooked corned beef browned with potatoes and onion, often served with a firm-cooked egg on top. Use leftover corned beef or canned corned beef.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons oil or butter
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 3 cups cooked potatoes, cubed, or 1 pound potatoes, cubed and already cooked until tender
- 2 cups chopped cooked corned beef
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- Salt, only if needed, because corned beef is salty
- 4 large eggs, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Corned beef feels firm and a little oily, with visible fat streaks. The hash should feel crispy in spots, not wet and stewed.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat.

2. Add the onion.

3. Cook 4 to 5 minutes, until it feels soft.

4. Add the potatoes and corned beef.

5. Season with pepper.

6. Press the mix into the pan.

7. Cook 4 to 5 minutes without stirring, until the bottom feels browned and crisp.

8. Turn sections and brown the other side.

9. Taste a cooled bite before you add salt.

10. If you want eggs, make wells and cook them covered until they are set, 160 degrees F.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: You should hear a sizzle, not a steam hiss. The bottom should feel crusty. Corned beef should be hot all the way through. Leftover meat in the hash should reach 165 degrees F.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The hash should have dark golden-brown crisp patches. It should not look gray and steamed.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The hash should have dark golden-brown crisp patches. It should not look gray and steamed.

SAFETY
Corned beef is already cooked, but leftovers must be reheated to 165 degrees F. Cook eggs until firm. The pan will spatter. Use a splatter screen. For steaks, roasts, and other whole pieces of beef, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. If this dish is a slow stew, keep cooking until the meat is also tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Canned corned beef works if you do not have leftovers. Do not stir too often or you will lose the crust.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
