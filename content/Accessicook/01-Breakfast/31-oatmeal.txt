ACCESSICOOK
Oatmeal
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple pot of rolled oats cooked in water or milk until creamy. This is stovetop oatmeal. A microwave method is included in the tips.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup rolled oats, not instant
- 2 cups water, or 1 cup water and 1 cup milk
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- Optional toppings: brown sugar, fruit, or a spoon of peanut butter

TACTILE TIPS
Rolled oats feel flaky. The finished oatmeal should feel creamy and soft, with no hard, raw flakes in the center of a bite.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the oats, liquid, and salt in a small pot.

2. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, stirring. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

3. Cook 5 to 8 minutes, stirring now and then, until the oats are thick and tender.

4. Let the pot sit 1 minute.

5. Serve with toppings if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The flakes should feel soft, and the mix should feel like a thick porridge. You should smell cooked grain, not scorching at the bottom.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The oatmeal should look creamy and thick, not watery, and not a dry paste.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The oatmeal should look creamy and thick, not watery, and not a dry paste.

SAFETY
The pot and steam are hot. Stir so the bottom does not stick and burn. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, with a splash of liquid. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For microwave oatmeal, use a large microwave-safe bowl, because it bubbles up. Cook 2 to 3 minutes, stir, then cook in 30-second bursts until thick. Instant oats cook faster and can turn pasty, so they need less time and less liquid.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
