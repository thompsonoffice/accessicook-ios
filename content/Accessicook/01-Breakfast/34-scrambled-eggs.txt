Scrambled Eggs
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Soft scrambled eggs cooked slowly in butter until they are set and no longer liquid. This is a good first egg recipe. The USDA advice is to cook eggs until the yolk and white are firm, and to cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F.

SERVINGS
1 to 2

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 or 3 large eggs
- 1 tablespoon milk or water, optional
- 1 pinch salt, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon)
- 1 pinch black pepper
- 1 tablespoon unsalted butter
- Optional: 2 tablespoons shredded cheese, stirred in at the end

TACTILE TIPS
A raw egg in the shell is oval, smooth, and cool. Beat eggs until they feel uniformly slick. Butter foams as it melts, then the foam quiets.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Crack each egg into a small bowl, then add it to a mixing bowl so you can feel for shell.

2. Add the milk or water, if using, plus the salt and pepper.

3. Beat with a fork until the mixture feels even, with no large streaks of unmixed white.

4. Melt the butter in a skillet over medium-low heat.

5. Pour in the eggs.

6. Stir slowly with a spatula, scraping the bottom, until the eggs form soft curds and look and feel set, not runny.

7. If you have a thermometer, the eggs should reach 160 degrees F.

8. Stir in cheese now if you are using it.

9. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The eggs should feel soft but set, with no liquid raw egg left. They should smell gently cooked and buttery, not burnt or strongly sulfuric.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The eggs should look opaque and moist, not shiny and raw, and not dry and brown.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The eggs should look opaque and moist, not shiny and raw, and not dry and brown.

SAFETY
Wash your hands and the bowl after you crack raw eggs. Do not taste raw egg. Keep the heat medium-low so the eggs do not scorch. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. There should be no liquid raw egg left.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For firmer eggs, cook a minute longer. For more servings, use a larger pan and do not pile the eggs too deep.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
