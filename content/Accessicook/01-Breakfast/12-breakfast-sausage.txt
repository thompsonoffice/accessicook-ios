Breakfast Sausage
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Pork or turkey breakfast sausage cooked in a skillet as patties or links until it reaches a safe temperature. Pork sausage should reach 160 degrees F. Turkey sausage should reach 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
4, about 8 patties

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound breakfast sausage, as patties or links, pork or turkey
- A little oil, only if the sausage is very lean

TACTILE TIPS
Raw sausage feels soft and cold. Patties should be even in thickness. Links are already shaped. Keep raw sausage on a plate so juices do not run across the counter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat a skillet over medium heat.

2. Add a film of oil only if the sausage is lean turkey or if the pan is very dry.

3. Add the patties or links in a single layer.

4. Cook, turning now and then, 8 to 12 minutes, depending on thickness.

5. Check a patty or the end of a link with a thermometer.

6. Pork should read 160 degrees F.

7. Turkey should read 165 degrees F.

8. The juices should run clear, not pink and thick, and the meat should feel firm, not pasty.

9. Drain on a plate lined with a paper towel. Drain means pour off the liquid.

10. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The sausage should feel firm. A thermometer must read 160 degrees F for pork or 165 degrees F for turkey. You should smell cooked meat, not raw fat.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sausage should be browned. There should be no raw, pink, pasty look in the center. Color alone is not enough. Use the thermometer.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sausage should be browned. There should be no raw, pink, pasty look in the center. Color alone is not enough. Use the thermometer.

SAFETY
Treat sausage as raw ground meat. Wash your hands, the plate, and the tongs. Use a splatter screen. Do not serve sausage that has not hit the safe temperature. Cook ground pork to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Do not press hard on patties or you will squeeze out juices. Keep cooked sausage in a 200 degree F oven while you make eggs.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
