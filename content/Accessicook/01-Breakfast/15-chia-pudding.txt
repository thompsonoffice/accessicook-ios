Chia Pudding
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A no-cook pudding made by soaking chia seeds in milk until they swell and thicken. It is a cold breakfast or a simple dessert.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes of work, plus at least 2 hours in the refrigerator

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) chia seeds
- 1 cup milk, or a milk alternative
- 1 tablespoon maple syrup or honey
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) vanilla extract, optional
- Fruit, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Dry chia seeds feel like tiny, hard ovals. After soaking they feel gelatinous and slippery, and the pudding should feel thick like yogurt, not watery, and not a solid block of dry seeds.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the chia, milk, sweetener, and vanilla in a jar.

2. Wait 5 minutes, then stir again so the seeds do not sit in a clump at the bottom.

3. Cover and refrigerate at least 2 hours, or overnight.

4. Stir.

5. Add a splash of milk if it is too thick.

6. Serve cold, with fruit if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The seeds should feel soft-gelled, not hard. The mix should smell fresh and milky, not sour.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The pudding should look thick and even, with no dry layer of seeds at the bottom and no standing liquid on top after you stir.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The pudding should look thick and even, with no dry layer of seeds at the bottom and no standing liquid on top after you stir.

SAFETY
Keep it refrigerated and eat it within 3 days. If it smells sour, throw it out. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cocoa powder or mashed banana can be stirred in before chilling. If you dislike the seed texture, blend the pudding until it is smooth.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
