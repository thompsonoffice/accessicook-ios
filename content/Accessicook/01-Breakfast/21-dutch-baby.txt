Dutch Baby Pancake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A Dutch baby is a puffed oven pancake baked in a hot skillet. It rises in the oven, then settles as it cools. The eggs must be set all the way through.

SERVINGS
2 to 3

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer
- An oven-safe 10 inch skillet

INGREDIENTS
- 3 large eggs
- 2/3 cup (two-thirds of a cup) milk
- 2/3 cup all-purpose flour
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract, optional
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter

TACTILE TIPS
The batter should feel thin and smooth, like heavy cream. The skillet and butter will be very hot when the batter goes in.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F with an oven-safe skillet inside.

2. Beat the eggs, milk, flour, salt, sugar, and vanilla until smooth.

3. Take the hot skillet out with mitts.

4. Add the butter and swirl until it melts.

5. Pour in the batter.

6. Bake 15 to 20 minutes, until the edges feel set and puffed and the center is not wet.

7. A thermometer in the center should read 160 degrees F.

8. Serve at once, with lemon and sugar or fruit if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set and the center should feel cooked, not raw and loose. You should smell toasted butter and egg.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The edges should be high, golden, and puffed. The center should look set, not liquid.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The edges should be high, golden, and puffed. The center should look set, not liquid.

SAFETY
The skillet is extremely hot. Use mitts the whole time. Cook until the center is 160 degrees F. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. There should be no liquid raw egg left.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Do not open the oven early or the pancake may collapse before it is set. It will settle some even when it is done.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
