Baked Eggs in Ramekins
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Eggs baked in small dishes with a little cream or butter until the whites and yolks are set. Ramekins are small oven-safe cups. This is a quiet oven method with a clear thermometer target of 160 degrees F.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- Butter, for the dishes
- 2 or 4 large eggs, 1 or 2 per dish
- 2 tablespoons cream or milk
- Salt and pepper
- Optional: a pinch of cheese or chopped cooked ham, already cooked

TACTILE TIPS
A ramekin is a small, heavy cup. Crack each egg into a bowl first, then pour it in.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Butter two small oven-safe dishes, called ramekins.

3. If you are using cheese or cooked ham, put a little in the bottom of each dish.

4. Crack the eggs into the dishes.

5. Spoon the cream over the top.

6. Add salt and pepper.

7. Set the dishes on a baking sheet.

8. Bake 12 to 18 minutes, until the whites are set and a thermometer in the center of a yolk reads 160 degrees F.

9. Let the dishes cool 1 minute.

10. Use mitts.

11. Serve in the dishes.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The whites should feel firm, and the yolks should feel set, not sloshy. A thermometer should read 160 degrees F in the center. The dishes will be very hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The whites should look opaque. The yolks should look set, not liquid. The edges may be a little puffed.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The whites should look opaque. The yolks should look set, not liquid. The edges may be a little puffed.

SAFETY
Use dry mitts. The cream and dishes hold heat. Cook eggs until firm. If you add ham, it should already be cooked. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. There should be no liquid raw egg left.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For two eggs per dish, use the longer time. You can bake more ramekins at once. Add herbs if you like.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
