ACCESSICOOK
Baked Oatmeal
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A pan of oats baked with milk, egg, and fruit until the center is set. It cuts into squares and reheats well.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 50 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups rolled oats
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) brown sugar
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 2 cups milk
- 1 large egg
- 2 tablespoons melted butter
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 cup berries or chopped apple

TACTILE TIPS
The mix will look loose going into the oven. It should set into a soft, sliceable block as it bakes.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease an 8 inch square pan.

3. Mix the oats, brown sugar, baking powder, cinnamon, and salt.

4. In a second bowl, mix the milk, egg, melted butter, and vanilla.

5. Pour the wet mix into the dry mix.

6. Stir in the fruit.

7. Spread the mix in the pan.

8. Bake 35 to 45 minutes, until the center feels set and a thermometer reads 160 degrees F.

9. Cool 10 minutes.

10. Cut into squares.

11. Serve warm.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The center should feel set, not liquid. You should smell toasted oats and cinnamon. A thermometer should read 160 degrees F because of the egg.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look set and lightly golden. The center should not look wet and jiggly.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look set and lightly golden. The center should not look wet and jiggly.

SAFETY
The mix contains raw egg. Bake until the center is 160 degrees F. Use mitts. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This reheats well. Add a splash of milk on a leftover square and warm it until it is steaming hot.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
