Grits
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A creamy Southern porridge made from ground corn. Use regular or stone-ground grits, not instant, for this method. Whisk as they cook so they stay smooth. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk until the mixture looks even.

SERVINGS
2 to 3

TOTAL TIME
About 20 to 30 minutes, depending on the grind

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup regular grits, not instant
- 4 cups water, or 3 cups water and 1 cup milk
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 2 tablespoons butter
- Optional: 1/2 cup (half a cup) shredded cheddar cheese

TACTILE TIPS
Dry grits feel like coarse sand. Finished grits should feel smooth and creamy, with no hard grains in a bite.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Bring the water or water-and-milk and the salt to a boil in a pot.

2. Whisk in the grits slowly so they do not clump. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk until the mix is even.

3. Turn the heat to low.

4. Simmer, stirring often, 15 to 25 minutes, until the grits are tender and thick. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

5. Stir in the butter, and the cheese if you are using it.

6. Taste a cooled bite and add salt if it needs it.

7. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A bite should feel soft, with no hard grain. The pot should smell like cooked corn and butter, not scorching.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The grits should look smooth and creamy, not watery, and not so thick that they stand in stiff peaks.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The grits should look smooth and creamy, not watery, and not so thick that they stand in stiff peaks.

SAFETY
Grits bubble and spit as they thicken. Stir and use a deep pot. The pot is hot. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If they get too thick, whisk in a splash of water or milk. For shrimp and grits, see the Supper chapter. Instant grits cook much faster. Follow the package time if that is what you have.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
