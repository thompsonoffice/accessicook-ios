Bagels with Cream Cheese
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A toasted or fresh bagel spread with cream cheese. This is a simple breakfast, and a good base for the smoked salmon bagel later in this chapter.

SERVINGS
1

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 bagel, split
- 2 tablespoons cream cheese, softened a few minutes at room temperature
- Optional: sliced tomato, cucumber, or a pinch of everything-bagel seasoning

TACTILE TIPS
Softened cream cheese spreads easily. Cold cream cheese tears the bagel. A bagel has a firm crust and a dense crumb.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Split the bagel with a knife, keeping your other hand out of the path of the blade, or use a bagel slicer if you have one.

2. Toast it if you like, until it smells toasted and the cut face feels slightly dry.

3. Spread the cream cheese on both halves.

4. Add optional toppings.

5. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The bagel should feel warm and slightly crisp if toasted. The cream cheese should feel smooth and spreadable.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cut face should be lightly golden if toasted. The cream cheese should look even, with no big dry clumps.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cut face should be lightly golden if toasted. The cream cheese should look even, with no big dry clumps.

SAFETY
Bagels are dense and can roll. Steady the bagel before you cut. Keep fingers back. Use a fork in the bagel as a handle if that feels safer, or ask a helper to split it. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a flavored spread, mix in a little chopped scallion or honey. See Smoked Salmon Bagel in this chapter for a fuller version.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
