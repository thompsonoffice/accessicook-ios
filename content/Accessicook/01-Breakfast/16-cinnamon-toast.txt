Cinnamon Toast
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Toast with butter, sugar, and cinnamon. You can make it in a toaster oven, a skillet, or under a broiler, which is the top heating element in an oven. This version uses a skillet or a toaster oven, which is easier to control.

SERVINGS
1 to 2

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 2 slices bread
- 1 tablespoon butter, softened
- 2 teaspoons sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) cinnamon

TACTILE TIPS
The butter should be soft enough to spread without tearing the bread. The cinnamon sugar should feel sandy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the sugar and cinnamon in a small bowl.

2. Toast the bread lightly, or leave it untoasted if you will finish it in a skillet.

3. Spread the butter on the bread.

4. Sprinkle the cinnamon sugar on top.

5. For a toaster oven, toast again until the sugar feels melted and the bread smells caramelized, often 2 to 4 minutes.

6. For a skillet, place the bread butter-side up over medium-low heat and cover for 2 to 3 minutes, until the bottom is crisp and the topping is warm.

7. Let it cool 30 seconds. The sugar can burn your mouth.

8. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: You should smell cinnamon and toasted sugar, not smoke. The top should feel tacky and warm, not dry and dusty with unmelted sugar, and not burnt and bitter.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look shiny and slightly melted, and the bread should be golden, not black at the edges.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look shiny and slightly melted, and the bread should be golden, not black at the edges.

SAFETY
Sugar burns quickly. Stay with the toaster oven or skillet. The topping is very hot at first. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Mix a small jar of cinnamon sugar to keep on hand. A 4 to 1 mix of sugar to cinnamon is a good start.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
