ACCESSICOOK
French Toast
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Bread soaked in a mix of egg and milk, then cooked in a skillet until the center is set and the outside feels dry and toasted.

SERVINGS
4 slices, 2 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 3 large eggs
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) milk
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract, optional
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) cinnamon, optional
- A pinch of salt
- 4 slices sturdy bread, such as sandwich bread that is a day old
- 1 tablespoon butter, plus more if needed
- Syrup or fruit, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
The soaked bread should feel wet through but still hold together, not fall apart in the bowl. Stale bread holds up better than very fresh bread.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Beat the eggs, milk, vanilla, cinnamon, and salt in a shallow bowl until even.

2. Heat a skillet over medium heat and add the butter.

3. Dip a slice of bread in the egg mix.

4. Turn it so both sides are wet.

5. Let extra drip off.

6. Lay the slice in the pan.

7. Cook 2 to 3 minutes per side, until the bread feels dry and set on the outside and the center is not wet with raw egg.

8. Repeat with the remaining slices, adding butter if the pan is dry.

9. Serve warm with syrup or fruit.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The surface should feel dry and slightly crisp. The center should feel custardy but set, not soggy and raw. You should smell toasted bread and egg, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: Each side should be golden brown, not pale and wet, and not dark brown. No raw egg should show on the surface.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. Each side should be golden brown, not pale and wet, and not dark brown. No raw egg should show on the surface.

SAFETY
The soak contains raw egg. Cook until the center is set, about 160 degrees F if you check a thick slice. Do not taste the raw mix. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. There should be no liquid raw egg left.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Thick bread needs a slightly longer soak and a slightly lower heat so the center can set. A talking timer helps you remember to turn the slices.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
