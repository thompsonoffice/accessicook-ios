ACCESSICOOK
Frittata
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A frittata is an open-faced egg dish cooked in a skillet, then finished in the oven until the eggs are set. It is like a crustless quiche. Add cooked vegetables or cheese you already have on hand.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer
- An oven-safe skillet
- Long oven mitts

INGREDIENTS
- 8 large eggs
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- 2 tablespoons milk or cream
- 1 tablespoon oil or butter
- 1 cup cooked vegetables, such as spinach or leftover roasted vegetables, squeezed dry if they are wet
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) shredded cheese

TACTILE TIPS
The beaten eggs should feel even and slick. Cooked spinach should feel dry, not dripping.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Beat the eggs, salt, pepper, and milk in a bowl until even.

3. Heat the oil in an oven-safe skillet over medium heat.

4. Add the vegetables and warm them for 1 to 2 minutes.

5. Pour in the eggs.

6. Sprinkle the cheese over the top.

7. Cook on the stove 2 to 3 minutes, until the edges feel set.

8. Move the skillet to the oven.

9. Use mitts.

10. Bake 8 to 12 minutes, until the center is set and a thermometer reads 160 degrees F.

11. Rest 2 minutes. Rest means let it sit so it finishes setting.

12. Slice and serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The center should feel firm and springy, not liquid. A thermometer should read 160 degrees F. You should smell cooked eggs and cheese, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look set and a little puffed, not wet and shiny in the center. The edges should be pale gold.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look set and a little puffed, not wet and shiny in the center. The edges should be pale gold.

SAFETY
Use an oven-safe skillet. The handle will be very hot coming out of the oven. Keep a mitt on the handle after it leaves the oven. Cook eggs to 160 degrees F. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. There should be no liquid raw egg left.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Do not use watery raw vegetables or the frittata will be soggy. Cook or squeeze them first. Leftover cooked potatoes work well.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
