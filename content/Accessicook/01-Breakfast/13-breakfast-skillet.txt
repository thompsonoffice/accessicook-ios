============================================================
ACCESSICOOK
Breakfast Skillet
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A one-pan breakfast of potatoes, cooked sausage or vegetables, and eggs finished on top until the eggs are set.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound potatoes, cut into 1/2 inch (half inch) cubes
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 8 ounces breakfast sausage, or 1 cup leftover cooked vegetables
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 4 large eggs
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) shredded cheese, optional
- Black pepper

TACTILE TIPS
The potatoes should be tender before the eggs go on, or the eggs will overcook while you wait for the potatoes.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium heat.

2. If you are using raw sausage, cook it until it is 160 degrees F for pork, or 165 degrees F for turkey, then break it up and move it to a plate.

3. Add the potatoes and salt to the pan.

4. Cook 15 to 20 minutes, stirring now and then, until they are tender and crisp in spots.

5. Return the sausage, or add the leftover vegetables, and heat until steaming.

6. Make 4 small wells in the pan.

7. Crack an egg into each well.

8. Cover and cook until the eggs are set, 160 degrees F, about 5 to 8 minutes.

9. Sprinkle cheese on top if you like.

10. Serve from the skillet, which will be very hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Potatoes should mash easily. Eggs should feel firm. The skillet should smell toasted and savory, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The potatoes should have golden spots. The egg whites should be opaque and the yolks set. The cheese should look melted.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The potatoes should have golden spots. The egg whites should be opaque and the yolks set. The cheese should look melted.

SAFETY
Cook sausage fully. Cook eggs until firm. The skillet handle and lid will be hot. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. There should be no liquid raw egg left.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
You can finish the eggs in a 375 degree F oven if your skillet is oven-safe, still to 160 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
