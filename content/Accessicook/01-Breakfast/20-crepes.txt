ACCESSICOOK
Crepes
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Thin French-style pancakes cooked one at a time in a lightly greased skillet. The batter is thinner than pancake batter. Fill them with fruit, cheese, or leftover ham and egg.

SERVINGS
About 8 crepes, 3 to 4 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes, plus 15 minutes of batter rest

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup all-purpose flour
- 2 large eggs
- 1 1/4 cups (one and a quarter cups) milk
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 2 tablespoons melted butter, plus more for the pan
- 1 tablespoon sugar, optional, for sweet crepes

TACTILE TIPS
The batter should feel very thin, like half-and-half or light cream. A little rest makes it smoother. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Beat the flour, eggs, milk, salt, melted butter, and sugar, if using, until smooth.

2. Let the batter rest 15 minutes.

3. Heat an 8 or 10 inch skillet over medium heat.

4. Add a thin film of butter.

5. Pour in about 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) of batter.

6. Tilt the pan so the batter coats the bottom in a thin layer.

7. Cook about 1 minute, until the top looks and feels dry and the edges loosen.

8. Flip and cook 20 to 30 seconds more.

9. Stack the crepes on a plate and repeat, buttering the pan as needed.

10. Fill and fold. Fold means gently turn the mixture over itself so you do not knock out the air.

11. Serve warm.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top will feel dry and the edges will loosen when it is ready to flip. You should smell a light toast, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The first side should be lightly golden with a few browned spots. The crepe should be thin and flexible, not pale and wet or dark and crisp like a cracker.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The first side should be lightly golden with a few browned spots. The crepe should be thin and flexible, not pale and wet or dark and crisp like a cracker.

SAFETY
The pan is hot and a thin crepe can burn quickly. Stay with the stove. The batter contains raw egg. Do not taste it. Cook eggs until the yolk and white are firm. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
The first crepe is often a test. Adjust the heat if it browns too fast or stays pale. Cover the stack with a towel to keep them soft.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
