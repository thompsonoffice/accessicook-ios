ACCESSICOOK
Pancakes
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple skillet pancake made from flour, milk, and egg. Cook until the centers are set. Do not leave raw batter in the middle.

SERVINGS
About 8 pancakes, 2 to 3 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) all-purpose flour
- 2 tablespoons sugar
- 2 teaspoons baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 1/4 cups (one and a quarter cups) milk
- 1 large egg
- 2 tablespoons melted butter or oil, plus more for the pan

TACTILE TIPS
The batter should feel like heavy cream, not like paste and not like water. A few small lumps are fine.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt in a bowl.

2. Add the milk, egg, and melted butter.

3. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour. A few lumps are fine.

4. Let the batter rest 5 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat. Rest here means let it sit so the flour can absorb the liquid.

5. Heat a skillet over medium heat.

6. Add a thin film of butter.

7. A drop of batter should sizzle softly.

8. Pour about 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) of batter per pancake, leaving space between them.

9. Cook until bubbles form and stay open on the surface and the edges feel set, about 2 to 3 minutes.

10. Flip.

11. Cook 1 to 2 minutes more, until the center feels springy, not wet.

12. Serve warm.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Bubbles form and stay open. The edges feel set. The flipped side feels springy. There is no liquid batter in the center if you nick one open.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The first side is ready when the top looks dull and bubbly and the underside is even golden brown, not pale or black.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The first side is ready when the top looks dull and bubbly and the underside is even golden brown, not pale or black.

SAFETY
Raw batter contains egg and flour. Do not taste it. Cook until the center is set. Wash the bowl and whisk. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk until the mixture looks even. Cook eggs until the yolk and white are firm. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Keep finished pancakes on a plate in a 200 degree F oven. For blueberry pancakes, sprinkle berries on the wet top before you flip.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
