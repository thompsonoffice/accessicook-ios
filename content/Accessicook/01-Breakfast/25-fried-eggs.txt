Fried Eggs Cooked Through
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Fried eggs cooked in a covered skillet until the whites and yolks are firm. This book uses a fully cooked yolk, which matches USDA guidance. A helper may be used to checking yolks by color, but firmness and, when you can measure it, 160 degrees F are the safety cues.

SERVINGS
1 or 2

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 or 2 large eggs
- 1 tablespoon butter or oil
- A pinch of salt and pepper

TACTILE TIPS
Crack the egg into a bowl first so you can fish out any shell. The white starts slippery and becomes opaque and firm as it cooks.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Melt the butter or heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat until it is hot and a drop of water sizzles softly.

2. Crack each egg into a small bowl, then slide it into the pan.

3. Cook until the white feels set at the edges, about 1 to 2 minutes.

4. Cover the pan.

5. Cook 2 to 4 minutes more, until the yolk feels firm when you press it very gently with a spatula, and the white is fully set.

6. If you can get a thermometer tip into the yolk without touching the pan, it should read 160 degrees F.

7. Sprinkle with salt and pepper.

8. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The white should feel solid, and the yolk should feel firm, not sloshy. You should hear a gentle sizzle, not an angry spit. The egg should smell cooked, not burnt.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The white should be opaque, not clear. The yolk should look set, not liquid and bright. The bottom should be pale or lightly golden, not dark brown.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The white should be opaque, not clear. The yolk should look set, not liquid and bright. The bottom should be pale or lightly golden, not dark brown.

SAFETY
Raw eggs can carry bacteria. Cook until the yolk and white are firm. Use a splatter screen if the fat pops. Wash your hands after cracking eggs. Cook eggs until the yolk and white are firm. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you prefer a traditional runny yolk, know that it does not meet USDA guidance. This book stays with a firm yolk, especially when cooking for people who are pregnant, older, or have a weaker immune system.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
