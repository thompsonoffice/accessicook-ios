Biscuits and Sausage Gravy
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A Southern breakfast of split biscuits topped with a milk gravy made from breakfast sausage drippings. Bake the biscuits from the Baked Goods chapter, or use good store-bought biscuits.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes, if the biscuits are already baked

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 8 baked biscuits
- 1 pound pork breakfast sausage
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) all-purpose flour
- 2 1/2 cups (two and a half cups) milk
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- Salt, to taste

TACTILE TIPS
The gravy should feel smooth and coat a spoon, not pasty and not as thin as milk. Flour lumps feel hard in the spoon if you have not stirred enough.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cook the sausage in a skillet over medium heat, breaking it up, until it is 160 degrees F.

2. Leave the drippings in the pan.

3. Sprinkle the flour over the sausage.

4. Stir 1 minute, until the raw flour smell fades.

5. Pour in the milk slowly, stirring the whole time.

6. Simmer, stirring, 3 to 6 minutes, until the gravy thickens and coats a spoon. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

7. Add the pepper.

8. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt only if it needs it.

9. Split the biscuits.

10. Spoon the hot gravy over them.

11. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The gravy should feel smooth and silky on a spoon. The sausage must be 160 degrees F before you add the milk. You should smell cooked pork and pepper, not raw flour.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The gravy should look opaque and pale, and it should coat a spoon. The sausage should be brown, with no pink.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The gravy should look opaque and pale, and it should coat a spoon. The sausage should be brown, with no pink.

SAFETY
Cook sausage to 160 degrees F. The gravy is very hot and can spatter as it thickens. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, and bring gravy back to a bubble. Cook ground pork to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the gravy is too thick, add a splash of milk. If it is too thin, simmer a few minutes more. Turkey sausage can be used, but cook it to 165 degrees F, and you may need a little oil in the pan.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
