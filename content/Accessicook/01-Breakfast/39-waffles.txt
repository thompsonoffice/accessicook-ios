Waffles
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A thicker batter cooked in a waffle iron until crisp outside and set inside. Learn how your iron signals that it is ready, whether by sound, light, or steam that slows down.

SERVINGS
About 8 waffles, 4 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A waffle iron

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 2 tablespoons sugar
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 2 large eggs
- 1 3/4 cups (one and three-quarters cups) milk
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) melted butter or oil
- Oil spray or a little oil, for the iron

TACTILE TIPS
The batter is a bit thicker than pancake batter. It should fall from a spoon in a ribbon, not a lump and not a thin stream.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt.

2. In a second bowl, beat the eggs, milk, and melted butter.

3. Add the wet mix to the dry mix.

4. Stir just until combined.

5. Heat the waffle iron.

6. Grease it lightly if the maker says to.

7. Spoon batter into the iron. Do not overfill.

8. Close the lid.

9. Cook until the steam slows and the waffle feels crisp, often 3 to 5 minutes.

10. Follow your iron if it beeps or a light changes, then still check by feel.

11. Lift the waffle out with a fork or tongs.

12. Keep it from the hot plates.

13. Repeat with the remaining batter.

14. Serve warm.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Steam will slow when the waffle is close to done. It should feel crisp at the edges and set in the center, not doughy. You should smell toasting batter, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The waffle should be golden and crisp, with a dry surface, not pale and floppy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The waffle should be golden and crisp, with a dry surface, not pale and floppy.

SAFETY
The iron is very hot. Use tongs or a fork, not your fingers. Raw batter contains egg. Do not taste it. Cook eggs until the yolk and white are firm. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the waffle sticks, the iron may not have been hot or greased enough. Keep finished waffles in a 200 degree F oven so they stay crisp.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
