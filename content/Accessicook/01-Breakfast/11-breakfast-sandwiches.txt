ACCESSICOOK
Breakfast Sandwiches
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A firm-cooked egg and cheese on a toasted English muffin or biscuit, with optional cooked bacon or sausage. This is a hand-held breakfast.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 English muffins, split, or 2 biscuits
- 2 large eggs
- 2 slices cheese
- 1 tablespoon butter or oil
- 2 slices cooked bacon, or 2 cooked sausage patties, optional, already cooked
- Salt and pepper

TACTILE TIPS
An English muffin has a rough split face that toasts well. The egg should be firm enough that it does not run out of the sandwich.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Toast the muffins until they feel dry and smell toasted.

2. Cook the eggs in a skillet until the whites and yolks are firm, or 160 degrees F, as in Fried Eggs Cooked Through.

3. Add a slice of cheese on each egg in the last 30 seconds so it softens.

4. If you are using bacon or sausage, make sure it is already cooked and hot.

5. Build each sandwich: muffin, egg and cheese, meat if using, muffin top.

6. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The muffin should feel crisp at the edges. The egg should feel firm. The sandwich should be hot in the center.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The muffin should look lightly browned. The egg should look set. The cheese should look melted.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The muffin should look lightly browned. The egg should look set. The cheese should look melted.

SAFETY
Cook eggs until firm. Cook bacon or sausage fully before they go in the sandwich. Reheat leftover meat to 165 degrees F. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. There should be no liquid raw egg left.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
You can cook the eggs in a greased jar lid ring in the pan if you want a neat round shape. A biscuit from the Baked Goods chapter also works here.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
