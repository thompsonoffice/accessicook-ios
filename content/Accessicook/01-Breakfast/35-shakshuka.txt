ACCESSICOOK
Shakshuka
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Shakshuka is eggs cooked in a spiced tomato and pepper sauce until the whites are set. In this book the yolks are also cooked until firm, so the dish meets USDA egg guidance.

SERVINGS
3 to 4

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 1 bell pepper, diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon paprika
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1 can (28 ounces) crushed tomatoes
- 4 to 6 large eggs
- Optional: chopped parsley or feta cheese, for serving
- Bread, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Crushed tomatoes feel thick and slightly chunky. A well is a small hollow in the sauce, felt with the back of a spoon.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and pepper. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Heat the oil in a wide skillet over medium heat.

3. Add the onion and pepper.

4. Cook 6 to 8 minutes, until they feel soft.

5. Add the garlic, cumin, paprika, and salt.

6. Cook 30 seconds, until fragrant.

7. Add the tomatoes.

8. Simmer 8 to 10 minutes, until the sauce feels slightly thicker. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

9. Make small wells in the sauce with a spoon.

10. Crack each egg into a cup, then pour it into a well.

11. Cover the pan.

12. Cook 6 to 10 minutes, until the whites are set and the yolks feel firm, or a thermometer in a yolk reads 160 degrees F.

13. Serve hot with bread.

14. Add parsley or feta if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The sauce should coat a spoon. The whites should feel set, and the yolks should feel firm. You should smell tomatoes, pepper, and warm spices.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look thick, not watery. The egg whites should be opaque. The yolks should look set, not liquid, in this version of the dish.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look thick, not watery. The egg whites should be opaque. The yolks should look set, not liquid, in this version of the dish.

SAFETY
Cook the eggs until the yolks are firm. The skillet and lid will be hot. Use a splatter screen if the sauce pops. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. There should be no liquid raw egg left.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you want a classic runny yolk, know that it does not meet USDA guidance. This book keeps the yolks firm. Serve with the toast recipes in this chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
