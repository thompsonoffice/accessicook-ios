Smoothie Bowl
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A thick smoothie served in a bowl and eaten with a spoon, with crunchy toppings. It is thicker than the Fruit Smoothie in this chapter.

SERVINGS
1

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A blender, with the lid

INGREDIENTS
- 1 frozen banana, sliced
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) frozen berries
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) yogurt or milk, plus a splash more if the blender stalls
- Toppings: granola, sliced fruit, or coconut

TACTILE TIPS
The blended base should feel thick, like soft-serve, not drinkable like a thin smoothie. Frozen fruit should feel rock-hard going in.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the yogurt or milk in the blender first, then the frozen fruit.

2. Blend, stopping to scrape down the sides, until the mix is even and very thick.

3. Add a small splash of liquid only if the blender will not move.

4. Spoon the mix into a bowl.

5. Add toppings.

6. Serve at once, before it melts.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The blender sound will be heavy and slow because the mix is thick. The bowl should feel spoonable and icy-cold.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The base should look thick and even. Toppings should sit on top, not sink into a thin drink.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The base should look thick and even. Toppings should sit on top, not sink into a thin drink.

SAFETY
Use the lid. Unplug the blender before you scrape near the blade. Eat it soon. Do not let it sit out. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Frozen banana is what makes it thick. If you only have fresh fruit, add a few ice cubes, knowing it may taste a little more watery.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
