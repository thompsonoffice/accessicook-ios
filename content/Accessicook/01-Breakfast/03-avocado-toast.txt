Avocado Toast
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Toast topped with mashed avocado, salt, and an optional firm-cooked egg. It is a fast savory breakfast.

SERVINGS
1

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 ripe avocado
- 1 or 2 slices bread
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, or to taste
- A squeeze of lemon or lime juice, optional
- 1 large egg, optional, cooked until firm
- Black pepper, optional

TACTILE TIPS
A ripe avocado yields gently when pressed and feels slightly soft, not mushy and not hard as a rock. The flesh inside should feel creamy, not stringy and brown all the way through.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Toast the bread until it feels dry and smells toasted.

2. Cut the avocado in half around the pit.

3. Twist the halves apart.

4. Carefully pop out the pit with a spoon. Do not strike the pit with a knife.

5. Scoop the flesh into a bowl.

6. Mash it with a fork.

7. Add salt and lemon if you like.

8. Spread the mash on the toast.

9. Top with a firm-cooked egg if you want one, cooked to the cues in Fried Eggs Cooked Through.

10. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The toast should feel crisp. The avocado should feel creamy and taste fresh, not sour or bitter. The optional egg should feel firm.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The toast should be golden. The avocado should look green and creamy, not dark brown and stringy. An egg, if used, should look set.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The toast should be golden. The avocado should look green and creamy, not dark brown and stringy. An egg, if used, should look set.

SAFETY
Use a spoon, not a chopping blow, to remove the pit, because that is a common way people cut their hands. Cook an egg until firm if you add one. Eat avocado toast soon. It browns as it sits. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the avocado is underripe, wait a day or two. If only part is brown, you can cut away the brown part and use the rest if it smells fresh. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
