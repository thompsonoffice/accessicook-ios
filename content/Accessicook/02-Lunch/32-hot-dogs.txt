ACCESSICOOK
Hot Dogs
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Cooked hot dogs in buns, with toppings you like. Most supermarket hot dogs are already fully cooked and only need to be heated through. Check the package if you are not sure.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 4 hot dogs
- 4 hot dog buns
- Optional toppings: ketchup, mustard, relish, or chopped onion

TACTILE TIPS
A raw or underheated hot dog feels cool and floppy. A heated one feels hot and slightly firmer. Fully cooked dogs still need to be heated until steaming.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Bring a pot of water to a simmer, not a hard rolling boil. Simmer means small, gentle bubbles.

2. Add the hot dogs.

3. Heat 5 to 6 minutes, until they are steaming hot throughout, 165 degrees F if you check.

4. You can also heat them in a skillet over medium heat, turning, until they feel hot and browned in spots, still to 165 degrees F if they were leftover or if you want a clear number.

5. Warm the buns if you like, in a dry skillet or a 300 degree F oven for a few minutes.

6. Put the hot dogs in the buns.

7. Add toppings.

8. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The hot dogs should feel hot all the way through and smell cooked, not sour. If a package smells off, throw it out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The hot dogs should look plump and steaming. If browned in a skillet, they should have golden blisters, not black scorched patches.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The hot dogs should look plump and steaming. If browned in a skillet, they should have golden blisters, not black scorched patches.

SAFETY
Even fully cooked hot dogs should be heated until steaming, especially for people at higher risk. Heat leftover dogs to 165 degrees F. Boiling water can splash. Use tongs, not fingers. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Do not boil them hard for a long time or the casings may split and the texture gets tough. For chili dogs, add leftover chili that has been reheated to 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
