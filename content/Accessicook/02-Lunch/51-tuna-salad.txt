Tuna Salad
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Canned tuna mixed with mayonnaise and crunchy add-ins. Eat it on bread, on crackers, or on a salad. This page is the salad itself. A tuna melt is a separate lunch recipe.

SERVINGS
2 to 3

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cans (5 ounces each) tuna, drained
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) mayonnaise, or more to taste
- 1 celery stalk, finely diced, optional
- 1 tablespoon diced onion or pickle relish, optional
- 1 teaspoon lemon juice or mustard, optional
- Salt and pepper, to taste

TACTILE TIPS
Drain the tuna well or the salad will be watery. Drain means pour off the liquid. The finished mix should feel moist and hold a spoonful, not soupy and not dry and crumbly.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Drain the tuna.

2. Flake it into a bowl with a fork.

3. Add the mayonnaise, celery, onion or relish, and lemon or mustard if you are using them.

4. Mix until even.

5. Taste a small bite and add salt and pepper if it needs it.

6. Serve, or cover and refrigerate.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tuna should smell clean and mild, not sharply fishy or sour. The mix should feel moist and hold together.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The salad should look even and moist, with no big dry lumps of tuna and no standing liquid.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The salad should look even and moist, with no big dry lumps of tuna and no standing liquid.

SAFETY
Refrigerate within 2 hours. Eat within 3 days. Do not leave tuna salad out. If you use it in a sandwich, keep the sandwich cold. Cook fish to 145 degrees F, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes, meaning it comes apart easily with a fork.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a lighter mix, use some yogurt in place of part of the mayonnaise. Dill and a pinch of celery seed are good extras. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
