Chicken Tortilla Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A tomato and chili soup with chicken, topped with crisp tortilla chips or strips. Use raw chicken cooked in the pot to 165 degrees F, or leftover chicken added at the end.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon chili powder
- 1 can (14 1/2 ounces) diced tomatoes
- 1 can (4 ounces) diced green chiles, optional
- 6 cups chicken broth
- 1 pound boneless chicken, or 2 cups cooked chopped chicken
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- Tortilla chips, lime, and optional cheese or avocado, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
The broth should taste warm and slightly spicy, not flat. Chips should stay crisp, so add them at the table.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mince the garlic. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

3. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

4. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

5. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

6. If using raw chicken, add it with the broth and cook until it is 165 degrees F, then shred it and return it to the pot.

7. If using cooked leftover chicken, add it in the last 5 minutes and heat the soup to 165 degrees F.

8. Add the tomatoes, optional chiles, broth, cumin, chili powder, and salt.

9. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

10. Simmer 15 minutes.

11. Add or return the chicken as described, then simmer until everything is hot.

12. Serve with chips and lime.

13. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

14. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Chicken must be 165 degrees F. The soup should smell like cumin, chili, and tomato. Lime at the end should smell bright.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and cooked. The soup should look red-gold and slightly brothy, with visible tomato pieces.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and cooked. The soup should look red-gold and slightly brothy, with visible tomato pieces.

SAFETY
Handle raw chicken carefully if you start with raw meat. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Warn people at the table that chips can be sharp and messy in a hot soup. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Crush a few chips into your own bowl, not the whole pot, so leftovers are not soggy. A can of black beans can be added for a heartier lunch.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
