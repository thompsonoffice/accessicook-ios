Caprese Sandwich
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A sandwich of tomato, fresh mozzarella, and basil, with a little oil or pesto, on sturdy bread. It is a good warm-weather lunch. There is no raw meat, but the cheese and tomato should be kept cold until you assemble.

SERVINGS
1

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food

INGREDIENTS
- 2 slices sturdy bread, or a split ciabatta roll
- 1 ripe tomato, sliced
- 3 to 4 ounces fresh mozzarella, sliced
- A few basil leaves, or 1 tablespoon pesto
- 1 teaspoon olive oil, if you are not using pesto
- A pinch of salt

TACTILE TIPS
Pat the tomato slices dry. Fresh mozzarella feels soft and moist. Too much liquid makes the bread soggy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Slice the tomato and mozzarella.

2. Pat the tomato dry.

3. Toast the bread if you like.

4. Lay the bread, roll, or wrap on the board.

5. Layer oil or pesto, tomato, salt, mozzarella, and basil.

6. Close the sandwich.

7. Cut in half if you like. Cut in means rub or cut cold butter into flour until the mix feels like crumbs.

8. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tomato should smell fresh and sweet. The cheese should smell clean and milky, not sour. The sandwich is best soon after you make it.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tomato should look ripe. The cheese should look fresh and white, not slimy or moldy. Basil should look green, not black and wilted.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tomato should look ripe. The cheese should look fresh and white, not slimy or moldy. Basil should look green, not black and wilted.

SAFETY
Wash the tomato and basil. Keep mozzarella refrigerated until you use it. Eat within a few hours. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Toast the bread to help it stand up to the juices. A drizzle of balsamic vinegar is a classic extra if you have it.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
