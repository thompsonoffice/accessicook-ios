ACCESSICOOK
Reuben
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A grilled sandwich of corned beef, Swiss cheese, sauerkraut, and Russian or Thousand Island dressing on rye bread. The corned beef is already cooked. Heat the sandwich until the center is hot and the cheese is melted.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food

INGREDIENTS
- 4 slices rye bread
- 2 tablespoons Russian or Thousand Island dressing
- 8 ounces sliced cooked corned beef
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sauerkraut, drained well and squeezed dry
- 4 slices Swiss cheese
- 1 tablespoon butter

TACTILE TIPS
Squeeze the sauerkraut dry or the bread will get soggy. The finished sandwich should feel crisp outside and hot and melted inside.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Drain and squeeze the sauerkraut. Drain means pour off the liquid.

2. Butter one side of each bread slice.

3. Lay the bread, roll, or wrap on the board.

4. Build the sandwiches with the buttered sides out: dressing, cheese, corned beef, sauerkraut, more dressing, and bread.

5. Heat a skillet over medium heat.

6. Butter the outside of the bread, if the ingredients include butter for grilling.

7. Cook over medium heat 3 to 5 minutes per side, until the bread feels crisp and the center is 165 degrees F or steaming hot.

8. Cut in half if you like. Cut in means rub or cut cold butter into flour until the mix feels like crumbs.

9. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The bread should feel crisp. The center should feel hot. The cheese should feel melted. You should smell toasted rye, warm beef, and tangy sauerkraut.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The bread should be deep golden. The cheese should look melted. A little dressing may show at the edges.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The bread should be deep golden. The cheese should look melted. A little dressing may show at the edges.

SAFETY
Corned beef is cooked, but leftover meat in a hot sandwich should be heated until the center is 165 degrees F. Drain the sauerkraut well. For steaks, roasts, and other whole pieces of beef, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. If this dish is a slow stew, keep cooking until the meat is also tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cook these slowly over medium heat so the filling heats before the bread burns. A panini press works if you have one.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
