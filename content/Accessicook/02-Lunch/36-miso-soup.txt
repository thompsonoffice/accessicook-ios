Miso Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A Japanese-style soup of dashi or vegetable broth mixed with miso paste, often with tofu and scallions. Miso is a salty fermented soybean paste. Do not boil the soup hard after the miso goes in, or the flavor gets harsh.

SERVINGS
2 to 4

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 4 cups water plus 1 teaspoon instant dashi powder, or 4 cups low-sodium vegetable broth
- 3 tablespoons miso paste, white or yellow
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) soft tofu, cut into small cubes
- 1 green onion, thinly sliced
- Optional: a small handful of dried wakame seaweed, soaked and drained

TACTILE TIPS
Miso paste is thick, salty, and fragrant. Dissolve it in a little hot broth first so you do not have lumps. Tofu cubes should be soft and small enough for a spoon.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Bring the water and dashi, or the broth, to a simmer. Simmer means small, gentle bubbles.

2. If you are using seaweed, add it now.

3. Lower the heat so the pot is hot but not boiling hard.

4. Whisk the miso with a ladle of hot broth in a small bowl until smooth, then stir it back into the pot. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk until the mixture looks even.

5. Add the tofu and heat 1 minute, without boiling hard.

6. Add the green onion.

7. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The soup should smell savory and fermented in a pleasant way, like miso, not sour or fishy in an off way. The tofu should be hot. You should not taste harsh, boiled-miso bitterness.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The broth should look slightly cloudy and pale gold or tan, with visible tofu cubes and green onion. It should not be boiling hard when you serve it.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The broth should look slightly cloudy and pale gold or tan, with visible tofu cubes and green onion. It should not be boiling hard when you serve it.

SAFETY
The pot is hot. Taste before you add salt. Miso is already salty. Use clean bowls. Reheat leftovers gently, and try not to boil them hard. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
White miso is milder than red miso. Add cooked mushrooms if you like. Instant dashi is a powder or packet in many grocery stores.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
