Broccoli Cheddar Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A creamy soup of chopped broccoli and melted cheddar. Add the cheese off a hard boil so the sauce stays smooth. This is a rich lunch.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 1 onion, diced
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) all-purpose flour
- 2 cups broth
- 2 cups milk
- 4 cups chopped broccoli florets, fresh or frozen
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- 2 cups shredded cheddar cheese

TACTILE TIPS
Small broccoli pieces cook more evenly. The soup should feel creamy and the broccoli should feel tender, not hard. The cheese should melt smoothly, not into stringy clumps.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Melt the butter in a pot over medium heat.

2. Add the onion and cook until it feels soft, about 5 minutes.

3. Stir in the flour.

4. Cook 1 minute.

5. Whisk in the broth, then the milk, until you feel no lumps. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk until the mixture looks even.

6. Add the broccoli, salt, and pepper.

7. Simmer 8 to 12 minutes, until the broccoli is tender. Simmer means small, gentle bubbles.

8. Take the pot off the heat.

9. Stir in the cheese until it melts.

10. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The broccoli should feel tender. The soup should feel smooth and coat a spoon. You should smell cheese and cooked broccoli, not scorched milk.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The soup should look thick and pale orange or gold, not oily and broken, and not thin like milk. The broccoli should look tender and bright to olive green, not raw and tight, and not gray and mushy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The soup should look thick and pale orange or gold, not oily and broken, and not thin like milk. The broccoli should look tender and bright to olive green, not raw and tight, and not gray and mushy.

SAFETY
The pot is hot. Do not boil hard after the cheese goes in. Reheat leftovers gently to 165 degrees F, stirring. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Shred the cheese yourself if you can, for a smoother melt. A pinch of mustard powder or paprika is a good extra. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). You can blend half the soup if you want it silkier.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
