ACCESSICOOK
French Dip
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A roll filled with sliced cooked roast beef, served with a small bowl of warm beef broth for dipping. The dip is called au jus, which means "with juice." Heat the meat in the broth until it is hot.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes, if the roast beef is already cooked

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 hoagie rolls or baguette sections, split
- 8 ounces sliced cooked roast beef
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) beef broth
- Optional: a few slices of cheese, or a pinch of garlic powder in the broth

TACTILE TIPS
The broth should be steaming hot. The meat should be thin and flexible. The roll should be sturdy enough to dip without falling apart at once.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the broth in a small pot until it is simmering. Simmer means small, gentle bubbles.

2. Add the roast beef to the broth for 1 to 2 minutes, until it is hot.

3. Leftover beef should reach 165 degrees F.

4. Lift the meat into the rolls.

5. Add cheese if you like.

6. Pour the broth into two small bowls.

7. Serve the sandwiches with the bowls of broth for dipping.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The broth should be steaming hot. The meat should feel hot throughout. The roll should feel sturdy.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The broth should be steaming. The meat should look hot and moist, not gray and dried out.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The broth should be steaming. The meat should look hot and moist, not gray and dried out.

SAFETY
Reheat leftover roast beef to 165 degrees F. The broth is very hot and can splash. Serve it in a stable bowl. For steaks, roasts, and other whole pieces of beef, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. If this dish is a slow stew, keep cooking until the meat is also tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Toast the rolls lightly so they hold up to dipping. A little horseradish on the bread is a classic extra.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
