ACCESSICOOK
Philly Cheesesteak
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Thinly sliced beef cooked with onions, then piled into a roll with melted cheese. Use shaved steak or very thinly sliced sirloin. The USDA minimum for whole-muscle beef is 145 degrees F with a 3-minute rest, but thin slices in a sandwich are often cooked further, until no longer red and raw-feeling. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound shaved steak or very thinly sliced beef
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 medium onion, thinly sliced
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 4 slices provolone or American cheese, or 1/2 cup (half a cup) cheese sauce
- 2 hoagie rolls, split

TACTILE TIPS
Shaved steak feels thin and floppy. It cooks fast. Keep the pieces moving so they do not steam into a clump.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat.

2. Add the onion and a pinch of salt. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

3. Cook 6 to 8 minutes, until it feels soft and smells sweet.

4. Push the onion to the side.

5. Add the beef and the remaining salt.

6. Cook, stirring and breaking up the meat, until it is browned and a thermometer in a thicker piece reads at least 145 degrees F, and the meat no longer feels raw and slimy.

7. Lay the cheese over the meat.

8. Cover 30 to 60 seconds, until the cheese feels melted.

9. Pile the mix into the rolls.

10. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The beef should feel cooked and browned, not slimy. You should hear a sizzle, not a steam hiss. The sandwich should be very hot in the center.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The beef should be browned, with no raw red look. The onions should look soft and translucent. The cheese should look melted.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The beef should be browned, with no raw red look. The onions should look soft and translucent. The cheese should look melted.

SAFETY
Use the claw grip for the onion. The skillet will spatter. Do not overcrowd the pan or the meat will steam. Leftovers should be refrigerated and reheated to 165 degrees F. For steaks, roasts, and other whole pieces of beef, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. If this dish is a slow stew, keep cooking until the meat is also tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Some cooks add sliced bell pepper with the onion. If you cannot slice the beef thin, ask the butcher to shave it, or freeze the meat 20 minutes to make slicing easier.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
