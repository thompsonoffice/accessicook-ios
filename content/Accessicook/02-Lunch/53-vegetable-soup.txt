Vegetable Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A mixed vegetable soup in a tomato or clear broth. Use what you have: carrots, celery, potatoes, green beans, or frozen mixed vegetables all work.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots, sliced
- 2 celery stalks, sliced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 potato, diced, optional
- 1 can (14 1/2 ounces) diced tomatoes, or 2 tablespoons tomato paste plus 1 cup water
- 6 cups vegetable broth
- 1 cup frozen green beans, corn, or mixed vegetables
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) dried thyme or Italian seasoning

TACTILE TIPS
Cut firm vegetables into even, bite-size pieces so they cook at the same rate. Frozen vegetables can go in later, still frozen.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mince the garlic. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

3. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

4. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

5. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

6. Add the tomatoes, broth, potato if using, salt, and herbs.

7. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

8. Simmer 15 minutes, then add the frozen vegetables.

9. Simmer 8 to 10 minutes more, until all the vegetables are tender.

10. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A potato or carrot cube should mash easily. The broth should smell savory and herby, not raw onion.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The vegetables should look tender, not gray and overcooked into mush, and not still raw and pale in the center of a potato cube.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The vegetables should look tender, not gray and overcooked into mush, and not still raw and pale in the center of a potato cube.

SAFETY
The pot and steam are hot. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. If you add leftover cooked meat, heat the soup until it is 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A handful of small pasta or leftover rice can go in for the last 8 to 10 minutes. Taste the salt after the soup reduces a little.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
