Lentil Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A hearty soup of brown or green lentils with onion, carrot, and broth. Lentils do not need to be soaked. They are done when they are tender and no longer hard in the center.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 50 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots, diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) brown or green lentils, rinsed
- 7 cups vegetable or chicken broth
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin or dried thyme
- 1 tablespoon vinegar or lemon juice, at the end

TACTILE TIPS
Dry lentils feel like small, hard discs. Rinse them until the water feels less dusty. A done lentil should feel tender, not chalky in the center, but should still hold its shape if you want a chunky soup.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mince the garlic. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

3. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

4. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

5. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

6. Add the rinsed lentils, broth, salt, and cumin or thyme.

7. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

8. Simmer 25 to 35 minutes, until the lentils are tender.

9. Add the vinegar or lemon juice at the end.

10. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Lentils should feel tender all the way through. The soup should smell earthy and savory. A splash of vinegar or lemon at the end should make the flavor feel brighter, not harsh if you start with a teaspoon and taste.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The lentils should look plump and the soup should look thick and stew-like, not watery with a layer of hard lentils on the bottom.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The lentils should look plump and the soup should look thick and stew-like, not watery with a layer of hard lentils on the bottom.

SAFETY
Rinse lentils and pick out any hard foreign bits you can feel, such as a small stone. The pot is hot. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Red lentils cook faster and fall apart into a smoother soup. If you use them, start checking at 15 to 20 minutes. This soup thickens as it sits. Add broth when you reheat.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
