ACCESSICOOK
Cobb Salad
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A composed salad with rows of cooked chicken, bacon, hard-boiled egg, tomato, avocado, and cheese on greens. All the meats and eggs should be fully cooked before you assemble.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes, if the chicken and eggs are already cooked

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 4 cups chopped lettuce, washed and dried
- 1 cup chopped cooked chicken
- 4 slices cooked bacon, crumbled
- 2 hard-boiled eggs, chopped
- 1 tomato, diced
- 1 avocado, diced
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) crumbled blue cheese or shredded cheddar
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) vinaigrette

TACTILE TIPS
Cut the toppings into small, even pieces. Avocado should be ripe and yield gently. Arrange in rows if you like, or toss just before serving.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Prepare all toppings.

2. Cook and cool the eggs and bacon if needed.

3. Dice the avocado just before serving. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

4. Put the salad base and the mix-ins in a large bowl.

5. Lay the lettuce in bowls.

6. Arrange the toppings in rows or sections.

7. Drizzle with vinaigrette, or serve it on the side.

8. Taste a leaf and add a pinch of salt if it needs it. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

9. Serve soon, while the greens are crisp.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Everything should feel fully cooked where that applies, and fresh where it is produce. The chicken should have been cooked to 165 degrees F. Avocado should smell fresh, not rancid.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: You should be able to see the different toppings. Chicken should look cooked and opaque. Egg yolks should look set. Bacon should look cooked.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. You should be able to see the different toppings. Chicken should look cooked and opaque. Egg yolks should look set. Bacon should look cooked.

SAFETY
Use chicken cooked to 165 degrees F and fully cooked bacon and eggs. Cut avocado as in Avocado Toast, using a spoon to remove the pit. Serve soon, because avocado browns. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Dressing on the side keeps the bacon and greens crisper. Leftover roast chicken works well.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
