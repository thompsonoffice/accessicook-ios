Garden Salad
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple green salad with tomato, cucumber, and a vinaigrette. Dry the leaves well so the dressing coats them instead of sliding off in a puddle of water.

SERVINGS
2 to 3

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 4 cups mixed salad greens, washed and dried
- 1 tomato, cut into wedges, or 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
- 1/2 cucumber, sliced
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) simple vinaigrette, from the Misc chapter, or a dressing you like
- Salt and pepper

TACTILE TIPS
Dry greens feel fluffy, not clumped and wet. Wet greens make a watery salad.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Wash and dry the greens, tomato, and cucumber.

2. Cut the vegetables.

3. Put the salad base and the mix-ins in a large bowl.

4. Drizzle the dressing over the salad, or serve it on the side.

5. Toss gently until the leaves feel lightly coated, not soaked.

6. Taste a leaf and add a pinch of salt if it needs it. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

7. Serve soon, while the greens are crisp.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The leaves should feel crisp and dry before you dress them. The salad should smell fresh and clean.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The leaves should look crisp and lively, not wilted or slimy. The dressing should look lightly coating, not pooled in the bottom.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The leaves should look crisp and lively, not wilted or slimy. The dressing should look lightly coating, not pooled in the bottom.

SAFETY
Wash all produce. Keep the salad cold if you are not eating it at once. Add dressing just before serving so the leaves stay crisp. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
See Simple Vinaigrette in the Misc chapter. Add leftover grilled chicken, reheated or kept cold properly, if you want a fuller meal.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
