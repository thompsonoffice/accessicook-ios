Fruit Salad
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A mix of cut fresh fruit for lunch or a side. Choose fruit that is ripe but still holds its shape. Add banana just before you serve, because it softens and browns fast.

SERVINGS
4 to 6

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 6 cups mixed cut fruit, such as melon, berries, grapes, apple, and orange sections
- 1 tablespoon lemon or lime juice
- 1 tablespoon honey, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Cut fruit into bite-size pieces you can spear with a fork. Ripe melon yields slightly when pressed. Wash berries gently and dry them.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Wash, dry, and cut the fruit.

2. Remove any rinds, pits, or cores.

3. Put the fruit in a large bowl.

4. Toss with the lemon or lime juice, and honey if you like.

5. Add banana now if you are using it.

6. Serve, or chill for up to a few hours.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The fruit should smell fresh and sweet. Soft spots that smell fermented or sour should be discarded. The pieces should feel ripe but not mushy, except for fruits that are meant to be soft, such as ripe banana.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The fruit should look bright and moist, not dull, slimy, or molded. Throw out any pieces that look or smell off.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The fruit should look bright and moist, not dull, slimy, or molded. Throw out any pieces that look or smell off.

SAFETY
Wash all fruit, even fruit you will peel, because a knife can carry dirt from the peel into the flesh. Keep the salad cold. Eat within a day for the best quality. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of mint is a nice extra. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). If you cut the salad ahead, wait to add banana and any very delicate berries.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
