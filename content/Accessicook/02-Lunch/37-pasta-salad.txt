ACCESSICOOK
Pasta Salad
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Cooked pasta tossed with vegetables, dressing, and optional cheese. It is a picnic and lunch-box staple. Cool the pasta before you add mayonnaise if you are using a creamy dressing, so the dressing does not get oily and thin.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes, plus chilling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A colander

INGREDIENTS
- 12 ounces short pasta, such as rotini or shells
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the pasta water
- 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved, or diced cucumber and pepper
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) sliced olives or diced cheese, optional
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) Italian dressing or vinaigrette, or 1/3 cup mayonnaise mixed with 1 tablespoon vinegar
- Salt and pepper

TACTILE TIPS
The pasta should be cooked through but still slightly firm, then cooled. The finished salad should feel moist and evenly dressed, not dry and not swimming.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cook the pasta in salted boiling water until it is tender but still slightly firm in the center.

2. See the Pasta Primer in the Supper chapter if you want more detail.

3. Drain. Drain means pour off the liquid.

4. Rinse briefly with cold water to cool it if you are making a cold salad.

5. Drain well.

6. Toss the pasta with the vegetables, olives or cheese, and dressing.

7. Taste and add salt and pepper if it needs it.

8. Chill at least 30 minutes.

9. Toss again before serving, because the pasta will drink some of the dressing.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The pasta should feel tender with a slight firmness. The salad should smell fresh and savory, not sour. After chilling, add a splash more dressing if it feels dry.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The pasta should look evenly coated. Vegetables should look fresh. There should not be a large puddle of dressing at the bottom after you toss.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The pasta should look evenly coated. Vegetables should look fresh. There should not be a large puddle of dressing at the bottom after you toss.

SAFETY
Cool the pasta quickly. Refrigerate the salad. Eat within 3 to 4 days. If the dressing is mayonnaise-based, keep it cold and do not leave it out more than 2 hours, or 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little leftover cooked chicken, reheated or kept properly cold, can make it a fuller lunch. Short shapes hold dressing better than long spaghetti.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
