ACCESSICOOK
Grilled Chicken Sandwich
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A seasoned chicken breast cooked in a skillet or grill pan, then served on a bun with lettuce, tomato, and a sauce. The chicken must reach 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 small boneless, skinless chicken breasts, or 2 cutlets, about 1 pound
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder or paprika
- 2 buns
- Lettuce, tomato, and mayonnaise or a sauce you like

TACTILE TIPS
If a breast is very thick, pound it or slice it horizontally so it cooks evenly. The cooked chicken should feel firm and springy, not squishy, and 165 degrees F in the thickest part.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pat the chicken dry.

2. Season both sides with salt and garlic powder or paprika.

3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat.

4. Cook the chicken 5 to 7 minutes per side, depending on thickness, until a thermometer in the thickest part reads 165 degrees F.

5. Rest the chicken 3 minutes. Rest means let it sit so the juices settle.

6. Toast the buns if you like.

7. Add sauce, chicken, lettuce, and tomato.

8. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The chicken should feel firm and springy. A thermometer must read 165 degrees F. You should smell cooked chicken, not raw meat or burning oil.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should be opaque and white or very light tan in the center, with no raw pink. The outside may be lightly browned.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should be opaque and white or very light tan in the center, with no raw pink. The outside may be lightly browned.

SAFETY
Wash the board and your hands after handling raw chicken. Do not put cooked chicken back on the raw plate. Use a splatter screen. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A lid on the skillet can help a thick piece finish without burning the outside. Leftover cooked chicken also works if you reheat it to 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
