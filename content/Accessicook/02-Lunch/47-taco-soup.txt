ACCESSICOOK
Taco Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A chili-like soup of ground beef, beans, corn, and tomatoes with taco seasoning. It is looser than the Chili in the Supper chapter, and it is a good lunch with chips or cornbread. Cook the beef to 160 degrees F before you add the canned ingredients.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound ground beef
- 1 onion, diced
- 1 packet taco seasoning, or 2 tablespoons chili powder plus 1 teaspoon cumin and 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 can (15 ounces) diced tomatoes
- 1 can (15 ounces) beans, drained and rinsed
- 1 can (15 ounces) corn, drained, or 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) frozen corn
- 2 cups beef or chicken broth
- Optional toppings: cheese, sour cream, or crushed chips

TACTILE TIPS
The finished soup should feel brothy but loaded with beans, corn, and meat, looser than chili, thicker than a clear soup.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

3. Add the onion and cook 4 minutes, until it feels soft.

4. Add the ground beef and cook, breaking it up, until it is 160 degrees F and no longer raw and pasty.

5. Stir in the seasoning.

6. Add the tomatoes, beans, corn, and broth.

7. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

8. Simmer 15 to 20 minutes.

9. Serve with toppings if you like.

10. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The beef must be 160 degrees F before you add the tomatoes. The soup should smell like chili powder and tomato. It should feel hot throughout, and leftovers must reach 165 degrees F when reheated.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The beef should be brown, with no pink, before the tomatoes go in. The soup should look red and chunky.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The beef should be brown, with no pink, before the tomatoes go in. The soup should look red and chunky.

SAFETY
Wash hands after handling raw ground beef. Use a thermometer. The pot is hot. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is a good pantry meal. For a vegetarian pot, skip the beef and add an extra can of beans, and use vegetable broth.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
