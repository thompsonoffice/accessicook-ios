Split Pea Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A thick soup of dried split peas, often simmered with a ham bone or diced cooked ham. Dice means cut into small, even cubes. The peas break down and thicken the pot on their own. Stir now and then so the bottom does not stick.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots, diced
- 2 celery stalks, diced
- 1 pound dried green or yellow split peas, rinsed
- 8 cups water or broth
- 1 ham bone, or 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) diced cooked ham
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste, added carefully because ham is salty
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper

TACTILE TIPS
Split peas feel like flat, hard halves of a pea. They should be rinsed. The finished soup should feel thick and almost creamy, with soft vegetables.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

3. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

4. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

5. Add the split peas, liquid, ham bone or ham, pepper, and only a little of the salt at first.

6. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

7. Simmer 45 to 60 minutes, stirring often, until the peas fall apart and the soup is thick.

8. Remove the bone if you used one.

9. Taste, then add salt if it needs it.

10. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The peas should feel completely soft, with no hard centers. The soup should coat a spoon. Stir if you smell anything like scorching. Taste before you add much salt.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The soup should look thick and olive-green or yellow, depending on the peas. There should not be a hard layer stuck and dark on the bottom.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The soup should look thick and olive-green or yellow, depending on the peas. There should not be a hard layer stuck and dark on the bottom.

SAFETY
If you use a ham bone, the soup should simmer long enough that any attached meat is steaming hot, 165 degrees F for leftovers. Stir so it does not burn. The pot is heavy and hot. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Remove the bone at the end, then cut off any meat and return it to the pot. The soup thickens a lot as it cools. Add water when you reheat, and bring it back to a boil.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
