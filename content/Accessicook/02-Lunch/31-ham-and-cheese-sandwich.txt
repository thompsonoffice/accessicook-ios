Ham and Cheese Sandwich
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Sliced ham and cheese on bread, served cold or grilled until the cheese melts. Use fully cooked ham from the package or leftovers.

SERVINGS
1

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes, or 15 if you grill it

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food

INGREDIENTS
- 2 slices bread
- 2 to 3 ounces sliced cooked ham
- 1 or 2 slices cheese
- 1 teaspoon mustard or mayonnaise, optional
- 1 tablespoon butter, if you are grilling the sandwich

TACTILE TIPS
Cooked ham feels firm and moist. If you grill the sandwich, the outside should feel crisp and the cheese should feel melted, not solid and cool.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Lay the bread, roll, or wrap on the board.

2. Spread a condiment on the bread if you are using one.

3. Add ham and cheese.

4. Close the sandwich.

5. Grill it in a buttered skillet if you want it hot, 3 to 4 minutes per side, until the bread feels crisp and the cheese feels melted.

6. Cut in half if you like. Cut in means rub or cut cold butter into flour until the mix feels like crumbs.

7. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: For a cold sandwich, the ham should smell clean and salty. For a grilled one, the bread should feel crisp and the center should feel hot and soft from melted cheese.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: A grilled sandwich should be golden on both sides, with cheese that looks melted. Ham should look cooked, not raw and shiny in a fresh-meat way.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. A grilled sandwich should be golden on both sides, with cheese that looks melted. Ham should look cooked, not raw and shiny in a fresh-meat way.

SAFETY
Use cooked ham. If you reheat leftover ham, USDA-inspected fully cooked ham can be reheated to 140 degrees F, and other leftover ham to 165 degrees F. Do not leave it out more than 2 hours. Fresh, uncooked ham should reach 145 degrees F, then rest 3 minutes. Fully cooked ham is only being reheated. USDA-inspected cooked ham can be reheated to 140 degrees F. Other cooked ham should reach 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
To grill, butter the outside of the bread and cook in a skillet over medium heat 3 to 4 minutes per side. This is close to a grilled ham and cheese, a cousin of the Grilled Cheese in Midnight Snacks.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
