Tuna Melt
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Tuna salad grilled with cheese on bread until the bread is crisp and the center is hot. Make the tuna salad first, or use leftover tuna salad from this chapter.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup tuna salad
- 4 slices bread
- 2 to 4 slices cheese
- 1 tablespoon butter, softened

TACTILE TIPS
Do not overfill or the salad will spill as you turn the sandwich. The finished sandwich should feel crisp outside and hot in the center.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Make or measure the tuna salad.

2. Butter one side of each bread slice.

3. Those buttered sides will face the pan.

4. Lay the bread, roll, or wrap on the board.

5. Lay 2 slices in the skillet, butter-side down.

6. Add tuna salad and cheese.

7. Top with the remaining bread, butter-side up.

8. Heat a skillet over medium heat.

9. Butter the outside of the bread, if the ingredients include butter for grilling.

10. Cook over medium heat 3 to 5 minutes per side, until the bread feels crisp and the center is steaming hot.

11. Cut in half if you like. Cut in means rub or cut cold butter into flour until the mix feels like crumbs.

12. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The bread should feel crisp. The center should feel hot. The cheese should feel melted. You should smell toasting bread and warm tuna.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The bread should be golden brown. The cheese should look melted. A little tuna may show at the edges and should look moist, not dried out.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The bread should be golden brown. The cheese should look melted. A little tuna may show at the edges and should look moist, not dried out.

SAFETY
Use tuna salad that has been kept cold. Heat the sandwich until the center is steaming hot, 165 degrees F if you check with a thermometer. Do not leave tuna salad at room temperature. Cook fish to 145 degrees F, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes, meaning it comes apart easily with a fork.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cook these slowly over medium heat so the cheese melts before the bread burns. English muffins also work.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
