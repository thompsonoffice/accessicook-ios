Egg Salad
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chopped hard-boiled eggs mixed with mayonnaise and a little mustard. Use the Hard-Boiled Eggs recipe in the Breakfast chapter, then cool and peel them before you mix.

SERVINGS
2 to 3

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes, including cooking the eggs

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 6 hard-boiled eggs, peeled
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) mayonnaise
- 1 teaspoon mustard
- Salt and pepper
- Optional: 1 tablespoon diced celery or pickle relish

TACTILE TIPS
The eggs should be fully set and cool. The salad should feel creamy with some texture from the whites, not watery.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cook and peel the eggs, using the Hard-Boiled Eggs recipe.

2. Cool them fully.

3. Chop the eggs on a board.

4. Move them to a bowl.

5. Add the mayonnaise, mustard, and any celery or relish.

6. Mix gently.

7. Add salt and pepper to taste.

8. Serve, or cover and refrigerate.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The eggs should feel fully set before chopping. The salad should smell like cooked egg and mayonnaise, not sulfur in a strong way and not sour.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The yolks should look pale and dry, not wet and raw, before you mix. The salad should look even and pale yellow.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The yolks should look pale and dry, not wet and raw, before you mix. The salad should look even and pale yellow.

SAFETY
Use fully cooked eggs. Refrigerate promptly. Eat within 3 to 4 days. Wash the board after chopping eggs. Cook eggs until the yolk and white are firm. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a smoother salad, mash more. For a chunkier one, chop less. Paprika on top is a classic finish.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
