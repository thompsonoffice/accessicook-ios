Butternut Squash Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A smooth, slightly sweet soup of roasted or simmered butternut squash and onion, blended until silky. Cutting hard squash takes care. You can buy pre-cut squash to make this easier.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 50 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil or butter
- 1 onion, diced
- 1 apple, peeled and diced, optional
- 2 pounds butternut squash, peeled, seeded, and cubed, or 2 pounds pre-cut squash
- 4 cups vegetable or chicken broth
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) cinnamon or curry powder, optional
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) cream or milk, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Raw squash is hard and slippery. If you cut it yourself, slice a thin piece off one side first so it sits flat, and use a cut-resistant glove. The finished soup should feel completely smooth if blended.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

3. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

4. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

5. Add the squash, optional apple, broth, salt, and optional spice.

6. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

7. Simmer 20 to 25 minutes, until the squash mashes easily.

8. Blend until smooth.

9. Return to the pot.

10. Stir in cream if using, and heat gently.

11. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

12. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The squash should feel completely soft before you blend. The soup should smell sweet and savory, like cooked squash and onion. It should feel silky, with no hard cubes left.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The soup should look smooth and golden-orange, not watery and not brown from scorching.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The soup should look smooth and golden-orange, not watery and not brown from scorching.

SAFETY
Cutting hard squash is a common time people slip. Buy pre-cut cubes if that is safer for you. Blend hot soup carefully. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Roasting the squash at 400 degrees F until tender, then blending it with hot broth, gives a deeper flavor. A swirl of cream at the end is nice.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
