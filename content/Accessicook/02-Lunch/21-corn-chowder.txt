ACCESSICOOK
Corn Chowder
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A creamy soup of corn, potato, and milk, sometimes with cooked bacon. It is thick, sweet, and good for a cooler day.

SERVINGS
4 to 6

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons butter, or drippings from 4 slices cooked bacon
- 1 onion, diced
- 1 potato, diced
- 3 cups corn kernels, fresh, frozen, or drained canned
- 2 cups broth
- 1 cup milk or cream
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- Optional: cooked bacon, crumbled, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
The chowder should feel thick and chunky, with tender potato and plenty of corn kernels you can feel in a spoonful.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

3. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

4. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

5. Add the potato, corn, broth, and salt.

6. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

7. Simmer 12 to 15 minutes, until the potato is tender.

8. Stir in the milk and pepper.

9. Heat gently until hot, but do not boil hard.

10. Top with cooked bacon if you like.

11. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

12. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The potato should mash easily. The soup should smell sweet from the corn and savory from the onion. It should feel creamy, not thin and watery, and not pasty.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chowder should look pale gold and creamy, with visible corn. It should not look oily or curdled.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chowder should look pale gold and creamy, with visible corn. It should not look oily or curdled.

SAFETY
If you use bacon, cook it fully first. Do not boil hard after the milk goes in, or it may curdle. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, gently, stirring. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Frozen corn works well and does not need to be thawed. A pinch of paprika or thyme is a good extra. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). Blend a cup of the soup and stir it back in if you want it thicker.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
