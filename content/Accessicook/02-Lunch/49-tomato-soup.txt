Tomato Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A smooth, simple tomato soup made from canned tomatoes, onion, and broth. It is a classic partner for the Grilled Cheese in the Midnight Snacks chapter, and it also works as a lunch on its own.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon olive oil or butter
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 can (28 ounces) crushed tomatoes
- 2 cups vegetable or chicken broth
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1 teaspoon sugar, optional, to balance acidity
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) cream, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Canned crushed tomatoes feel thick and slightly seedy. The finished soup should feel smooth and creamy if you blend it, or gently rustic if you leave it as is.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mince the garlic. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

3. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

4. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

5. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

6. Add the tomatoes, broth, salt, and optional sugar.

7. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

8. Simmer 15 to 20 minutes, stirring now and then.

9. Blend until smooth if you want a silky soup, then return it to the pot.

10. Stir in cream, if using, after you take the pot off a hard boil.

11. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

12. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: It should smell sweet and cooked, not raw and tinny. It should feel smooth on the tongue if blended. A spoon should be lightly coated.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The soup should look even and red to orange-red, and it should look slightly thick, not like thin tomato juice. If you add cream, it will look paler and silkier.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The soup should look even and red to orange-red, and it should look slightly thick, not like thin tomato juice. If you add cream, it will look paler and silkier.

SAFETY
The pot is hot. If you blend, let the soup cool slightly, do not fill the blender more than halfway, hold the lid with a towel, and vent steam. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until the soup bubbles. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A grilled cheese on the side turns this into a fuller meal. Basil is a good finish. An immersion blender in the pot is easier and safer than a jar blender for hot soup.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
