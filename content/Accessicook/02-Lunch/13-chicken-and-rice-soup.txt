Chicken and Rice Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple soup of chicken, rice, and vegetables in broth. The rice cooks in the pot and thickens the soup slightly. Cook raw chicken to 165 degrees F, or add leftover chicken at the end.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots, sliced
- 2 celery stalks, sliced
- 8 cups chicken broth
- 1 pound boneless chicken, or 2 cups cooked chopped chicken
- 2/3 cup (two-thirds of a cup) long-grain white rice, rinsed
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper

TACTILE TIPS
Rinse the rice until the water feels less cloudy. The finished rice should feel tender, not hard in the center. The soup will thicken as the rice releases starch.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

3. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

4. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

5. If using raw chicken, simmer it in the broth until it is 165 degrees F, then shred it and return it to the pot. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

6. Add leftover cooked chicken in the last 5 minutes, and heat the soup to 165 degrees F.

7. Add the broth, salt, pepper, and raw chicken if you are using it.

8. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

9. Add the rice after the chicken is cooking or is already cooked, then simmer 15 to 18 minutes, until the rice is tender.

10. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Chicken must be 165 degrees F. Rice should feel tender. Vegetables should feel soft. You should smell savory chicken and rice, not a scorched bottom, so stir now and then.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and cooked. The rice should look plump, not chalky. The broth may look a little cloudy, which is normal.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and cooked. The rice should look plump, not chalky. The broth may look a little cloudy, which is normal.

SAFETY
Handle raw chicken carefully if you start with raw meat. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. The rice will keep absorbing broth in the fridge, so add liquid when you reheat. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you use leftover rice instead of dry rice, add it in the last 5 minutes so it does not fall apart. A squeeze of lemon at the end is nice.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
