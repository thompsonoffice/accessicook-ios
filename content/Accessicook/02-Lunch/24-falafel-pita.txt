Falafel Pita
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Warm pita bread filled with falafel, vegetables, and a yogurt or tahini sauce. Use baked or pan-warmed falafel, store-bought or homemade if you already have some. This page focuses on building the sandwich safely and neatly.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes, using already-made or frozen falafel

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food

INGREDIENTS
- 4 to 6 cooked falafel patties or balls
- 2 pita breads, cut in half to make pockets, or left whole and wrapped
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) chopped cucumber and tomato
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) yogurt sauce or hummus
- Lettuce or pickled peppers, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Pita should feel soft. If it is dry, warm it so the pocket does not split. Falafel should feel hot and crisp outside, and cooked in the center, not pasty and cold.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the falafel until the centers are 165 degrees F and the outsides feel crisp.

2. Warm the pita until it feels flexible.

3. Lay the bread, roll, or wrap on the board.

4. Open the pita pocket gently.

5. Spread sauce inside.

6. Add falafel, vegetables, and more sauce.

7. Cut in half if you like. Cut in means rub or cut cold butter into flour until the mix feels like crumbs.

8. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The falafel should feel hot and cooked through. The pita should feel warm and flexible. Vegetables should feel crisp.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The falafel should look browned. The pita should look soft and puffed, not burnt.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The falafel should look browned. The pita should look soft and puffed, not burnt.

SAFETY
Reheat leftover or frozen falafel until the center is 165 degrees F. Warm pita in an oven or dry skillet, not by holding it over a burner with your fingers. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
See Hummus and Tzatziki-style Ranch or yogurt dip ideas in the Misc chapter. A drizzle of hot sauce is good if you like heat.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
