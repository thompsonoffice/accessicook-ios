Black Bean Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A thick soup of canned or cooked black beans with onion, garlic, and cumin. You can blend part of it for a creamier body without cream.

SERVINGS
4 to 6

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 1 bell pepper, diced, optional
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 teaspoons ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon chili powder
- 3 cans (15 ounces each) black beans, drained and rinsed, or 5 cups cooked beans
- 3 cups vegetable or chicken broth
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- Lime wedges and optional cilantro, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Rinse canned beans. The finished soup should feel thick and hearty. If you blend some of it, it should feel creamy with some whole beans left for texture.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mince the garlic. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

3. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

4. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

5. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

6. Add the cumin and chili powder, then the beans, broth, and salt.

7. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

8. Simmer 15 minutes.

9. Mash or blend about half the soup, then stir it back in.

10. Heat 2 minutes more.

11. Serve with lime.

12. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

13. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The beans should feel soft. The soup should smell earthy, with cumin and garlic. Lime at the end should smell bright. A spoon should stand a little in a thick soup, but it should still be spoonable.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The soup should look dark and thick, not watery. Lime and cilantro, if used, should look fresh on top.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The soup should look dark and thick, not watery. Lime and cilantro, if used, should look fresh on top.

SAFETY
If you blend hot soup, do it carefully, with the lid vented and a towel on top, or use an immersion blender in the pot. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A spoon of sour cream or yogurt on top is good. Leftover taco seasoning can stand in for the cumin and chili powder.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
