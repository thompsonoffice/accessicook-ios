ACCESSICOOK
Minestrone
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
An Italian-style vegetable soup with beans and a little pasta in a tomato broth. It is flexible. Use what vegetables you have.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots, diced
- 2 celery stalks, diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 can (14 1/2 ounces) diced tomatoes
- 6 cups vegetable broth
- 1 can (15 ounces) beans, drained and rinsed
- 1 cup small pasta or diced potato
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1 teaspoon Italian seasoning
- 1 cup chopped spinach or zucchini, optional
- Parmesan, for serving, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Cut vegetables into even, bite-size pieces. The pasta will swell, so a small amount is enough.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mince the garlic. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

3. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

4. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

5. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

6. Add the tomatoes, broth, beans, salt, and Italian seasoning.

7. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

8. Simmer 15 minutes, then add the pasta or potato, and any zucchini.

9. Simmer 10 minutes more, until tender.

10. Stir in spinach at the end, if using, until it wilts.

11. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

12. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Vegetables and pasta should feel tender. The broth should smell tomatoey and herby. Beans should feel soft, not hard and dusty.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The soup should look chunky and colorful, with tender vegetables and beans, and the pasta should look cooked, not chalky in the center.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The soup should look chunky and colorful, with tender vegetables and beans, and the pasta should look cooked, not chalky in the center.

SAFETY
The pot is hot. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. The pasta will keep absorbing broth in the fridge, so add a splash of water when you reheat. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Add the pasta only in the last 10 minutes. A rind of Parmesan simmered in the pot, then removed, adds a lot of flavor if you have one.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
