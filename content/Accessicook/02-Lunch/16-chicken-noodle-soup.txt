Chicken Noodle Soup
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A comforting soup of chicken, vegetables, broth, and noodles. You can start with raw chicken and cook it in the broth to 165 degrees F, or use leftover cooked chicken and add it at the end, reheated in the soup until the soup is 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots, sliced
- 2 celery stalks, sliced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 8 cups chicken broth
- 1 pound boneless chicken thighs or breasts, or 2 cups chopped cooked chicken
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- 4 ounces egg noodles or other small pasta
- Optional: a little chopped parsley

TACTILE TIPS
The vegetables should be cut into even, bite-size pieces. Noodles will swell in the broth, so do not add the whole box.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mince the garlic. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

3. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

4. Add the onion, and the other aromatics.

5. Cook, stirring, until they feel soft and smell sweet, about 4 to 6 minutes.

6. If you are using raw chicken, add it with the broth, salt, and pepper, and simmer until a thermometer in the thickest piece reads 165 degrees F, then lift it out, shred it, and return it to the pot. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

7. If you are using leftover cooked chicken, add it in the last 5 minutes, and heat the soup until it is 165 degrees F.

8. Add the broth, salt, and pepper, and the raw chicken if you are using it. The carrots and celery should already be in the pot with the onion.

9. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

10. Simmer 15 to 20 minutes, until the vegetables are tender.

11. Add the noodles in the last 8 minutes, or follow the pasta package time.

12. Taste and add salt if it needs it.

13. Add parsley if you like.

14. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

15. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Raw chicken must reach 165 degrees F before you shred it. Noodles should feel tender. Vegetables should feel soft, not hard in the center. You should smell savory broth, onion, and chicken.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with no raw pink. The soup should look clear-to-golden and gently bubbling, not cloudy and muddy from over-boiled noodles, though some cloudiness is normal.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with no raw pink. The soup should look clear-to-golden and gently bubbling, not cloudy and muddy from over-boiled noodles, though some cloudiness is normal.

SAFETY
Wash hands and the board after raw chicken. If you use leftovers, bring the soup back to 165 degrees F. The pot and steam are hot. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cook the noodles in the soup only for the last 8 minutes so they do not turn to mush. For a clearer broth, simmer gently, not at a hard boil.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
