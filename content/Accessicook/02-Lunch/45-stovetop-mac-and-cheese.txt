Stovetop Mac and Cheese
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A pot of macaroni in a quick cheese sauce made with milk and shredded cheese. It is a classic lunch or side. This is the stovetop version. A baked version is in the Supper chapter.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A colander

INGREDIENTS
- 8 ounces elbow macaroni
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the pasta water
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) milk
- 2 cups shredded cheddar cheese
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, or to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper or paprika

TACTILE TIPS
The sauce should feel smooth and coat the pasta, not grainy and not as thin as milk. Shredded cheese melts more evenly if you add it off the hard boil.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cook the macaroni in salted boiling water until it is tender but still slightly firm.

2. Drain, saving 1/2 cup (half a cup) of the pasta water. Drain means pour off the liquid.

3. In the same pot, melt the butter over medium-low heat.

4. Stir in the flour.

5. Cook 1 minute, until the raw flour smell fades.

6. Whisk in the milk slowly. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk until the mixture looks even.

7. Cook, stirring, until the sauce thickens slightly and you feel no lumps.

8. Take the pot off the heat.

9. Stir in the cheese until it melts.

10. Add the pasta, salt, and pepper.

11. Toss, using a splash of pasta water if it feels tight.

12. Serve at once, while the sauce is creamy.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The sauce should feel silky and the pasta should feel tender. You should smell butter and cheese, not scorched milk or raw flour.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look smooth and even, not oily or grainy. The pasta should look well coated, not dry.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look smooth and even, not oily or grainy. The pasta should look well coated, not dry.

SAFETY
The pot and the sauce are very hot. Do not leave dairy sauce out. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F with a splash of milk. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Take the pot off the heat before you add the cheese so the sauce stays smooth. Pre-shredded cheese can be grainier. A pinch of mustard powder deepens the flavor. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
