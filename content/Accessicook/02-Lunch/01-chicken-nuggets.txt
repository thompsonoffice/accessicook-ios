Chicken Nuggets
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
This recipe was on the original request list for the book.

These baked chicken nuggets are bite-size pieces of boneless chicken with a crisp breadcrumb coating, meant for lunch, a casual supper, or a plate of snacks. They are cousins of the boneless wings in the Misc chapter, but they are not tossed in hot sauce. Bake them until a thermometer in the center of a thick nugget reads 165 degrees F. An air-fryer method is included as a variation.

SERVINGS
4 as a lunch, or 6 as a snack

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- Two shallow bowls or pie plates for breading
- Tongs or a fork
- A wire rack for the pan, optional
- An air fryer, optional, for the variation

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds) boneless, skinless chicken breasts or thighs
- 1 teaspoon salt, divided
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) garlic powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) paprika, optional
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) all-purpose flour
- 2 large eggs
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) dry breadcrumbs or panko
- Oil spray, or 2 tablespoons oil for brushing
- Dipping sauces, such as ketchup, barbecue sauce, or honey mustard, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Chicken breasts feel smooth and firm, with a thicker middle; thighs are a bit looser and more even in thickness. Cut the meat into chunks about 1 to 1 1/2 inches, roughly the size of a large walnut or the first two joints of a finger, so they cook at the same rate. Flour feels silky. Breadcrumbs feel coarser and dry. The breaded nuggets should feel dry and sandy on the outside, with no slick, bare spots.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F and set a rack in the middle.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment or foil, set a rack on it if you have one, and oil the surface lightly.

3. Cut the chicken into even 1 to 1 1/2 inch (one to one and a half inch) pieces.

4. Pat them dry and toss with 1/2 teaspoon salt, the pepper, garlic powder, and paprika.

5. Set up three bowls: flour mixed with the remaining 1/2 teaspoon salt; beaten eggs; and breadcrumbs.

6. Using one hand for wet and one for dry if you can, dip each piece in flour, then egg, then breadcrumbs, pressing so the crumbs stick.

7. Set the pieces on the prepared pan with space between them.

8. Spray or brush the tops lightly with oil.

9. Bake 10 minutes, then turn the pieces and bake 6 to 10 minutes more.

10. Check doneness in the center of two or three of the thickest nuggets.

11. They are done at 165 degrees F.

12. If any are under, return the pan for 2 to 3 minutes and check again.

13. Serve hot with dipping sauce.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The coating should feel firm and a little crisp, and the meat should feel springy, not squishy. You should smell cooked chicken and toasted crumbs, not raw meat. A thermometer in the center of the thickest piece must read 165 degrees F.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The coating should look golden and dry, not pale and pasty or dark brown. When you cut a thick nugget, the chicken should be opaque and white or very light tan all the way through, with no raw pink or glassy look, and the juices should run clear.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The coating should look golden and dry, not pale and pasty or dark brown. When you cut a thick nugget, the chicken should be opaque and white or very light tan all the way through, with no raw pink or glassy look, and the juices should run clear.

SAFETY
Use a raw-poultry board and wash your hands, the knife, and any tongs that touched raw chicken before you handle sauces or plates. Do not taste raw egg or raw flour from the breading bowls. Use long mitts on the hot pan. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Panko crumbs give a lighter crunch than fine breadcrumbs. For an air fryer, cook a single layer at 375 to 400 degrees F for about 10 to 14 minutes, turning once, still to 165 degrees F. Do not crowd the basket. Thighs stay a bit juicier than breasts. To freeze, bread the nuggets, freeze them on a tray, then bag them and bake from frozen, adding a few minutes and still checking 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Reheat leftover nuggets in a 375 degree F oven or air fryer until the center reaches 165 degrees F. A microwave will work for safety but softens the coating. Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
