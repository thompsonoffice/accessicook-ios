Caprese Skewers
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Small skewers of tomato, mozzarella, and basil, finished with oil, salt, and optional balsamic vinegar. This is a no-cook appetizer. Use ripe but still firm tomatoes so they stay on the skewer.

SERVINGS
About 12 skewers

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 12 small mozzarella balls, or 8 ounces mozzarella, cut into 12 cubes
- 12 cherry or grape tomatoes
- 12 fresh basil leaves, or as many as you have, folded if they are large
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- Optional: 1 teaspoon balsamic vinegar, or a thick balsamic glaze, for drizzling
- 12 small skewers or sturdy toothpicks

TACTILE TIPS
The mozzarella should feel moist and slightly springy, not slimy. The tomatoes should feel ripe but still firm, not collapsed. A finished skewer should feel stable, with the basil tucked so it does not slip off.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Drain the mozzarella if it is packed in water, and pat it dry. Drain means pour off the liquid.

2. Thread a tomato, a basil leaf, and a mozzarella ball or cube onto each skewer, in any order you can handle easily.

3. Repeat if the skewers are longer.

4. Arrange the skewers on a plate.

5. Drizzle with oil and optional vinegar, and sprinkle with salt.

6. Serve at once, or cover and refrigerate for up to 2 hours, then add the oil and salt just before serving if you want them to stay fresh-looking.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The mozzarella should feel moist and fresh, and it should smell clean and milky, not sour or ammonia-like. Tomatoes should smell fresh and sweet. Basil should smell green and fragrant, not black and slimy.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The skewers should look neat and colorful, with shiny oil, not watery and not wilted if you serve them soon.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The skewers should look neat and colorful, with shiny oil, not watery and not wilted if you serve them soon.

SAFETY
Skewer points are sharp. Work slowly, and set a guard or a cork on the point if you are passing a platter among children or anyone who might grab the point. Keep the platter refrigerated until serving, and do not leave it out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you do not have basil, a little dried oregano in the oil is an acceptable stand-in, though the plate will be less fragrant. See also Caprese Salad in the Lunch chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
