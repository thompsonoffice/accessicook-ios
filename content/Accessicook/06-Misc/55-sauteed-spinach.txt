Sauteed Spinach
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A large pile of fresh spinach cooked quickly in a little oil or butter with garlic until it is wilted, meaning collapsed and tender, but still bright. Spinach shrinks a lot, so start with more than you think you need. Saute means cook quickly in a little fat, stirring.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound fresh spinach, washed and dried well, or 2 bags, about 10 ounces each
- 1 tablespoon olive oil or butter
- 2 garlic cloves, minced, or 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) garlic powder
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- Optional: a squeeze of lemon, or a pinch of red pepper flakes

TACTILE TIPS
Washed spinach should feel dry enough that it will saute, not steam into a puddle, though some moisture is all right. Wilted spinach should feel tender and collapsed, not raw and springy, and not slimy and khaki-colored from a long cook. Mince means cut the garlic into very small pieces.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil or butter in a wide skillet over medium heat.

2. Add the garlic and cook 20 to 30 seconds, until it smells fragrant, not brown and bitter.

3. Add the spinach in handfuls, stirring as it wilts, so more leaves will fit.

4. Cook 2 to 4 minutes, until all the leaves feel wilted and tender, and most of the liquid has cooked off.

5. Add the salt and any optional lemon or red pepper flakes.

6. Taste a cooled bite, and add salt if it needs it.

7. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The leaves should feel wilted and tender. You should smell garlic and cooked greens, not burning garlic. The pan should not have a large puddle of water, though a little moisture is normal.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The spinach should look dark and glossy and collapsed, still fairly green, not dull olive and watery in a big puddle.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The spinach should look dark and glossy and collapsed, still fairly green, not dull olive and watery in a big puddle.

SAFETY
The pan is hot and can spatter. Wet spinach will spatter more, so dry it well. Mince garlic on a stable board. A large pile of spinach can overflow the pan, so add it in handfuls. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Frozen spinach can be thawed, squeezed very dry, and heated in the same oil and garlic until it is 165 degrees F and hot all the way through. A little butter at the end is a good finish.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
