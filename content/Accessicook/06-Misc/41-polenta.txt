Polenta
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A creamy cornmeal porridge, served soft as a side, often under meats or vegetables, or cooled and later sliced. Stir often so it does not scorch. Instant polenta is faster, and you should follow the package liquid amount if it differs. This is similar in comfort to the Grits in the Breakfast chapter, but usually made with a finer cornmeal.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes for regular polenta, or about 10 minutes for instant

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup regular or instant polenta or yellow cornmeal, not self-rising
- 4 cups water or a mix of water and milk, or as the package directs
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) grated Parmesan cheese, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The cooked polenta should feel smooth and creamy, like a thick porridge, not gritty and raw, and not so stiff that it tears away from the sides in a hard mass unless you want it firm enough to slice later. A spoon should stand for a moment, then slowly fall.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Bring the water or water-and-milk and the salt to a boil in a heavy pot, meaning large, rolling bubbles.

2. Whisk in the polenta slowly, so it does not clump. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk as you pour.

3. Reduce the heat to low. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens.

4. For regular polenta, cook 20 to 30 minutes, stirring often, until it feels creamy and the raw cornmeal taste is gone.

5. For instant polenta, cook 3 to 5 minutes, or as the package directs, stirring.

6. Stir in the butter and optional Parmesan.

7. If it is too thick, add a splash of water or milk.

8. Taste a cooled spoonful, and add salt if it needs it.

9. Serve at once, because soft polenta firms as it sits.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The polenta should feel creamy and smooth, not gritty. You should smell cooked corn, butter, and optional cheese, not raw meal or scorching. If you smell scorching, take the pot off the heat and stop scraping the bottom into the serving bowl.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The polenta should look smooth and golden, not lumpy, and not scorched and brown at the edges of the pot. Soft polenta should look pourable but thick, like a loose mash.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The polenta should look smooth and golden, not lumpy, and not scorched and brown at the edges of the pot. Soft polenta should look pourable but thick, like a loose mash.

SAFETY
Bubbling polenta can spatter and is very hot, like thick lava. Stir with a long-handled spoon, and keep your face back. Use a heavy pot and low heat. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F with extra liquid, stirring, or slice cooled polenta and brown the slices in a skillet until they are hot in the center. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For firm, sliceable polenta, spread the cooked mix in a greased dish, chill until set, then cut and brown. Soft polenta is a good bed for the Garlic Butter Shrimp or the Chickpea Curry in the Supper chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
