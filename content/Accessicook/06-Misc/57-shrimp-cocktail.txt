Shrimp Cocktail
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Shrimp simmered just until they are opaque, firm, and loosely curled, then chilled and served with Cocktail Sauce from this chapter. Shrimp cook very fast. They should curl into a loose C shape, not a tight O, which can mean they are overcooked. Keep them cold after cooking.

SERVINGS
4 as an appetizer

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes, plus chilling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound large raw shrimp, peeled and deveined, tails on if you like, for a handle
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the water
- Optional: a slice of lemon, or 1 bay leaf, for the water
- 1 recipe Cocktail Sauce, for serving
- Lemon wedges, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Raw shrimp feel slightly translucent and soft. Deveined means the dark vein along the back has been removed. Done shrimp feel firm and springy, not mushy, and not tough as rubber, and they should be opaque. After chilling, they should feel cold and firm.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Bring a pot of water, the salt, and any optional lemon or bay leaf to a boil, meaning large, rolling bubbles, then reduce to a simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

2. Add the shrimp.

3. Cook 2 to 4 minutes, until they are opaque, firm, and loosely curled.

4. Drain, then cool the shrimp quickly in an ice bath, meaning a bowl of ice and water, or under cold running water, until they feel cold. Drain means pour off the liquid.

5. Drain well and pat dry.

6. Refrigerate until you serve them, at least 20 minutes if they are not already fully cold.

7. Serve cold with cocktail sauce and lemon wedges.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The shrimp should feel firm, springy, and cold, and they should smell sweet and briny, not ammonia-like or sour. If they smell off before cooking, throw them out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The shrimp should look opaque and pink or pearly, not gray and translucent, and they should curl into a loose C, not a tight O. They should look moist, not dry and stringy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The shrimp should look opaque and pink or pearly, not gray and translucent, and they should curl into a loose C, not a tight O. They should look moist, not dry and stringy.

SAFETY
Keep raw shrimp cold, then wash the board, knife, and your hands. Do not leave cooked shrimp out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Keep the platter on ice if it will sit out at a party. Ice-bath water is messy. Work near the sink. Cook shrimp until the flesh is pearly, opaque, and firm. It should curl into a loose C shape, not a tight O, which can mean it is overcooked.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
You can also buy already-cooked chilled shrimp and skip the simmer, then still serve them very cold with sauce. Taste one after cooking and cooling so you can adjust the time next time, because size changes the minutes a lot.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
