ACCESSICOOK
Pizza Sauce
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A thick, no-cook or barely cooked tomato sauce for the Sheet-Pan Pizza in the Supper chapter. Because it does not simmer long, it should start with a thick canned tomato product, such as tomato puree or thick crushed tomatoes, and enough salt and herbs to taste good even before it bakes. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

SERVINGS
About 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups), enough for 2 sheet-pan pizzas

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 can (15 ounces) tomato puree or thick crushed tomatoes
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) garlic powder, or 1 small garlic clove, minced
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) sugar, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The sauce should feel thick enough to spread on dough without running off, like a loose paste, not watery. Mince means cut the garlic into very small pieces if you use fresh garlic.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the tomato puree, oil, oregano, salt, garlic, and optional sugar in a bowl until even.

2. Taste a little, and add salt or a pinch of sugar if it needs it. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

3. Use at once on pizza, or refrigerate in a closed container for up to 5 days.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The sauce should feel thick and spreadable. You should smell tomato, oregano, and garlic. If you use fresh garlic, it will taste sharper than garlic powder, which is all right on a pizza that will bake.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look thick and even, red and slightly glossy from the oil, not watery.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look thick and even, red and slightly glossy from the oil, not watery.

SAFETY
If you use fresh garlic, mince it on a stable board. Refrigerate the sauce, and do not leave it out more than 2 hours. If you want a fully cooked sauce, simmer this mix 10 minutes instead, then cool, and reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is intentionally thicker than marinara. If it seems thin, stir in 1 tablespoon tomato paste.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
