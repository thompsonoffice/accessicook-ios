Deviled Eggs
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Hard-boiled eggs halved and filled with a creamy yolk mix. Cook the eggs until the whites and yolks are firm, as in Hard-Boiled Eggs in the Breakfast chapter. Cool them fully before you peel and fill them. Keep the finished eggs cold.

SERVINGS
12 halves, 6 appetizer servings

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes, including the time to hard-boil and cool the eggs

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 6 large eggs, hard-boiled and cooled, from the Breakfast chapter
- 3 tablespoons mayonnaise
- 1 teaspoon mustard
- 1 teaspoon vinegar or pickle juice, optional
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- Paprika or chopped chives, for a topping, optional

TACTILE TIPS
A well-cooked yolk should feel firm and dry-crumbly, not wet and liquid, and not with a gray-green dry ring if you can help it, though that ring is a sign of a longer cook, not unsafety, once the egg was fully cooked and then cooled. The filling should feel smooth and pipeable or spoonable, not stiff and dry, and not runny.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Peel the cooled eggs.

2. Cut each egg in half lengthwise on a stable board.

3. Scoop the yolks into a bowl.

4. Set the whites on a plate, cut-side up.

5. Mash the yolks with the mayonnaise, mustard, optional vinegar, and salt until the mix feels smooth.

6. Taste a little, and add salt if it needs it.

7. If the filling is too stiff, add mayonnaise 1 teaspoon at a time.

8. Spoon or pipe the filling into the whites.

9. Add optional paprika or chives.

10. Serve cold, or cover and refrigerate until you serve them.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The filling should feel smooth and creamy. You should smell cooked egg, mayonnaise, and mustard, not a sulfur smell so strong it seems off, and not a sour mayonnaise smell. The whites should feel firm and cool, not slimy.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The filling should look smooth and pale yellow, mounded neatly in the whites, not watery, and not dry and crumbly. The whites should look intact, not badly torn.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The filling should look smooth and pale yellow, mounded neatly in the whites, not watery, and not dry and crumbly. The whites should look intact, not badly torn.

SAFETY
Use fully cooked, firm hard-boiled eggs that were cooled and refrigerated. Keep deviled eggs refrigerated, and do not leave them out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Eat within 3 to 4 days. Wash the board after cutting the eggs. Cook eggs until the yolk and white are firm. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little pickle relish or a pinch of paprika in the filling is a good extra. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). If the shells are hard to peel, try older eggs, and peel them under a thin stream of cold water.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
