ACCESSICOOK
Queso
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A party-size warm cheese dip, similar to the Queso and Chips in the Midnight Snacks chapter, written here for a larger platter. Keep the pot on very low heat, and do not leave the dip out more than 2 hours. Reheat leftovers until bubbling, 165 degrees F, stirring, with a splash of milk.

SERVINGS
8 as an appetizer, about 3 cups

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 3 cups shredded melting cheese, such as cheddar or a Mexican blend
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) milk, plus more if the dip is too thick
- 1 can (10 ounces) diced tomatoes with green chiles, drained a little, or 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) salsa
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, if the cheese is not salty
- Tortilla chips, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Shredded cheese melts more evenly than cubes. The dip should feel smooth and pourable, thick enough to coat a chip, not oily and split, and not a solid clump. If it feels grainy, lower the heat and add a splash of milk, and stir.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Set a pot over low to medium-low heat.

2. Add the milk and the tomatoes or salsa.

3. When the mix is hot and steaming, add the cheese a handful at a time, stirring until it melts before you add more.

4. If the dip is too thick, add milk 1 tablespoon at a time.

5. If it is too thin, cook, stirring, another minute or two.

6. Taste a cooled spoonful, and add a pinch of salt if it needs it. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

7. Serve warm with chips, and keep the pot on the lowest heat, stirring now and then.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The dip should feel smooth and hot, and it should coat a chip. You should smell melted cheese and chiles, not scorched milk.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The queso should look smooth and evenly orange or red-orange, not oily with a puddle of separated fat, and not lumpy with unmelted cheese.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The queso should look smooth and evenly orange or red-orange, not oily with a puddle of separated fat, and not lumpy with unmelted cheese.

SAFETY
Use low heat. Cheese sauces scorch and split on high heat. The pot and dip are very hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, and reheat to 165 degrees F, or until the dip is bubbling again, stirring often. Add a splash of milk when you reheat. Do not leave the dip out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of cumin is a good extra. A slow cooker on the warm setting can hold the dip for a party if you stir now and then and still follow the 2-hour guidance for food sitting in the temperature danger zone, or keep it above 140 degrees F if you are holding it hot.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
