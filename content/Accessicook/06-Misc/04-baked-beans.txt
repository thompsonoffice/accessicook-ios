ACCESSICOOK
Baked Beans
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A sweet-savory pot of beans baked with onion, a little mustard, and a sweetener, until the sauce is thick and the beans are hot, 165 degrees F in the center. This version starts with canned beans to keep the time reasonable. It is a classic picnic and supper side.

SERVINGS
6 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour with canned beans, or much longer if you start from dried beans

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cans (15 ounces each) navy beans or pinto beans, drained and rinsed, or 3 cups cooked beans
- 1/2 onion, diced
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) ketchup or tomato sauce
- 2 tablespoons brown sugar or molasses
- 1 tablespoon mustard
- 1 tablespoon vinegar
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) water or broth, plus more if the pot looks dry
- Optional: 2 slices cooked bacon, crumbled, for stirring in at the end

TACTILE TIPS
The sauce should feel thick enough to coat the beans, not watery, and not pasty and dry. The beans should feel tender and hot. If you add leftover bacon, it should already be fully cooked, then heated through in the pot.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F, or plan to simmer the beans on the stove. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

2. Mix the beans, onion, ketchup or tomato sauce, brown sugar or molasses, mustard, vinegar, salt, and water in a baking dish or in a pot.

3. Cover and bake 45 to 60 minutes, stirring once, until the sauce feels thick and the beans are bubbling and 165 degrees F, or simmer on the stove over low heat 25 to 35 minutes, stirring often so they do not scorch.

4. Add a splash of water if the mix gets too thick before the onion is soft.

5. Stir in optional cooked bacon.

6. Taste a cooled spoonful, and add salt or vinegar if it needs it.

7. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The beans should feel tender and steaming hot, 165 degrees F. The sauce should feel thick and should coat a spoon. You should smell tomato, sweet molasses or brown sugar, and onion, not scorching.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look thick, dark, and glossy, coating the beans, not watery, and not dried to the edges in a hard crust unless you like a little browned edge.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look thick, dark, and glossy, coating the beans, not watery, and not dried to the edges in a hard crust unless you like a little browned edge.

SAFETY
Use mitts if you bake the dish. The pot and sauce are very hot, and a sugar-tomato sauce can scorch on the bottom if you stop stirring on the stove. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, and reheat to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Molasses gives a deeper flavor than brown sugar. These are good with cornbread from the Baked Goods chapter, and with pulled pork from the Supper chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
