Potato Salad
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Cooked potato chunks mixed with a creamy dressing, often with celery, onion, and chopped hard-boiled egg. Cool the potatoes until they are just warm before you dress them, then chill the salad. If you add eggs, they should be fully cooked, firm hard-boiled eggs, as in the Breakfast chapter.

SERVINGS
6 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes, plus chilling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 2 pounds waxy potatoes, such as red or Yukon Gold, cut into 3/4 inch (three-quarters of an inch) chunks
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the cooking water, plus 3/4 teaspoon (three-quarters of a teaspoon) for the dressing, or to taste
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) mayonnaise
- 1 tablespoon mustard
- 1 tablespoon vinegar or pickle juice
- 2 celery stalks, diced
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) diced onion, or 2 sliced green onions
- 2 hard-boiled eggs, chopped, optional
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper

TACTILE TIPS
Waxy potatoes hold their shape better than russets in a salad. A fork should slide into a chunk when they are done, but the chunks should not fall apart. The dressed salad should feel creamy and cohesive, with tender potato pieces, not watery and not mashed.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the potatoes in a pot.

2. Cover with cold water, add the 1 tablespoon salt, and bring to a simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

3. Cook 8 to 12 minutes, until a fork slides in and the chunks still hold their shape.

4. Drain well. Drain means pour off the liquid.

5. Let the potatoes cool until they are just warm, about 15 minutes, stirring once so they cool more evenly.

6. Mix the mayonnaise, mustard, vinegar or pickle juice, 3/4 teaspoon (three-quarters of a teaspoon) salt, and pepper in a large bowl.

7. Add the potatoes, celery, onion, and optional eggs.

8. Toss gently until coated.

9. Taste a cooled bite, and add salt or vinegar if it needs it.

10. Chill at least 1 hour before serving.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The potatoes should feel tender and hold their shape. You should smell potato, mayonnaise, and a little vinegar or pickle, not a sour, off leftover smell. The salad should feel cool and cohesive after chilling.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The salad should look creamy, with visible chunks of potato and optional egg, not a mash and not dry.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The salad should look creamy, with visible chunks of potato and optional egg, not a mash and not dry.

SAFETY
Cool and refrigerate the salad within 2 hours of cooking the potatoes. Because of mayonnaise and optional eggs, keep it cold, and do not leave it out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use hard-boiled eggs that were cooked until firm and then chilled. Reheat is not the usual path here. Serve cold, and throw the salad out after 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. There should be no liquid raw egg left.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little chopped pickle or fresh dill is a good extra. If the salad seems dry after chilling, fold in 1 tablespoon mayonnaise. Fold means gently turn the mixture over itself so you do not knock out the air.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
