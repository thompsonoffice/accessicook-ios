Seven Layer Dip
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A cold party dip built in layers, usually beans, guacamole or avocado, sour cream, salsa, cheese, and toppings, served with chips. The exact seven layers can vary. Keep the dip cold, and do not leave it out more than 2 hours.

SERVINGS
10 as an appetizer

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 can (16 ounces) refried beans, or 1 recipe Refried Beans from this chapter, cooled
- 1 cup guacamole, from this chapter, or 1 ripe avocado, mashed with a pinch of salt and 1 teaspoon lime juice
- 1 cup sour cream
- 1 cup salsa, drained a little if it is very watery
- 1 cup shredded cheddar or a Mexican-blend cheese
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sliced olives or diced tomato
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) sliced green onion, or chopped cilantro
- Tortilla chips, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Each layer should feel spreadable so it does not pull up the layer under it. Refried beans should feel thick, not watery. The finished dip should feel cool, with distinct layers you can scoop through, not a soupy mix.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Spread the beans in a shallow, clear dish if you have one, or in a pie plate, in an even layer.

2. Add the guacamole or mashed avocado in a gentle layer.

3. A small spoonful at a time, dropped and then spread, helps.

4. Add the sour cream in the same gentle way, then the salsa.

5. Sprinkle the cheese, then the olives or tomato, then the green onion or cilantro.

6. Cover and refrigerate until you serve it, then serve with chips, keeping the dish cold.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The dip should feel cool and scoopable. You should smell beans, avocado, salsa, and cheese, not a sour, off dairy smell, and not rancid avocado. If any component smells or looks wrong before you layer, throw it out and skip or replace that layer.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The layers should look distinct, especially in a clear dish, with a colorful top of cheese and toppings, not watery and not already mixed.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The layers should look distinct, especially in a clear dish, with a colorful top of cheese and toppings, not watery and not already mixed.

SAFETY
Keep the dip refrigerated. Do not leave it out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Because it includes guacamole, sour cream, and salsa, treat it as highly perishable. Eat within 2 days. Provide a serving spoon so people are not double-dipping if that matters for your household. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A layer of shredded lettuce can go under the cheese, added just before serving so it stays crisp. Warm bean dip is a different recipe. This one is served cold.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
