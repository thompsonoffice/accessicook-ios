Fruit and Cheese Plate
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A larger, shareable version of the Cheese and Crackers snack in the Midnight Snacks chapter, with fruit added. Keep the cheese and any cut fruit cold until you plate them, then serve soon. Arrange items with a little space so people can find each one by touch.

SERVINGS
6 as an appetizer

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 8 ounces cheese, such as a mix of cheddar, Swiss, and a softer cheese, sliced or cubed
- 2 cups fruit, such as apple slices, grapes, or berries
- 1 handful of crackers, about 16 to 20
- Optional: a small bowl of nuts, only if no one has a nut allergy, or a small bowl of olives

TACTILE TIPS
Cheese straight from the refrigerator is firmer and easier to slice. Fruit should feel fresh and ripe, not fermented or mushy. Keep wet fruit, such as cut melon, in a small bowl so it does not sog the crackers.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Slice or cube the firmer cheeses on a stable board, using a cut-resistant glove on the hand that holds the cheese if you need it.

2. Leave a very soft cheese in a small bowl with a knife.

3. Wash the fruit.

4. Slice apples just before serving, or toss them with a little lemon juice so they brown more slowly.

5. Arrange the cheese, fruit, and crackers on a large plate or board, with a little space between groups, and put any nuts or olives in small bowls.

6. Serve soon, or cover and refrigerate for up to 1 hour, then bring it out just before people eat.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The cheese should feel cool and clean-cut, not slimy, and it should smell as you expect, not of ammonia or sourness. Fruit should smell fresh. Crackers should feel crisp. Nuts, if used, should smell toasted, not rancid.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The plate should look uncrowded, with distinct groups of cheese, fruit, and crackers, not a jumble, so each item is easy to pick up.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The plate should look uncrowded, with distinct groups of cheese, fruit, and crackers, not a jumble, so each item is easy to pick up.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a sharp knife, slowly. Do not serve nuts to anyone with a nut allergy. Keep perishable cheese and cut fruit refrigerated until serving, and do not leave the plate out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A small bowl of honey or jam is a good extra with a sharper cheese. Label the cheeses if they look or feel similar, so people can choose by name as well as by taste.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
