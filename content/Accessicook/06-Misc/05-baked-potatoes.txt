ACCESSICOOK
Baked Potatoes
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Whole russet potatoes baked until the skins are dry and the insides are fluffy. Pierce the skins before they go in the oven so steam can escape. For a faster method, see Microwave Baked Potato in the Midnight Snacks chapter.

SERVINGS
4 as a side or a simple meal

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 4 medium russet potatoes, scrubbed
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- Butter, sour cream, cheese, or other toppings, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
The potatoes should feel firm and heavy, with no soft or wet spots. After baking, a potato should yield when you squeeze it gently with a mitt on your hand, and a fork should slide into the center with almost no resistance.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Scrub the potatoes and dry them.

3. Pierce each one 5 or 6 times with a fork, all around.

4. Rub with oil and salt.

5. Set them on a pan or directly on the oven rack, with a pan on a lower rack to catch drips if you use the rack.

6. Bake 45 to 60 minutes, until a fork slides into the center easily and the potato feels soft when squeezed with a mitt.

7. Let them sit 5 minutes.

8. Cut open carefully, fluff the insides with a fork, and add toppings.

9. If you add leftover meat or chilled cooked bacon, those toppings should be heated to 165 degrees F, or added very hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide into the center easily. The potato should feel soft when squeezed with a mitt. You should smell baked potato, not burning. Steam will escape when you cut one open.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The skins should look dry and slightly crisp, not tight and raw, and not scorched. The insides should look fluffy and dry, not wet and hard in the center.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The skins should look dry and slightly crisp, not tight and raw, and not scorched. The insides should look fluffy and dry, not wet and hard in the center.

SAFETY
Pierce the skins before baking. Use mitts to squeeze-test and to cut the potatoes open. Keep your face back from the steam. The inside is extremely hot. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A large potato can take longer than 1 hour. For crispier skin, do not wrap the potatoes in foil. Foil makes a softer, steamed skin.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
