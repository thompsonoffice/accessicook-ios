Creamed Corn
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Corn kernels heated in a creamy sauce of milk, butter, and a little flour or cornstarch until the mix is thick and bubbling hot. Frozen corn works well and does not need to be thawed first.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 3 cups corn kernels, frozen or fresh, or two 15 ounce cans, drained
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 1 tablespoon flour, or 1 teaspoon cornstarch mixed with 1 tablespoon cold water
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) milk, plus more if the sauce is too thick
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper, optional
- 1 teaspoon sugar, optional, if the corn is not very sweet

TACTILE TIPS
The sauce should feel smooth and thick enough to coat the corn, not watery, and not pasty. The corn should feel tender and hot. Frozen kernels will go from icy to hot in a few minutes.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat.

2. Stir in the flour and cook 1 minute if you are using flour.

3. Whisk in the milk until the mix feels smooth. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk so there are no lumps.

4. Add the corn, salt, optional pepper, and optional sugar.

5. Cook, stirring, 5 to 8 minutes, until the corn is hot and the sauce feels slightly thick and is bubbling.

6. If you are using cornstarch instead of flour, stir it in now and cook 1 minute more.

7. If the mix is too thick, add a little milk.

8. Taste a cooled spoonful, and add salt if it needs it.

9. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The corn should feel tender and hot. The sauce should feel smooth and slightly thick, and it should bubble gently. You should smell sweet corn, butter, and milk, not scorched milk.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The mix should look creamy and pale gold, coating the kernels, not watery, and not scorched at the edges of the pan.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The mix should look creamy and pale gold, coating the kernels, not watery, and not scorched at the edges of the pan.

SAFETY
Stir often so the milk does not scorch. The pot and sauce are very hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, and reheat to 165 degrees F, or until the sauce bubbles again, stirring, with a splash of milk if it has thickened. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of paprika or a little grated cheddar at the end makes a richer side. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
