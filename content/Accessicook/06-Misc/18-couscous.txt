ACCESSICOOK
Couscous
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A very fast grain-like side made from tiny pasta pellets, usually sold as couscous. Most packaged couscous is precooked and only needs to sit in boiling water or broth until it is tender. This is faster than rice and is a good weeknight side.

SERVINGS
4 as a side, about 3 cups cooked

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup couscous
- 1 cup water or broth, or as the package directs
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, or less if the broth is salty
- 1 tablespoon olive oil or butter

TACTILE TIPS
Uncooked couscous feels like tiny dry pellets. After it sits in the hot liquid, it should feel tender and fluffy, not hard in the center, and not wet and clumped. Fluff means loosen the grains with a fork.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Bring the water or broth, salt, and oil or butter to a boil in a small pot, meaning large, rolling bubbles.

2. Stir in the couscous.

3. Take the pot off the heat and cover it.

4. Let it sit 5 minutes, or as the package directs, until the liquid is absorbed and the couscous feels tender.

5. Fluff with a fork.

6. Taste a cooled bite, and add salt if it needs it.

7. Serve hot, or cool and use in a salad.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The pellets should feel tender, not hard. You should smell a mild wheat and butter or oil smell, not raw, starchy pasta, and not scorching from a pot left on the heat.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The couscous should look fluffy and separate, not wet and not a tight cake, though it will pack together until you fluff it.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The couscous should look fluffy and separate, not wet and not a tight cake, though it will pack together until you fluff it.

SAFETY
The pot and steam are very hot. Tilt the lid away from you. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F with a splash of water, or serve cold and keep refrigerated. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A squeeze of lemon, chopped herbs, or leftover roasted vegetables can be folded in after fluffing. This is a good base for a quick lunch bowl.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
