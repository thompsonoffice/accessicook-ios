Iced Tea
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Black tea brewed strong, then cooled and served over ice, with optional lemon and sugar. Do not brew the tea for a very long time, or it can taste bitter and astringent, meaning dry and puckery. Cool the tea before you add a lot of ice, or the ice will melt and dilute it more than you want, unless you make an extra-strong brew on purpose.

SERVINGS
About 2 quarts, 8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 8 cups water, divided
- 4 to 6 tea bags, or 2 tablespoons loose black tea in a strainer
- 1/4 to 1/2 cup (a quarter to half a cup) sugar, optional, or to taste
- Lemon slices, optional
- Ice, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
The hot tea should smell fragrant, like black tea, not scorched. After steeping, it should taste strong, because it will be diluted a little by ice. Steep means let the tea sit in hot water so the flavor comes out.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Bring 4 cups of the water to a boil, meaning large, rolling bubbles, then take the pot off the heat.

2. Add the tea bags or loose tea.

3. Steep 3 to 5 minutes, then remove the tea.

4. If you are using sugar, stir it in now, while the tea is hot, until it dissolves.

5. Add the remaining 4 cups of cold water.

6. Cool the tea to room temperature, then refrigerate until it is cold.

7. Serve over ice, with optional lemon.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tea should smell like black tea, and it should taste strong and clean, not bitter from a long steep, and not sour from tea that sat too long at room temperature in a warm kitchen. If the tea smells musty, throw it out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tea should look clear and amber or deep brown, not cloudy in an unexpected way, and not murky with broken tea leaves if you strained it.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tea should look clear and amber or deep brown, not cloudy in an unexpected way, and not murky with broken tea leaves if you strained it.

SAFETY
The pot and steam are very hot. Pour slowly. Cool the tea and refrigerate it within 2 hours. Sweetened tea is perishable. Use within 3 to 4 days, and keep it cold. Do not leave a pitcher out all afternoon in a hot room. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For sun tea, use a refrigerator cold-brew instead in this book: put tea bags in cold water in a pitcher, refrigerate 8 to 12 hours, then remove the bags. That avoids tea sitting in the sun in the temperature danger zone.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
