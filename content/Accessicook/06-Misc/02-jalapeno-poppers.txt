Jalapeno Poppers
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
This recipe was on the original request list for the book.

Jalapeno poppers are jalapeno peppers halved, seeded, filled with a cream cheese mix, and baked until the peppers are tender and the filling is hot. This is an oven method, which is safer than deep frying. Wear gloves when you cut jalapenos, and do not touch your eyes or face. The peppers are spicy, even after the seeds are out.

SERVINGS
4 to 6 as an appetizer, about 16 popper halves

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate, because of pepper cutting and hot filling

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A spoon, for scooping seeds
- Disposable or reusable kitchen gloves
- A small knife or teaspoon
- An air fryer, optional

INGREDIENTS
- 8 jalapeno peppers, about 3 to 4 inches long
- 8 ounces cream cheese, softened at room temperature for about 20 minutes
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) shredded cheddar or Monterey Jack cheese
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) garlic powder
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 2 green onions, thinly sliced, optional
- 8 slices bacon, raw, optional, if you want bacon-wrapped poppers
- Oil spray, or 1 teaspoon oil, for the pan

TACTILE TIPS
A jalapeno is a smooth, firm, tapered pepper, smaller than a bell pepper, with a stem at the wide end. After you cut it in half the long way, the seeds and pale ribs inside feel slippery and are the most spicy part. Softened cream cheese should give easily when pressed, like soft butter, not like a cold block. Bacon, if you use it, feels slippery and should stay raw only until it is wrapped and baked.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with foil and oil it lightly.

3. Put on gloves.

4. Cut each jalapeno in half from stem to tip.

5. Leave the stem on if you want a little handle, or trim it off.

6. Use a spoon to scrape out the seeds and pale ribs.

7. Drop them in the trash. Do not touch your face.

8. In a bowl, mix the cream cheese, shredded cheese, garlic powder, salt, and optional green onion until even.

9. Spoon the filling into each pepper half.

10. Fill them full but not overflowing.

11. If you are using bacon, wrap a half slice around each stuffed pepper and set it seam-side down.

12. Raw bacon is a raw-meat product, so keep it on a separate plate until it is wrapped, then wash your hands.

13. Set the poppers on the pan, filling-side up, with a little space between them.

14. Bake 15 to 20 minutes, until the peppers feel tender when pierced with a fork and the filling is hot in the center.

15. If you used bacon, it should feel firm and cooked, not raw and soft, and the filling should still be hot.

16. Bake a few minutes more if needed.

17. Let the pan rest 5 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat. The filling will be molten at first.

18. Serve warm.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The pepper walls should feel tender, not crisp and raw, when you pierce them. The filling should feel hot all the way through, not cool in the middle. You should smell cooked peppers and melted cheese, and cooked bacon if you used it. Let them cool a few minutes so the filling does not burn your mouth.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The peppers should look slightly wrinkled and duller, not raw and tight. The filling should look bubbly and set, not icy-cold in the center. Bacon, if used, should look browned and cooked, even if some cured bacon stays a little pink. The filling may be lightly browned on top.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The peppers should look slightly wrinkled and duller, not raw and tight. The filling should look bubbly and set, not icy-cold in the center. Bacon, if used, should look browned and cooked, even if some cured bacon stays a little pink. The filling may be lightly browned on top.

SAFETY
Wear gloves when you cut jalapenos. Capsaicin, the spicy oil, can burn your skin and eyes. Do not touch your face. Wash the board, knife, and your hands with soap after you finish cutting, even if you wore gloves. If you wrap with bacon, treat it like raw meat and cook it thoroughly. Use long mitts. The filling can be hotter than it feels on the outside. If you want a fried version, skip it until you are very comfortable with hot oil, and use the baked method first. An air fryer can cook a single layer at 375 degrees F for about 8 to 12 minutes, until the same doneness cues are met. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For milder poppers, scrape the ribs very clean, or use mini sweet peppers instead of jalapenos. For spicier poppers, leave a few seeds in. You can mix a little cooked, crumbled bacon into the filling instead of wrapping. Serve with ranch dip from the Misc chapter if you like.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate within 2 hours. Reheat in a 350 degree F oven or air fryer until the center is 165 degrees F. The microwave works, but the peppers will be softer. Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
