Cocktail Sauce
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A cold, sharp ketchup-and-horseradish sauce for shrimp cocktail and other seafood. Prepared horseradish is very strong. Start with less, then add more. See Shrimp Cocktail in this chapter.

SERVINGS
About 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup)

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) ketchup
- 1 to 2 tablespoons prepared horseradish, starting with 1 tablespoon
- 1 teaspoon lemon juice
- A dash of hot sauce or Worcestershire sauce, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Prepared horseradish feels wet and fiery in the nose as well as on the tongue. The sauce should feel thick and scoopable, like a loose ketchup, and it should smell sharp and tomatoey, not only sweet.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the ketchup, 1 tablespoon horseradish, lemon juice, and any optional hot sauce or Worcestershire in a bowl.

2. Taste a tiny amount, and add more horseradish if you want more heat.

3. Chill until you serve it, then serve cold.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The sauce should feel thick and should smell sharp, tomatoey, and a little peppery from the horseradish. It should not smell fermented or off.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look thick and red, evenly mixed, not watery.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look thick and red, evenly mixed, not watery.

SAFETY
Taste a very small amount when you adjust the horseradish, because it can sting. Keep the sauce refrigerated, and use within 1 week. Do not leave it out more than 2 hours at a party, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little extra lemon on the shrimp, not only in the sauce, makes the plate brighter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
