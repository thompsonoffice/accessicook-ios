Spinach Artichoke Dip
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A hot dip of chopped spinach, artichoke hearts, cream cheese, and melted cheese, baked until it is bubbling, 165 degrees F in the center. Squeeze the spinach very dry, or the dip will be watery. Serve with pita, crackers, or cut vegetables.

SERVINGS
8 as an appetizer

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 8 ounces cream cheese, softened
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sour cream or mayonnaise
- 1 cup shredded mozzarella cheese, plus 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) for the top
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) grated Parmesan cheese
- 1 package (10 ounces) frozen chopped spinach, thawed and squeezed very dry, or 10 ounces fresh spinach, wilted, cooled, and squeezed dry
- 1 can (14 ounces) artichoke hearts, drained and chopped
- 1 garlic clove, minced, or 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) garlic powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt

TACTILE TIPS
Squeezed spinach should feel damp but not dripping. Softened cream cheese should feel smooth and spreadable. The baked dip should feel hot and bubbling in the center, 165 degrees F, and creamy, not watery, and not oily and split.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease a small baking dish, about 1 quart, or an 8 inch square dish.

3. Mix the cream cheese, sour cream or mayonnaise, 1 cup mozzarella, Parmesan, garlic, and salt until even.

4. Stir in the spinach and artichokes until they are evenly distributed.

5. Spread the mix in the dish.

6. Sprinkle the remaining mozzarella on top.

7. Bake 20 to 25 minutes, until the dip is bubbling at the edges and the center is 165 degrees F and hot.

8. Cool 5 minutes, then serve with dippers.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The center should be 165 degrees F and feel steaming hot and creamy. You should smell cheese, spinach, and garlic, not scorching. The dip should feel thick, not watery.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look melted and bubbling, with a few light brown spots, not pale and still, and not dark brown and dried out.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look melted and bubbling, with a few light brown spots, not pale and still, and not dark brown and dried out.

SAFETY
The dish and dip are very hot, and bubbling cheese can burn your mouth. Cool 5 minutes. Use mitts. Squeeze spinach in a clean towel. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, and reheat to 165 degrees F. Cook casseroles to 165 degrees F in the center.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A fresh jalapeno, minced, with gloves on, adds heat. Mince means cut into very small pieces. This can be mixed ahead, refrigerated, and baked just before serving, adding a few minutes if it goes in cold.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
