Roasted Brussels Sprouts
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Brussels sprouts halved and roasted until the cut sides are browned and the centers are tender. High heat is what makes them sweet instead of dull and cabbage-like. Cut even halves so they finish together.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds) Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 3/4 teaspoon (three-quarters of a teaspoon) salt
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- Optional: 1 tablespoon balsamic vinegar or maple syrup, tossed on at the end

TACTILE TIPS
Trim means cut off the dry stem end and pull off any loose, damaged outer leaves. A done sprout should feel tender when pierced in the center, and the cut side should feel browned and slightly crisp, not burnt and bitter, and not hard in the core.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Line a sheet pan with parchment, or oil the pan.

3. Trim the sprouts and cut them in half on a stable board, using a cut-resistant glove on the hand that holds the sprout if you need it.

4. Toss with oil, salt, and pepper, until they feel coated.

5. Spread them cut-side down in a single layer.

6. Roast 20 to 30 minutes, until the centers feel tender when pierced and the cut sides feel browned.

7. Toss with optional vinegar or maple syrup.

8. Taste a cooled piece, and add salt if it needs it.

9. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide into the center. The cut sides should feel browned and slightly crisp. You should smell toasted, nutty sprouts, not burning or a strong raw-cabbage smell.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cut sides should look deeply browned, and the outer leaves may look darker and crisp. The interiors should look tender, not pale and raw in the core, and not black.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cut sides should look deeply browned, and the outer leaves may look darker and crisp. The interiors should look tender, not pale and raw in the core, and not black.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a sharp knife, slowly. Use mitts. The pan is very hot. Loose leaves can darken faster than the halves, which is all right if they are crisp, not burnt. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Starting cut-side down helps the sprouts brown. A little bacon, already cooked, can be tossed in at the end, heated until 165 degrees F if it was leftover and chilled.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
