Mashed Potatoes
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Potatoes boiled until tender, then mashed with butter and milk until they are smooth and fluffy, or left a little lumpy if you prefer. This is a standalone side to go with beef tips, meatloaf, or roasted meats in the Supper chapter.

SERVINGS
4 to 6 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 2 pounds russet or Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and cut into even chunks
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the cooking water, plus more to taste
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1/2 to 3/4 cup (half to three-quarters of a cup) warm milk
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Even chunks cook at the same rate. A fork should slide into a chunk with no resistance when the potatoes are done. The finished mash should feel smooth and fluffy, or softly lumpy, not watery and not stiff as paste.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the potatoes in a pot.

2. Add cold water to cover them by about 1 inch, and add the 1 tablespoon salt.

3. Bring to a boil, meaning large, rolling bubbles, then reduce to a simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

4. Cook 12 to 18 minutes, until a fork slides into a chunk easily.

5. Drain the potatoes well in a colander. Drain means pour off the liquid.

6. Return them to the hot pot for 1 minute so extra steam can escape.

7. Add the butter.

8. Mash until the butter is mixed in.

9. Add 1/2 cup (half a cup) of the warm milk.

10. Mash or beat until the potatoes feel fluffy.

11. Add more milk if they are too stiff.

12. Add salt and optional pepper to taste.

13. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide into the boiled chunks easily before you mash. The finished mash should feel fluffy and hot. You should smell potato and butter, not a raw, starchy smell or scorching.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The mash should look fluffy and even, not gluey and shiny, and not dry with lumps of unmashed potato unless you left them on purpose.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The mash should look fluffy and even, not gluey and shiny, and not dry with lumps of unmashed potato unless you left them on purpose.

SAFETY
The pot, steam, and colander are very hot. Drain slowly, away from your body. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F with a splash of milk. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Do not overmash, especially with a blender or food processor, or the potatoes can turn gluey. For garlic mashed potatoes, simmer a few peeled cloves with the potatoes and mash them in. See Beef Tips and Mashed Potatoes in the Supper chapter for a combined plate.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
