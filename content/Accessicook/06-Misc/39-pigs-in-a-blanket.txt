ACCESSICOOK
Pigs in a Blanket
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Small sausages wrapped in dough and baked until the dough is golden and the sausages are hot. Use cocktail wieners, or cut regular hot dogs into short pieces. If the sausages are already fully cooked, you are heating them through, often to 165 degrees F to be safe if they were leftover or chilled, or until they are steaming hot. The dough should be cooked, not raw on the inside.

SERVINGS
24 pieces, 8 appetizer servings

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 package refrigerated crescent-roll dough, or 1 pound pizza dough, thawed if frozen
- 24 cocktail wieners, or 6 hot dogs, each cut into 4 pieces, patted dry
- 1 egg, beaten with 1 teaspoon water, optional, for brushing
- Mustard or ketchup, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
The dough should feel soft and easy to wrap, not sticky and not dry and cracked. After baking, the dough should feel set and golden, not doughy next to the sausage, and the sausage should feel steaming hot.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F, or to the temperature on the dough package if it is different.

2. Line a pan with parchment.

3. If you are using crescent dough, separate it into triangles and cut each triangle into smaller strips if needed.

4. If you are using pizza dough, roll it thin and cut it into 24 small strips.

5. Wrap each sausage in a strip of dough, leaving the ends slightly visible if you like, and pinch the seam shut. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

6. Set the pieces seam-side down on the pan.

7. Brush with the optional egg wash.

8. Bake 12 to 18 minutes, until the dough feels set and golden, and the sausages are steaming hot, 165 degrees F if they were leftover or if you want a clear target.

9. Cool 5 minutes.

10. Serve with mustard or ketchup.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The dough should feel set and lightly crisp in spots, not doughy. The sausages should feel steaming hot. You should smell toasted dough and sausage, not raw flour or burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The dough should look golden, not pale and not dark brown. A little grease may leak, which is common. The dough next to the sausage, if you break one open to test, should look cooked, not raw and doughy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The dough should look golden, not pale and not dark brown. A little grease may leak, which is common. The dough next to the sausage, if you break one open to test, should look cooked, not raw and doughy.

SAFETY
Use mitts. The pan, filling, and steam are very hot. Cool 5 minutes. If you use an egg wash, wash your hands after handling the raw egg. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, and reheat to 165 degrees F. Do not taste raw dough. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of everything-bagel seasoning on the egg wash is a good extra. These are best eaten warm.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
