Limeade
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
The lime version of lemonade, made with lime juice, sugar, and water. Dissolve the sugar in a little hot water first so the drink is not gritty. Limes can be even more tart than lemons, so taste as you go.

SERVINGS
About 1 1/2 quarts (one and a half quarts), 6 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes, plus chilling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) lime juice, from about 6 to 8 limes, or bottled juice
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) sugar, plus more to taste
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) hot water, for dissolving the sugar
- 4 cups cold water, plus more if the limeade is too strong
- Ice, and optional lime slices, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
The finished limeade should feel smooth, not gritty, and it should taste bright and tart-sweet, not bitter from too much lime pith if you juiced sloppily, and not only sugary.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Stir the sugar and hot water in a pitcher until the sugar dissolves completely.

2. Add the lime juice and the cold water.

3. Stir well.

4. Taste a little, and add more sugar, more lime, or more water if it needs it.

5. Chill, then serve over ice, with optional lime slices.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The drink should feel smooth and should smell bright and limey. If fresh limes smell fermented or look moldy, throw them out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The limeade should look pale green-yellow and clear, maybe with a little pulp, not muddy, and not with undissolved sugar at the bottom.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The limeade should look pale green-yellow and clear, maybe with a little pulp, not muddy, and not with undissolved sugar at the bottom.

SAFETY
Use a stable board if you cut and juice limes. Keep limeade refrigerated, and use within 3 to 4 days. Do not leave a sweetened pitcher out more than 2 hours in a warm room. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A few mint leaves, clapped between your hands and dropped in the pitcher, are a good extra. This is a good partner for Fish Tacos and for other lime-bright suppers.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
