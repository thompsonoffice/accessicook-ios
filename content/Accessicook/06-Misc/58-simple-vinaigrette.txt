Simple Vinaigrette
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A basic oil-and-vinegar dressing for green salads. The usual starting ratio is about 3 parts oil to 1 part vinegar, plus salt and an optional dab of mustard to help the dressing stay mixed. A vinaigrette is a dressing made from oil and an acid such as vinegar or lemon juice. Shake or whisk it again just before you use it, because it will separate as it sits. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk until the mixture looks even.

SERVINGS
About 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup), enough for several salads

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) olive oil, or another oil you like
- 3 tablespoons vinegar, such as red wine, white wine, or cider vinegar, or lemon juice
- 1 teaspoon mustard, optional, to help it stay mixed
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) honey or sugar, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The mixed dressing should feel smooth and slightly thick if you used mustard, and thinner if you did not. It should taste bright and balanced, not only oily, and not only sharp. It will separate as it sits, which is normal.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the vinegar, optional mustard, salt, pepper, and optional honey or sugar in a jar or a small bowl.

2. Add the oil.

3. Close the jar and shake until the dressing looks even, or whisk it in the bowl. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk.

4. Taste a little on a piece of lettuce if you can, and add salt or a little more vinegar or oil if it needs it.

5. Shake or whisk again just before you dress a salad.

6. Start with less dressing than you think you need, then add more.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The dressing should feel smooth and should taste bright. You should smell vinegar or lemon, oil, and optional mustard. If the oil smells rancid, like old crayons or paint, throw it out and start over with fresh oil.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: Just after shaking, the dressing should look even and slightly cloudy, not a layer of oil sitting on a layer of vinegar, though it will separate again as it sits.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. Just after shaking, the dressing should look even and slightly cloudy, not a layer of oil sitting on a layer of vinegar, though it will separate again as it sits.

SAFETY
A glass jar can slip. Work over the sink or a bowl when you shake it. Store the dressing in the refrigerator if it contains fresh garlic or if you will keep it more than a day, and use within 1 week. Oil may thicken in the refrigerator. Let the jar sit at room temperature a few minutes, then shake again. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is the usual dressing for Garden Salad and Spinach Salad in the Lunch chapter. A small minced garlic clove is a good extra if you will use the dressing within a day or two. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
