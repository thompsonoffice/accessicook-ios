Rice Pilaf
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Rice toasted briefly in butter or oil with onion, then simmered in broth instead of water. This gives a more flavorful side than plain rice. Simmer means cook with small, gentle bubbles.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons butter or oil
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) diced onion, or 1 small shallot, diced
- 1 cup long-grain white rice
- 1 3/4 cups (one and three-quarters cups) broth
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, or less if the broth is salty

TACTILE TIPS
After toasting, the rice should smell nutty and feel coated with fat, not browned and burnt. After simmering and resting, the grains should feel tender and separate.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Melt the butter, or heat the oil, in a pot over medium heat.

2. Add the onion and cook until it feels soft, about 3 to 5 minutes.

3. Add the rice.

4. Stir 1 to 2 minutes, until the grains look slightly translucent at the edges and smell toasty, not burnt.

5. Add the broth and salt.

6. Bring to a boil, then cover and reduce to a low simmer. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens.

7. Cook 15 to 18 minutes, without lifting the lid.

8. Take the pot off the heat.

9. Rest, covered, 10 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

10. Fluff with a fork.

11. Taste a cooled bite, and add salt if it needs it.

12. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The grains should feel tender and separate. You should smell toasted rice, onion, and broth, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The rice should look fluffy and lightly golden, not wet and not scorched at the bottom.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The rice should look fluffy and lightly golden, not wet and not scorched at the bottom.

SAFETY
The pot and steam are very hot. Tilt the lid away from you. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Cool leftover rice quickly and refrigerate within 2 hours. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A handful of frozen peas can be stirred in when the pot comes off the heat, so they warm during the rest. Toasted nuts can go on top if no one has a nut allergy.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
