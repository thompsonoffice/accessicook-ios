Steamed Broccoli
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Broccoli cooked over simmering water until it is tender-crisp, meaning it is hot and slightly bent but not mushy. You can use a steamer basket, or a colander that sits above the water in a pot without touching the water, with a lid on top.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 large bunch broccoli, cut into florets, or 6 cups florets, or a 12 ounce bag of florets
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1 tablespoon butter or olive oil, optional
- Lemon wedges, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Florets are the tree-like heads, cut into even, bite-size pieces. Stems can be peeled and sliced and cooked with the florets if you like them. Done broccoli should feel bright and tender-crisp, not raw and squeaky, and not dull and mushy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pour about 1 inch of water into a pot.

2. Set a steamer basket in the pot, making sure the water does not sit above the bottom of the basket.

3. Bring the water to a simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

4. Add the broccoli to the basket.

5. Sprinkle with salt.

6. Cover the pot.

7. Steam 5 to 8 minutes, until a stem feels tender-crisp when pierced, not hard and not collapsing.

8. Move the broccoli to a bowl.

9. Add optional butter or oil, and more salt if it needs it.

10. Serve with optional lemon.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A stem should feel tender-crisp when pierced. You should smell fresh, cooked broccoli, not sulfur from a long overcook, and not raw vegetable. The florets should feel hot and slightly springy.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The broccoli should look bright green, not olive-drab and mushy, and not still dusty and raw-looking. The stems should look a little translucent at the cut ends.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The broccoli should look bright green, not olive-drab and mushy, and not still dusty and raw-looking. The stems should look a little translucent at the cut ends.

SAFETY
Steam can burn you. Tilt the lid away from you. Use mitts or tongs to lift the basket. The water and pot are very hot. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you do not have a basket, microwave the florets in a microwave-safe dish with 2 tablespoons water, covered, 3 to 5 minutes, then check and add time as needed. Do not overcook. A sprinkle of grated Parmesan is a good finish.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
