Tartar Sauce
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A cold mayonnaise sauce with chopped pickle and a little lemon, for fish, fish sandwiches, and crab cakes. This is the usual partner for the Fish Sandwich in the Lunch chapter and for Baked White Fish, Salmon Patties, and Crab Cakes in the Supper chapter.

SERVINGS
About 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup)

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) mayonnaise
- 2 tablespoons finely chopped pickle or pickle relish, squeezed a little if it is very wet
- 1 teaspoon lemon juice or pickle juice, plus more to taste
- 1 teaspoon mustard, optional
- 1 tablespoon minced onion or shallot, optional
- A pinch of salt and black pepper

TACTILE TIPS
The sauce should feel creamy and thick, with small, even pieces of pickle, not watery, and not pasty. Mince means cut into very small pieces if you use onion.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the mayonnaise, pickle, lemon juice or pickle juice, optional mustard, optional onion, and a pinch of salt and pepper in a bowl until even. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

2. Taste a little, and add lemon or salt if it needs it.

3. Chill until you serve it, then serve cold with fish.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The sauce should feel creamy and thick. You should smell mayonnaise, pickle, and lemon, not a sour, off mayonnaise smell. If the mayonnaise or pickles smell wrong, throw them out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look creamy and pale, flecked with pickle, not watery and not separated.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look creamy and pale, flecked with pickle, not watery and not separated.

SAFETY
Keep the sauce refrigerated. Do not leave it out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use within 4 days. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of dried dill or a little caper, chopped, is a good extra. Cocktail Sauce in this chapter is the usual alternative for shrimp.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
