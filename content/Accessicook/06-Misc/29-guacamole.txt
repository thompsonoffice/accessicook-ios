Guacamole
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Mashed avocado with lime, salt, and optional onion, tomato, or chili. A ripe avocado gives slightly when you press it gently, but it should not feel collapsed and stringy. Lime and plastic wrap pressed onto the surface help slow browning.

SERVINGS
About 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups), 4 to 6 snack servings

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 2 ripe avocados
- 1 tablespoon lime juice, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- Optional: 2 tablespoons diced onion, 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) diced tomato, 1 tablespoon minced jalapeno, or 1 tablespoon chopped cilantro

TACTILE TIPS
A ripe avocado should yield to gentle pressure, not feel rock-hard, and not feel mushy and hollow. After you cut it, the flesh should feel creamy, not fibrous and brown all the way through. If the flesh is brown only in a thin layer under the peel, you can scoop away that layer. The finished guacamole should feel creamy, with some small chunks if you like them, not watery, and not stiff as paste.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cut each avocado in half lengthwise, around the pit, on a stable board.

2. Twist the halves apart.

3. Remove the pit by scooping it out with a spoon, or by carefully easing it out. Do not strike the pit with a knife.

4. Scoop the flesh into a bowl.

5. Add the lime juice and salt.

6. Mash with a fork until the mix feels as smooth or as chunky as you like.

7. Stir in any optional add-ins.

8. Taste a little, and add salt or lime if it needs it.

9. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The mix should feel creamy. You should smell avocado, lime, and salt, and any optional onion or cilantro. It should not smell rancid or fermented. If an avocado is brown, stringy, and off-smelling inside, throw it out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The guacamole should look creamy and green, maybe with a few yellow-green chunks, not gray-brown all the way through, and not watery. A little browning on the surface after it sits is common.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The guacamole should look creamy and green, maybe with a few yellow-green chunks, not gray-brown all the way through, and not watery. A little browning on the surface after it sits is common.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a sharp knife, slowly. Do not strike the pit with a knife, because the knife can slip. Wear gloves if you mince a chili, and do not touch your eyes. Mince means cut into very small pieces. Press plastic wrap onto the surface of leftovers, refrigerate, and eat within 1 day. Do not leave guacamole out more than 2 hours. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A ripe avocado can be ripened at room temperature and then kept in the refrigerator for a day or two. This is the usual partner for tacos and for Chips and Salsa in the Midnight Snacks chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
