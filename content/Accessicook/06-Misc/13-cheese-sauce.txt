Cheese Sauce
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A smooth sauce of milk thickened with a butter-and-flour roux, then finished with shredded cheese. A roux is fat and flour cooked together. This is the sauce for vegetables, oven fries, or a quick stovetop pasta. Use low heat after the cheese goes in so the sauce does not split, meaning the fat and liquid separate.

SERVINGS
About 2 cups, 4 to 6 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) milk
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) shredded cheddar cheese, or a mix of cheddar and American
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) paprika or mustard powder, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The roux should feel smooth and smell slightly toasty. The milk sauce, before cheese, should feel smooth and slightly thick, like heavy cream. The finished sauce should feel smooth and pourable, thick enough to coat a spoon, not grainy, and not oily and split.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat.

2. Stir in the flour and cook 1 minute.

3. Whisk in the milk slowly until the mix feels smooth. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk so there are no lumps.

4. Cook, stirring, until the sauce is slightly thick and you see small bubbles, 3 to 5 minutes.

5. Take the pot off the heat.

6. Stir in the cheese, salt, and optional paprika or mustard, until the cheese is melted and the sauce feels smooth.

7. If it is too thick, add a splash of milk.

8. If the cheese has not fully melted, return the pot to very low heat and stir, without boiling.

9. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The sauce should feel smooth and should coat a spoon. You should smell melted cheese and milk, not scorched milk or a grainy, split sauce. If you smell scorching, take the pot off the heat and stop scraping the bottom into the serving dish.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look smooth and evenly yellow or orange, not lumpy, and not oily with a pool of separated fat.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look smooth and evenly yellow or orange, not lumpy, and not oily with a pool of separated fat.

SAFETY
Stir often so the milk does not scorch. The pot and sauce are very hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftover cheese sauce to 165 degrees F, or until it is bubbling again, on low heat, stirring, with a splash of milk. Do not boil it hard, or it can split. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is a close cousin of the sauce in Stovetop Mac and Cheese in the Lunch chapter. A pinch of cayenne is a good extra. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
