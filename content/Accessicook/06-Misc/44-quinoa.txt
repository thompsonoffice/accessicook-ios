Quinoa
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A pot of quinoa, which is a small seed cooked like a grain. Rinse it first, unless the package says it is pre-rinsed, because the natural coating can taste bitter. The grains are done when they look slightly popped and feel tender, and the water is absorbed.

SERVINGS
4 as a side, about 3 cups cooked

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes, plus a 5-minute rest

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup quinoa
- 2 cups water or broth
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, or less if the broth is salty

TACTILE TIPS
Uncooked quinoa feels small, dry, and slightly oval. After cooking, the grains should feel tender, not chalky, and you may feel a little ring or tail on each grain, which is normal. The pot should feel fluffy, not watery, and not dry and stuck.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Rinse the quinoa in a fine strainer under cold water, unless the package says it is pre-rinsed.

2. Put the quinoa, water or broth, and salt in a pot.

3. Bring to a boil, meaning large, rolling bubbles.

4. Cover, reduce to a low simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles, and cook 12 to 15 minutes, until the water is absorbed and the grains feel tender. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

5. Take the pot off the heat.

6. Rest, covered, 5 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

7. Fluff with a fork, meaning gently loosen the grains.

8. Serve hot, or cool and use in a salad.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The grains should feel tender, not chalky. You should smell a mild, nutty grain smell, not bitterness from unrinsed quinoa, and not scorching.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The quinoa should look fluffy, with visible little rings or tails, and no puddle of water in the pot. It should not look dry and crusted unless the heat was too high.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The quinoa should look fluffy, with visible little rings or tails, and no puddle of water in the pot. It should not look dry and crusted unless the heat was too high.

SAFETY
The pot and steam are very hot. Tilt the lid away from you. Cool leftovers quickly and refrigerate within 2 hours. Reheat to 165 degrees F, or serve cold in a salad that has been kept refrigerated. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A squeeze of lemon and a little oil turn leftover quinoa into a simple salad base. This is a good partner for the roasted vegetables in this chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
