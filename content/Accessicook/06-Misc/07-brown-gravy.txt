Brown Gravy
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A pan gravy made from butter or pan drippings, flour, and broth. Drippings are the juices and fat left in a pan after you cook meat. A roux, which is fat and flour cooked together, thickens the gravy. The gravy should come back to a bubble and feel smooth. Reheat leftover gravy to a boil, 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
About 2 cups, 6 to 8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 4 tablespoons butter, or 4 tablespoons pan drippings, or a mix
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) all-purpose flour
- 2 cups broth, beef or chicken, warmed
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, or to taste, less if the drippings or broth are salty
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper

TACTILE TIPS
The roux should feel smooth and smell toasty, not raw and pasty, and not burnt. The finished gravy should feel smooth and thick enough to coat a spoon, not watery, and not lumpy and pasty.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Melt the butter, or heat the drippings, in a saucepan over medium heat.

2. Stir in the flour.

3. Cook 1 to 2 minutes, stirring, until the mix smells toasty and looks a shade darker. This is the roux.

4. Add the warm broth a little at a time, whisking so there are no lumps. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk.

5. Simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles, 3 to 5 minutes, stirring, until the gravy feels smooth and coats a spoon. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

6. Add salt and pepper to taste.

7. If it is too thick, add a splash of broth.

8. If it is too thin, simmer 1 or 2 minutes more.

9. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The gravy should feel smooth and should coat a spoon. You should smell toasted flour and broth, not raw flour or burning. If you used drippings, you should also smell the meat they came from.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The gravy should look smooth and brown, not pale and pasty, and not dark and speckled with burnt bits unless you strained it. It should coat a spoon, not run off like broth.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The gravy should look smooth and brown, not pale and pasty, and not dark and speckled with burnt bits unless you strained it. It should coat a spoon, not run off like broth.

SAFETY
The pan and bubbling gravy are very hot and can spatter. Whisk with a long-handled tool. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftover gravy to a boil, 165 degrees F, stirring, and add a little broth if it has thickened. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you get lumps, pour the gravy through a strainer. A splash of Worcestershire sauce or a pinch of dried thyme is a good extra. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). This is the usual partner for Mashed Potatoes and for roast meats in the Supper chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
