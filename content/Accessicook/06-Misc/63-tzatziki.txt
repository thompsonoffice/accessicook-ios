Tzatziki
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A cold yogurt and cucumber dip with garlic and lemon, often served with pita, roasted meats, or falafel. Squeeze the cucumber dry, or the dip will be watery. This is a good partner for the Falafel Pita in the Lunch chapter and for lamb in the Supper chapter.

SERVINGS
About 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups)

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes, plus a short chill

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup plain Greek yogurt, or thick plain yogurt
- 1/2 cucumber, peeled if the skin is thick, grated or finely diced, then squeezed dry
- 1 small garlic clove, minced, or 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) garlic powder
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice, plus more to taste
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1 tablespoon chopped fresh dill or mint, or 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) dried dill

TACTILE TIPS
Grated cucumber holds a lot of water. Squeeze it in a clean towel or with clean hands until it feels damp, not dripping. The finished dip should feel thick and creamy, with small bits of cucumber, not watery. Mince means cut the garlic into very small pieces.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Grate or dice the cucumber, then squeeze it dry. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mix the yogurt, cucumber, garlic, lemon juice, oil, salt, and dill or mint in a bowl until even.

3. Taste a little, and add salt or lemon if it needs it.

4. Chill 15 to 30 minutes if you have time, then stir and serve cold.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The dip should feel thick and creamy. You should smell yogurt, cucumber, garlic, and herbs, not a sour, off yogurt smell. If the yogurt smells or looks wrong before you mix, throw it out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The dip should look thick and pale, flecked with cucumber and herbs, not watery, and not separated.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The dip should look thick and pale, flecked with cucumber and herbs, not watery, and not separated.

SAFETY
Keep the dip refrigerated. Do not leave it out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use within 3 days. Mince garlic on a stable board. Wash the grater and board after use. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you only have regular yogurt, not Greek yogurt, you can let it sit in a strainer lined with a paper towel for 20 minutes to thicken.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
