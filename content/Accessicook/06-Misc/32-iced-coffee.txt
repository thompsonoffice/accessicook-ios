Iced Coffee
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Coffee served cold over ice, either by cooling a strong brew or by steeping coffee in cold water overnight, which is called cold brew. Steep means let the coffee sit in water so the flavor comes out. If you pour hot coffee straight over a lot of ice, brew it stronger than usual, or the ice will dilute it.

SERVINGS
2 glasses

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes, plus time to brew and cool the coffee, or overnight for a cold brew

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups strong coffee, cooled, or 1/2 cup (half a cup) coarsely ground coffee plus 2 cups cold water, for a small cold brew
- Ice, for serving
- Milk or cream, optional
- Sugar or simple syrup, optional, meaning sugar dissolved in an equal amount of hot water, then cooled

TACTILE TIPS
Cooled coffee should smell like coffee, not scorched or stale. Cold brew, after straining, should feel smooth and less sharp than a hot brew that was cooled, and it should not feel sludgy if you strained it well.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. For cooled coffee: brew coffee at about twice your usual strength if you will pour it over ice, or brew it as usual if you will cool it fully first and use less ice.

2. Cool it, then refrigerate.

3. For a small cold brew: mix the ground coffee and cold water in a jar.

4. Cover and refrigerate 12 to 18 hours.

5. Strain through a fine strainer or a coffee filter, slowly.

6. Fill two glasses with ice.

7. Pour the coffee over the ice.

8. Add optional milk and optional sugar or syrup.

9. Stir and serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The coffee should smell like coffee, not scorched or rancid, and it should taste strong enough after the ice. If the coffee smells stale or oily in an off way, throw it out and start with fresh beans or grounds.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The coffee should look dark and clear or only slightly cloudy, not muddy with a thick layer of grounds if you strained it.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The coffee should look dark and clear or only slightly cloudy, not muddy with a thick layer of grounds if you strained it.

SAFETY
If you brew with hot water, the pot and steam are very hot. Cool hot coffee within 2 hours and refrigerate it. Keep milk cold, and do not leave a milky iced coffee out for a long time. Use cold brew or cooled coffee within 3 to 4 days, kept refrigerated. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Sweetening works better with a syrup than with dry sugar in a very cold glass, because dry sugar can sit undissolved at the bottom.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
