Fruit Punch
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple, nonalcoholic punch of fruit juices and cold water or a clear soda, served in a pitcher or a bowl over ice. Keep it cold. If you add cut fruit, treat the punch as perishable and do not leave it out more than 2 hours.

SERVINGS
About 3 quarts, 12 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes, plus chilling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 4 cups unsweetened pineapple juice, or a mix of pineapple and orange juice
- 2 cups cranberry juice
- 2 cups cold water, or a clear lemon-lime soda, added just before serving if you want bubbles
- 2 tablespoons lemon or lime juice, optional
- Ice, and optional orange or lemon slices, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
The punch should feel cold and taste fruity and balanced, not only sweet, and not watery once the ice starts to melt, which is why you should not add all the ice to the pitcher hours ahead if you can add it to the glasses instead.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the pineapple or mixed juice, cranberry juice, and optional lemon or lime juice in a pitcher or a punch bowl.

2. Add the water now, or wait and add the soda just before serving if you are using soda.

3. Chill the punch.

4. Serve over ice, with optional fruit slices.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The punch should smell fruity and clean, not fermented. It should taste balanced. If a juice smelled or tasted off before you mixed, throw it out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The punch should look bright and clear or only slightly cloudy, depending on the juices, not dull and brown from sitting out a long time with cut fruit in a warm room.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The punch should look bright and clear or only slightly cloudy, depending on the juices, not dull and brown from sitting out a long time with cut fruit in a warm room.

SAFETY
Keep the punch refrigerated. If you add cut fruit or soda is not the only perishable concern, do not leave the bowl out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use a ladle, not shared cups dipped into the bowl. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Frozen fruit can stand in for some of the ice and will dilute the punch less as it thaws. This is a party drink, not a cocktail, in this book.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
