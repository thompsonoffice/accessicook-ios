Salsa
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A fresh tomato dip with onion, cilantro or parsley, lime, and chili. This is a no-cook salsa. If you want a milder bowl, use less chili and take out the seeds. Serve with chips, tacos, or eggs.

SERVINGS
About 2 cups, 6 to 8 snack servings

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 4 ripe tomatoes, diced, or 1 can (14 1/2 ounces) diced tomatoes, drained a little
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) diced onion
- 1 jalapeno or other small chili, minced, or 2 tablespoons mild canned green chiles
- 2 tablespoons chopped cilantro or parsley
- 1 tablespoon lime juice, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1 garlic clove, minced, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Dice means cut into small, even cubes. Mince means cut into very small pieces. Wear disposable gloves when you cut a chili, and do not touch your eyes. The salsa should feel juicy and scoopable, with distinct pieces, not watery, and not a smooth puree unless you prefer it blended.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. If you are using a fresh chili, cut it in half, remove the seeds if you want less heat, and mince it on a stable board, using gloves.

2. Mix the tomatoes, onion, chili, cilantro or parsley, lime juice, salt, and optional garlic in a bowl.

3. Taste a little, and add salt or lime if it needs it.

4. Let it sit 10 minutes, then stir and serve, or refrigerate until you need it.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The salsa should feel juicy and scoopable. You should smell tomato, lime, onion, and optional cilantro, which some people find soapy, in which case use parsley instead. It should smell fresh, not fermented.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The salsa should look chunky and moist, with visible tomato, onion, and herbs, not dry, and not a thin, watery liquid with everything sunk.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The salsa should look chunky and moist, with visible tomato, onion, and herbs, not dry, and not a thin, watery liquid with everything sunk.

SAFETY
Wear gloves when you cut chiles, and wash the board, knife, and your hands well afterward. Do not touch your face. Keep the salsa refrigerated, and do not leave it out more than 2 hours. Use within 3 to 4 days. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of cumin is a good extra. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). For a smoother salsa, pulse the mix a few times in a food processor, carefully, or mash it with a fork. See Chips and Salsa in the Midnight Snacks chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
