Applesauce
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Apples cooked until they fall apart, then mashed into a sauce. You can peel them for a smoother sauce, or leave the peels on for more texture and then mash or blend carefully. This is a side for pork, a snack, or a topping for pancakes and pork chops.

SERVINGS
4 to 6 as a side, about 3 cups

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 2 pounds apples, peeled if you want a smoother sauce, cored and cut into chunks
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) water, or apple juice
- 2 tablespoons sugar, or to taste, or skip if the apples are very sweet
- 1 teaspoon lemon juice
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) cinnamon, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Apple chunks should become very soft and collapse under a spoon when they are done. The sauce should feel like a thick puree, either smooth or lightly chunky, not watery and not like raw, firm pieces in syrup.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the apples, water or juice, lemon juice, optional sugar, and optional cinnamon in a pot.

2. Cover and cook over medium-low heat 15 to 25 minutes, stirring now and then, until the apples feel very soft and fall apart when pressed.

3. If the pot looks dry before the apples are soft, add 1 or 2 tablespoons water.

4. Mash with a potato masher or a large fork for a chunky sauce, or puree carefully once it is not piping hot if you want it smooth.

5. Taste a cooled spoonful, and add sugar or lemon if it needs it.

6. Serve warm or chilled.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The apples should feel completely soft before you mash. The sauce should smell like cooked apple and optional cinnamon, not burning. A cooled spoonful should feel thick and tender, not starchy and raw.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look even and golden or pale, depending on the apples, not brown and scorched, and not watery with floating firm pieces unless you left some chunks on purpose.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look even and golden or pale, depending on the apples, not brown and scorched, and not watery with floating firm pieces unless you left some chunks on purpose.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a sharp knife to core and cut the apples. The pot and steam are hot, and bubbling fruit can spatter. Cool a spoonful before you taste. Reheat leftovers until steaming hot if you want them warm, or serve chilled. Refrigerate within 2 hours. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A mix of sweet and tart apples tastes better than one kind alone. This freezes well.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
