Whipped Cream
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Cold cream beaten until it is fluffy and holds a shape, then lightly sweetened. This is the topping for pies, shortcake, cocoa, and sundaes in this book. Stop beating when the cream holds a soft or firm peak, or it can turn grainy and then become butter.

SERVINGS
About 2 cups, 6 to 8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup heavy cream, very cold
- 1 to 2 tablespoons powdered sugar, or to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) vanilla extract, optional

TACTILE TIPS
A soft peak means a ridge of cream stands up and then slightly folds over. A firm peak means the ridge stands up and holds. The cream should feel cold, smooth, and fluffy, not grainy, and not so stiff that it looks and feels like wet butter, which means it has gone too far.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put a mixing bowl and beaters or a whisk in the refrigerator for 10 minutes if you have time. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk until the mixture looks even.

2. Pour in the cold cream.

3. Beat on medium speed, or whisk by hand, until the cream thickens and you see soft tracks.

4. Add the sugar and optional vanilla.

5. Beat until the cream holds a soft or firm peak, then stop at once.

6. Use at once, or cover and refrigerate for a few hours, then whisk once more if it has loosened.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The cream should feel cold, smooth, and fluffy, and it should hold a soft or firm peak. You should smell cream and vanilla, not a sour cream smell. If you hear the beaters change to a heavy slosh and then a slap, and the cream looks grainy, stop. You are close to making butter.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cream should look smooth and fluffy, with soft or firm peaks, not grainy and yellow, and not still liquid and bubbly only at the surface.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cream should look smooth and fluffy, with soft or firm peaks, not grainy and yellow, and not still liquid and bubbly only at the surface.

SAFETY
Do not leave whipped cream out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Keep it cold. A splash guard or a deep bowl helps, because cream splatters as it thickens. If you overwhip and it just starts to look grainy, you can sometimes save it by gently folding in a few tablespoons of unwhipped cream. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is the usual topping for Pumpkin Pie, Berry Crumble, and Strawberry Shortcake in the Baked Goods chapter, and for Ice Cream Sundaes and Hot Chocolate in the Midnight Snacks chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
