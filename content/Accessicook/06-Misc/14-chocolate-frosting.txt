Chocolate Frosting
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A chocolate buttercream made with cocoa powder, butter, and powdered sugar. Use this on Chocolate Cake or Chocolate Cupcakes in the Baked Goods chapter. The cake must be fully cool before you frost it.

SERVINGS
About 2 cups, enough for 12 cupcakes or a thin layer on a 9 by 13 inch cake

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) unsalted butter, softened
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) unsweetened cocoa powder
- 2 1/2 cups (two and a half cups) powdered sugar, plus more if the frosting is too thin
- 3 tablespoons milk, plus more as needed
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- A pinch of salt

TACTILE TIPS
Softened butter should give like cool clay. Cocoa and powdered sugar are dusty, so add them slowly. The finished frosting should feel smooth, fluffy, and spreadable, not dry and crumbly, and not soupy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Beat the butter until it feels smooth and slightly fluffy.

2. Add the cocoa, 1 cup of the powdered sugar, and 1 tablespoon of the milk.

3. Mix slowly, then more firmly, until combined.

4. Add the remaining powdered sugar, the remaining milk, the vanilla, and the salt.

5. Beat until the frosting feels fluffy and spreadable.

6. If it is too thick, add milk 1 teaspoon at a time.

7. If it is too thin, add powdered sugar 2 tablespoons at a time.

8. Spread on a fully cooled cake or cupcakes.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The frosting should feel smooth, fluffy, and spreadable. You should smell chocolate, butter, and vanilla, not raw, dusty cocoa once it is fully mixed.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The frosting should look smooth and evenly chocolate-colored, not streaky with dry cocoa, and not oily or soupy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The frosting should look smooth and evenly chocolate-colored, not streaky with dry cocoa, and not oily or soupy.

SAFETY
Mix slowly at first so cocoa and sugar do not puff into the air. There are no raw eggs in this frosting. The cake must be cool, or the frosting will melt. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of espresso powder, if you have it, deepens the chocolate flavor and will not make the frosting taste like coffee. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
