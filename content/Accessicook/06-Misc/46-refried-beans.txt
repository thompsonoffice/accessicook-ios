Refried Beans
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Canned or leftover pinto or black beans mashed and heated with a little oil or butter until they are thick, creamy, and steaming hot, 165 degrees F. These are a side for tacos, burritos, and rice bowls.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cans (15 ounces each) pinto or black beans, drained, with 1/2 cup (half a cup) of the liquid reserved, or 3 cups cooked beans plus a little broth
- 1 tablespoon oil or butter
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) ground cumin, optional
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- Optional: 2 tablespoons diced onion, cooked in the oil first until soft

TACTILE TIPS
The finished beans should feel thick and creamy, with some texture left if you like them that way, not watery, and not a dry paste. They should be steaming hot, 165 degrees F.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil or butter in a skillet over medium heat.

2. Cook the optional onion until it feels soft.

3. Add the beans, cumin if using, and salt.

4. Add a splash of the reserved liquid or broth.

5. Cook, mashing with a spoon or a potato masher, 5 to 8 minutes, until the beans are thick, creamy, and 165 degrees F.

6. Add more liquid if they get too thick, or cook a minute more if they are too loose.

7. Taste a cooled spoonful, and add salt if it needs it.

8. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The beans should feel creamy and steaming hot, 165 degrees F. You should smell beans, optional cumin, and oil or butter, not scorching.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The beans should look thick and a little glossy, not dry and cracked, and not soupy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The beans should look thick and a little glossy, not dry and cracked, and not soupy.

SAFETY
The pan is hot and can spatter if the beans bubble. Stir often. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F with a splash of water. Refrigerate within 2 hours. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little shredded cheese on top, melted in the last minute, is a good finish. These are the usual partner for the Rice and Bean Bowl in the Lunch chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
