Glazed Carrots
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Carrot slices or baby carrots cooked until tender, then coated in a light butter-and-sugar glaze. A glaze is a thin, shiny coating. The carrots should be tender, not crunchy and raw, and not collapsing.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound carrots, peeled and sliced into 1/2 inch (half inch) coins, or 1 pound baby carrots
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 1 tablespoon brown sugar or honey
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) water

TACTILE TIPS
Even slices cook at the same rate. A fork should slide into a carrot with little resistance when they are done. The glaze should feel slightly thick and shiny, coating the carrots, not watery, and not a hard candy shell.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the carrots, water, butter, sugar or honey, and salt in a skillet or wide pot.

2. Bring to a simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

3. Cover and cook 8 to 12 minutes, until a fork slides in easily.

4. Take off the lid.

5. Cook, stirring, 2 to 4 minutes more, until most of the liquid has thickened into a glaze that coats the carrots.

6. Taste a cooled piece, and add salt if it needs it.

7. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide in easily. The glaze should feel slightly thick and should coat the carrots. You should smell sweet carrot and butter, not burning sugar.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The carrots should look shiny and bright, not dry and not sitting in a puddle of watery syrup. They should look tender, not stiff.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The carrots should look shiny and bright, not dry and not sitting in a puddle of watery syrup. They should look tender, not stiff.

SAFETY
The pan is hot, and a sugar glaze can be hotter than it seems. Let a test piece cool a few seconds. Use a stable board if you peel and slice the carrots. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of cinnamon or thyme is a good extra. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). If the pan goes dry before the carrots are tender, add a tablespoon of water.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
