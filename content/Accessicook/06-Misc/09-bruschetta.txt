ACCESSICOOK
Bruschetta
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Toasted bread topped with a fresh mix of tomato, oil, salt, and basil or parsley. The topping should go on just before you serve, so the bread stays crisp. Bruschetta is toasted bread with a topping, often tomato.

SERVINGS
8 to 12 pieces

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 baguette, cut into 1/2 inch (half inch) slices, about 12 slices
- 2 tablespoons olive oil, divided
- 3 ripe tomatoes, diced and drained of extra juice
- 1 small garlic clove, minced, plus 1 whole clove for rubbing the toast, optional
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 2 tablespoons chopped fresh basil or parsley

TACTILE TIPS
Dice means cut into small, even cubes. Mince means cut the garlic into very small pieces. The toast should feel crisp at the edges. The tomato mix should feel juicy but not watery, after a brief drain, and it should smell fresh. Drain means pour off the liquid.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Set the bread slices on a pan.

3. Brush them with 1 tablespoon of the oil.

4. Bake 6 to 10 minutes, until the edges feel crisp and the centers feel lightly toasted, not hard as a cracker unless you prefer them very crisp, and not burnt.

5. Cool 1 or 2 minutes.

6. If you like, rub a cut garlic clove lightly on the warm toast.

7. Mix the tomatoes, remaining 1 tablespoon oil, minced garlic, salt, and basil or parsley. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

8. Taste a little, and add salt if it needs it.

9. Spoon the tomato mix onto the toast just before you serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The toast should feel crisp at the edges and should smell toasted, not burnt. The tomato mix should smell fresh, tomatoey, and herby, not fermented. The bread will soften after it is topped, so serve at once.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The toast should look lightly golden, not pale and not dark brown. The topping should look moist and red, with visible herbs, not watery and puddled on the plate if you drained the tomatoes a little.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The toast should look lightly golden, not pale and not dark brown. The topping should look moist and red, with visible herbs, not watery and puddled on the plate if you drained the tomatoes a little.

SAFETY
Use mitts. Use a stable board and a sharp knife, slowly, for the bread and tomatoes. Mince garlic on a stable board. Tomato topping left on bread gets soggy, so top only what you will eat soon. Keep leftover topping refrigerated, and use within 2 days. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little balsamic vinegar in the tomato mix is a good extra. If tomatoes are out of season, this will taste dull, and you may prefer a caprese-style topping with better tomatoes, or skip to Caprese Skewers in this chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
