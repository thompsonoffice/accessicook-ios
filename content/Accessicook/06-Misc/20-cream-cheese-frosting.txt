Cream Cheese Frosting
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A tangy frosting made with cream cheese, butter, and powdered sugar. This is the usual frosting for Carrot Cake in the Baked Goods chapter, and it also works on cupcakes. Because it contains cream cheese, keep frosted cakes refrigerated.

SERVINGS
About 2 cups, enough for a 9 by 13 inch carrot cake or 12 cupcakes

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 8 ounces cream cheese, softened
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, softened
- 2 1/2 cups (two and a half cups) powdered sugar, plus more if needed
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- A pinch of salt

TACTILE TIPS
Softened cream cheese and butter should feel smooth and spreadable, not cold and lumpy, and not melted and oily. The finished frosting should feel smooth, thick, and spreadable, a little looser than vanilla buttercream, not soupy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Beat the cream cheese and butter together until the mix feels completely smooth, with no lumps.

2. Add the powdered sugar 1 cup at a time, mixing slowly at first, then more firmly.

3. Add the vanilla and salt.

4. Beat until the frosting feels smooth and spreadable.

5. If it is too thin, add more powdered sugar 2 tablespoons at a time, or chill the frosting 15 minutes, then beat again.

6. If it is too thick, add milk 1 teaspoon at a time.

7. Spread on a fully cooled cake.

8. Refrigerate the frosted cake.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The frosting should feel smooth and thick, and it should smell like cream cheese, butter, and vanilla, not sour in an off way. If the cream cheese smelled or looked wrong before you mixed, throw it out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The frosting should look smooth and pale, not lumpy with bits of cold cream cheese, and not soupy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The frosting should look smooth and pale, not lumpy with bits of cold cream cheese, and not soupy.

SAFETY
The cake must be fully cool. Keep frosted cake refrigerated because of the cream cheese, and do not leave it out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Let a slice sit out a few minutes before you eat it if you like the frosting softer. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little lemon zest is a good extra on carrot cake. This frosting is softer than vanilla buttercream, so a thick layer on a warm day can slump. Chill the cake briefly after frosting if you need neater slices.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
