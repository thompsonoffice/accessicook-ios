ACCESSICOOK
Stuffed Mushrooms
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Mushroom caps filled with a breadcrumb, cheese, and herb mix, then baked until the mushrooms are tender and the filling is hot. Pull the stems out and chop them for the filling so nothing is wasted.

SERVINGS
About 16 mushrooms, 6 to 8 appetizer servings

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 16 medium white or cremini mushrooms, stems pulled out and chopped, caps left whole
- 2 tablespoons oil or butter, divided
- 2 tablespoons minced onion or shallot
- 1 garlic clove, minced
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) breadcrumbs
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) grated Parmesan cheese
- 2 tablespoons chopped parsley, optional
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste

TACTILE TIPS
After you pull the stems, the caps should feel intact, like little bowls. The filling should feel moist enough to mound, not dry and sandy, and not wet. After baking, the caps should feel tender, and the filling should feel hot and slightly crisp on top.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Line a pan with parchment, or oil the pan.

3. Toss the caps with 1 tablespoon of the oil and a pinch of salt. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

4. Set them cavity-side up on the pan.

5. Heat the remaining oil or butter in a skillet over medium heat.

6. Cook the chopped stems and the onion until they feel soft, about 5 minutes.

7. Add the garlic for the last 30 seconds.

8. Take the skillet off the heat.

9. Stir in the breadcrumbs, Parmesan, optional parsley, and 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt.

10. If the mix feels dry, add 1 teaspoon oil or water.

11. Mound the filling in the caps.

12. Bake 15 to 20 minutes, until the caps feel tender and the filling is hot and lightly crisp on top.

13. Cool 3 minutes, then serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The caps should feel tender. The filling should feel hot and slightly crisp on top. You should smell roasted mushroom, garlic, and toasted crumbs, not burning, and not a slimy, off mushroom smell. If raw mushrooms are slimy or smell sour, throw them out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The caps should look shrunken and tender, and the filling should look lightly golden, not dry and dark, and not wet and pale.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The caps should look shrunken and tender, and the filling should look lightly golden, not dry and dark, and not wet and pale.

SAFETY
Use mitts. The pan and filling are very hot. Use a stable board for chopping. Mushrooms can be sandy, so wipe or rinse them and dry them well. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little cooked sausage, cooked to 160 degrees F for pork sausage or 165 degrees F for poultry sausage, can be mixed into the filling. Smaller caps are easier to eat in one or two bites.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
