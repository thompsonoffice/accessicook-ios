Hot Apple Cider
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Apple cider or apple juice heated with a few warm spices, served in a mug. This is not hard cider, and it is not alcoholic in this recipe. Heat it until it is steaming hot, but do not boil it hard, or the flavor can taste duller and the pot can scorch.

SERVINGS
4 mugs

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 4 cups apple cider or apple juice
- 2 cinnamon sticks, or 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) ground cinnamon
- 2 whole cloves, optional, or a pinch of ground cloves
- 1 thin orange slice, optional
- Optional: 1 to 2 teaspoons brown sugar or maple syrup, if the cider is very tart

TACTILE TIPS
The cider should feel steaming hot and should smell like apple and cinnamon, not scorched. If you use whole spices, they should stay in the pot or be strained out so no one bites a clove by surprise.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the cider, cinnamon, optional cloves, and optional orange slice in a pot.

2. Heat over medium-low heat, stirring now and then, until the cider is steaming hot and you see small bubbles at the edge, not a rolling boil.

3. Taste a cooled sip, and add optional sugar or syrup if it needs it.

4. Strain out whole spices if you used them, or ladle carefully around them.

5. Pour into mugs.

6. Let it sit 1 minute, then drink.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The cider should feel steaming hot and should smell like apple and cinnamon, not scorching or fermentation. If the cider smelled fermented or looked fizzy before heating, and it was not sold as a fizzy drink, throw it out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cider should look clear and amber, maybe with a little spice sediment if you used ground cinnamon, not dark and scorched, and not foaming from a hard boil.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cider should look clear and amber, maybe with a little spice sediment if you used ground cinnamon, not dark and scorched, and not foaming from a hard boil.

SAFETY
The pot, steam, and mugs are very hot. Pour slowly. Let the drink sit 1 minute so you do not burn your mouth. Whole cloves are a choking or biting hazard if they are left in a mug, so strain them out. Reheat leftovers until steaming hot, without a hard boil. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A cinnamon stick in each mug is a good stirrer and a warning that spices were used. This is a good winter drink next to Molasses Cookies or Gingerbread in the Baked Goods chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
