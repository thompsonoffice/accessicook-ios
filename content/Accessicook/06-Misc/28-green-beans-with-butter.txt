Green Beans with Butter
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Green beans cooked until tender-crisp, then tossed with butter and salt. You can boil, steam, or saute them. This version simmers them in a little water, then finishes them in butter. Saute means cook quickly in a little fat, stirring.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound green beans, trimmed
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 2 tablespoons butter
- Optional: 1 garlic clove, minced, or a squeeze of lemon

TACTILE TIPS
Trim means snap or cut off the stem ends. Raw beans should feel firm and snap, not limp. Done beans should feel tender-crisp, not squeaky and raw, and not floppy and dull.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Wash the beans.

2. Trim the stem ends on a stable board.

3. Put the beans in a wide skillet or pot with 1/2 cup (half a cup) water and the salt.

4. Cover and cook over medium heat 6 to 10 minutes, until the beans feel tender-crisp, and most of the water is gone.

5. If water remains, take off the lid and let it cook off.

6. Add the butter and optional garlic.

7. Toss 1 minute, until the butter is melted and the garlic, if used, smells fragrant, not brown and bitter.

8. Add a squeeze of lemon if you like, and more salt if it needs it.

9. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The beans should feel tender-crisp. You should smell butter and green beans, and optional garlic or lemon, not burning butter.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The beans should look bright green and glossy, not olive-drab, and not wrinkled and dry.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The beans should look bright green and glossy, not olive-drab, and not wrinkled and dry.

SAFETY
Use a stable board for trimming. The pan is hot and can spatter when the butter goes in. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Frozen green beans can be used. Cook them from frozen, and start checking a minute earlier, because they can go from tender to mushy quickly.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
