ACCESSICOOK
Corn on the Cob
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Whole ears of corn cooked in simmering water until the kernels are tender and hot. You can also steam the ears or cook them in the microwave. Butter and salt at the table are plenty.

SERVINGS
4 ears

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 4 ears corn, husks and silk removed
- Butter and salt, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Husk means the leafy outer layers. Silk means the fine threads under the husk. After cooking, a kernel should feel tender and release a little juice when you press it, not hard and starchy, and not mushy and collapsed.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil, meaning large, rolling bubbles.

2. Add the corn.

3. When the water returns to a simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles, cook 4 to 8 minutes, until a kernel feels tender. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

4. Lift the ears out with tongs.

5. Let them sit 1 minute.

6. Serve with butter and salt.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A kernel should feel tender and juicy. You should smell sweet corn, not sour or fermented corn, which should be thrown out before cooking. The ears will be very hot and wet.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The kernels should look bright yellow and plump, not dull and shrunken, and not still pale and raw if the corn was very young and pale to start, though some varieties stay paler.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The kernels should look bright yellow and plump, not dull and shrunken, and not still pale and raw if the corn was very young and pale to start, though some varieties stay paler.

SAFETY
Boiling water can burn you. Use tongs, not your fingers, to lift the ears. Let them sit 1 minute. Butter can make the cob slippery, so use corn holders or a fork if you need a better grip. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a microwave ear, leave the husk on, microwave 3 to 4 minutes, then let it sit 2 minutes, and husk carefully, because steam will escape. For a skillet version, cook shucked ears in a little butter over medium heat, turning, until some kernels feel browned and all feel tender.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
