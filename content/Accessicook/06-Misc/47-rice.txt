ACCESSICOOK
Rice
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A basic pot of white long-grain rice. The rice simmers in a measured amount of water, then rests off the heat so it can finish steaming. Simmer means cook in liquid with small, gentle bubbles, not a hard boil. Do not lift the lid during the simmer and rest if you can help it.

SERVINGS
4 as a side, about 3 cups cooked

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes, plus a 10-minute rest

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup long-grain white rice
- 1 3/4 cups (one and three-quarters cups) water, or as the package directs, because brands vary
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 teaspoon butter or oil, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Uncooked rice feels hard and dry. After cooking and resting, the grains should feel tender, not chalky in the center, and not mushy, and they should be separate, not a wet clump.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Rinse the rice in a fine strainer under cold water until the water runs less cloudy, unless the package says not to rinse.

2. Put the rice, water, salt, and optional butter or oil in a small pot with a tight lid.

3. Bring to a boil, meaning large, rolling bubbles, then stir once, cover, and reduce the heat to low so the pot stays at a gentle simmer. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens.

4. Cook 15 to 18 minutes, or as the package directs, without lifting the lid.

5. Take the pot off the heat.

6. Let it rest, still covered, 10 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

7. Uncover and fluff the rice with a fork, meaning gently loosen the grains.

8. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The grains should feel tender, not chalky in the center. You should smell cooked rice, not scorching. If you smell scorching, take the pot off the heat and leave the lid on 10 minutes, then spoon out the undamaged rice from the top.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The rice should look fluffy and separate, with steam rising, not wet and soupy, and not dry with a hard, crusted bottom unless a thin toasted layer formed, which can happen on slightly higher heat.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The rice should look fluffy and separate, with steam rising, not wet and soupy, and not dry with a hard, crusted bottom unless a thin toasted layer formed, which can happen on slightly higher heat.

SAFETY
The pot and steam are very hot. Tilt the lid away from you when you open it. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Cool leftover rice quickly in a shallow container and refrigerate within 2 hours. Do not leave cooked rice out overnight. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the rice is still firm after the rest, add 2 tablespoons water, cover, and return to low heat for 3 to 5 minutes, then rest again. For fried rice, cool this rice, refrigerate it, then see Leftover Fried Rice in the Midnight Snacks chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
