ACCESSICOOK
Bread Stuffing
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Cubed bread mixed with cooked onion and celery, broth, and eggs, then baked until the top is crisp and the center is set and hot, 165 degrees F. This is baked in a dish, not inside a bird, which is a safer and more even method. Dry or slightly stale bread works better than very fresh, soft bread.

SERVINGS
8 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 10 cups cubed sturdy bread, about 3/4 inch (three-quarters of an inch) pieces, left out to dry for a few hours, or lightly toasted
- 6 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 celery stalks, diced
- 2 teaspoons dried sage, or 1 tablespoon chopped fresh sage, plus 1 teaspoon dried thyme or poultry seasoning
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 2 to 2 1/2 cups (two to two and a half cups) broth
- 2 large eggs, beaten

TACTILE TIPS
The bread cubes should feel dry at the edges, not soft and squishy, before you mix. After mixing, the cubes should feel moistened, not swimming in broth, and not dry in the middle of the bowl. After baking, the center should feel set and steaming hot, 165 degrees F, and the top should feel crisp.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Butter a 9 by 13 inch baking dish, or a similar 3 quart dish.

3. Melt the butter in a skillet over medium heat.

4. Add the onion and celery, and cook until they feel soft, 6 to 8 minutes.

5. Stir in the herbs, salt, and pepper.

6. Put the bread cubes in a large bowl.

7. Add the onion mix and toss.

8. Mix 2 cups of the broth with the eggs.

9. Pour over the bread and toss until the cubes feel evenly moist.

10. Add more broth if dry pockets remain.

11. Spread the mix in the dish.

12. Cover with foil.

13. Bake 25 minutes.

14. Uncover and bake 20 to 25 minutes more, until the top feels crisp and the center is 165 degrees F and set, not wet and liquid.

15. Cool 10 minutes, then serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The center should be 165 degrees F and feel set and steaming hot. The top should feel crisp. You should smell toasted bread, butter, onion, and sage, not raw egg or burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look golden and crisp. The center, when you scoop it, should look moist and set, not soupy and not dry as crumbs.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look golden and crisp. The center, when you scoop it, should look moist and set, not soupy and not dry as crumbs.

SAFETY
Do not taste the raw mix. It contains eggs. Use mitts. The dish is very hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, and reheat to 165 degrees F. This recipe is for a baking dish, not for stuffing the cavity of a bird, where the center can stay too cool. Cook casseroles to 165 degrees F in the center.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you want a softer stuffing, leave the foil on for more of the bake. Sausage stuffing starts with cooked, drained sausage, cooked to 160 degrees F for pork sausage or 165 degrees F for poultry sausage, then mixed in with the bread.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
