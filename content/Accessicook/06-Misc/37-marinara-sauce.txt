Marinara Sauce
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple tomato sauce with garlic, oil, and dried herbs. This is the sauce to keep for pasta, pizza, chicken Parmesan, and baked ziti in the Supper chapter. Simmer means cook with small, gentle bubbles so the sauce thickens and the raw tomato taste cooks off.

SERVINGS
About 4 cups, 6 to 8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 3 garlic cloves, minced, or 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 can (28 ounces) crushed tomatoes, or whole tomatoes you crush with a spoon
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano or Italian seasoning
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) sugar, optional, if the tomatoes taste very sharp
- A pinch of red pepper flakes, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Mince means cut the garlic into very small pieces. The finished sauce should feel slightly thick, like a loose puree that coats pasta, not watery like tomato juice, and not a stiff paste. It should smell cooked and round, not raw and tinny.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil in a pot over medium heat.

2. Add the garlic and optional red pepper flakes, and cook 30 seconds, until the garlic smells fragrant, not brown and bitter.

3. Add the tomatoes, salt, oregano or Italian seasoning, and optional sugar.

4. Bring to a simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles.

5. Reduce the heat to low. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens.

6. Simmer 15 to 25 minutes, stirring now and then, until the sauce feels slightly thick and the raw tomato smell is gone.

7. Taste a cooled spoonful, and add salt or a pinch of sugar if it needs it. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

8. Serve hot, or cool and store.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The sauce should feel slightly thick and should coat a spoon. You should smell cooked tomato, garlic, and herbs, not raw tomato, tin, or burning garlic.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look red and slightly thick, with a little oil on the surface, not watery, and not dark and scorched at the edges of the pot.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look red and slightly thick, with a little oil on the surface, not watery, and not dark and scorched at the edges of the pot.

SAFETY
The pot and bubbling sauce are very hot and can spatter. Use a lid that is slightly ajar if it spatters a lot. Mince garlic on a stable board. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, and reheat to 165 degrees F, or until the sauce bubbles again. The sauce also freezes well. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little chopped basil at the end is a good finish. If you want a meat sauce, see Spaghetti with Meat Sauce in the Supper chapter, instead of adding raw meat to this page. For pizza, you can use this sauce as it is, or see Pizza Sauce in this chapter for a thicker, no-simmer option.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
