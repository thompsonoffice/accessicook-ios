Buttered Noodles
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Plain pasta tossed with butter, salt, and optional cheese. This is a simple side, or a mild main dish for a night when you want something easy. See the Pasta Primer in the Supper chapter for the basic boiling method. Save a little pasta water so the butter coats the noodles.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 12 ounces egg noodles or any short or long pasta
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the pasta water
- 3 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) grated Parmesan cheese, optional
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The pasta should feel tender but still slightly firm in the center, not hard, and not mushy. The dressed noodles should feel evenly coated and slightly glossy, not dry, and not swimming in melted butter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cook the pasta in salted boiling water until it is tender but still slightly firm in the center.

2. Save 1/2 cup (half a cup) of the pasta water, then drain. Drain means pour off the liquid.

3. Return the pasta to the pot.

4. Add the butter and a splash of pasta water.

5. Toss until the butter is melted and the noodles feel coated, adding more pasta water if they are tight.

6. Add optional Parmesan and pepper.

7. Taste a cooled noodle, and add salt if it needs it.

8. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The pasta should feel tender with a slight firmness. You should smell butter and pasta, and optional cheese, not a starchy, undercooked smell.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The noodles should look glossy and evenly coated, not dry and clumped, and not sitting in a puddle of butter.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The noodles should look glossy and evenly coated, not dry and clumped, and not sitting in a puddle of butter.

SAFETY
Boiling water can burn you. See the Pasta Primer for draining safety. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F with a splash of water. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little garlic, cooked briefly in the butter before you add the pasta, is a good extra. Peas or leftover chicken, reheated to 165 degrees F, can turn this into a fuller plate.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
