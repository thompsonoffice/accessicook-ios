ACCESSICOOK
Roasted Vegetables
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A mixed tray of vegetables tossed with oil and salt, then roasted until they are tender and browned at the edges. Cut denser vegetables smaller so they finish with the quicker ones. This is one of the most useful sides in the book.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 8 cups cut vegetables, such as broccoli florets, carrot chunks, bell pepper strips, and onion wedges
- 3 tablespoons oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- Optional: 1 teaspoon dried herbs, or 2 garlic cloves, minced, added in the last 10 minutes

TACTILE TIPS
Pieces should be bite-size and fairly even. After roasting, denser vegetables such as carrots should feel tender when pierced, and quicker vegetables should feel tender with slightly crisp, browned edges, not raw and not burnt and bitter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Line a sheet pan, or use two pans if the vegetables will be crowded.

3. Toss the vegetables with oil, salt, and pepper, until they feel lightly coated.

4. Spread in a single layer with a little space.

5. Crowding makes them steam.

6. Roast 15 minutes.

7. Turn the pieces.

8. Roast 10 to 20 minutes more, until a fork slides into the denser pieces and the edges feel browned.

9. Add optional garlic or herbs in the last 10 minutes.

10. Taste a cooled piece, and add salt if it needs it.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide into carrots and other dense pieces. You should smell roasted vegetables and oil, not burning. Edges should feel slightly crisp and browned, not leathery and not raw.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The vegetables should look browned at the edges and still a little bright, not gray and soggy, and not black.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The vegetables should look browned at the edges and still a little bright, not gray and soggy, and not black.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a sharp knife, slowly. Use mitts. The pan is very hot. Let a test piece cool a few seconds. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Keep watery vegetables such as zucchini on their own pan, or add them halfway, so they do not make everything soggy. Leftovers reheat well to 165 degrees F and can go into rice bowls or omelettes.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
