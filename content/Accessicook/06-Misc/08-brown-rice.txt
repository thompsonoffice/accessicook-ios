ACCESSICOOK
Brown Rice
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A pot of brown rice, which still has the bran layer and takes longer than white rice. Use the package water amount if it differs from this page. The rice should be tender, with a slight chew, not hard in the center.

SERVINGS
4 as a side, about 3 cups cooked

TOTAL TIME
About 50 minutes, plus a 10-minute rest

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup brown rice
- 2 1/4 cups (two and a quarter cups) water, or as the package directs
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 teaspoon oil, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Brown rice feels a little harder and more elongated than white rice when raw. After cooking and resting, the grains should feel tender with a slight chew, not chalky and hard in the center, and not mushy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Rinse the rice in a fine strainer, unless the package says not to.

2. Put the rice, water, salt, and optional oil in a pot with a tight lid.

3. Bring to a boil, meaning large, rolling bubbles, then stir once, cover, and reduce the heat to a low simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

4. Cook 40 to 45 minutes, or as the package directs, without lifting the lid.

5. Take the pot off the heat.

6. Let it rest, still covered, 10 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

7. Fluff with a fork, meaning gently loosen the grains.

8. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The grains should feel tender with a slight chew, not hard in the center. You should smell a nutty, cooked-rice smell, not scorching.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The rice should look separate and slightly nutty-brown, with steam rising, not wet and not a hard, dry crust unless the heat was too high.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The rice should look separate and slightly nutty-brown, with steam rising, not wet and not a hard, dry crust unless the heat was too high.

SAFETY
The pot and steam are very hot. Tilt the lid away from you. Cool leftovers quickly and refrigerate within 2 hours. Reheat to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the rice is still firm, add 2 tablespoons water, cover, and cook 5 minutes more, then rest again. Brown rice keeps well and is a good base for the Rice and Bean Bowl in the Lunch chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
