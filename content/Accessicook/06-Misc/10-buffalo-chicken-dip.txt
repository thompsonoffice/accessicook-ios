Buffalo Chicken Dip
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A hot dip of cooked, shredded chicken mixed with cream cheese, a buffalo-style hot sauce, and cheese, baked until bubbling, 165 degrees F in the center. Use already-cooked chicken, such as a leftover baked breast, reheated in the dip to 165 degrees F. This is a party cousin of the Boneless Wings in this chapter.

SERVINGS
8 as an appetizer

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups cooked, shredded chicken
- 8 ounces cream cheese, softened
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) buffalo-style hot sauce, or to taste
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) ranch dressing or sour cream, or Ranch Dip from this chapter
- 1 cup shredded cheddar or a cheddar-jack mix, divided

TACTILE TIPS
The mix should feel thick and even before it bakes, with visible shreds of chicken. After baking, the center should feel steaming hot, 165 degrees F, and creamy, not watery, and not oily.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease a small baking dish.

3. Mix the cream cheese, hot sauce, and ranch or sour cream until even.

4. Stir in the chicken and 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) of the cheese.

5. Spread the mix in the dish.

6. Sprinkle the remaining cheese on top.

7. Bake 20 to 25 minutes, until the dip is bubbling and the center is 165 degrees F.

8. Cool 5 minutes, then serve with celery, chips, or crackers.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The center should be 165 degrees F and feel steaming hot. You should smell chicken, hot sauce, and melted cheese, not scorching. The dip should feel creamy and thick.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look melted and bubbling, with a little orange oil from the sauce, which is common, not dried and dark brown.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look melted and bubbling, with a little orange oil from the sauce, which is common, not dried and dark brown.

SAFETY
Use already-cooked chicken. The finished dip must reach 165 degrees F. Use mitts. Cool 5 minutes, because bubbling sauce and cheese can burn your mouth. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, and reheat to 165 degrees F. Wash the board after handling the chicken, even though it is cooked, if you also cut raw vegetables on it later, or use a clean board. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Rotisserie chicken, skin and bones removed, works well. Start with less hot sauce if you are not sure about the heat, then add more.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
