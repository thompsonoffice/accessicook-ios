ACCESSICOOK
Garlic Bread
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A loaf or baguette split or sliced, spread with garlic butter, and baked until the edges are crisp and the butter is melted. This is the supper-table version. For a cheesy late-night slice, see Garlic Cheese Toast in the Midnight Snacks chapter.

SERVINGS
8 slices

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 baguette or small Italian loaf, or 8 slices sturdy bread
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, softened
- 2 garlic cloves, minced, or 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) garlic powder
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, if the butter is unsalted
- Optional: 1 tablespoon chopped parsley, or 2 tablespoons grated Parmesan cheese

TACTILE TIPS
Softened butter should spread without tearing the bread. Mince means cut the garlic into very small pieces. The finished bread should feel crisp at the edges and soft in the middle, and it should smell toasty and garlicky, not burnt.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Mix the butter, garlic, salt, and any optional parsley or Parmesan until even.

3. Split the loaf, or slice the bread.

4. Spread the garlic butter on the cut sides.

5. Set the bread on a pan, buttered side up.

6. Bake 8 to 12 minutes, until the edges feel crisp and the butter is melted and bubbling.

7. For a slightly crisper top, you can switch to a brief broil, but do not walk away.

8. Cool 1 minute, then cut into pieces if you used a whole loaf, and serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel crisp. You should smell toasted bread, butter, and garlic, not burning garlic or burning bread. The bread will be very hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The edges should look golden, and the butter should look melted. The garlic should look pale, not dark brown. The bread should not look black at the tips.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The edges should look golden, and the butter should look melted. The garlic should look pale, not dark brown. The bread should not look black at the tips.

SAFETY
Use mitts. Mince garlic on a stable board. If you broil, do not walk away, and keep the pan a safe distance from the element. Melted butter can drip. Use a sheet pan. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Assemble the loaf, wrap it, and refrigerate it for a few hours if you want to bake it later, then add a minute or two. This is best eaten at once.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
