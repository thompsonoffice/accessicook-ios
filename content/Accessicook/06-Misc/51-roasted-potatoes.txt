ACCESSICOOK
Roasted Potatoes
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Potato chunks tossed with oil and salt, then roasted until the edges are crisp and the centers are tender. Cut the pieces as even as you can so they finish together.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 pounds potatoes, cut into 1 to 1 1/2 inch (one to one and a half inch) chunks, scrubbed, peeled if you prefer
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper or paprika, optional
- Optional: 1 teaspoon dried rosemary or thyme, or 2 garlic cloves, minced, added in the last 10 minutes so the garlic does not burn

TACTILE TIPS
Even chunks cook more evenly. After roasting, a fork should slide into a piece easily, and the edges should feel crisp, not leathery and not soggy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Line a sheet pan with parchment, or oil the pan.

3. Toss the potatoes with oil, salt, and optional pepper or paprika, until they feel lightly coated.

4. Spread them in a single layer with space between the pieces.

5. Roast 20 minutes.

6. Turn the pieces.

7. Roast 15 to 20 minutes more, until a fork slides in easily and the edges feel crisp.

8. Add optional garlic or herbs in the last 10 minutes if you are using them.

9. Taste a cooled piece, and add salt if it needs it.

10. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide in easily. The edges should feel crisp. You should smell toasted potato and oil, not burning or raw potato.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The potatoes should look golden brown with darker crisp edges, not pale and not burnt. The insides should look fluffy and dry, not wet and hard.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The potatoes should look golden brown with darker crisp edges, not pale and not burnt. The insides should look fluffy and dry, not wet and hard.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a sharp knife, slowly. Use mitts. The pan is very hot. Let a test piece cool a few seconds, because the inside holds heat. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Yukon Golds get creamy centers. Russets get fluffier interiors and crisper edges. Do not crowd the pan, or the potatoes will steam.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
