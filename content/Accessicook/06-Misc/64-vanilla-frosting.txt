Vanilla Frosting
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple buttercream, meaning a frosting of butter and powdered sugar beaten until it is fluffy, flavored with vanilla. The cake or cupcakes must be fully cool before you frost them, or the frosting will melt and slide. See Yellow Cake and Vanilla Cupcakes in the Baked Goods chapter.

SERVINGS
About 2 cups, enough for 12 cupcakes or a thin layer on a 9 by 13 inch cake

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) unsalted butter, softened
- 3 cups powdered sugar, plus 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) more if the frosting is too thin
- 2 to 3 tablespoons milk
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
- A pinch of salt

TACTILE TIPS
Softened butter should give like cool clay, not feel melted. Powdered sugar is very fine and can puff into the air, so add it slowly. The finished frosting should feel smooth, fluffy, and spreadable, not stiff as paste, and not soupy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Beat the butter in a bowl until it feels smooth and slightly fluffy.

2. Add the powdered sugar 1 cup at a time, mixing slowly at first so it does not puff out of the bowl, then more firmly, until it is combined.

3. Add 2 tablespoons milk, the vanilla, and the salt.

4. Beat until the frosting feels fluffy and spreadable.

5. If it is too thick, add more milk 1 teaspoon at a time.

6. If it is too thin, add more powdered sugar 2 tablespoons at a time.

7. Spread or pipe the frosting on a fully cooled cake or cupcakes.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The frosting should feel smooth, fluffy, and spreadable. You should smell butter and vanilla, not raw, dusty sugar once it is fully mixed. Softened butter should not feel greasy and melted, or the frosting can go slack.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The frosting should look pale, smooth, and slightly fluffy, not grainy, and not oily and split or soupy and slack.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The frosting should look pale, smooth, and slightly fluffy, not grainy, and not oily and split or soupy and slack.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw frosting if you are concerned about raw flour on shared spoons from a baking project. There are no raw eggs in this frosting. Powdered sugar can puff. Mix slowly at first. If the kitchen is very warm, chill the frosting 10 minutes, then beat again. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a thinner glaze, add more milk until it is pourable, and spoon it over a cooled loaf, such as the Lemon Loaf in the Baked Goods chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
