Hummus
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A chickpea dip blended with tahini, which is sesame paste, plus lemon, garlic, and oil. A food processor or blender makes it smooth. If you do not have tahini, a little extra oil and a pinch of cumin still make a good dip, though the flavor will be lighter. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

SERVINGS
About 2 cups, 6 to 8 snack servings

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 can (15 ounces) chickpeas, drained and rinsed, with 2 tablespoons of the liquid reserved
- 3 tablespoons tahini, or 2 tablespoons extra oil if you do not have tahini
- 2 tablespoons lemon juice, plus more to taste
- 1 small garlic clove, minced, or 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) garlic powder
- 2 tablespoons olive oil, plus more for serving
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) cumin, optional
- 2 to 4 tablespoons reserved chickpea liquid or water, as needed

TACTILE TIPS
Tahini feels thick, oily, and a little bitter on its own, then tastes nutty and creamy in the dip. The finished hummus should feel very smooth and thick, like a dip that holds a soft peak on a spoon, not pasty and dry, and not runny. Mince means cut the garlic into very small pieces.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the chickpeas, tahini or extra oil, lemon juice, garlic, olive oil, salt, and optional cumin in a food processor or blender.

2. Blend until the mix feels smooth, stopping to scrape the sides.

3. Add reserved liquid or water 1 tablespoon at a time, and blend again, until the hummus feels creamy and thick but spreadable.

4. Taste a little, and add salt or lemon if it needs it.

5. Spoon into a bowl.

6. Add a little olive oil on top if you like.

7. Serve with pita, crackers, or vegetables, or see Hummus and Pita in the Midnight Snacks chapter.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The hummus should feel smooth and creamy. You should smell sesame, lemon, chickpea, and garlic, not a sour or fermented smell. If canned chickpeas smell off, throw them out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The hummus should look smooth and pale tan, maybe with a swirl of oil on top, not grainy and dry, and not watery.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The hummus should look smooth and pale tan, maybe with a swirl of oil on top, not grainy and dry, and not watery.

SAFETY
A food processor blade is sharp. Unplug the machine and wait for the blade to stop before you scrape the bowl. Mince garlic on a stable board. Keep hummus refrigerated, and do not leave it out more than 2 hours. Use within 4 days. If it smells sour or looks moldy, throw it out. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of paprika on top is traditional. For a roasted-red-pepper variation, blend in 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) drained roasted red peppers.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
