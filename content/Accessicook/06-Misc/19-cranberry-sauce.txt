Cranberry Sauce
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Fresh cranberries simmered with sugar and a little water until they burst and the sauce thickens. The sauce will thicken more as it cools. This is the usual partner for roast turkey or turkey breast in the Supper chapter.

SERVINGS
8 as a side, about 2 cups

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 12 ounces fresh or frozen cranberries, about 3 cups
- 3/4 to 1 cup sugar, depending on how tart you like it
- 1 cup water
- Optional: 1 teaspoon orange zest, or 2 tablespoons orange juice

TACTILE TIPS
Zest is the fragrant orange outer skin, not the bitter white pith, if you use it. Raw cranberries feel firm and very tart. After simmering, most of the berries should have burst, and the sauce should feel slightly thick, like a loose jam, and it will thicken more as it cools.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the cranberries, sugar, and water in a pot.

2. Add optional zest or juice.

3. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, meaning small, gentle bubbles, stirring until the sugar dissolves. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

4. Cook 8 to 12 minutes, stirring now and then, until most of the berries have burst and the sauce feels slightly thick.

5. Take the pot off the heat.

6. Cool at least 30 minutes. The sauce will thicken as it cools.

7. Taste a cooled spoonful, and add a little sugar if it is too tart, stirring until it dissolves.

8. Serve at room temperature or chilled.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Most of the berries should feel burst. The sauce should feel slightly thick in the pot and thicker after it cools. You should smell cranberry and sugar, not burning sugar.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look glossy and deep red, with some whole and some burst berries, not watery and pale, and not dark and scorched.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look glossy and deep red, with some whole and some burst berries, not watery and pale, and not dark and scorched.

SAFETY
The pot and bubbling sugar-fruit mix are very hot and can spatter. Stir with a long-handled spoon. Cool before you taste for sweetness. Refrigerate leftovers, and use within 1 week, or freeze. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This can be made 2 or 3 days ahead. If you want a smoother sauce, mash the berries more as they cook, or puree after cooling, carefully, once they are not hot.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
