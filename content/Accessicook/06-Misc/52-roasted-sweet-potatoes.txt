Roasted Sweet Potatoes
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Sweet potato chunks roasted until the edges are browned and the centers are soft and creamy. They need the same high heat as regular roasted potatoes, and they can brown faster because of their natural sugar, so check a little earlier.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 pounds sweet potatoes, peeled if you prefer, cut into 1 inch chunks
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 3/4 teaspoon (three-quarters of a teaspoon) salt
- Optional: 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) cinnamon or smoked paprika

TACTILE TIPS
Even chunks cook at the same rate. A fork should slide in with almost no resistance when they are done, and the edges should feel slightly caramelized, meaning browned and a little sticky-sweet, not burnt and bitter, and not hard in the center.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Line a sheet pan with parchment, or oil the pan.

3. Toss the sweet potatoes with oil, salt, and any optional spice, until they feel lightly coated.

4. Spread in a single layer with space between the pieces.

5. Roast 15 minutes.

6. Turn the pieces.

7. Roast 10 to 20 minutes more, until a fork slides in easily and the edges feel browned, not burnt.

8. Taste a cooled piece, and add salt if it needs it.

9. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide in easily. The edges should feel browned and a little caramelized. You should smell sweet potato and toasted sugar, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The pieces should look browned and slightly shiny at the edges, with orange, creamy centers, not pale, and not blackened.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The pieces should look browned and slightly shiny at the edges, with orange, creamy centers, not pale, and not blackened.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a sharp knife, slowly. Sweet potatoes can be awkward to cut. Use a cut-resistant glove on the hand that holds the potato, and cut a thin slice off one side first so the potato sits flat. Use mitts. The pan is very hot. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A drizzle of maple syrup in the last 5 minutes makes a sweeter side. Leftovers are good in a lunch bowl, reheated to 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
