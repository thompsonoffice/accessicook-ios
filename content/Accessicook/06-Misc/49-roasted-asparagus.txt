ACCESSICOOK
Roasted Asparagus
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Asparagus spears roasted until they are tender and slightly browned at the tips. Snap off the woody ends before you season the spears. Thicker spears need a few more minutes than thin ones.

SERVINGS
4 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound asparagus
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- Optional: lemon wedges, or 2 tablespoons grated Parmesan cheese, added at the end

TACTILE TIPS
Hold a spear and bend it near the end. The woody part will often snap off where the tender part begins. You can also trim the bottom 1 inch if snapping is awkward. Done asparagus should feel tender and slightly flexible, not raw and squeaky, and not floppy and mushy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Line a sheet pan with parchment, or oil the pan.

3. Trim or snap the woody ends on a stable board.

4. Toss the spears with oil and salt, until they feel lightly coated.

5. Spread them in a single layer.

6. Roast 8 to 14 minutes, depending on thickness, until a spear feels tender and the tips feel slightly crisp and browned, not burnt.

7. Add optional lemon or Parmesan.

8. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A spear should feel tender when pierced near the middle. You should smell roasted asparagus and oil, not burning. The tips can turn from toasted to burnt quickly, so check at the early time.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The spears should look bright green with browned tips, not olive-drab, and not pale and raw-looking.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The spears should look bright green with browned tips, not olive-drab, and not pale and raw-looking.

SAFETY
Use mitts. The pan is very hot. The tips are thin and can burn. Wash the asparagus, because sand can hide near the ends. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A squeeze of lemon is almost always a good idea. Leftovers can be chopped into a salad or an omelette, after a chill, or reheated to 165 degrees F if you want them hot.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
