ACCESSICOOK
Coleslaw
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Shredded cabbage in a creamy dressing, served cold. This is the slaw to go with Fish Tacos, pulled pork, or fried-style foods in this book. You can start with a bag of shredded coleslaw mix to skip the knife work.

SERVINGS
6 as a side

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes, plus 15 minutes of rest

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 6 cups shredded cabbage, or one 14 ounce bag of coleslaw mix
- 1 carrot, shredded, if your mix does not already include it
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) mayonnaise
- 1 tablespoon vinegar or lemon juice
- 1 teaspoon sugar, or to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper

TACTILE TIPS
Shredded cabbage should feel crisp and dry before you dress it. The dressing should feel smooth and pourable, like a thick cream, not pasty and not watery. After a short rest, the slaw should feel slightly softened but still crisp, not soupy. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. If you are shredding your own cabbage, cut it into thin shreds on a stable board, using a cut-resistant glove on the hand that holds the cabbage if you need it.

2. Shred the carrot.

3. Mix the mayonnaise, vinegar or lemon juice, sugar, salt, and pepper in a large bowl until smooth.

4. Add the cabbage and carrot.

5. Toss until the shreds feel evenly coated.

6. Taste a piece, and add salt, sugar, or vinegar if it needs it.

7. Rest 15 minutes in the refrigerator, then toss again and serve cold.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The cabbage should feel crisp at first, then slightly softened after the rest. The dressing should taste balanced, tangy and a little creamy. You should smell fresh cabbage and vinegar, not a sour, off smell. If bagged mix smells sour or sulfurous in an off way, throw it out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The slaw should look moist and evenly coated, not dry and not sitting in a puddle of dressing. The cabbage should still look fairly crisp and pale, not dull and limp if you serve it the same day.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The slaw should look moist and evenly coated, not dry and not sitting in a puddle of dressing. The cabbage should still look fairly crisp and pale, not dull and limp if you serve it the same day.

SAFETY
Keep the slaw refrigerated. Do not leave it out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Because it contains mayonnaise, treat it as a perishable side. Wash the board and grater after use. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A spoon of Dijon mustard or a pinch of celery seed is a good extra. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). For a vinegar slaw without mayonnaise, toss the cabbage with 3 tablespoons vinegar, 1 tablespoon oil, 1 teaspoon sugar, and salt.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
