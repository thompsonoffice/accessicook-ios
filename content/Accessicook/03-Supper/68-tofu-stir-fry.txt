ACCESSICOOK
Tofu Stir-Fry
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Cubes of tofu and vegetables cooked in a hot pan with a soy sauce. Pressing the tofu first helps it brown instead of steaming. This is a meatless stir-fry that uses the same vegetable method as the chicken and beef versions in this chapter.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 14 ounces firm or extra-firm tofu, drained and pressed, then cut into cubes
- 2 tablespoons oil, divided
- 4 cups cut vegetables
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) stir-fry sauce, or 3 tablespoons soy sauce mixed with 1 tablespoon sugar and 1 minced garlic clove
- Rice, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Press the tofu by wrapping it in a clean towel and setting a heavy plate on it for 10 to 15 minutes. It should feel drier and springy. Cubes should hold together in the pan.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cut the protein and vegetables into pieces about the same size so they cook evenly.

2. Mix the sauce ingredients in a small bowl.

3. Pat the protein dry.

4. Season it with the salt, if listed.

5. Heat the oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat until it smells hot and a piece of vegetable sizzles at once.

6. Cook the tofu cubes in 1 tablespoon oil, turning now and then, until they feel lightly golden on a few sides, then move them to a plate.

7. Move the protein to a plate if the pan is crowded.

8. Add a little more oil if the pan is dry.

9. Add the firmer vegetables first.

10. Stir and cook until the vegetables are tender-crisp, meaning they are hot and slightly bent but not mushy.

11. Return the protein to the pan.

12. Add the sauce.

13. Stir until the sauce thickens slightly and coats everything, 1 to 2 minutes.

14. Serve hot over rice if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tofu should feel hot and slightly crisp at the edges, not wet and raw-tasting. Vegetables should feel tender-crisp. The sauce should smell savory, like soy and garlic, not burnt.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tofu should look lightly golden on a few sides. The vegetables should look bright and tender-crisp, not soggy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tofu should look lightly golden on a few sides. The vegetables should look bright and tender-crisp, not soggy.

SAFETY
Use a clean towel for pressing. The pan is very hot and can spatter. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cornstarch tossed on the tofu cubes before they go in the pan can help them crisp. Do not stir too often in the first few minutes, or they will crumble.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
