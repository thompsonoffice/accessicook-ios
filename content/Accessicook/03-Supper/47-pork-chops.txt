ACCESSICOOK
Pork Chops
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Pork chops cooked in a skillet until they reach 145 degrees F, then rested 3 minutes. That is the USDA minimum. The center may still look slightly pink, which is safe when the thermometer confirms the number.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 pork chops, about 3/4 to 1 inch (three-quarters to one inch) thick
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) garlic powder or paprika, optional
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 tablespoon butter, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Pat the chops dry. If they have a fat edge, you can stand them on that edge for a minute to render a little fat. Render means cook until some of the fat melts out.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pat the meat or fish dry.

2. Season both sides with the salt, pepper, and any rub listed.

3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat until it smells hot and the food sizzles firmly when it touches the pan.

4. Lay the pieces in the pan in a single layer. Do not crowd them.

5. Cook the first side 4 to 5 minutes, until browned and released.

6. Turn the pieces.

7. Turn and cook 4 to 6 minutes more, checking temperature at 4 minutes.

8. Take the chops off the heat at 145 degrees F.

9. Add optional butter and spoon it over them.

10. Rest the meat on a plate for the time in the recipe. Rest means let it sit so the juices settle.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the thickest part, not touching bone, must read 145 degrees F, then the chops should rest at least 3 minutes. They should feel springy-firm, not soft and raw, and not dry and hard.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The outside should be golden brown. At 145 degrees F the center may still look faintly pink, which is normal and safe with a thermometer check. The juices should not look thick and raw.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The outside should be golden brown. At 145 degrees F the center may still look faintly pink, which is normal and safe with a thermometer check. The juices should not look thick and raw.

SAFETY
Use a splatter screen. Wash the board. Do not press all the juices out. If the chops are much thicker than 1 inch, finish them in a 375 degree F oven after searing, still to 145 degrees F plus a rest. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A lid for the last few minutes can help a thick chop finish. A simple pan sauce of broth and mustard can be made after you take the chops out.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
