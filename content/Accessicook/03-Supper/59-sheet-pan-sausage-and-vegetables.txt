Sheet-Pan Sausage and Vegetables
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Sausages and cut vegetables roasted together on one pan. Confirm the sausage type and cook pork or beef sausage to 160 degrees F, and poultry sausage to 165 degrees F. Cut the vegetables so they finish at the same time.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound sausage links
- 5 cups cut vegetables, such as potatoes, peppers, onion, and broccoli
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon Italian seasoning or paprika

TACTILE TIPS
Cut dense potatoes smaller than the sausages. Leave space on the pan so everything roasts instead of steaming.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the middle while the oven is cold.

3. Pat the food dry.

4. Season it as the ingredients describe.

5. Toss the vegetables with oil, salt, and seasoning.

6. Spread them on a lined pan.

7. Nestle the sausages among them.

8. Set the food in the pan in a single layer, or as described.

9. Roast 25 to 35 minutes. Roast means cook uncovered in the hot oven.

10. Turn the pieces once halfway through.

11. Check a sausage in the center.

12. Pork or beef should read 160 degrees F, and poultry should read 165 degrees F.

13. A potato cube should pierce easily.

14. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Pork or beef sausage must be 160 degrees F in the center. Poultry sausage must be 165 degrees F. Vegetables should feel tender when pierced. You should smell roasted vegetables and sausage.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sausages should look browned, and the vegetables should look browned in spots and tender, not raw in the center of a potato, and not black.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sausages should look browned, and the vegetables should look browned in spots and tender, not raw in the center of a potato, and not black.

SAFETY
Check whether the sausage is raw or fully cooked. Raw sausage needs the full temperature check. Use mitts. Line the pan for easier cleanup. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Add quicker-cooking vegetables, such as broccoli, in the last 15 minutes. A drizzle of vinegar or mustard at the table brightens the pan.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
