ACCESSICOOK
Sloppy Joes
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Ground beef cooked in a slightly sweet, tangy tomato sauce, served on buns. The mix is meant to be messy. Cook the beef to 160 degrees F before or as the sauce simmers, confirming the temperature in the meat.

SERVINGS
4 to 6

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound ground beef
- 1 onion, diced
- 1 cup tomato sauce or ketchup
- 1 tablespoon brown sugar, optional
- 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce or vinegar
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 4 to 6 buns

TACTILE TIPS
The finished mix should feel loose and saucy, not dry, and not as thin as soup. It should mound on a spoon and drip a little, which is why the buns matter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cook the onion and beef in a skillet over medium heat, breaking up the meat, until the beef is 160 degrees F.

2. Stir in the tomato sauce or ketchup, brown sugar, Worcestershire or vinegar, and salt.

3. Simmer 8 to 10 minutes, until the sauce feels slightly thick and coats the meat. Simmer means small, gentle bubbles.

4. Taste a cooled bite and add salt if it needs it.

5. Spoon onto buns.

6. Serve with a plate underneath, because they drip.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Beef must be 160 degrees F. The mix should smell sweet-tangy and savory. It should feel saucy and hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The beef should be brown, with no pink. The sauce should look thick and red, clinging to the meat, not watery.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The beef should be brown, with no pink. The sauce should look thick and red, clinging to the meat, not watery.

SAFETY
Wash hands after raw beef. The sauce can spatter as it simmers. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Toast the buns so they hold up. A slice of cheese under the meat helps. Leftovers freeze well.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
