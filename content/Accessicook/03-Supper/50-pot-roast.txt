ACCESSICOOK
Pot Roast
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A large cut of beef, usually chuck, browned and then cooked slowly with broth and vegetables until it is tender. The USDA minimum is 145 degrees F with a 3-minute rest, but pot roast is not really done until a fork slides in easily, which takes much longer. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 3 to 3 1/2 hours, mostly unattended simmering

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 3 to 4 pounds beef chuck roast
- 2 teaspoons salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 1 onion, cut into wedges
- 4 carrots, cut into large pieces
- 2 potatoes, cut into large chunks, optional
- 2 cups beef broth
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste or Worcestershire sauce, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Chuck feels well marbled, with fat seams running through the meat. Early in cooking it will feel tough. When it is done, a fork should slide in with little resistance and the meat should start to shred.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 325 degrees F, or plan to simmer on the stove. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

2. Pat the beef dry.

3. Season it with salt and pepper.

4. Heat the oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat.

5. Brown the roast 3 to 4 minutes per side, until it smells deeply browned.

6. Move it out for a moment if the pot is crowded.

7. Add the onion.

8. Cook 2 minutes.

9. Add the broth and optional tomato paste or Worcestershire, scraping up the browned bits.

10. Return the beef to the pot. The liquid should come partway up the sides, not cover it completely.

11. Cover.

12. Cook in the oven, or at a gentle simmer on the stove, 2 hours.

13. Add the carrots and potatoes.

14. Cover and cook 45 to 60 minutes more, until the meat is fork-tender and the vegetables are soft.

15. Rest the meat 10 minutes.

16. Slice or shred it.

17. Serve with the vegetables and juices.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide in with little resistance. The meat should feel tender, not bouncy. The juices should smell rich and beefy. A thermometer will be well above 145 degrees F by this point. Tenderness is the finishing cue.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The beef should look browned on the outside and dull and cooked inside, not red and raw, and it should pull apart easily. The vegetables should look soft, not hard in the center.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The beef should look browned on the outside and dull and cooked inside, not red and raw, and it should pull apart easily. The vegetables should look soft, not hard in the center.

SAFETY
Use mitts with a heavy pot. Open the lid away from your face. Wash the board after raw beef. Cool leftovers in shallow containers and reheat to 165 degrees F. For steaks, roasts, and other whole pieces of beef, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. If this dish is a slow stew, keep cooking until the meat is also tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the juices are thin, simmer them uncovered for a few minutes, or thicken with a flour-and-water mix, after you take the meat out. This is a good make-ahead supper.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
