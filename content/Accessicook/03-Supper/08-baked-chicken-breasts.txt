Baked Chicken Breasts
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Boneless chicken breasts baked on a sheet pan until they reach 165 degrees F. Even thickness is the key, so the thin end does not dry out before the thick end is safe.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 boneless, skinless chicken breasts, about 2 pounds
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 1 teaspoon paprika or garlic powder, optional

TACTILE TIPS
If one end is much thicker, pound it gently under a piece of parchment, or slice the thick end almost in half and open it like a book, so the pieces are even.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the middle while the oven is cold.

3. Pat the food dry.

4. Season it as the ingredients describe.

5. Pat the chicken dry.

6. Toss it with oil, salt, pepper, and optional paprika or garlic powder.

7. Set the pieces on a lined pan with space between them.

8. Set the food in the pan in a single layer, or as described.

9. Roast 20 to 30 minutes, depending on thickness. Roast means cook uncovered in the hot oven.

10. Check at 20 minutes. The thickest part must read 165 degrees F.

11. Add time in 3-minute steps if needed.

12. Rest 5 to 10 minutes, then serve. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

13. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the thickest part must read 165 degrees F. The meat should feel firm and springy, not squishy. You should smell cooked chicken, not raw meat.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and white or very light tan in the center, with no raw pink or glassy look. The outside may be lightly golden.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and white or very light tan in the center, with no raw pink or glassy look. The outside may be lightly golden.

SAFETY
Use a raw-poultry board. Wash your hands. Use mitts. Do not put cooked chicken on the raw plate. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A 10-minute rest keeps the meat juicier. For more flavor, marinate in a little oil, lemon, and garlic for 20 minutes before baking, then still cook to 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
