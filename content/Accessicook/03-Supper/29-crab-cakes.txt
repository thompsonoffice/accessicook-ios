Crab Cakes
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Patties of crab meat, crumbs, and a little egg, cooked in a skillet until browned and hot in the center. The egg binder should reach 160 degrees F. Use lump crab if you can, and handle the mix gently so the lumps stay intact.

SERVINGS
4, about 8 small cakes

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes, plus brief chilling if the mix is soft

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound lump crab meat, picked over for shells
- 1 large egg
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) mayonnaise
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) breadcrumbs, plus more if the mix is wet
- 1 teaspoon mustard or Worcestershire sauce
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, or to taste
- 2 tablespoons oil or butter, for the pan

TACTILE TIPS
Feel through the crab for hard shell pieces before you mix. The mix should just hold a cake. Too many crumbs make it pasty. Too few and it falls apart.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the egg, mayonnaise, mustard or Worcestershire, and salt.

2. Gently fold in the crab and crumbs. Fold means turn the mixture over itself gently.

3. Shape 8 small cakes.

4. If they feel very soft, chill 15 to 20 minutes.

5. Heat the oil or butter in a skillet over medium heat.

6. Cook the cakes 3 to 5 minutes per side, until they feel browned and the centers are hot, 160 degrees F because of the egg.

7. Serve with lemon or a simple sauce.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The centers should be hot, 160 degrees F. You should smell toasted crumbs and sweet crab, not burning butter or a sour seafood smell. If you find a shell piece while eating, stop and remove it.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cakes should look golden brown, and the centers should look set, not wet and loose.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cakes should look golden brown, and the centers should look set, not wet and loose.

SAFETY
Pick through the crab carefully. The oil will spatter. Use a splatter screen. Because of the egg, heat the centers to 160 degrees F. Keep leftover cakes refrigerated and reheat to 165 degrees F. Cook fish to 145 degrees F, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes, meaning it comes apart easily with a fork.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
These are rich, so smaller cakes are easier to handle and cook through. A little lemon in a yogurt sauce is plenty of accompaniment.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
