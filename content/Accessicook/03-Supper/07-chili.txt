ACCESSICOOK
Chili
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
This recipe was on the original request list for the book.

This is a pot of classic beef and bean chili: browned ground beef, onion, garlic, tomatoes, beans, and chili powder, simmered until the flavors come together. It is a good supper on its own, with rice, or with cornbread from the Baked Goods chapter. The ground beef must reach 160 degrees F before you simmer it with the tomatoes.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour, including a 30-minute simmer

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A can opener
- A wooden spoon

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 medium yellow onion, diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 pound ground beef
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 2 tablespoons chili powder
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) paprika or smoked paprika
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) cayenne pepper, optional
- 1 can (15 ounces) tomato sauce, or 1 can (14 1/2 ounces) diced tomatoes plus 1/2 cup (half a cup) water
- 1 can (15 ounces) kidney beans or pinto beans, drained and rinsed
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) water, if the chili gets thicker than you like
- Optional toppings: shredded cheese, chopped onion, or sour cream

TACTILE TIPS
Ground beef is cool, damp, and packed. An onion is papery on the outside and slick inside after you peel it. Dice means cut into small, even cubes. Chili powder smells warm and earthy. Beans are soft and slippery once they are rinsed. Drain and rinse them in a colander so the can liquid does not make the chili taste metallic. Drain means pour off the liquid.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mince the garlic. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

3. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat.

4. Add the onion and a pinch of salt. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

5. Cook, stirring, 4 to 6 minutes, until the onion feels soft and smells sweet.

6. Add the garlic.

7. Cook about 30 seconds, until it is fragrant.

8. Add the ground beef and the remaining salt.

9. Break it into small pieces as it cooks.

10. Cook until the beef is brown and a thermometer in several pieces reads 160 degrees F. This usually takes 6 to 8 minutes.

11. Stir in the chili powder, cumin, paprika, and cayenne, if you are using it.

12. Cook 30 seconds so the spices smell toasty.

13. Add the tomato sauce or tomatoes, and the beans.

14. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

15. Cook uncovered 20 to 30 minutes, stirring now and then so it does not stick.

16. If the chili gets too thick, add a splash of water.

17. If it is still thin, simmer a few minutes more.

18. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

19. Serve hot, with toppings if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The beef should feel firm and crumble into pieces, not pasty and raw. A thermometer should read 160 degrees F in the meat before it simmers in the sauce. Finished chili should mound on a spoon and smell warm, tomatoey, and spicy, not raw or tinny.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The beef should be brown all the way through, with no pink, before you add the tomatoes. The finished chili should look thick, not like soup, and the oil may shine a little on the surface. The beans should look whole and hot.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The beef should be brown all the way through, with no pink, before you add the tomatoes. The finished chili should look thick, not like soup, and the oil may shine a little on the surface. The beans should look whole and hot.

SAFETY
Wash your hands, the board, and the spoon after they touch raw ground beef. Keep toppings such as cheese and sour cream cold until you serve. Reheat leftover chili to 165 degrees F, or until it bubbles throughout. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a milder chili, skip the cayenne and start with 1 tablespoon chili powder. For no beans, leave them out and add an extra half pound of beef, still cooked to 160 degrees F. For a vegetarian pot, see Vegetarian Chili in this chapter and skip this beef version. Chili often tastes even better the next day.

LEFTOVERS
Cool in shallow containers. Chili freezes well for 3 to 4 months. Reheat to 165 degrees F. Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
