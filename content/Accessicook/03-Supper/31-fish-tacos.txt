Fish Tacos
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Baked or pan-cooked fish in warm tortillas with a crisp slaw and a simple sauce. Cook the fish to 145 degrees F, or until it flakes. This is a lighter taco night than the ground-beef tacos in this chapter.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds) mild white fish fillets
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 teaspoon chili powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 8 small corn or flour tortillas
- 2 cups coleslaw or shredded cabbage
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) sour cream mixed with 1 teaspoon lime juice, or salsa

TACTILE TIPS
The fish should flake into large, moist pieces for filling tacos. Warm tortillas are less likely to crack. Keep the slaw crisp and relatively dry.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Toss the fish with oil, chili powder, and salt.

2. Bake at 400 degrees F for 12 to 18 minutes, or cook in a skillet over medium heat, until the fish is 145 degrees F or flakes easily.

3. Warm the tortillas until they feel flexible.

4. Flake the fish into large pieces.

5. Fill the tortillas with fish, slaw, and sauce.

6. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Fish should be 145 degrees F or flaky and opaque. Tortillas should feel warm and soft. You should smell chili, lime, and cooked fish, not a sour off smell.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The fish should look opaque and flake into moist pieces, not look wet and raw or dry and crumbly. The slaw should look crisp.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The fish should look opaque and flake into moist pieces, not look wet and raw or dry and crumbly. The slaw should look crisp.

SAFETY
Wash the board after raw fish. If you use leftover fish, reheat it to 165 degrees F or serve it cold and fully cooked, kept refrigerated until assembly. Cook fish to 145 degrees F, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes, meaning it comes apart easily with a fork.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A squeeze of lime on the fish just before serving helps. See Coleslaw in the Misc chapter if you want a homemade slaw.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
