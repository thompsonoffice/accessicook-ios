Chickpea Curry
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A quick curry of chickpeas in a tomato and coconut or spiced tomato sauce. It is a meatless supper with rice. Canned chickpeas keep the time short.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tablespoon curry powder, or 2 teaspoons plus 1 teaspoon cumin
- 1 can (14 1/2 ounces) diced tomatoes
- 1 can (13 1/2 ounces) coconut milk, or 1 cup broth plus 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 2 cans (15 ounces each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- Optional: 2 cups spinach, to wilt at the end
- Rice, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Curry powder is a dry, fragrant blend. Blooming it briefly in oil, meaning cooking it 30 seconds until it smells toasty, makes it taste rounder. The sauce should feel thick enough to coat the chickpeas.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mince the garlic. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

3. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

4. Add the onion and cook until it feels soft, about 5 minutes.

5. Add the garlic and curry powder, and cook 30 seconds, until the spices smell toasty.

6. Add the tomatoes, coconut milk or broth, chickpeas, and salt.

7. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

8. Simmer 10 to 15 minutes, until the sauce feels slightly thick.

9. Stir in spinach, if using, until it wilts.

10. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The chickpeas should feel tender and hot. The sauce should smell toasty and spiced, not raw and dusty from uncooked spices, and not burnt. Spinach, if used, should feel wilted.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look thick and golden or red-gold, depending on the tomatoes and curry powder, and the chickpeas should look hot and coated, not dry and not lost in a watery broth.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look thick and golden or red-gold, depending on the tomatoes and curry powder, and the chickpeas should look hot and coated, not dry and not lost in a watery broth.

SAFETY
The pot is hot. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Taste before adding a lot of salt, because canned chickpeas and broth vary. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A squeeze of lemon at the end brightens the pot. Leftovers are even better the next day. Serve with rice or warm flatbread.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
