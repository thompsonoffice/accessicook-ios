Baked Chicken Drumsticks
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chicken drumsticks roasted on a pan until the thickest meat, not touching bone, is 165 degrees F. They are easy to season and easy to eat with your hands after a short rest. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 50 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 8 chicken drumsticks, about 2 1/2 pounds (two and a half pounds)
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) garlic powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper

TACTILE TIPS
Pat the skin dry so it can crisp. Measure temperature in the thickest meat, coming in from the end, without touching bone.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the middle while the oven is cold.

3. Pat the food dry.

4. Season it as the ingredients describe.

5. Pat the drumsticks dry.

6. Toss with oil and seasonings.

7. Set them on a lined pan with space between them.

8. Set the food in the pan in a single layer, or as described.

9. Roast 35 to 45 minutes. Roast means cook uncovered in the hot oven.

10. Turn the pieces once halfway through.

11. The thickest meat, not touching bone, must read 165 degrees F.

12. Rest 5 minutes, then serve.

13. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the thickest meat, off the bone, must read 165 degrees F. The skin should feel crisper than when it was raw. You should smell roasted chicken, not burning spice or raw poultry.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The skin should look golden and crisp, and the meat near the bone should look opaque, not red and raw. Some pink right at the bone can remain even when the meat is 165 degrees F, so trust the thermometer.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The skin should look golden and crisp, and the meat near the bone should look opaque, not red and raw. SAFETY
Wash hands and the board. The pan will have hot drippings. Use mitts. Do not rinse raw chicken in the sink. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Line the pan with foil for easier cleanup. For saucy drumsticks, brush barbecue sauce on in the last 10 minutes.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
