ACCESSICOOK
Meatloaf
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A baked loaf of seasoned ground beef, often with a ketchup topping. Because this is a dense ground-meat loaf, cook it until the center is 160 degrees F. Many people also treat it like a casserole and are comfortable at 160 to 165 degrees F. This book uses 160 degrees F as the USDA minimum for ground beef, checked in the center.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 pounds ground beef
- 1 cup dry breadcrumbs or crushed crackers
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) milk
- 2 large eggs
- 1 small onion, finely diced
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 1 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce, optional
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) ketchup, plus more for the top

TACTILE TIPS
Mix with your hands or a fork until just combined. A tight, overworked loaf gets dense. The center of the cooked loaf should feel set and a thermometer should read 160 degrees F.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease the baking dish if the ingredients include butter or oil for the dish.

3. Mix the milk and breadcrumbs, then add the beef, eggs, onion, salt, pepper, Worcestershire, and 1/3 cup ketchup.

4. Mix gently.

5. Shape into a loaf on a rimmed pan.

6. Spread more ketchup on top if you like.

7. Bake 55 to 70 minutes.

8. The center must read 160 degrees F.

9. If the top is getting too dark, tent the loaf loosely with foil.

10. Rest 5 to 10 minutes so the dish can settle, then serve. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the center must read 160 degrees F. The loaf should feel firm, not squishy. You should smell roasted meat and onion, not raw beef.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The loaf should look browned on the outside, and the ketchup top should look slightly caramelized. The center, if you check a slice, should look cooked, not raw and red, though a little pink can remain in cured-mix loaves, so trust the thermometer.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The loaf should look browned on the outside, and the ketchup top should look slightly caramelized. The center, if you check a slice, should look cooked, not raw and red, though a little pink can remain in cured-mix loaves, so trust the thermometer.

SAFETY
Wash hands after mixing raw meat and eggs. Use a rimmed pan, because fat will render out. Render means the fat will melt out as it cooks. Use mitts. Rest before slicing so it does not crumble. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A lick of ketchup or brown sugar on top is classic. Leftover slices make good sandwiches the next day, reheated to 165 degrees F or eaten cold if they have been kept refrigerated and you are comfortable with that for fully cooked leftovers.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
