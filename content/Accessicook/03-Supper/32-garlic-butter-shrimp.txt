Garlic Butter Shrimp
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Shrimp cooked quickly in butter and garlic. This is a fast supper over rice, pasta, or grits. The shrimp are done when they are opaque, firm, and loosely curled.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 3 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- A pinch of red pepper flakes, optional
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice

TACTILE TIPS
Pat the shrimp dry so they sear. Mince the garlic into very small pieces so it flavors the butter without lingering as harsh, half-raw chunks. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pat the shrimp dry.

2. Season them with salt.

3. Melt the butter in a skillet over medium heat.

4. Add the garlic and optional red pepper flakes.

5. Cook 30 seconds, until fragrant, not brown and bitter.

6. Add the shrimp in a single layer.

7. Cook 1 to 2 minutes per side, until they are opaque, firm, and loosely curled.

8. Add the lemon juice.

9. Taste a cooled shrimp and add salt if it needs it.

10. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The shrimp should feel firm and springy, and they should smell like garlic, butter, and sweet shrimp, not ammonia or burning garlic.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The shrimp should look opaque and pearly or pink, not gray and raw, and they should curl into a loose C, not a tight O. The garlic should look pale gold, not dark brown.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The shrimp should look opaque and pearly or pink, not gray and raw, and they should curl into a loose C, not a tight O. The garlic should look pale gold, not dark brown.

SAFETY
Keep raw shrimp cold, then wash the board. Garlic burns fast. Do not walk away. Overcooked shrimp get tough. Cook shrimp until the flesh is pearly, opaque, and firm. It should curl into a loose C shape, not a tight O, which can mean it is overcooked.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Serve over rice, pasta, or the Shrimp and Grits idea by spooning these shrimp over the Grits from the Breakfast chapter. A little chopped parsley is a good finish.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
