Pork Stir-Fry
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Thin strips of pork and vegetables cooked quickly in a hot pan. Pork strips should reach 145 degrees F and lose their raw, slimy feel. Have everything cut before you start, because the cooking is fast.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound pork tenderloin or loin, thinly sliced
- 2 tablespoons oil, divided
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 4 cups cut vegetables, such as snap peas, bell pepper, or broccoli
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) stir-fry sauce, or 3 tablespoons soy sauce mixed with 1 tablespoon honey and 1 minced garlic clove
- Rice, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Slice the pork thin so it cooks in minutes. The vegetables should stay a little crisp.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cut the protein and vegetables into pieces about the same size so they cook evenly.

2. Mix the sauce ingredients in a small bowl.

3. Pat the protein dry.

4. Season it with the salt, if listed.

5. Heat the oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat until it smells hot and a piece of vegetable sizzles at once.

6. Cook the pork in 1 tablespoon oil until it is at least 145 degrees F and no longer raw-looking or slimy, then move it to a plate.

7. Move the protein to a plate if the pan is crowded.

8. Add a little more oil if the pan is dry.

9. Add the firmer vegetables first.

10. Stir and cook until the vegetables are tender-crisp, meaning they are hot and slightly bent but not mushy.

11. Return the protein to the pan.

12. Add the sauce.

13. Stir until the sauce thickens slightly and coats everything, 1 to 2 minutes.

14. Serve hot over rice if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thicker strip should be at least 145 degrees F. The pork should feel cooked, not slimy. Vegetables should feel tender-crisp. You should hear a sizzle and smell soy and browning meat.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The pork should look opaque and browned in spots, not raw and red or gray and wet. The vegetables should look bright.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The pork should look opaque and browned in spots, not raw and red or gray and wet. The vegetables should look bright.

SAFETY
Use a sharp knife and a cut-resistant glove. The pan is very hot. Wash the board after raw pork. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is a good way to use leftover pork tenderloin, reheated in the sauce to 165 degrees F. Serve over rice.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
