Vegetable Enchiladas
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Tortillas rolled around cooked vegetables and beans or cheese, covered with enchilada sauce, and baked until bubbling hot. This is a meatless version of the beef enchiladas in this chapter.

SERVINGS
4 to 6

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 4 cups mixed vegetables, such as peppers, onion, zucchini, or corn
- 1 can (15 ounces) beans, drained and rinsed, optional
- 8 tortillas
- 2 cups enchilada sauce
- 2 cups shredded cheese
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt

TACTILE TIPS
Cook the vegetables until they are tender and not watery, or the tortillas will get soggy. Warm the tortillas so they roll without cracking.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease the baking dish if the ingredients include butter or oil for the dish.

3. Cook the vegetables in oil with salt until they are tender.

4. Stir in the beans if using, and 1/2 cup cheese.

5. Spread a little sauce in a baking dish.

6. Fill the warmed tortillas, roll, and set them seam-side down.

7. Pour the remaining sauce over the top.

8. Add the rest of the cheese. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

9. Bake 20 to 25 minutes.

10. The sauce should be bubbling, the cheese should feel melted, and the center should be steaming hot.

11. Rest 5 to 10 minutes so the dish can settle, then serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The filling should be steaming hot, 165 degrees F if assembled ahead. You should smell roasted vegetables, chili sauce, and melted cheese. The tortillas should feel soft and soaked with sauce at the edges, not dry and hard.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cheese should look melted and lightly browned in spots. The sauce should be bubbling at the edges.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cheese should look melted and lightly browned in spots. The sauce should be bubbling at the edges.

SAFETY
The dish is very hot. Use mitts. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little sauce in the bottom of the dish keeps the bottoms from sticking. Spinach should be wilted and squeezed dry if you use it, or the filling will be wet.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
