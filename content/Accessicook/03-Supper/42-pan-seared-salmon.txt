Pan-Seared Salmon
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Salmon cooked in a hot skillet, often starting skin-side down if the skin is on, until it is 145 degrees F or flakes easily. The skin can get crisp if it is dry and the pan is hot.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 salmon fillets, about 6 ounces each
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- 1 tablespoon oil
- Lemon wedges, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Pat the skin very dry if the fillets have skin. The done fish should flake and feel opaque in the center, or read 145 degrees F.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pat the meat or fish dry.

2. Season both sides with the salt, pepper, and any rub listed.

3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat until it smells hot and the food sizzles firmly when it touches the pan.

4. Lay the pieces in the pan in a single layer. Do not crowd them.

5. If there is skin, start skin-side down and cook 4 to 6 minutes, until the skin feels crisp and the fish is opaque most of the way up the sides.

6. Turn the pieces.

7. Turn and cook 2 to 4 minutes more.

8. The thickest part should read 145 degrees F, or the fish should flake easily.

9. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer should read 145 degrees F, or the fish should flake and feel opaque and hot. You should hear a steady sizzle. You should smell toasted skin and cooked salmon, not burning oil.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The skin, if present, should look crisp and browned. The flesh should look opaque, not glassy in the center, and it should flake with a fork.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The skin, if present, should look crisp and browned. The flesh should look opaque, not glassy in the center, and it should flake with a fork.

SAFETY
Use a splatter screen. Wash the board after raw fish. The pan is very hot. Do not force the fish if it is stuck. Give it another 30 seconds and it will often release. Cook fish to 145 degrees F, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes, meaning it comes apart easily with a fork.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the fillets are very thick, finish them in a 375 degree F oven after searing, still to 145 degrees F. A pat of butter and a squeeze of lemon at the end is plenty of sauce.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
