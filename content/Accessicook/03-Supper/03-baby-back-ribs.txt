Baby Back Ribs
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
This recipe was on the original request list for the book.

Baby back ribs become tender when they cook low and slow, wrapped so they steam in their own juices, then finish unwrapped with sauce so the outside turns sticky and glazed. This is an oven method, which is easier to control than a grill. The USDA minimum for pork is 145 degrees F with a 3-minute rest, but ribs are not really done until the meat is tender and starts to give on the bone. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 3 to 3 1/2 hours, mostly unattended oven time

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- Heavy-duty foil
- A large roasting pan or rimmed baking sheet with a rack if you have one
- Tongs
- A small bowl for the rub
- A brush or a spoon for the sauce

INGREDIENTS
- 2 racks baby back pork ribs, about 1 1/2 to 2 pounds (one and a half to two pounds) each, or about 3 to 4 pounds total
- 2 tablespoons brown sugar
- 1 tablespoon paprika
- 2 teaspoons kosher salt, or 1 1/4 teaspoons (one and a quarter teaspoons) table salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 teaspoon onion powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) chili powder or cayenne, optional
- 1 cup barbecue sauce, plus more for serving

TACTILE TIPS
A rack of baby back ribs is curved and flexible, with a row of bones you can feel under a layer of meat. One side often has a thin, slippery membrane. If it is still on, you can loosen a corner with a knife tip and pull it off with a paper towel for better texture. The rub should feel like damp sand once it is mixed, and it should coat the meat in a thin, even crust.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 275 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the lower-middle position while the oven is still cold.

3. Line a roasting pan or a rimmed baking sheet with foil.

4. If needed, remove the membrane from the bone side of each rack: slide a knife tip under a corner, grip the skin with a paper towel, and pull.

5. Pat the ribs dry.

6. Mix the brown sugar, paprika, salt, pepper, garlic powder, onion powder, and optional chili powder.

7. Rub the mixture over both sides of the ribs until the meat feels evenly coated.

8. Set the racks meat-side up on the pan.

9. Wrap the pan tightly in foil so steam stays in.

10. Bake for 2 hours 15 minutes to 2 hours 30 minutes.

11. Carefully open the foil away from your face, watching for steam. The meat should have pulled back a little from the bone ends and should give when you press it. A thermometer in the thickest meat, not touching bone, will usually read well above 145 degrees F at this point.

12. If the meat still feels tight and rubbery, rewrap and bake 20 to 30 minutes more.

13. Brush the top with barbecue sauce.

14. Return the ribs unwrapped and bake 15 to 25 minutes more, until the sauce feels tacky and set, not wet and runny.

15. Rest 10 minutes, then cut between the bones with a knife or kitchen shears and serve with more sauce.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The meat should feel tender when pressed and should tear with only a little effort. A bone should twist slightly in its socket. The kitchen will smell rich, porky, and sweet-smoky from the sauce, not raw or scorched. A thermometer in the thickest meat, off the bone, should be at least 145 degrees F; tenderness is the real finishing cue.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The finished ribs should look glazed and a little browned at the edges, with meat that has shrunk back from the bone ends. The sauce should look sticky, not watery. If you twist a bone, it should start to move in the meat without the rack falling completely apart unless you want them extra soft.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. SAFETY
Raw pork juices belong on the raw-meat board only. Wash your hands, the knife, and the board before you handle the sauce brush or serving platter. Use long mitts; the foil-wrapped pan holds a lot of steam. Open foil away from your face. If you finish the ribs on a grill instead of in the oven, keep them over controlled heat so the sauce does not flare and burn. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For spicier ribs, add more cayenne to the rub or use a hot barbecue sauce. For a drier, less sticky finish, sauce only at the table. You can cook the wrapped stage a day ahead, refrigerate the racks, and finish with sauce at 350 degrees F until hot and glazed, checking that leftovers reach 165 degrees F if they were fully cooked and chilled. An air fryer is too small for a full rack; use the oven for this recipe.

LEFTOVERS
Cut leftover ribs off the bone before storing so they cool faster. Reheat, covered, in a 300 degree F oven or in a covered skillet with a splash of water until the meat is 165 degrees F. Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
