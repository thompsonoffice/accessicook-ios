ACCESSICOOK
Sausage and Peppers
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Italian sausages cooked with sliced bell peppers and onions until the sausages are done and the vegetables are tender. For pork or beef sausages that are sold raw, cook to 160 degrees F. For poultry sausages, cook to 165 degrees F. Some sausages are sold fully cooked and only need to be heated through, still to 165 degrees F if they are leftovers or poultry.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound Italian sausage links, sweet or hot
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 2 bell peppers, sliced
- 1 onion, sliced
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) Italian seasoning or black pepper
- Optional: 1/2 cup (half a cup) tomato sauce, or toasted rolls, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Raw sausages feel soft and even. As they cook they feel firmer. Check temperature in the end of a link, in the center of the meat, not just on the surface.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium heat.

2. Add the sausages.

3. Brown them 5 to 6 minutes, turning, then move them to a plate if the pan is crowded.

4. Add the peppers, onion, and salt.

5. Cook 8 to 10 minutes, until they feel soft and smell sweet.

6. Return the sausages.

7. Add seasoning, and tomato sauce if you like.

8. Cover and cook 8 to 12 minutes more, until pork or beef sausages are 160 degrees F, or poultry sausages are 165 degrees F, and the vegetables are tender.

9. Serve on plates or in rolls.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Pork or beef sausage must be 160 degrees F. Poultry sausage must be 165 degrees F. Peppers and onions should feel soft and sweet-smelling. You should smell browned sausage and peppers, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sausages should look browned, and the peppers should look soft and slightly browned, not raw and stiff. A cut sausage should not look raw and pasty in the center, but a thermometer is the test.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sausages should look browned, and the peppers should look soft and slightly browned, not raw and stiff. A cut sausage should not look raw and pasty in the center, but a thermometer is the test.

SAFETY
Check the package to see if the sausage is raw or fully cooked. Raw sausage needs the full temperature check. The skillet will spatter. Use a splatter screen. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is good over pasta or in a hoagie roll. If the sausages brown too fast, lower the heat and finish with the lid on.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
