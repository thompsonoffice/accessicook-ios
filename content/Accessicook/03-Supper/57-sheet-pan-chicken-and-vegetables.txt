ACCESSICOOK
Sheet-Pan Chicken and Vegetables
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chicken pieces and cut vegetables roasted together on one pan. Cut the vegetables so they cook in the same time as the chicken, and confirm the chicken is 165 degrees F before you serve.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds) boneless chicken thighs or breasts, cut into large chunks, or 4 small thighs
- 4 cups cut vegetables, such as potatoes, carrots, broccoli, or bell peppers
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon dried herbs or paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper

TACTILE TIPS
Cut dense vegetables such as potatoes smaller than the chicken pieces, so they get tender in time. Leave a little space on the pan so everything roasts instead of steaming.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the middle while the oven is cold.

3. Pat the food dry.

4. Season it as the ingredients describe.

5. Toss the chicken and vegetables separately with oil, salt, pepper, and herbs, then spread them on a lined pan.

6. If you can, keep the chicken in one area so you can check temperature easily.

7. Set the food in the pan in a single layer, or as described.

8. Roast 25 to 35 minutes. Roast means cook uncovered in the hot oven.

9. Turn the pieces once halfway through.

10. The thickest chicken piece must read 165 degrees F.

11. A vegetable cube should mash or pierce easily.

12. Rest the chicken 5 minutes, then serve with the vegetables. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

13. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Chicken must be 165 degrees F in the thickest piece. Vegetables should feel tender when pierced. You should smell roasted vegetables and chicken, not burning oil.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with browned edges. The vegetables should look browned in spots and tender, not raw in the center of a potato cube, and not black.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with browned edges. The vegetables should look browned in spots and tender, not raw in the center of a potato cube, and not black.

SAFETY
Use a raw-poultry board and keep raw chicken off the vegetable cutting area, or cut vegetables first and wash the board. Use mitts. The pan is very hot. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If some vegetables brown too fast, take them off early and let the chicken finish. Broccoli cooks faster than potatoes, so add it in the last 15 minutes if you mix them.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
