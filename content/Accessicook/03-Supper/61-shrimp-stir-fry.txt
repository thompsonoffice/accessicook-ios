ACCESSICOOK
Shrimp Stir-Fry
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Shrimp and vegetables cooked quickly in a hot pan with a simple sauce. Shrimp cook very fast. They are done when they are opaque, pearly, and firm, and they curl into a loose C shape, not a tight O, which can mean they are overcooked.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 2 tablespoons oil, divided
- 4 cups cut vegetables, such as bell pepper, snap peas, or broccoli
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) stir-fry sauce, or 3 tablespoons soy sauce mixed with 1 tablespoon honey and 1 minced garlic clove
- Rice, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Raw shrimp feel slightly translucent and soft. Deveined means the dark vein along the back has been removed. Done shrimp feel firm and springy, not mushy, and not tough as rubber.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cut the protein and vegetables into pieces about the same size so they cook evenly.

2. Mix the sauce ingredients in a small bowl.

3. Pat the protein dry.

4. Season it with the salt, if listed.

5. Heat the oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat until it smells hot and a piece of vegetable sizzles at once.

6. Cook the shrimp in 1 tablespoon oil 1 to 2 minutes per side, until they are opaque, firm, and loosely curled, then move them to a plate so they do not overcook while the vegetables finish.

7. Move the protein to a plate if the pan is crowded.

8. Add a little more oil if the pan is dry.

9. Add the firmer vegetables first.

10. Stir and cook until the vegetables are tender-crisp, meaning they are hot and slightly bent but not mushy.

11. Return the protein to the pan.

12. Add the sauce.

13. Stir until the sauce thickens slightly and coats everything, 1 to 2 minutes.

14. Serve hot over rice if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The shrimp should feel firm and opaque, and they should smell sweet and briny, not ammonia-like or sour. If they smell off before cooking, throw them out. Vegetables should feel tender-crisp.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The shrimp should look opaque and pink or pearly, not gray and translucent. They should curl into a loose C, not a tight O. The vegetables should look bright.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The shrimp should look opaque and pink or pearly, not gray and translucent. They should curl into a loose C, not a tight O. The vegetables should look bright.

SAFETY
Wash the board after raw shrimp, and keep them cold until they go in the pan. Do not overcook. The pan is very hot. Cook shrimp until the flesh is pearly, opaque, and firm. It should curl into a loose C shape, not a tight O, which can mean it is overcooked.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the shrimp are frozen, thaw them in the refrigerator, then pat them dry so they sear instead of steam. Add shrimp near the end if your vegetables need more time, because shrimp can be done in 3 to 5 minutes.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
