Lamb Stew
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Cubes of lamb simmered with vegetables until the meat is tender. The USDA minimum for lamb is 145 degrees F with a rest, but stew meat is not done until it is fork-tender, which takes much longer. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 2 hours 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 pounds lamb stew meat, or a lamb shoulder cut into 1 1/2 inch (one and a half inch) cubes
- 1 1/2 teaspoons (one and a half teaspoons) salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 3 carrots, cut into chunks
- 2 tablespoons flour
- 3 cups broth
- 1 teaspoon dried rosemary or thyme
- 2 potatoes, cut into chunks, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Lamb stew meat feels firmer than ground meat and may have some fat. When it is done, a fork should slide in with little resistance.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Season the lamb with salt and pepper.

2. Heat the oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat.

3. Brown the lamb in batches, 3 to 4 minutes per side, then move it to a plate.

4. Add the onion and cook until it feels soft.

5. Stir in the flour for 1 minute.

6. Add the broth and herbs, scraping up the browned bits.

7. Return the lamb to the pot.

8. Bring to a simmer, then cover and cook 1 hour. Simmer means small, gentle bubbles.

9. Add the carrots and optional potatoes.

10. Cover and cook 30 to 45 minutes more, until the meat and vegetables are tender.

11. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

12. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide into the lamb easily. The stew should smell herby and rich, not raw or scorched. A thermometer will be well above 145 degrees F. Tenderness is the finishing cue.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The lamb should look browned on the outside and dull and cooked inside, and it should come apart easily. The gravy should look brown and slightly thick.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The lamb should look browned on the outside and dull and cooked inside, and it should come apart easily. The gravy should look brown and slightly thick.

SAFETY
Wash the board after raw lamb. The pot is heavy and hot. Cool leftovers in shallow containers and reheat to 165 degrees F. For lamb chops and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. The center may still look slightly pink at this temperature.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is even better the next day. Serve with bread or mashed potatoes from the Misc chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
