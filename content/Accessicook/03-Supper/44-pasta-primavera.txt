Pasta Primavera
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Pasta tossed with plenty of cooked vegetables and a light sauce of oil, butter, or a little cream. Primavera means spring, but you can use year-round vegetables. Cook the pasta as in the Pasta Primer.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A colander

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound pasta
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the pasta water
- 2 tablespoons olive oil or butter
- 4 cups mixed vegetables, such as broccoli, peas, zucchini, and bell pepper
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) reserved pasta water, plus more as needed
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) grated Parmesan cheese
- Salt and pepper

TACTILE TIPS
Cut the vegetables small so they cook in the time the pasta cooks. The finished dish should feel saucy from the pasta water and cheese, not dry, and not like vegetables sitting in a puddle of oil.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Start the pasta in salted boiling water.

2. While it cooks, heat the oil or butter in a large skillet.

3. Cook the firmer vegetables first, then the quicker ones, until they are tender-crisp.

4. Add the garlic for the last 30 seconds.

5. Save 1 cup of pasta water.

6. Drain the pasta when it is tender but still slightly firm in the center. Drain means pour off the liquid.

7. Add the pasta to the skillet with 1/2 cup of the pasta water and the Parmesan.

8. Toss until the sauce feels lightly creamy and coats the pasta.

9. Add salt and pepper to taste.

10. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Pasta should feel tender with a slight firmness. Vegetables should feel tender-crisp. You should smell garlic, butter or oil, and cheese, not burning garlic.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The vegetables should look bright and tender, not gray and mushy. The pasta should look lightly coated, not dry and not oily.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The vegetables should look bright and tender, not gray and mushy. The pasta should look lightly coated, not dry and not oily.

SAFETY
Boiling water is a burn hazard. The skillet is hot. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F with a splash of water. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Frozen peas can go in during the last 2 minutes. A little cream, added with the pasta water, makes a richer sauce.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
