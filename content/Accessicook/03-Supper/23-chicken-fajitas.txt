Chicken Fajitas
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Strips of chicken and bell peppers cooked in a hot skillet and served in warm tortillas with toppings. The chicken must reach 165 degrees F. A fajita is a sizzling strip of meat and peppers, wrapped at the table.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds) boneless chicken, cut into strips
- 2 tablespoons oil, divided
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon chili powder
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 2 bell peppers, sliced
- 1 onion, sliced
- 8 flour tortillas
- Optional toppings: salsa, sour cream, or cheese

TACTILE TIPS
Cut the chicken and vegetables into similar-size strips so they cook evenly. The peppers should finish tender-crisp, meaning hot and slightly bent but not mushy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cut the protein and vegetables into pieces about the same size so they cook evenly.

2. Mix the sauce ingredients in a small bowl.

3. Pat the protein dry.

4. Season it with the salt, if listed.

5. Heat the oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat until it smells hot and a piece of vegetable sizzles at once.

6. Toss the chicken with salt, chili powder, and cumin.

7. Cook it in 1 tablespoon oil until it is 165 degrees F, then move it to a plate.

8. Move the protein to a plate if the pan is crowded.

9. Add a little more oil if the pan is dry.

10. Add the firmer vegetables first.

11. Stir and cook until the vegetables are tender-crisp, meaning they are hot and slightly bent but not mushy.

12. Return the protein to the pan.

13. Add the sauce.

14. Stir until the sauce thickens slightly and coats everything, 1 to 2 minutes.

15. Serve hot over rice if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Chicken must be 165 degrees F. You should hear a sizzle. Peppers should feel tender-crisp. You should smell chili, onion, and browned chicken.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with some browned edges. The peppers should look bright and slightly browned, not gray and limp, and not raw and stiff.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with some browned edges. The peppers should look bright and slightly browned, not gray and limp, and not raw and stiff.

SAFETY
Wash hands and the board. Do not crowd the pan or everything will steam. Warm leftover fillings to 165 degrees F. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Warm the tortillas in a dry skillet until they feel flexible. Serve the filling in a hot skillet or a bowl, and let people build their own wraps.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
