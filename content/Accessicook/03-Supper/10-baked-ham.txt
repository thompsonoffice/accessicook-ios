ACCESSICOOK
Baked Ham
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A fully cooked ham reheated in the oven, often with a simple glaze. Most supermarket hams are already cooked and only need to be heated through. USDA-inspected cooked ham can be reheated to 140 degrees F. Other cooked hams, and leftovers later, should reach 165 degrees F. Read the label, or have a helper read it, so you know what you have.

SERVINGS
8 or more, depending on size

TOTAL TIME
About 2 hours for a 7 to 8 pound cooked ham, plus a 15-minute glaze

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 fully cooked ham, 6 to 8 pounds, spiral-sliced or not
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) brown sugar or jam, optional, for a glaze
- 2 tablespoons mustard or apple juice, optional, for the glaze
- Water or juice, about 1/2 cup, for the pan

TACTILE TIPS
A spiral-sliced ham is already cut in slices that stay attached at the bottom. Cut in means rub or cut cold butter into flour until the mix feels like crumbs. The center should feel hot, and a thermometer should confirm the safe reheating temperature from the label type.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 325 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the lower third while the oven is cold.

3. Put the ham, cut-side down if it has a flat face, in a roasting pan.

4. Add 1/2 cup water or juice.

5. Cover loosely with foil.

6. Bake about 12 to 15 minutes per pound, until a thermometer in the thickest part, not touching bone, reads 140 degrees F for USDA-inspected cooked ham, or 165 degrees F for other cooked ham.

7. If you are glazing, mix the sugar or jam with mustard or juice.

8. Uncover, brush on the glaze, and bake 15 minutes more, until the glaze feels tacky.

9. Rest 10 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

10. Slice, or for a spiral ham, just release the slices.

11. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer must read 140 degrees F or 165 degrees F, depending on the type of cooked ham, as described above. The ham should feel hot in the center. The glaze, if used, should smell sweet, not burnt.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The outside should look glossy if glazed, and the interior should look moist and hot, not dry. Cured ham may stay pink even when it is fully hot, so use a thermometer, not color.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The outside should look glossy if glazed, and the interior should look moist and hot, not dry. Cured ham may stay pink even when it is fully hot, so use a thermometer, not color.

SAFETY
Use mitts. The pan will have hot juices. Read the package if you can, because fresh uncooked ham is different and must reach 145 degrees F with a 3-minute rest. Leftovers should be reheated to 165 degrees F. Fresh, uncooked ham should reach 145 degrees F, then rest 3 minutes. Fully cooked ham is only being reheated. USDA-inspected cooked ham can be reheated to 140 degrees F. Other cooked ham should reach 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A spiral ham is easier to serve. Save the bone for split pea soup. Leftover slices work in breakfast sandwiches and chef salad.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
