Baked White Fish
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Mild white fish such as cod, tilapia, or haddock, baked with oil, salt, and lemon until it reaches 145 degrees F or flakes easily. This is a gentle, fast supper.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 white fish fillets, about 6 ounces each
- 1 tablespoon olive oil or melted butter
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) paprika or dried herbs, optional
- Lemon wedges, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
White fish feels more delicate than salmon and can break if you flip it too often, which is why baking is helpful. Done fish flakes into moist segments and is opaque, not wet and translucent.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the middle while the oven is cold.

3. Pat the food dry.

4. Season it as the ingredients describe.

5. Pat the fillets dry.

6. Set them on a lined pan.

7. Brush with oil or butter and season.

8. Set the food in the pan in a single layer, or as described.

9. Roast 12 to 20 minutes, depending on thickness. Roast means cook uncovered in the hot oven.

10. The thickest part should read 145 degrees F, or the fish should flake easily and look opaque.

11. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer should read 145 degrees F, or the fish should flake easily and feel hot and opaque. You should smell cooked fish and lemon, not a sour or ammonia-like smell. If it smells off before cooking, throw it out.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The fish should look opaque and white, not shiny and raw in the center, and it should flake when pressed with a fork. The top may be faintly golden if you used paprika.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The fish should look opaque and white, not shiny and raw in the center, and it should flake when pressed with a fork. The top may be faintly golden if you used paprika.

SAFETY
Wash the board after raw fish. Use mitts. Thin tilapia can cook in under 12 minutes, so check early. Cook fish to 145 degrees F, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes, meaning it comes apart easily with a fork.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A sprinkle of breadcrumbs mixed with a little oil can add a light crust. Serve with rice or roasted vegetables from the Misc chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
