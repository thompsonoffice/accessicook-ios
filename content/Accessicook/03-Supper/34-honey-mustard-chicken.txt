Honey Mustard Chicken
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chicken baked with a simple sauce of honey and mustard. The sauce is sweet and tangy. Add it in time to glaze the chicken, and still cook the meat to 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 boneless chicken breasts or thighs, about 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds)
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) Dijon or yellow mustard
- 1/4 cup honey
- 1 tablespoon oil

TACTILE TIPS
The sauce should feel smooth and thick, like a loose syrup. Honey is sticky. A liquid measuring cup sprayed with a little oil can help it pour.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Mix the mustard, honey, and oil.

3. Season the chicken with salt and pepper.

4. Set it in a small baking dish.

5. Spoon most of the sauce over the chicken, saving a little for later if you want a clean finish.

6. Bake 20 to 30 minutes, until the thickest part is 165 degrees F.

7. Spoon the pan juices over the top.

8. Rest 5 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

9. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer must read 165 degrees F. The glaze should smell sweet and tangy, not burnt. The meat should feel firm and springy.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with a glossy golden glaze, not raw in the center and not burnt at the edges.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with a glossy golden glaze, not raw in the center and not burnt at the edges.

SAFETY
Wash hands and the board. Honey sauce can brown quickly in a small, hot dish. If the top is getting too dark before the chicken is 165 degrees F, cover the dish loosely with foil. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This sauce also works on salmon, cooked to 145 degrees F. Serve with rice or roasted vegetables from the Misc chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
