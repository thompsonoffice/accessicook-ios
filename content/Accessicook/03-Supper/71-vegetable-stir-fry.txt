ACCESSICOOK
Vegetable Stir-Fry
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A mixed vegetable supper cooked quickly in a hot pan with a simple sauce, served over rice. Cut denser vegetables smaller so they finish with the quicker ones. This is a good way to use leftover vegetables too.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 6 cups cut vegetables, such as broccoli, carrots, bell peppers, snap peas, and mushrooms
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) stir-fry sauce, or 3 tablespoons soy sauce mixed with 1 tablespoon brown sugar and 1 teaspoon cornstarch
- Rice, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Even, bite-size pieces cook more evenly. Tender-crisp means the vegetables are hot and slightly bent but not mushy. Carrots take longer than mushrooms, so they should go in first.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cut the protein and vegetables into pieces about the same size so they cook evenly.

2. Mix the sauce ingredients in a small bowl.

3. Pat the protein dry.

4. Season it with the salt, if listed.

5. Heat the oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat until it smells hot and a piece of vegetable sizzles at once.

6. Skip a separate protein step, or add cubed tofu or leftover cooked protein after the firmer vegetables have started to soften, and heat leftover meat to 165 degrees F.

7. Move the protein to a plate if the pan is crowded.

8. Add a little more oil if the pan is dry.

9. Add the firmer vegetables first.

10. Stir and cook until the vegetables are tender-crisp, meaning they are hot and slightly bent but not mushy.

11. Return the protein to the pan.

12. Add the sauce.

13. Stir until the sauce thickens slightly and coats everything, 1 to 2 minutes.

14. Serve hot over rice if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: You should hear a sizzle, not a steam hiss. Vegetables should feel tender-crisp. The sauce should smell savory and should coat, not puddle. Garlic should be fragrant, not burnt.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The vegetables should look bright and slightly browned in spots, not dull, gray, and watery.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The vegetables should look bright and slightly browned in spots, not dull, gray, and watery.

SAFETY
The pan is very hot. Have everything cut before you start. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Add tofu, leftover chicken reheated to 165 degrees F, or a fried egg cooked until firm if you want more protein. Serve at once over rice.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
