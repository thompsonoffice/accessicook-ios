Shrimp and Grits
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Creamy grits topped with shrimp cooked in a savory sauce, sometimes with bacon or sausage. Make the Grits from the Breakfast chapter, or follow the short method below, then cook the shrimp at the end so they do not sit and get tough.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup regular grits, cooked as in the Breakfast chapter, with butter and optional cheese
- 1 pound raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 1 tablespoon oil or bacon drippings
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) broth
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- Optional: 4 slices cooked bacon, crumbled, or a pinch of red pepper flakes

TACTILE TIPS
Time it so the grits are creamy and hot when the shrimp are just done. The shrimp should feel firm, opaque, and loosely curled. The grits should feel smooth, not lumpy and not stiff.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cook the grits as in the Breakfast chapter, and keep them warm over very low heat, adding a splash of water if they tighten.

2. Pat the shrimp dry.

3. Season them with salt.

4. Heat the oil or drippings in a skillet over medium heat.

5. Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds.

6. Add the shrimp.

7. Cook 1 to 2 minutes per side, until they are opaque, firm, and loosely curled.

8. Add the broth and optional red pepper flakes.

9. Simmer 1 minute. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

10. Add bacon if you are using it.

11. Spoon the grits into bowls.

12. Top with the shrimp and sauce.

13. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Grits should feel silky and hot. Shrimp should feel firm and smell sweet, buttery, and garlicky, not ammonia-like or burnt.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The grits should look creamy. The shrimp should look opaque and loosely curled, not gray, and not tight and small from overcooking.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The grits should look creamy. The shrimp should look opaque and loosely curled, not gray, and not tight and small from overcooking.

SAFETY
Cook bacon fully if you use it. Keep raw shrimp cold, then wash the board. Do not overcook the shrimp. The bowls will be hot. Cook shrimp until the flesh is pearly, opaque, and firm. It should curl into a loose C shape, not a tight O, which can mean it is overcooked.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cheddar in the grits makes this more filling. If the grits sit, they thicken a lot. Whisk in hot water or milk to loosen them. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk until the mixture looks even.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
