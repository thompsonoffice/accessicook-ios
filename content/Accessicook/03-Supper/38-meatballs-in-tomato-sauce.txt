Meatballs in Tomato Sauce
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Seasoned ground beef meatballs simmered in marinara until they are 160 degrees F in the center. Serve them over pasta or in a meatball sub.

SERVINGS
4 to 6

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound ground beef
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) breadcrumbs
- 1 large egg
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) garlic powder or Italian seasoning
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 3 cups marinara sauce

TACTILE TIPS
The mix should feel moist and hold a ball. Pack the balls gently. A 1 1/2 inch (one and a half inch) ball is about the size of a golf ball.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the beef, breadcrumbs, egg, salt, and seasoning.

2. Shape into balls about 1 1/2 inches across.

3. Heat the oil in a wide pot over medium heat.

4. Brown the meatballs on a few sides, 6 to 8 minutes, turning gently.

5. They do not have to be cooked through yet.

6. Pour in the marinara.

7. Bring to a simmer. Simmer means small, gentle bubbles.

8. Cover and simmer 10 to 15 minutes, until a meatball's center is 160 degrees F.

9. Serve over pasta, or use in a sandwich.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the center of a meatball must read 160 degrees F. The sauce should smell tomatoey and savory. The meatballs should feel firm, not pasty.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The meatballs should look browned, and the sauce should look thick and bubbling gently. A cut meatball should look cooked in the center, not raw and red, though a thermometer is the test.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The meatballs should look browned, and the sauce should look thick and bubbling gently. A cut meatball should look cooked in the center, not raw and red, though a thermometer is the test.

SAFETY
Wash hands after mixing raw meat and egg. The sauce can spatter. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
You can bake the raw meatballs at 400 degrees F for 15 minutes, then finish them in the sauce, still to 160 degrees F. For a meatball sub, see a similar idea in the Lunch chapter list, or pile them into a roll with cheese.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
