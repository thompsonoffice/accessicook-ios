ACCESSICOOK
Pork Tenderloin
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A lean, mild roast of pork tenderloin, seared then baked until the thickest part is 145 degrees F, then rested 3 minutes. Do not confuse this with a larger pork loin roast, which is a different, thicker cut and takes longer.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 pork tenderloins, about 1 pound each, silverskin trimmed if it is still on
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper or a dry rub you like

TACTILE TIPS
A tenderloin is a long, narrow, tapering muscle. Silverskin is a thin, tough, shiny membrane you can slip a knife under and peel off. Measure temperature in the thickest part.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the middle while the oven is cold.

3. Pat the food dry.

4. Season it as the ingredients describe.

5. Pat the tenderloins dry.

6. Trim silverskin if needed.

7. Season with oil, salt, and pepper.

8. For more browning, sear all sides in a hot skillet 2 minutes per side, then move the skillet to the oven if it is oven-safe, or move the meat to a pan.

9. Set the food in the pan in a single layer, or as described.

10. Roast 15 to 20 minutes if starting in the oven, or 12 to 18 minutes after a stove sear. Roast means cook uncovered in the hot oven.

11. The thickest part must read 145 degrees F.

12. Rest at least 3 minutes, then slice into medallions. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

13. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the thickest part must read 145 degrees F, then the meat should rest at least 3 minutes. It should feel springy-firm, not squishy, and not hard. You should smell roasted pork, not burning spice.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The outside should be browned if you seared it. The center at 145 degrees F may still look faintly pink, which is safe after a rest. It should not look raw and dark red, and it should not look dry and chalky from overcooking.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The outside should be browned if you seared it. The center at 145 degrees F may still look faintly pink, which is safe after a rest. It should not look raw and dark red, and it should not look dry and chalky from overcooking.

SAFETY
Use a cut-resistant glove if you trim silverskin. The skillet and oven pan are hot. Do not skip the rest. Wash the board. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Sear in a skillet first for more flavor, then finish in a 400 degree F oven 12 to 18 minutes. If the thin tail is much slimmer, tuck it under and tie it so it does not overcook.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
