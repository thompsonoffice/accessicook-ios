Beef Enchiladas
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Corn or flour tortillas rolled around seasoned beef, covered with enchilada sauce and cheese, and baked until hot and bubbling. Cook the beef to 160 degrees F before you roll.

SERVINGS
4 to 6

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound ground beef, cooked with taco seasoning to 160 degrees F
- 8 corn or flour tortillas
- 2 cups enchilada sauce, canned or from the Misc chapter if you have a sauce page, or a mild red sauce
- 2 cups shredded cheese
- Oil spray, for the dish

TACTILE TIPS
Warm the tortillas so they roll without cracking. The sauce should coat the rolled tortillas so the edges do not dry out in the oven.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease the baking dish if the ingredients include butter or oil for the dish.

3. Spread a little sauce in a baking dish.

4. Warm the tortillas.

5. Fill each with beef and a little cheese, then roll and set them seam-side down.

6. Pour the remaining sauce over the top.

7. Sprinkle with the rest of the cheese. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

8. Bake 20 to 25 minutes.

9. The sauce should be bubbling, the cheese should feel melted, and the center should be steaming hot.

10. Rest 5 to 10 minutes so the dish can settle, then serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The beef must be 160 degrees F before assembly. The baked center should be steaming hot, 165 degrees F if assembled ahead. You should smell toasted tortillas, chili sauce, and melted cheese.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cheese should look melted and lightly browned in spots. The sauce should be bubbling at the edges.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cheese should look melted and lightly browned in spots. The sauce should be bubbling at the edges.

SAFETY
Wash hands after raw beef. The dish is very hot. Use mitts. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little sauce in the bottom of the dish keeps the bottoms from sticking. Sour cream and chopped onion are good at the table.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
