ACCESSICOOK
Burgers
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Ground beef patties cooked in a skillet or on a grill pan, served on buns. Ground beef must reach 160 degrees F. A pink center is not a safe test for ground meat.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound ground beef, 80 or 85 percent lean is a good middle ground
- 3/4 teaspoon (three-quarters of a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 4 buns
- Toppings: cheese, lettuce, tomato, onion, ketchup, or mustard

TACTILE TIPS
Shape the patties lightly, a little thinner in the center. Do not pack them like meatballs. Raw ground beef feels cold and sticky.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pat the meat or fish dry.

2. Season both sides with the salt, pepper, and any rub listed.

3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat until it smells hot and the food sizzles firmly when it touches the pan.

4. Lay the pieces in the pan in a single layer. Do not crowd them.

5. Cook the first side 4 to 5 minutes, until browned and released.

6. Turn the pieces.

7. Turn and cook 3 to 5 minutes more.

8. The center must read 160 degrees F.

9. Add cheese now if you want it, then cover briefly.

10. Rest the meat on a plate for the time in the recipe. Rest means let it sit so the juices settle.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the center must read 160 degrees F. The meat should feel firm, not pasty. You should hear a sizzle and smell browning beef, not a steam hiss from a crowded pan.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The outside should be browned. The center should not look raw and red, but color can mislead, so use a thermometer. Cheese, if used, should look melted.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The outside should be browned. The center should not look raw and red, but color can mislead, so use a thermometer. Cheese, if used, should look melted.

SAFETY
Wash hands and the board. Do not put cooked burgers on the raw plate. Use a splatter screen. Ground beef is 160 degrees F, not 145. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Add cheese in the last minute and cover the pan. Do not press hard on the patties. For turkey burgers, see the turkey burger recipe in this chapter, which must reach 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
