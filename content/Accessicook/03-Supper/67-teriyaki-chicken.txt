Teriyaki Chicken
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chicken cooked in a sweet-salty soy glaze. You can use a bottled teriyaki sauce or a quick mix of soy sauce, sugar, and garlic. The chicken must reach 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds) boneless chicken, cut into strips or small pieces
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) teriyaki sauce, or 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) soy sauce mixed with 2 tablespoons brown sugar, 1 minced garlic clove, and 1 teaspoon oil
- 1 teaspoon cornstarch mixed with 2 teaspoons water, optional, to thicken
- Sesame seeds or sliced green onion, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Cut the chicken into even strips so they finish together. The glaze should feel shiny and slightly thick, not as thin as soy sauce, and not a burnt sticky tar.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat.

2. Add the chicken.

3. Cook, stirring, until it is mostly opaque, 5 to 7 minutes.

4. Add the teriyaki sauce.

5. Simmer 2 to 4 minutes, until the chicken is 165 degrees F in a thick piece. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

6. If you want a thicker glaze, stir in the cornstarch mix and cook 30 to 60 seconds, until the sauce feels slightly syrupy.

7. Serve over rice, with sesame or green onion if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Chicken must be 165 degrees F. The sauce should smell sweet and savory, like soy and sugar, not burnt. It should coat the chicken lightly.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with a shiny brown glaze, not pale and raw, and not black.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with a shiny brown glaze, not pale and raw, and not black.

SAFETY
Wash hands and the board. The sugary sauce can burn if the heat is too high. Lower the heat once the sauce goes in if it is spattering hard or smelling sharp. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Add steamed broccoli in the last few minutes if you want a one-pan meal. Bottled sauce is fine. Taste it first, because some are very salty.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
