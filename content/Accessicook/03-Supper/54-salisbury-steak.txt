Salisbury Steak
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Seasoned ground beef patties simmered in a brown gravy, often with onions and mushrooms. The patties must reach 160 degrees F. The gravy should feel smooth and savory.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound ground beef
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) breadcrumbs
- 1 large egg
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 onion, sliced
- 1 cup sliced mushrooms, optional
- 2 cups beef broth
- 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
- 2 tablespoons flour mixed with 2 tablespoons water

TACTILE TIPS
The patties should hold together but not feel like hard pucks. The gravy should coat a spoon.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the beef, breadcrumbs, egg, salt, and pepper.

2. Shape 4 oval patties.

3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat.

4. Brown the patties 3 to 4 minutes per side, then move them to a plate.

5. They do not have to be cooked through yet.

6. Add the onion and optional mushrooms.

7. Cook until they feel soft.

8. Add the broth and Worcestershire.

9. Bring to a simmer. Simmer means small, gentle bubbles.

10. Stir in the flour-and-water mix.

11. Return the patties to the pan.

12. Simmer 8 to 10 minutes, until the patties are 160 degrees F in the center and the gravy has thickened.

13. Serve hot, often over mashed potatoes from the Misc chapter.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Patties must be 160 degrees F. The gravy should feel silky and should smell beefy and savory, not raw flour. Onions should feel soft.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The patties should look browned, and the gravy should look brown and smooth, not pale and watery, and not lumpy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The patties should look browned, and the gravy should look brown and smooth, not pale and watery, and not lumpy.

SAFETY
Wash hands after mixing raw beef and egg. The skillet will spatter when you brown the patties. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the gravy is too thin, simmer a few minutes more. If it is too thick, add a splash of broth.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
