ACCESSICOOK
Pulled Pork
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A pork shoulder, also called a Boston butt, cooked low and slow until it shreds easily. The USDA minimum is 145 degrees F with a rest, but pulled pork is not done until it is tender enough to pull apart, which is much hotter and much later. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat. This is an oven method.

SERVINGS
8

TOTAL TIME
About 5 to 6 hours, mostly unattended oven time

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 to 5 pounds pork shoulder, bone-in or boneless
- 2 tablespoons brown sugar
- 1 tablespoon salt
- 1 tablespoon paprika
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 cup barbecue sauce, plus more for serving, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The shoulder is a large, well-marbled cut. Early on it will feel tough. When it is done, two forks should shred it with little effort, and a bone, if present, should twist and start to come free.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 300 degrees F.

2. Mix the sugar, salt, paprika, pepper, and garlic powder, then rub it all over the pork.

3. Set the pork in a roasting pan or Dutch oven.

4. Add 1/2 cup (half a cup) water to the pan.

5. Cover tightly with a lid or foil.

6. Bake 4 to 5 hours, until a fork slides in easily and the meat starts to shred.

7. Uncover for the last 20 to 30 minutes if you want more browned edges.

8. Rest 15 minutes.

9. Pull the meat into shreds with two forks, discarding large pieces of fat and the bone if there is one.

10. Toss with barbecue sauce if you like, or serve the sauce on the side.

11. Serve on buns or with sides.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Two forks should shred the meat easily. A bone should wiggle free if there is one. You should smell rich, roasted pork and spices, not burning. A thermometer will be well above 145 degrees F. Tenderness is the finishing cue.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The meat should look browned on the outside and dull, moist, and fibrous inside, not raw and red, and it should pull into shreds rather than slice like a firm roast.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The meat should look browned on the outside and dull, moist, and fibrous inside, not raw and red, and it should pull into shreds rather than slice like a firm roast.

SAFETY
Use mitts with a heavy, steam-filled pan. Open the foil or lid away from your face. Cool leftovers in shallow containers. Reheat to 165 degrees F. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This freezes well. For a faster weeknight version, use leftover cooked pork or a small piece and shorter time, but a full shoulder really does need the long cook.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
