ACCESSICOOK
Mushroom Risotto
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A creamy rice dish made by adding broth to short-grain rice a little at a time and stirring as the rice releases starch. You do not need cream for the creaminess, though a little butter or cheese at the end is traditional. Risotto takes attention, but the method is simple.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes, with frequent stirring

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons butter or oil, divided
- 8 ounces mushrooms, sliced
- 1 onion or shallot, finely diced
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) arborio or other short-grain rice
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) dry white wine, optional, or extra broth
- 5 cups warm broth
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) grated Parmesan cheese

TACTILE TIPS
Arborio rice feels short and oval. As it cooks it should feel tender but still slightly firm in the center, and the mix should feel creamy, like loose porridge, not a dry pilaf and not a soup.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Warm the broth in a pot and keep it hot.

2. Cook the mushrooms in 1 tablespoon butter over medium heat until they feel soft and have given off their liquid, then move them to a plate.

3. Add the remaining butter and the onion.

4. Cook until the onion feels soft.

5. Add the rice.

6. Stir 1 to 2 minutes, until the edges look and feel slightly translucent.

7. Add the wine, if using, and stir until it is mostly absorbed.

8. Add a ladle of hot broth.

9. Stir often until the rice has absorbed most of it.

10. Repeat, a ladle at a time, 18 to 25 minutes, until the rice is tender but still slightly firm in the center and the mix feels creamy.

11. Stir in the mushrooms, cheese, and salt to taste.

12. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The rice should feel tender with a slight firmness in the center. The mix should feel creamy. You should smell mushrooms, butter, and cheese. If the rice still feels hard in the center, add more broth and keep cooking.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The risotto should look creamy and loose, not stiff and mounded like sticky rice, and not watery. The mushrooms should look browned, not gray and raw.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The risotto should look creamy and loose, not stiff and mounded like sticky rice, and not watery. The mushrooms should look browned, not gray and raw.

SAFETY
The pots are hot. Add broth carefully so it does not splash. Serve soon, because risotto tightens as it sits. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F with extra broth. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Stir often, but you do not have to stir every second. If you run out of broth before the rice is tender, add hot water. A splash of lemon at the end brightens it.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
