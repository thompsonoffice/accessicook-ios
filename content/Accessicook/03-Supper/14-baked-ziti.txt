ACCESSICOOK
Baked Ziti
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Ziti or other short pasta mixed with marinara, optional cooked ground beef or sausage, and cheese, then baked until bubbling. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F, or poultry sausage to 165 degrees F, before you assemble. Cook the pasta just shy of tender, because it keeps cooking in the oven.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 50 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound ziti or penne
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the pasta water
- 1 pound ground beef or Italian sausage, optional
- 4 cups marinara sauce
- 2 cups shredded mozzarella cheese, divided
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) ricotta or cottage cheese, optional
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) grated Parmesan cheese

TACTILE TIPS
The pasta should be slightly firm when it goes in the dish, not fully soft. The assembled dish should look saucy, because the pasta will absorb more sauce as it bakes.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease the baking dish if the ingredients include butter or oil for the dish.

3. Cook the pasta in salted water until it is slightly firmer than you want the finished dish, then drain. Drain means pour off the liquid.

4. Cook any meat to a safe temperature and drain it.

5. Mix the pasta, sauce, meat if using, ricotta if using, and half the mozzarella.

6. Spread in a baking dish.

7. Top with the remaining mozzarella and the Parmesan.

8. Bake 20 to 25 minutes.

9. The dish should be bubbling, the cheese should feel melted, and the center should be steaming hot.

10. Rest 5 to 10 minutes so the dish can settle, then serve. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Meat, if used, must be safely cooked before assembly. The baked center should be steaming hot, 165 degrees F if it was assembled ahead. You should smell tomato, cheese, and toasted pasta edges, not burning cheese.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look bubbly, with melted, lightly browned cheese. The edges should look saucy, not dry and hard.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look bubbly, with melted, lightly browned cheese. The edges should look saucy, not dry and hard.

SAFETY
Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F. The dish is very hot. Use mitts. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A vegetarian version can skip the meat. Cover with foil for the first 15 minutes if you want a softer top, then uncover to brown.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
