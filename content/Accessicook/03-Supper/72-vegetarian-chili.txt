Vegetarian Chili
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A meatless chili of beans, tomatoes, and vegetables with chili powder. It is the bean-based cousin of the beef Chili in this chapter. Simmer until the vegetables are tender and the flavors have come together. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 1 onion, diced
- 1 bell pepper, diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 tablespoons chili powder
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 1 can (28 ounces) diced or crushed tomatoes
- 2 cans (15 ounces each) beans, drained and rinsed
- 1 cup corn, optional
- Optional toppings: cheese, sour cream, or chopped onion

TACTILE TIPS
The chili should feel thick and stew-like, similar to beef chili, with tender vegetables and beans you can feel in a spoonful.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Dice the onion and any other vegetables listed. Dice means cut into small, even cubes.

2. Mince the garlic. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

3. Heat the oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat.

4. Add the onion and pepper, and cook until they feel soft, about 5 to 7 minutes.

5. Add the garlic, chili powder, cumin, and salt, and cook 30 seconds, until the spices smell toasty.

6. Add the tomatoes, beans, and optional corn.

7. Bring the pot to a simmer. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

8. Simmer 20 minutes, stirring now and then, until slightly thick.

9. Taste and add salt if it needs it.

10. Serve with toppings.

11. Taste a cooled spoonful and add salt if it needs it.

12. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The vegetables should feel tender. The chili should smell warm, tomatoey, and spicy, not raw or tinny. A spoon should stand a little in the pot, but the mix should still be scoopable.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chili should look thick and red, not watery, with visible beans and vegetables.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chili should look thick and red, not watery, with visible beans and vegetables.

SAFETY
The pot is hot. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Taste before adding a lot of salt, because canned beans and tomatoes vary. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A square of unsweetened chocolate or a teaspoon of cocoa is a common trick for a deeper flavor. This freezes well. Serve with cornbread from the Baked Goods chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
