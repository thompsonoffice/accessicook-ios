INTRODUCTION
This recipe was on the original request list for the book.

This is the basic method for cooking dried pasta, then dressing it with butter and cheese. Once you can do this, you can use the same pot method for spaghetti with meat sauce, Alfredo, and the other pasta suppers in this book. The main skills are a safe pot of boiling water, a doneness bite test, and saving a little of the cooking water before you drain. Drain means pour off the liquid.

SERVINGS
2 to 4, depending on how much pasta you cook

TOTAL TIME
About 20 to 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A colander
- Tongs or a pasta fork
- A heat-safe mug or measuring cup
- Long oven mitts or sturdy pot holders
- A liquid level indicator, optional

INGREDIENTS
- 8 ounces dried pasta, such as spaghetti, penne, or another shape you like, for 2 servings, or 1 pound for 4 servings
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the pasta water
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, or 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) grated Parmesan cheese, plus more if you like
- Black pepper, to taste

TACTILE TIPS
Dried pasta feels hard and smooth. A standard 1 pound box is a tight bundle or a full box of short shapes. Butter is firm when cold. Parmesan feels dry and grainy. The cooking water will be hot, so use a mitt when you dip out a cup of it.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Fill a large pot no more than two-thirds full with water.

2. Set the pot on the burner, then turn the heat to high.

3. Bring the water to a rolling boil. A rolling boil means large bubbles break hard across the surface and you can hear a steady roar of steam.

4. Add the salt.

5. Add the pasta.

6. Fan long noodles with tongs as they soften so they slide under the water.

7. Stir in the first 30 seconds so the pasta does not clump.

8. Start testing 1 minute before the package time.

9. Lift a piece, let it cool, and bite it.

10. Stop cooking when the pasta is al dente. Al dente means it is tender but still slightly firm in the center, with no hard, chalky core.

11. Dip a heat-safe mug into the pot and save about 1 cup of the cooking water.

12. Use a mitt. This is reserved pasta water, meaning starchy water you will use to loosen the sauce. Reserved pasta water is a cup of the starchy cooking water you save before you drain the pot.

13. Turn off the heat.

14. Drain the pasta in a colander in the sink.

15. Stand back from the steam.

16. Return the pasta to the pot.

17. Add the butter or oil, about 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) of the reserved pasta water, the cheese, and the pepper.

18. Toss until the pasta feels coated.

19. Add a little more pasta water if it feels dry or sticky.

20. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A rolling boil is loud and steamy. Done pasta bends easily and feels slightly firm in the center when you bite it, not hard and raw, and not mushy. The dressed pasta should feel silky and evenly coated.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The water should be bubbling hard before the pasta goes in. Cooked pasta should look flexible, not stiff. A bitten piece may look slightly paler in the very center, but it should not look chalky white or raw. The butter and cheese should cling to the noodles, not sit in a puddle.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The water should be bubbling hard before the pasta goes in. Cooked pasta should look flexible, not stiff. A bitten piece may look slightly paler in the very center, but it should not look chalky white or raw. The butter and cheese should cling to the noodles, not sit in a puddle.

SAFETY
Boiling water can burn you. Do not fill the pot to the top. Turn the heat off before you move a full pot. If the pot is too heavy, dip out some water first, or cook only 8 ounces at a time. Keep handles turned inward. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Salt the water so it tastes plainly salty, like seawater, once the salt dissolves. Do not rinse the pasta. The surface starch helps sauce cling. Use this same boiling method for the other pasta recipes in the Supper chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Leftover buttered pasta will firm up in the fridge. Reheat it with a splash of water until it is steaming hot, at least 165 degrees F if you added perishable toppings. Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
