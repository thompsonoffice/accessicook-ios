Sheet-Pan Pizza
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A rectangular pizza baked on a sheet pan, using store-bought dough to keep this weeknight-friendly. The crust should feel set and lightly crisp, and any meat toppings should be already cooked or be toppings that cook through in the oven, such as fully cooked sausage.

SERVINGS
4 to 6

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes, plus dough rest if you make dough, or 25 minutes with store-bought dough

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound pizza dough, store-bought, at room temperature for 20 minutes
- 2 tablespoons oil, for the pan and the crust
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) pizza sauce or marinara
- 2 cups shredded mozzarella cheese
- Toppings you like, such as cooked sausage, sliced peppers, or olives

TACTILE TIPS
Let the dough sit out so it is easier to stretch. Oiled fingers help. The dough should feel elastic, not ice-cold and tight, and not so warm that it tears into holes you cannot pinch shut. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 450 degrees F.

2. Oil a sheet pan.

3. Press or stretch the dough to fit the pan.

4. If it shrinks back, wait 5 minutes and try again.

5. Brush the dough with a little oil.

6. Add sauce, then cheese, then toppings.

7. Keep toppings lighter in the center so the middle can cook.

8. Bake 12 to 18 minutes, until the crust feels set and lightly crisp, and the cheese is melted and bubbling.

9. Rest 3 minutes, then cut and serve. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

10. Use a cutter or a knife, and a cut-resistant glove on the hand that holds the pizza steady if you need it.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The crust should feel set and lightly crisp, not doughy and raw in the center. You should smell toasted dough, tomato, and cheese, not burning. The pan will be extremely hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The crust should look golden at the edges. The cheese should look melted and lightly browned in spots, not pale and stretchy-raw, and not black.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The crust should look golden at the edges. The cheese should look melted and lightly browned in spots, not pale and stretchy-raw, and not black.

SAFETY
Use mitts. If you use meat toppings, they should be already cooked, or be ingredients that can reach a safe temperature in this short bake. Reheat leftover pizza to 165 degrees F. See Midnight Snacks for a leftover pizza reheat method. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cornmeal or extra oil on the pan helps the crust not stick. If the center stays pale and soft, move the pan to a lower rack for a few minutes.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
