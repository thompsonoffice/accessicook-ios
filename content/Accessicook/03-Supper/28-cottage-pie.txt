ACCESSICOOK
Cottage Pie
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A baked dish of seasoned ground beef and vegetables in gravy, topped with mashed potatoes and browned in the oven. If you use lamb, some people call it shepherd's pie. This version is beef, which is cottage pie. The beef must reach 160 degrees F before it is baked, and the finished center should be 165 degrees F if you are reheating leftovers later. For a first bake of freshly cooked filling, heat it until the top is browned and the filling is bubbling hot.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 pound ground beef
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 carrots, diced
- 1 cup frozen peas
- 2 tablespoons flour
- 1 cup beef broth
- 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce, optional
- 1 teaspoon salt, plus more to taste
- 4 cups mashed potatoes, from the Misc chapter or leftovers

TACTILE TIPS
The filling should feel loose and gravy-like, not dry, before the potatoes go on. The potato topping should be spread to the edges so it is a lid.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Grease the baking dish if the ingredients include butter or oil for the dish.

3. Cook the onion, carrot, and beef in oil until the beef is 160 degrees F.

4. Stir in the flour, then the broth, Worcestershire, salt, and peas.

5. Simmer until slightly thick. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

6. Spread in a baking dish.

7. Cover with mashed potatoes, going all the way to the edges.

8. Bake 20 to 25 minutes.

9. The filling should be bubbling at the edges, and the top should feel hot and slightly browned.

10. If the filling was made ahead, the center should reach 165 degrees F.

11. Rest 5 to 10 minutes so the dish can settle, then serve. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The beef must be 160 degrees F as you cook the filling. The finished pie should be bubbling hot throughout. You should smell browned potatoes and savory gravy. A thermometer in the center should read at least 165 degrees F if the filling was made ahead and chilled.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look golden with browned spots. The filling should be bubbling at the edges.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look golden with browned spots. The filling should be bubbling at the edges.

SAFETY
Wash hands after raw beef. The dish is very hot. Use mitts. Cool leftovers in the dish or in shallow containers, then reheat to 165 degrees F. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Leftover mashed potatoes work perfectly. A fork dragged over the top makes ridges that brown nicely for a sighted helper to check.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
