ACCESSICOOK
Chicken Parmesan
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Breaded chicken cutlets baked or pan-cooked, then topped with marinara and mozzarella and finished until the cheese melts and the chicken is 165 degrees F. It is a hearty supper with pasta.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 thin chicken cutlets, about 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds)
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) flour
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 1 cup dry breadcrumbs
- 2 tablespoons oil, if you are browning the cutlets in a pan
- 1 cup marinara sauce
- 1 cup shredded mozzarella cheese
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) grated Parmesan cheese

TACTILE TIPS
Cutlets should be even and fairly thin so they cook through without burning the crumbs. The breading should feel dry and sandy, with no bare, slick spots.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Season the chicken with salt.

3. Coat each piece in flour, then egg, then breadcrumbs.

4. Brown the cutlets in oil 2 to 3 minutes per side, or bake them 10 minutes on a greased pan, until the crumbs feel set.

5. Top each piece with marinara, mozzarella, and Parmesan.

6. Bake 10 to 15 minutes more, until the chicken is 165 degrees F in the thickest part and the cheese feels melted and hot.

7. Rest 3 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

8. Serve with pasta if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer must read 165 degrees F. The cheese should feel melted and hot. You should smell toasted crumbs, tomato, and cheese, not burning breading.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The crumbs should look golden. The cheese should look melted and lightly browned in spots. The chicken should look opaque if you cut a piece.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The crumbs should look golden. The cheese should look melted and lightly browned in spots. The chicken should look opaque if you cut a piece.

SAFETY
Raw chicken and raw egg are in the breading setup. Use one hand for wet and one for dry if you can. Wash everything after. The pan is hot. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Use thin cutlets, or pound thicker breasts under parchment. A wire rack on the pan helps the bottom stay crisper.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
