ACCESSICOOK
Corned Beef and Cabbage
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A pot of corned beef brisket simmered until tender, with cabbage and other vegetables added near the end. Packaged corned beef is already cured and often comes with a spice packet. Tenderness, not just temperature, is the finishing cue, though leftovers should later be reheated to 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 3 hours, mostly simmering

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 corned beef brisket, about 3 to 4 pounds, with spice packet if included
- Water, to cover the meat by about 1 inch
- 6 carrots, cut into large pieces
- 8 small potatoes, or 3 large potatoes, cut into chunks
- 1 small head cabbage, cut into wedges

TACTILE TIPS
The brisket is a thick, firm, often vacuum-packed piece of meat. After a long simmer it should feel tender when pierced, and a slice should shred a little under a fork, not feel like a rubber eraser. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Rinse the brisket if the package says to, then put it in a large pot and cover it with water by about 1 inch.

2. Add the spice packet.

3. Bring to a boil, then lower to a gentle simmer. Simmer means small, gentle bubbles, not a hard, rattling boil.

4. Cover and cook about 2 1/2 hours, until a fork goes in with less resistance than at the start.

5. Add the potatoes and carrots.

6. Simmer 15 minutes.

7. Add the cabbage wedges.

8. Simmer 15 to 20 minutes more, until the vegetables are tender and the meat is easy to pierce.

9. Lift the meat out and rest it 10 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

10. Slice it across the grain, meaning across the long fibers, so it is easier to chew.

11. Serve with the vegetables and a little of the broth.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide into the meat and into a potato with little resistance. The kitchen should smell salty, spicy, and savory. Cabbage should feel tender.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The meat should look dull pink from curing, which can remain even when it is fully cooked, so do not use color alone. The vegetables should look tender, not raw in the center, and not falling into mush unless you like them very soft.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. SAFETY
Use a large, stable pot. The water is hot. Lift the meat with large forks or a sturdy spatula, and use mitts. Cool leftovers quickly. Reheat to 165 degrees F. For steaks, roasts, and other whole pieces of beef, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. If this dish is a slow stew, keep cooking until the meat is also tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Save some leftover meat for hash in the Breakfast chapter. If the broth is too salty, serve the meat with a little mustard rather than a lot of the cooking liquid.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
