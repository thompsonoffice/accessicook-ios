Salmon Patties
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Patties made from canned salmon, egg, and crumbs, cooked in a skillet until they are browned and hot in the center, 145 degrees F for fish, or 165 degrees F if you are using leftover cooked salmon that has been chilled and you are reheating it in the patty. Canned salmon is already cooked, so you are mainly heating the patties through and setting the egg binder, which should reach 160 degrees F.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cans (6 to 7 ounces each) salmon, drained
- 1 large egg
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) breadcrumbs or crushed crackers
- 2 tablespoons diced onion or green onion, optional
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice or mustard
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt, plus more to taste, because canned salmon can be salty
- 2 tablespoons oil, for the pan

TACTILE TIPS
Drain the salmon well and flake it, removing large, hard bones if you prefer, though the soft bones are edible. Drain means pour off the liquid. The mix should hold a patty, not feel wet and loose, and not dry and crumbly.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the salmon, egg, crumbs, onion, lemon or mustard, and salt.

2. Shape 8 small patties.

3. If the mix is too wet, add a spoon of crumbs.

4. If it is too dry, add a teaspoon of water or another bit of egg.

5. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat.

6. Cook the patties 3 to 5 minutes per side, until they feel browned and firm, and the center is hot, 160 degrees F because of the egg.

7. Drain on a plate lined with a paper towel.

8. Serve with lemon or a simple sauce.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The centers should feel hot and set, 160 degrees F. You should smell toasted crumbs and salmon, not burning oil or a sour fish smell.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The patties should look golden brown on both sides, and the center should look set, not wet and raw with liquid egg.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The patties should look golden brown on both sides, and the center should look set, not wet and raw with liquid egg.

SAFETY
The canning liquid is messy, so drain over the sink. The oil will spatter. Use a splatter screen. Because of the egg, treat the centers as an egg dish and heat them to 160 degrees F. Cook fish to 145 degrees F, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes, meaning it comes apart easily with a fork.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
These are good with a yogurt-dill sauce or with the Ranch Dip in the Misc chapter. Leftover cooked salmon can replace canned, and the patties should then be heated to 165 degrees F if that salmon was leftover and chilled.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
