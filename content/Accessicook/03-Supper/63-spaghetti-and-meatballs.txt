Spaghetti and Meatballs
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Spaghetti tossed with meatballs and tomato sauce. Use the Meatballs in Tomato Sauce recipe in this chapter, and the Pasta Primer for the spaghetti. This page is the combined supper, with the same safety temperatures.

SERVINGS
4 to 6

TOTAL TIME
About 50 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A colander
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound spaghetti
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the pasta water
- 1 recipe Meatballs in Tomato Sauce, already cooked so the meatballs are 160 degrees F in the center
- Grated Parmesan cheese, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Time it so the meatballs are hot when the pasta is drained. The pasta should feel tender but still slightly firm, and the sauce should coat it.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Make the meatballs in tomato sauce, or reheat leftover meatballs in sauce until the sauce bubbles and the meatballs are 165 degrees F in the center.

2. Cook the spaghetti in salted boiling water until it is tender but still slightly firm in the center.

3. Save 1/2 cup (half a cup) of the pasta water, then drain. Drain means pour off the liquid.

4. Toss the pasta with the sauce and a splash of pasta water if it needs loosening.

5. Serve the meatballs on top, with Parmesan if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Meatballs must be 160 degrees F on a first cook, or 165 degrees F when reheated. Pasta should feel tender with a slight firmness. You should smell tomato, meat, and pasta, not raw beef or burning sauce.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The meatballs should look browned and coated with sauce. The pasta should look evenly sauced, not dry and not watery. A meatball center should look cooked, not raw, though the thermometer is the test.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The meatballs should look browned and coated with sauce. The pasta should look evenly sauced, not dry and not watery. A meatball center should look cooked, not raw, though the thermometer is the test.

SAFETY
See the meatball recipe for raw-meat handling. Boiling water can burn you. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is a good family supper. Leftovers pack well for lunch, reheated until the center is 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
