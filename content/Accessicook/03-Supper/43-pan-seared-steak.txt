ACCESSICOOK
Pan-Seared Steak
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A steak cooked in a hot skillet until it reaches at least 145 degrees F, then rested 3 minutes. That is the USDA minimum for whole-muscle beef. The center may still look slightly pink at that temperature, which is expected and safe when the thermometer confirms the number.

SERVINGS
2

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes, plus 20 minutes to take the chill off the meat

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 steaks, such as sirloin, ribeye, or strip, about 1 inch thick
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 tablespoon butter, optional, for finishing

TACTILE TIPS
Pat the steaks very dry so they brown instead of steam. Let them sit out 15 to 20 minutes so they are not ice-cold in the center, but do not leave raw meat out longer than needed.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pat the meat or fish dry.

2. Season both sides with the salt, pepper, and any rub listed.

3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat until it smells hot and the food sizzles firmly when it touches the pan.

4. Lay the pieces in the pan in a single layer. Do not crowd them.

5. Cook the first side 3 to 4 minutes, without moving the steak, until it releases easily and smells deeply browned.

6. Turn the pieces.

7. Turn and cook 3 to 5 minutes more, checking temperature at 3 minutes.

8. Take the steaks off the heat when they reach 145 degrees F.

9. Add optional butter to the pan and spoon it over the meat.

10. Rest the meat on a plate for the time in the recipe. Rest means let it sit so the juices settle.

11. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A firm sizzle means the pan is hot enough. A thermometer in the thickest part, from the side if you can, must read 145 degrees F, then the steaks should rest at least 3 minutes. They should feel springy-firm, not soft and raw, and not hard like overcooked meat.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The outside should be well browned, with some darker spots in the fat. At 145 degrees F the center may still look faintly pink. There should not be a cool, raw, dark-red look in the middle.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The outside should be well browned, with some darker spots in the fat. At 145 degrees F the center may still look faintly pink. There should not be a cool, raw, dark-red look in the middle.

SAFETY
Use a splatter screen. The pan will smoke a little if it is hot enough. Turn on a fan if you have one. Wash the board. Do not press all the juices out with a spatula. For steaks, roasts, and other whole pieces of beef, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. If this dish is a slow stew, keep cooking until the meat is also tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a thicker steak, finish it in a 400 degree F oven after searing both sides, still to 145 degrees F plus a rest. A pat of garlic butter at the end is nice.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
