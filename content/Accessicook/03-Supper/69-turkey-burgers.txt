Turkey Burgers
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Patties made from ground turkey, cooked until they reach 165 degrees F. Turkey burgers are leaner than beef burgers and can dry out if they are overcooked, so use a thermometer and take them off the heat as soon as they are safe.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound ground turkey
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- 1 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce or soy sauce, optional, for moisture and flavor
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 4 buns and toppings, such as lettuce, tomato, and ketchup

TACTILE TIPS
Handle the meat lightly. Tight, dense patties get tough. A slight dimple in the center of each patty helps them stay flatter as they cook.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pat the meat or fish dry.

2. Season both sides with the salt, pepper, and any rub listed.

3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat until it smells hot and the food sizzles firmly when it touches the pan.

4. Lay the pieces in the pan in a single layer. Do not crowd them.

5. Cook the first side 5 to 6 minutes, until browned and released from the pan.

6. Turn the pieces.

7. Turn and cook 4 to 6 minutes more.

8. The center must read 165 degrees F.

9. Rest the meat on a plate for the time in the recipe. Rest means let it sit so the juices settle.

10. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the center must read 165 degrees F. The meat should feel firm, not pasty and raw. You should smell cooked poultry, not raw meat.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The burgers should look cooked through, with no raw, pink, pasty look in the center. The outside should be browned. Color is not enough. Use the thermometer.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The burgers should look cooked through, with no raw, pink, pasty look in the center. The outside should be browned. Color is not enough. Use the thermometer.

SAFETY
Ground turkey must reach 165 degrees F, not 160. Wash hands and the board. Use a splatter screen. Do not put cooked patties on the raw plate. Cook ground chicken or ground turkey to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A tablespoon of mayonnaise or grated onion in the mix can help moisture. Cheese can go on in the last minute. Do not press the patties hard with a spatula.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
