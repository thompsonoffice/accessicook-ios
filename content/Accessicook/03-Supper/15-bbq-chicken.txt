ACCESSICOOK
BBQ Chicken
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chicken baked, then brushed with barbecue sauce for the last part of cooking so the sauce gets tacky but does not burn. The chicken must reach 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 pounds chicken pieces, such as thighs or drumsticks, or 4 boneless breasts
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 1 cup barbecue sauce, divided

TACTILE TIPS
Sauce early in a hot oven can burn because of the sugar in it. That is why you add most of the sauce near the end. The finished sauce should feel tacky, not wet and raw, and not black and bitter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Line a pan with foil.

3. Season the chicken with salt and pepper.

4. Set it on the pan.

5. Bake 20 minutes for boneless pieces, or 30 minutes for bone-in pieces.

6. Brush with about half the sauce.

7. Bake 10 to 15 minutes more, until the thickest meat is 165 degrees F, not touching bone, and the sauce feels set and tacky.

8. Brush with the remaining sauce.

9. Rest 5 minutes. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

10. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer must read 165 degrees F. The sauce should smell sweet and smoky, not burnt. The meat should feel firm and juicy, not squishy.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with a glossy, slightly browned sauce, not black and charred all over.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with a glossy, slightly browned sauce, not black and charred all over.

SAFETY
Use a raw-poultry board. Wash the brush if it touched raw chicken before it touches the cooked sauce at the end, or use a clean brush for the last coat. Use mitts. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a grill, cook the chicken most of the way, then sauce it at the end over more moderate heat. The same 165 degrees F rule applies.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
