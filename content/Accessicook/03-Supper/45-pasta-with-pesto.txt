Pasta with Pesto
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Hot pasta tossed with pesto, which is a sauce of basil, oil, nuts, and cheese, usually bought in a jar or tub for this weeknight version. Save some pasta water so the sauce coats the noodles. See the Pasta Primer in this chapter for the basic boiling method.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A colander

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound pasta
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the pasta water
- 1/2 to 2/3 cup (half to two-thirds of a cup) pesto
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) grated Parmesan cheese, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Pesto is thick, oily, and very fragrant. The dressed pasta should feel evenly coated and slightly loose, not sticky and not swimming in oil.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cook the pasta in salted boiling water until it is tender but still slightly firm in the center.

2. Save 1 cup of the pasta water, then drain. Drain means pour off the liquid.

3. Return the pasta to the pot.

4. Add the pesto and a splash of pasta water.

5. Toss until the pasta feels coated, adding more water if it is tight.

6. Add Parmesan if you like.

7. Serve at once, because pesto pasta firms as it sits.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The pasta should feel tender with a slight firmness. You should smell basil, garlic, and cheese. The sauce should feel silky on the noodles.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The pasta should look evenly green-coated, not dry and not oily with a puddle at the bottom.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The pasta should look evenly green-coated, not dry and not oily with a puddle at the bottom.

SAFETY
Boiling water can burn you. See the Pasta Primer for draining safety. If the pesto contains nuts, do not serve it to anyone with a nut allergy. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F with a splash of water. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A squeeze of lemon brightens jarred pesto. Cherry tomatoes or leftover cooked chicken, reheated to 165 degrees F, can be added at the end.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
