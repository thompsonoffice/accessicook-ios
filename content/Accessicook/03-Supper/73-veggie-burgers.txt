ACCESSICOOK
Veggie Burgers
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Plant-based or bean burgers cooked in a skillet until they are hot in the center and browned on the outside. This page is for cooking store-bought or already-formed patties. Follow the package if the time differs, and heat leftovers or fully cooked patties to 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes, using store-bought or already-made patties

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 4 veggie burger patties, thawed if frozen, unless the package says to cook from frozen
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 4 buns
- Toppings: lettuce, tomato, onion, cheese, or condiments

TACTILE TIPS
Some patties are fragile when cold and firm up as they cook. Turn them gently. The center should feel hot, not icy or pasty-cold, if they started frozen.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat.

2. Add the patties.

3. Cook according to the package, often 4 to 6 minutes per side, until they feel browned and the centers are hot, 165 degrees F for leftover or fully cooked patties, or as the package directs for raw-style plant patties.

4. Add cheese in the last minute if you like, and cover the pan.

5. Serve on buns with toppings.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The centers should feel hot. You should smell toasted grains, beans, or vegetables, depending on the patty, not burning oil. A thermometer can confirm 165 degrees F on leftover or fully cooked patties.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The patties should look browned, not pale and raw-looking on the outside, and not black. Cheese, if used, should look melted.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The patties should look browned, not pale and raw-looking on the outside, and not black. Cheese, if used, should look melted.

SAFETY
Read the package, because some plant patties are meant to be cooked more like raw burgers and some are already cooked. Frozen patties can be icy in the center if the heat is too high and the time is too short. Use a spatula, not a fork that breaks them. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A lightly toasted bun helps. Some patties brown better in a nonstick skillet. You can also bake or air-fry them if the package lists that method, still checking that the center is hot.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
