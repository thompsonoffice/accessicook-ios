ACCESSICOOK
Eggplant Parmesan
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Slices of eggplant baked with marinara and cheese until the eggplant is tender and the center is bubbling hot. Salting and roasting or baking the eggplant first keeps the dish from getting watery and greasy. This is a meatless supper.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 2 medium eggplants, sliced into 1/2 inch (half inch) rounds
- 1 teaspoon salt, for sweating the eggplant, plus more for the sauce layers
- 2 tablespoons oil
- 3 cups marinara sauce
- 2 cups shredded mozzarella cheese
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) grated Parmesan cheese

TACTILE TIPS
Eggplant feels spongy when raw. After salting and baking, it should feel more flexible and less full of water. The finished dish should feel tender all the way through a slice, not tough and raw in the middle.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease the baking dish if the ingredients include butter or oil for the dish.

3. Salt the eggplant slices and let them sit 20 minutes, then pat them dry.

4. Brush with oil and bake or roast until they feel flexible, about 20 minutes at 400 degrees F.

5. In a baking dish, layer sauce, eggplant, and cheeses, repeating, and finish with cheese.

6. Bake 25 to 35 minutes after the dish is layered.

7. The dish should be bubbling at the edges, the cheese should feel melted, and a slice of eggplant should feel tender all the way through.

8. Rest 5 to 10 minutes so the dish can settle, then serve. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A slice should feel tender all the way through. The dish should be bubbling hot in the center, 165 degrees F if it was assembled ahead. You should smell roasted eggplant, tomato, and cheese, not burning cheese.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look bubbly, with melted, lightly browned cheese. The eggplant slices should look collapsed and tender, not pale and stiff.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look bubbly, with melted, lightly browned cheese. The eggplant slices should look collapsed and tender, not pale and stiff.

SAFETY
The baking dish is very hot. Use mitts. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. If you bread and fry the eggplant instead, use the same hot-oil cautions as in other fried variations in this book, and consider the baked method first. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Salting the slices, letting them sit 20 minutes, then patting them dry, helps a lot. You can roast the slices at 400 degrees F for 20 minutes before you layer the casserole.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
