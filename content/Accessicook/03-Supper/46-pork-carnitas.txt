ACCESSICOOK
Pork Carnitas
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Pork shoulder simmered until it shreds, then crisped in a hot pan or under the broiler, which is the top heating element in an oven. The long cook makes the meat tender. Then a short, hot finish makes the edges crisp. Serve in tortillas.

SERVINGS
6 to 8

TOTAL TIME
About 3 hours, mostly simmering, plus a crisp finish

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 3 to 4 pounds pork shoulder, cut into large chunks
- 1 tablespoon salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 onion, quartered
- 4 garlic cloves
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) orange juice or water
- Tortillas, salsa, and lime, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
The pork starts as firm, large chunks. When it is ready to shred, it should give easily under a fork. After the crisp finish, some edges should feel browned and chewy-crisp, not all soft, and not burnt and bitter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Season the pork with salt, pepper, and cumin.

2. Put it in a heavy pot with the onion, garlic, and orange juice or water.

3. Bring to a simmer, then cover and cook on low, or in a 300 degree F oven, 2 1/2 to 3 hours, until the meat shreds easily. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

4. Shred the pork, discarding large pieces of fat.

5. Crisp some of the meat in a hot skillet with a little of the juices, or spread it on a pan and broil 3 to 5 minutes, until the edges feel crisp.

6. Stay with the broiler, because food can burn quickly under it.

7. Serve in warm tortillas with salsa and lime.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The meat should shred easily before the crisp step. After the finish, some edges should feel crisp. You should smell citrus, cumin, and roasted pork, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The meat should look browned at the edges after the finish, and moist inside, not gray and boiled-looking, and not black.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The meat should look browned at the edges after the finish, and moist inside, not gray and boiled-looking, and not black.

SAFETY
Use mitts with a heavy pot. Open the lid away from your face. A broiler is very hot and needs you to stay in the kitchen. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. For pork chops, ribs, and roasts, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. For ribs or pulled pork, also cook until the meat is tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is a good party supper. Leftovers freeze well and also work in breakfast tacos, reheated to 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
