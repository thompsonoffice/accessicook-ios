ACCESSICOOK
Roast Chicken
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A whole chicken roasted until the thickest part of the thigh is 165 degrees F. This is a useful Sunday supper, and the leftovers can become chicken salad, soup, or sandwiches.

SERVINGS
4 to 6

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 30 minutes to 1 hour 45 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 whole chicken, about 4 to 5 pounds
- 1 tablespoon oil or softened butter
- 1 1/2 teaspoons (one and a half teaspoons) salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder or dried herbs, optional
- 1 onion, quartered, optional, for the cavity

TACTILE TIPS
Pat the chicken very dry so the skin can crisp. The thigh is the thickest dark-meat part, near the joint of the drumstick. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the middle while the oven is cold.

3. Pat the food dry.

4. Season it as the ingredients describe.

5. Pat the chicken dry.

6. Rub it with oil or butter, salt, pepper, and optional herbs.

7. Put onion in the cavity if you like.

8. Set the chicken breast-side up in a roasting pan.

9. Set the food in the pan in a single layer, or as described.

10. Roast about 15 minutes, then reduce the oven to 375 degrees F and continue 45 to 70 minutes, depending on size. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens. Roast means cook uncovered in the hot oven.

11. Start checking at 1 hour. The thickest thigh, not touching bone, must read 165 degrees F.

12. Check the breast as well.

13. Rest the chicken 10 to 15 minutes before you carve. Rest means let it sit so the juices settle.

14. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the thickest thigh, not touching bone, must read 165 degrees F. Check the breast too. The juices should feel clear, not thick and raw. You should smell roasted chicken, not burning fat or raw meat.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The skin should look golden and crisp, not pale and rubbery, and not black. Juices should run clear, but a thermometer is the real test. The thickest thigh and the thickest breast should both look opaque, not raw and red at the bone.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The skin should look golden and crisp, not pale and rubbery, and not black. Juices should run clear, but a thermometer is the real test. The thickest thigh and the thickest breast should both look opaque, not raw and red at the bone.

SAFETY
Wash your hands and the board after handling raw poultry. Use long mitts. The pan will have hot fat. Do not rinse the raw chicken in the sink, because that can splash bacteria. Roast until 165 degrees F, then rest. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Rest 10 to 15 minutes before you carve, so the juices settle. A simple roast potato pan can go in the oven for the last 45 minutes. Use leftovers within 3 to 4 days, reheated to 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
