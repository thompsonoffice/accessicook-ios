Fettuccine Alfredo
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
This recipe was on the original request list for the book.

Fettuccine Alfredo is long pasta in a smooth sauce of butter, cream, and Parmesan cheese. This version does not use raw egg, which keeps the method simpler and safer. The sauce should stay gentle and hot, not a hard boil, so the cheese stays smooth.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A colander
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer
- Tongs
- A heat-safe mug
- Long oven mitts or pot holders

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound dried fettuccine, or another long pasta
- 1 tablespoon salt, for the pasta water
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1 cup heavy cream
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) finely grated Parmesan cheese, plus more for serving
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper, or to taste
- A pinch of salt, if the cheese is not very salty
- Optional: cooked chicken or cooked shrimp, already heated, for serving on top

TACTILE TIPS
Fettuccine feels like flat, wide ribbons. Heavy cream is thick and coats a cup. Finely grated Parmesan feels like damp sand, not long shreds, and melts more evenly. If you add chicken or shrimp, they should already be fully cooked and hot.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Fill a large pot no more than two-thirds full with water and bring it to a rolling boil.

2. Add the salt, then add the fettuccine.

3. Cook, stirring now and then, until the pasta is al dente. Al dente means tender but still slightly firm in the center.

4. Save 1 cup of the pasta cooking water, then drain the pasta. Drain means pour off the liquid.

5. Turn off the pasta pot.

6. Melt the butter in a wide skillet over medium-low heat.

7. Add the cream.

8. Warm it until it is steaming and just barely bubbling at the edges. This gentle heat is a simmer, meaning small bubbles, not a hard boil. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

9. Lower the heat.

10. Add the Parmesan a handful at a time, stirring until it melts.

11. Add the pepper.

12. Add the drained pasta to the skillet.

13. Toss, adding splashes of reserved pasta water, until the sauce coats the noodles and feels silky, not pasty and not watery. Reserved pasta water is a cup of the starchy cooking water you save before you drain the pot.

14. Taste a cooled bite and add a pinch of salt only if it needs it. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

15. Serve at once, with more cheese if you like.

16. Add already-cooked hot chicken or shrimp now, if you are using them.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The sauce should feel smooth and coat a spoon. It should smell buttery and cheesy, not scorched. The pasta should feel tender with a slight firmness in the center. The dish should be served hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The sauce should look smooth, pale, and glossy. It should cling to the noodles. It should not look grainy, oily, or like a thin puddle in the pan. The pasta should look flexible, not stiff.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The sauce should look smooth, pale, and glossy. It should cling to the noodles. It should not look grainy, oily, or like a thin puddle in the pan. The pasta should look flexible, not stiff.

SAFETY
Boiling pasta water can burn you. Stand back from steam when you drain. Keep the cream sauce at a gentle heat so it does not boil over. If you add chicken, it should already have been cooked to 165 degrees F. If you add shrimp, they should already be opaque and firm. Leftovers must be reheated to 165 degrees F. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Grate the cheese yourself if you can. Pre-grated cheese sometimes makes a grainy sauce. If the sauce tightens too much, add more pasta water. If you want garlic, cook 1 minced clove in the butter for 30 seconds before you add the cream. Mince means cut into very small pieces.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate promptly. The sauce will thicken in the fridge. Reheat it gently with a splash of milk or water until it is 165 degrees F and smooth again. Do not keep cream sauces out at room temperature. Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
