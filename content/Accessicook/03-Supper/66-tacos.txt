ACCESSICOOK
Tacos
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Seasoned ground beef in tortillas with toppings you like. Cook the beef to 160 degrees F. This is a taco-night supper, and leftover meat can become taco salad or a rice bowl the next day.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound ground beef
- 1 onion, diced, optional
- 1 packet taco seasoning, or 1 tablespoon chili powder plus 1 teaspoon cumin and 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) water
- 8 taco shells or small tortillas
- Toppings: shredded lettuce, cheese, tomato, salsa, or sour cream

TACTILE TIPS
The cooked beef should feel crumbly and moist, not raw and pasty, and not dry as dust. Warm tortillas are more flexible than cold ones.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cook the onion, if using, in a skillet over medium heat until it feels soft.

2. Add the beef.

3. Cook, breaking it up, until it is 160 degrees F and no pink, pasty meat remains.

4. Stir in the seasoning and water.

5. Simmer 2 to 3 minutes, until the mix looks moist but not watery. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

6. Warm the tortillas or shells.

7. Serve the meat in a bowl and let people build their own tacos with toppings.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer must read 160 degrees F. You should smell chili and cooked beef. The meat should feel crumbly and cooked, not pasty.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The beef should be brown, with no pink. The mix should look moist and evenly seasoned, not gray and watery, and not dry and hard.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The beef should be brown, with no pink. The mix should look moist and evenly seasoned, not gray and watery, and not dry and hard.

SAFETY
Wash hands after raw beef. Keep cold toppings cold until you serve. Reheat leftover meat to 165 degrees F. Cook ground beef to 160 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For chicken tacos, use leftover cooked chicken warmed in the seasoning and a little water, to 165 degrees F. Soft flour tortillas are easier to handle than some hard shells, which crack easily.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
