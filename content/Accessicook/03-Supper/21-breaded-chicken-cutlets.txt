Breaded Chicken Cutlets
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Thin chicken pieces coated in crumbs and cooked until crisp and 165 degrees F. They are good on their own, with a salad, or in a sandwich the next day.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 thin chicken cutlets, about 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds)
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) flour
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 1 cup dry breadcrumbs or panko
- 3 tablespoons oil, for the skillet

TACTILE TIPS
The coating should feel even and dry. Panko crumbs feel larger and lighter than fine breadcrumbs and often stay crisper.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Season the chicken with salt.

2. Coat in flour, then egg, then crumbs, pressing so they stick.

3. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium heat until a crumb sizzles at once.

4. Cook the cutlets 3 to 5 minutes per side, until the coating feels crisp and a thermometer in the thickest part reads 165 degrees F.

5. Drain on a rack or a plate lined with a paper towel. Drain means pour off the liquid.

6. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer must read 165 degrees F. The coating should feel crisp. You should hear a sizzle, and you should smell toasted crumbs and cooked chicken, not burning oil.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The crumbs should look golden, not pale and not dark brown. The chicken inside should look opaque, with no raw pink.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The crumbs should look golden, not pale and not dark brown. The chicken inside should look opaque, with no raw pink.

SAFETY
Raw chicken and egg are in the breading bowls. Do not taste raw coating. Use a splatter screen. Do not crowd the pan. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Keep finished cutlets in a 200 degree F oven while you cook the rest. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat. An air fryer can cook a single layer at 375 degrees F, still to 165 degrees F, often 10 to 14 minutes, turning once.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
