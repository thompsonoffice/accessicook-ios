ACCESSICOOK
Baked Salmon
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Salmon fillets baked until they reach 145 degrees F, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes, meaning it comes apart easily with a fork. This is one of the fastest supper proteins in the book.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 salmon fillets, about 6 ounces each
- 1 tablespoon oil or melted butter
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) black pepper
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Raw salmon feels moist and slightly firm, with layers you can feel. Pin bones, if present, feel like tiny stiff needles and can be pulled with clean tweezers or pliers. Done salmon flakes into moist layers, not wet and translucent sheets, and not dry crumbs.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the middle while the oven is cold.

3. Pat the food dry.

4. Season it as the ingredients describe.

5. Pat the salmon dry.

6. Pull any pin bones.

7. Set the fillets on a lined pan.

8. Brush with oil or butter and season with salt and pepper.

9. Set the food in the pan in a single layer, or as described.

10. Roast 12 to 18 minutes, depending on thickness. Roast means cook uncovered in the hot oven.

11. The thickest part should read 145 degrees F, or the fish should flake easily and look opaque.

12. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the thickest part should read 145 degrees F, or the fish should flake easily and feel opaque and hot. You should smell cooked fish and lemon, not a sour or strongly fishy off smell.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The fish should look opaque and slightly lighter in color, not glassy and raw in the center, and it should flake when pressed with a fork. The top may look lightly glossy from the oil or butter.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The fish should look opaque and slightly lighter in color, not glassy and raw in the center, and it should flake when pressed with a fork. The top may look lightly glossy from the oil or butter.

SAFETY
Wash the board after raw fish, and do not use that board for the lemon wedges unless you wash it first. Use mitts. Serve soon. Cook fish to 145 degrees F, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes, meaning it comes apart easily with a fork.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little garlic or dill is a good extra. If the fillets are very thin, start checking at 10 minutes. Leftovers make a good cold salad, kept refrigerated and eaten within 3 days, or reheated to 165 degrees F if you prefer them hot.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
