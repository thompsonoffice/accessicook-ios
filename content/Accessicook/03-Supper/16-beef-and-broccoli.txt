ACCESSICOOK
Beef and Broccoli
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple takeout-style skillet of thinly sliced beef and broccoli in a savory sauce. The broccoli should stay a little crisp, and the beef should be browned and at least 145 degrees F.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound flank or sirloin, thinly sliced
- 4 cups broccoli florets
- 2 tablespoons oil, divided
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) soy sauce mixed with 2 tablespoons brown sugar, 2 minced garlic cloves, 1 teaspoon grated ginger, optional, and 1 teaspoon cornstarch
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) water, if the pan is dry
- Rice, for serving

TACTILE TIPS
Cut the broccoli into even florets. The sauce should thicken just enough to coat the beef and broccoli, not turn gluey.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cut the protein and vegetables into pieces about the same size so they cook evenly.

2. Mix the sauce ingredients in a small bowl.

3. Pat the protein dry.

4. Season it with the salt, if listed.

5. Heat the oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat until it smells hot and a piece of vegetable sizzles at once.

6. Cook the beef in 1 tablespoon oil until it is browned and at least 145 degrees F, then move it to a plate.

7. Move the protein to a plate if the pan is crowded.

8. Add a little more oil if the pan is dry.

9. Add the firmer vegetables first.

10. Stir and cook until the vegetables are tender-crisp, meaning they are hot and slightly bent but not mushy.

11. Return the protein to the pan.

12. Add the sauce.

13. Stir until the sauce thickens slightly and coats everything, 1 to 2 minutes.

14. Serve hot over rice if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Beef should be at least 145 degrees F and should feel cooked, not slimy. Broccoli should feel tender-crisp. The sauce should smell like soy, garlic, and a little sweetness, not burning sugar.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The broccoli should look bright green, with some browned edges, not olive-gray and mushy. The beef should look browned, not raw and red.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The broccoli should look bright green, with some browned edges, not olive-gray and mushy. The beef should look browned, not raw and red.

SAFETY
Slice the beef with care. The pan is hot. Wash the board after raw beef. For steaks, roasts, and other whole pieces of beef, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. If this dish is a slow stew, keep cooking until the meat is also tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you like softer broccoli, add a splash of water and cover the pan for 1 to 2 minutes after the broccoli goes in. Serve at once over rice.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
