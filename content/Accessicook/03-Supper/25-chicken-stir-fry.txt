ACCESSICOOK
Chicken Stir-Fry
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Bite-size chicken and vegetables cooked quickly in a hot pan with a simple soy sauce. Cut everything first, because the cooking goes fast. The chicken must reach 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound boneless chicken, cut into bite-size pieces
- 2 tablespoons oil, divided
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 4 cups mixed cut vegetables, such as broccoli, bell pepper, snap peas, or carrots
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) stir-fry sauce, or 3 tablespoons soy sauce mixed with 1 tablespoon sugar, 1 minced garlic clove, and 1 teaspoon cornstarch
- Rice, for serving, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Even, small pieces cook faster and more evenly. The vegetables should stay a little crisp. The sauce should coat, not puddle like water or turn into a gluey paste.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cut the protein and vegetables into pieces about the same size so they cook evenly.

2. Mix the sauce ingredients in a small bowl.

3. Pat the protein dry.

4. Season it with the salt, if listed.

5. Heat the oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat until it smells hot and a piece of vegetable sizzles at once.

6. Cook the chicken in 1 tablespoon oil until it is 165 degrees F in a thick piece, then move it to a plate.

7. Move the protein to a plate if the pan is crowded.

8. Add a little more oil if the pan is dry.

9. Add the firmer vegetables first.

10. Stir and cook until the vegetables are tender-crisp, meaning they are hot and slightly bent but not mushy.

11. Return the protein to the pan.

12. Add the sauce.

13. Stir until the sauce thickens slightly and coats everything, 1 to 2 minutes.

14. Serve hot over rice if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Chicken must be 165 degrees F. Vegetables should feel tender-crisp. The sauce should smell savory, like soy and garlic, not burnt. You should hear a sizzle the whole time if the pan is hot enough.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with no raw pink. The vegetables should look bright and slightly browned in spots, not dull and soggy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with no raw pink. The vegetables should look bright and slightly browned in spots, not dull and soggy.

SAFETY
Wash hands and the board. Have all ingredients cut before you start. The pan is very hot and can spatter. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cook in batches if your skillet is small. Serve over stovetop rice from the Misc chapter. Shrimp or tofu can use the same vegetable and sauce method, with their own safe doneness cues.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
