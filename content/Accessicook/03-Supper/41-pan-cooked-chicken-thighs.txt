Pan-Cooked Chicken Thighs
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chicken thighs cooked in a skillet until they reach 165 degrees F. Thighs are more forgiving than breasts and stay moist. You can use boneless or bone-in pieces. Bone-in takes a little longer.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 to 6 chicken thighs, about 2 pounds, boneless or bone-in
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) garlic powder or paprika, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Pat the skin dry if the thighs have skin, so it can crisp. For bone-in pieces, measure temperature in the thickest meat, not touching bone.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pat the meat or fish dry.

2. Season both sides with the salt, pepper, and any rub listed.

3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat until it smells hot and the food sizzles firmly when it touches the pan.

4. Lay the pieces in the pan in a single layer. Do not crowd them.

5. Cook the first side 6 to 8 minutes, until it releases easily and smells browned.

6. Turn the pieces.

7. Turn and cook 6 to 10 minutes more, longer for bone-in pieces.

8. The thickest meat, not touching bone, must read 165 degrees F.

9. Rest the meat on a plate for the time in the recipe. Rest means let it sit so the juices settle.

10. Serve hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the thickest meat, off the bone, must read 165 degrees F. Skin should feel crisp, not flabby and wet, if you wanted it crisp. You should hear a steady sizzle, not a steam hiss, when they first go in.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The skin, if present, should look golden and crisp. The meat should look opaque all the way through, with no raw red at the bone.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The skin, if present, should look golden and crisp. The meat should look opaque all the way through, with no raw red at the bone.

SAFETY
Wash hands and the board. Use a splatter screen. The fat will pop. Do not crowd the pan. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the outside is brown before the center is 165 degrees F, lower the heat and cover the pan, or finish in a 375 degree F oven. An air fryer can cook a single layer at 375 degrees F, still to 165 degrees F, often 16 to 22 minutes, turning once.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
