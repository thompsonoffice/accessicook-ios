ACCESSICOOK
Beef Stir-Fry
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Thin strips of beef and vegetables cooked quickly in a hot pan with a soy-based sauce. Slice the beef thin so it cooks fast. Whole-muscle beef is safely done at 145 degrees F, and thin strips often go a little further, until no raw red remains.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound flank or sirloin steak, thinly sliced against the grain
- 2 tablespoons oil, divided
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 4 cups cut vegetables, such as broccoli, bell pepper, or snap peas
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) stir-fry sauce, or 3 tablespoons soy sauce mixed with 1 tablespoon brown sugar, 1 minced garlic clove, and 1 teaspoon cornstarch
- Rice, for serving, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Against the grain means you cut across the long muscle fibers, which makes the meat easier to chew. The strips should feel thin and even.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cut the protein and vegetables into pieces about the same size so they cook evenly.

2. Mix the sauce ingredients in a small bowl.

3. Pat the protein dry.

4. Season it with the salt, if listed.

5. Heat the oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat until it smells hot and a piece of vegetable sizzles at once.

6. Cook the beef in 1 tablespoon oil in a single layer, in batches if needed, until it is browned and at least 145 degrees F, then move it to a plate.

7. Move the protein to a plate if the pan is crowded.

8. Add a little more oil if the pan is dry.

9. Add the firmer vegetables first.

10. Stir and cook until the vegetables are tender-crisp, meaning they are hot and slightly bent but not mushy.

11. Return the protein to the pan.

12. Add the sauce.

13. Stir until the sauce thickens slightly and coats everything, 1 to 2 minutes.

14. Serve hot over rice if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thick strip should be at least 145 degrees F, and the meat should not feel slimy and raw. Vegetables should feel tender-crisp. You should hear a sizzle and smell soy and browning meat.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The beef should look browned, with no large raw red areas. The vegetables should look bright and slightly tender, not soggy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The beef should look browned, with no large raw red areas. The vegetables should look bright and slightly tender, not soggy.

SAFETY
A sharp knife and a cut-resistant glove help with thin slicing. The pan is very hot. Wash the board after raw beef. For steaks, roasts, and other whole pieces of beef, the USDA minimum is 145 degrees F, then a 3-minute rest. If this dish is a slow stew, keep cooking until the meat is also tender.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Frozen stir-fry vegetables work if you cook off their extra water. Serve over rice from the Misc chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
