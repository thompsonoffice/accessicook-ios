Roast Turkey Breast
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A boneless or bone-in turkey breast roasted until the thickest part is 165 degrees F. This is easier than a whole turkey and is useful for smaller gatherings and for leftover sandwiches.

SERVINGS
6

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 30 minutes, depending on size

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 turkey breast, about 3 to 5 pounds, boneless or bone-in
- 1 tablespoon oil or softened butter
- 1 1/2 teaspoons (one and a half teaspoons) salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 teaspoon dried herbs, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Tie a boneless breast with kitchen twine if it is uneven, so it cooks more evenly, or ask a butcher to do that. Measure in the thickest middle, not touching the pan or bone.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Set a rack in the middle while the oven is cold.

3. Pat the food dry.

4. Season it as the ingredients describe.

5. Pat the turkey dry.

6. Rub with oil or butter, salt, pepper, and herbs.

7. Set it in a roasting pan or baking dish.

8. Set the food in the pan in a single layer, or as described.

9. Roast 20 minutes per pound, starting to check 15 minutes early. Roast means cook uncovered in the hot oven.

10. The thickest part must read 165 degrees F, not touching bone or the pan.

11. Rest 10 to 15 minutes, then slice. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

12. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A thermometer in the thickest part must read 165 degrees F. The juices should feel clear, not thick and raw. You should smell roasted turkey, not burning butter.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The skin, if present, should look golden. The meat should look opaque and white or very light tan, with no raw pink in the center.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The skin, if present, should look golden. The meat should look opaque and white or very light tan, with no raw pink in the center.

SAFETY
Treat turkey like all poultry. Wash hands and the board. Do not rinse the raw turkey in the sink. Use mitts. Rest before slicing. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Start checking early. A small boneless breast can be done sooner than you think. Save the drippings for Pan Gravy in the Misc chapter. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
