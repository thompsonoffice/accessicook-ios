Lemon Garlic Chicken
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chicken cooked in a skillet with lemon juice, garlic, and a little broth or butter. The sauce is bright and savory. The chicken must still reach 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
4

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 boneless, skinless chicken breasts or thighs, about 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds)
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) black pepper
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) chicken broth
- 2 tablespoons lemon juice
- 2 tablespoons butter

TACTILE TIPS
Mince the garlic into very small pieces so it does not burn in large chunks. Mince means cut into very small pieces. The sauce should feel thin but flavorful, and it should coat the chicken lightly.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pat the chicken dry.

2. Season it with salt and pepper.

3. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat.

4. Cook the chicken 5 to 7 minutes per side, until it is 165 degrees F in the thickest part.

5. Move it to a plate.

6. Add the garlic to the pan.

7. Cook 30 seconds, until it is fragrant, not bitter and burnt.

8. Add the broth and lemon juice.

9. Simmer 2 minutes, scraping up the browned bits. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles. This is called deglazing. Deglaze means add liquid to a hot pan and scrape up the browned bits.

10. Stir in the butter until the sauce feels slightly glossy.

11. Return the chicken to the pan.

12. Spoon the sauce over it.

13. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: Chicken must be 165 degrees F. The sauce should smell of lemon, butter, and garlic, not burning. Garlic should be fragrant, not bitter.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with a lightly browned outside. The sauce should look glossy and pale, not dark and burnt from scorched garlic.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chicken should look opaque and cooked, with a lightly browned outside. The sauce should look glossy and pale, not dark and burnt from scorched garlic.

SAFETY
Wash hands and the board after raw chicken. The skillet and lemon sauce can spatter. Do not leave garlic on high heat. Cook all poultry to 165 degrees F. Measure in the thickest part. Do not let the thermometer tip touch bone or the pan.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little zest, which is the thin colorful outer lemon peel, adds more aroma. Zest is the thin, colorful outer peel of a lemon or other citrus fruit, not the bitter white layer under it. Serve with rice or the sides in the Misc chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
