ACCESSICOOK
Oatmeal Bars
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chewy bars with oats and brown sugar, somewhere between a cookie and a granola bar. They pack well for a snack. Optional add-ins include raisins, chocolate chips, or coconut.

SERVINGS
16 squares

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) rolled oats, not instant
- 1 cup all-purpose flour
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) packed brown sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) baking soda
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) cinnamon, optional
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) unsalted butter, melted
- 1 large egg
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) raisins or chocolate chips, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The mix should feel thick and sticky, with visible oats. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The edges should feel set, and the center should feel slightly soft. It will firm as it cools.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease the pan, or line it with parchment, leaving a little overhang as a handle.

3. Mix the oats, flour, brown sugar, baking soda, salt, and optional cinnamon.

4. Add the melted butter, egg, and vanilla, and stir until the mix feels evenly moist.

5. Stir in the raisins or chips, if using.

6. Spread the batter or dough evenly in the pan.

7. Bake 20 to 25 minutes.

8. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The edges should feel set, and the center should feel slightly soft.

9. Cool in the pan before you cut, unless the recipe tips say otherwise.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The center should feel slightly soft, not wet. You should smell oats, brown sugar, and vanilla, not raw flour or burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look golden, and the edges should look slightly darker. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look golden, and the edges should look slightly darker. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Cool before you cut. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Press the batter firmly and evenly in the pan so the bars are not thin at the corners. These keep 4 days in a closed container, or freeze well.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
