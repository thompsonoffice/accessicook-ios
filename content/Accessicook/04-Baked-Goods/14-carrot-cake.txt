Carrot Cake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A moist spiced cake made with grated carrots, often finished with cream cheese frosting. Grate the carrots finely so they soften in the batter. Cool the cake completely before you frost it.

SERVINGS
12 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 2 teaspoons baking soda
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 2 teaspoons cinnamon
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) sugar
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) oil
- 3 large eggs
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
- 2 cups finely grated carrots
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) chopped walnuts or raisins, optional
- Cream cheese frosting, optional, from the Misc chapter or store-bought

TACTILE TIPS
Finely grated carrots feel damp and feathery, not in thick matchsticks. The batter will feel thick and heavy. A toothpick in the baked cake should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease a 9 by 13 inch pan, or two 9 inch round pans.

3. Mix the flour, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon.

4. In a second bowl, mix the sugar, oil, eggs, and vanilla until even.

5. Add the flour mix and stir just until combined.

6. Fold in the carrots and any walnuts or raisins. Fold means turn the batter over itself gently.

7. Spread the batter in the pan or pans.

8. Bake 30 to 40 minutes, until a toothpick in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs, and the top feels springy.

9. Cool completely, then frost if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel springy. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs. You should smell cinnamon, carrot, and toasted sugar, not raw flour.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look golden brown and set. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs. The crumb should look moist and flecked with carrot, not wet in the center.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look golden brown and set. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs. The crumb should look moist and flecked with carrot, not wet in the center.

SAFETY
A box grater is sharp. Work slowly on a stable board and watch your knuckles. Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Cool completely before frosting. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This cake is even better the next day. Keep frosted cake refrigerated because of the cream cheese, and let a slice sit out a few minutes before you eat it if you like it softer.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
