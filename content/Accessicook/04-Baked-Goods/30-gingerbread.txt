ACCESSICOOK
Gingerbread
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A moist, dark spice cake sweetened with molasses, which is a thick, dark syrup with a slightly bitter, deep sweetness. This is a snack cake, not a cookie, and it is good plain or with whipped cream.

SERVINGS
9 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 50 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 2 teaspoons ground ginger
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) unsalted butter, melted, or oil
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) packed brown sugar
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) molasses
- 1 large egg
- 1 cup hot water

TACTILE TIPS
Molasses feels thick, sticky, and heavy. A little oil on the measuring cup can help it slide out. The batter will feel thin after you add the water. A toothpick in the baked cake should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease a 9 inch square pan.

3. Mix the flour, baking soda, salt, ginger, and cinnamon.

4. In a second bowl, mix the melted butter or oil, brown sugar, molasses, and egg until even.

5. Add the flour mix and stir until combined.

6. Add the hot water carefully and stir until the batter feels smooth and thin.

7. Pour the batter into the pan.

8. Bake 30 to 38 minutes, until a toothpick in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs, and the top feels springy.

9. Cool in the pan at least 15 minutes before you cut.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel springy. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs. You should smell ginger, molasses, and warm spice, not raw flour or burning sugar.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cake should look dark brown and set, slightly pulled from the sides. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs. The crumb should look moist and even, not wet.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cake should look dark brown and set, slightly pulled from the sides. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs. The crumb should look moist and even, not wet.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Molasses is sticky. Add hot water slowly. The cake is very hot in the center at first. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A dusting of powdered sugar or a dollop of Whipped Cream from the Misc chapter is plenty. This cake keeps well, covered, for several days.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
