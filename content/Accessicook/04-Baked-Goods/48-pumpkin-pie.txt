Pumpkin Pie
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A spiced pumpkin custard baked in a pie crust. The filling contains eggs and should set in the oven. A slight jiggle in the very center is all right and will finish as the pie cools. Use plain pumpkin puree, not pumpkin pie filling, unless you are following a shortcut on a can and adjust the sugar and spice.

SERVINGS
8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 15 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pie crust, store-bought or homemade, fitted into a 9 inch pie pan
- 1 can (15 ounces) pumpkin puree
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) sugar
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) ginger
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 2 large eggs
- 1 can (12 ounces) evaporated milk, or 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) half-and-half

TACTILE TIPS
The filling should feel smooth and pourable before baking. After baking, the edges should feel set, and the very center should jiggle slightly, not slosh like liquid. After cooling, a slice should feel creamy and hold its shape.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Mix the pumpkin, sugar, cinnamon, ginger, and salt until even.

3. Add the eggs and mix well, then stir in the evaporated milk.

4. Pour the filling into the crust.

5. Bake 15 minutes, then reduce the oven to 350 degrees F and bake 35 to 45 minutes more, until the edges feel set and the center jiggles slightly. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens.

6. A knife near the center should come out mostly clean.

7. Cool at least 2 hours.

8. Refrigerate if you are not serving it the same day.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The center should jiggle slightly, not slosh. You should smell pumpkin, spice, and baked custard, not raw egg or burning crust. After cooling, a slice should feel creamy and firm enough to cut.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The filling should look set and slightly puffed at the edges, with a small, softer-looking center that is not wet and liquid. A little cracking can happen. After cooling, a slice should hold its shape.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The filling should look set and slightly puffed at the edges, with a small, softer-looking center that is not wet and liquid. A little cracking can happen. After cooling, a slice should hold its shape.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw filling. It contains eggs. Use mitts. Cool, then refrigerate within 2 hours. Keep the pie refrigerated, and do not leave slices out more than 2 hours. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pie shield or a ring of foil keeps the crust edge from getting too dark. Serve with Whipped Cream from the Misc chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
