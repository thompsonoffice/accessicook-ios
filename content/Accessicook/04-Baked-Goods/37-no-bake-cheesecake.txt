ACCESSICOOK
No-Bake Cheesecake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A cream-cheese pie on a graham-cracker crust that sets in the refrigerator, not the oven. There are no eggs, so you are not baking to a temperature. The filling must chill long enough to slice cleanly.

SERVINGS
10 to 12 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes, plus at least 4 hours of chilling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) graham-cracker crumbs
- 3 tablespoons sugar, for the crust
- 6 tablespoons melted butter
- 16 ounces cream cheese, softened
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sugar, for the filling
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 cup heavy cream, cold, or one 8 ounce tub of whipped topping

TACTILE TIPS
Softened cream cheese should feel smooth, not lumpy. The filling should feel thick and fluffy before it goes into the crust. After a full chill, a slice should feel set and creamy, not soft and collapsing.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the crumbs, 3 tablespoons sugar, and melted butter until the mix feels like damp sand.

2. Press it firmly into a 9 inch pie pan, up the sides a little.

3. Chill the crust 15 minutes, or bake it at 350 degrees F for 8 minutes and cool completely if you want a firmer crust.

4. Beat the cream cheese, 1/2 cup (half a cup) sugar, and vanilla until the mix feels smooth.

5. In a second bowl, beat the cold cream until it holds a soft peak, meaning a ridge of cream stands up and then slightly folds over, or fold in the whipped topping.

6. Fold the cream into the cream-cheese mix. Fold means turn the mixture over itself gently so you do not knock out all the air.

7. Spread the filling in the crust.

8. Smooth the top.

9. Refrigerate at least 4 hours, or overnight, until a slice holds its shape.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: After chilling, the filling should feel set and creamy, not loose. You should smell cream cheese, vanilla, and the graham crust. A knife should cut a neat slice.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The filling should look smooth and pale, and a chilled slice should hold a clean edge, not slump.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The filling should look smooth and pale, and a chilled slice should hold a clean edge, not slump.

SAFETY
Soften the cream cheese at room temperature, but do not leave the finished pie out more than 2 hours. Keep it refrigerated. If you bake the crust, use mitts and cool it fully before filling. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Fruit, chocolate sauce, or a little lemon zest are good toppings. Zest is the thin, colorful outer peel of a lemon or other citrus fruit, not the bitter white layer under it. This is lighter than baked cheesecake and is a good make-ahead dessert.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
