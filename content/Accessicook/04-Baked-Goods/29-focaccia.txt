ACCESSICOOK
Focaccia
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A dimpled flatbread with olive oil and salt, baked until the top is golden and the interior is tender. You can start with store-bought pizza dough, or with a simple yeast dough if you are comfortable waiting for it to rise. Rise, or proof, means giving yeast dough time in a warm place so it becomes puffy. Proof means let yeast dough rest until it feels puffy and risen.

SERVINGS
1 pan, about 12 squares

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 30 minutes, including rise time, or less with pizza dough

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds) pizza dough, or homemade dough that has already risen once
- 3 tablespoons olive oil, divided
- 3/4 teaspoon (three-quarters of a teaspoon) coarse salt
- Optional toppings: rosemary, thinly sliced garlic, or olives

TACTILE TIPS
The dough should feel soft, oily, and puffy after it rests in the pan. Your fingertips should leave dimples that stay. The baked bread should feel golden and lightly crisp on top, and tender inside, not wet and raw in the middle.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Oil a 9 by 13 inch pan, or a similar sheet pan, with 1 tablespoon of the oil.

2. Press the dough into the pan.

3. If it shrinks back, wait 10 minutes and try again.

4. Cover and let it rest until it feels puffy, 20 to 40 minutes, or according to the dough you are using. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

5. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

6. Dimple the dough with your fingertips, pressing to the pan in spots but not tearing it.

7. Drizzle the remaining oil over the top.

8. Sprinkle with salt and any optional toppings.

9. Bake 18 to 24 minutes, until the top feels golden and set, and the bottom feels lightly crisp, not soft and pale.

10. Cool 10 minutes, then cut into squares.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel set and lightly crisp. You should smell toasted dough, olive oil, and any herbs, not raw flour or burning oil. The center should feel cooked and tender.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look golden, with darker spots in the dimples. The interior should look open and cooked, not dense and raw.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look golden, with darker spots in the dimples. The interior should look open and cooked, not dense and raw.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. Use mitts. The oiled pan is very hot and can drip. Set it on a stable landing zone. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is good with soup or with the dips in the Misc chapter. Leftovers make sandwiches, or they can be warmed until the center is hot. A little extra oil on the pan helps the bottom crisp.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
