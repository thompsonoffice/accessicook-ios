ACCESSICOOK
Pie Crust
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A basic butter pie dough for the pies in this chapter. Keep the butter cold. Cut in means rub or mash the butter into the flour until you feel coarse crumbs with some pea-size pieces. Those pieces make a flaky crust. This page also covers baking a single crust empty, which is called baking blind, meaning you bake the crust before you add a filling that does not need a long bake.

SERVINGS
One 9 inch double crust, or two single crusts

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes, plus at least 30 minutes of chilling

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 1/2 cups (two and a half cups) all-purpose flour, plus more for rolling
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 tablespoon sugar, optional, for a dessert crust
- 1 cup cold unsalted butter, cut into cubes
- 6 to 8 tablespoons ice water

TACTILE TIPS
The butter should feel cold and firm. After you cut it in, you should still feel small pieces. The dough should feel shaggy, then hold together when pressed, without feeling wet and sticky. After chilling, it should feel firm and rollable, not rock-hard and not sticky. A baked empty crust should feel dry, set, and lightly golden.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the flour, salt, and optional sugar.

2. Cut in the cold butter until the mix feels like coarse crumbs with some pea-size bits.

3. Add ice water 1 tablespoon at a time, stirring, just until the dough holds when you press it.

4. Divide the dough in two.

5. Flatten each piece into a disk.

6. Wrap and refrigerate at least 30 minutes, or up to 2 days.

7. On a floured board, roll a disk from the center out, turning it as you go, until it is about 12 inches across and about 1/8 inch (an eighth of an inch) thick for a 9 inch pan.

8. If it cracks, let it warm 2 or 3 minutes and pinch the crack shut. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

9. Ease the dough into the pan without stretching it.

10. Trim or fold the edges, and crimp, meaning pinch a pattern around the rim. Fold means gently turn the mixture over itself so you do not knock out the air.

11. Chill 15 minutes before you fill or bake.

12. To bake a crust empty, line it with parchment, fill it with dry beans or pie weights, and bake at 375 degrees F for 15 minutes.

13. Remove the weights and parchment, then bake 8 to 12 minutes more, until the crust feels dry and lightly golden.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The dough should feel shaggy, then firm after a chill, and it should roll without sticking if it is well floured and still cool. A baked empty crust should feel dry and set, and you should smell toasted butter and flour, not raw dough or burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: A rolled crust should look even, without big cracks. A baked empty crust should look dry and light golden, not pale and raw, and not dark brown.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. A rolled crust should look even, without big cracks. A baked empty crust should look dry and light golden, not pale and raw, and not dark brown.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. It contains raw flour. Use mitts when you bake. Pie weights and beans will be very hot. Cool them before you store them. A rolling pin and a board can slide, so work on a stable surface, and use a bench scraper or a spatula to lift the dough. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the dough gets warm and sticky, chill it again. You can freeze wrapped disks for a month and thaw them in the refrigerator. Store-bought crust is a fair shortcut when you want a pie without this step.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
