Oatmeal Raisin Cookies
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chewy cookies made with rolled oats and raisins. The oats make the dough thicker than a plain chocolate chip dough, so the cookies spread less.

SERVINGS
About 24 cookies

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
- 1 cup packed brown sugar
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) granulated sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 3 cups rolled oats, not instant
- 1 cup raisins

TACTILE TIPS
Rolled oats feel flaky and dry. Instant oats are finer and can make the cookies mushy, so skip them here.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.

3. Stir the dry ingredients in a bowl until they are combined.

4. In a second bowl, beat the butter and sugar until the mix feels fluffy and even. This is called creaming.

5. Add the eggs and vanilla, if listed.

6. Beat until combined.

7. Add the dry mix.

8. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour.

9. Stir in the oats and raisins.

10. Drop rounded tablespoons of dough onto the sheet, leaving about 2 inches of space.

11. Bake 11 to 13 minutes.

12. Check at the early time. The edges should feel set and the centers should feel soft and slightly moist.

13. Cool on the pan 2 to 3 minutes, then move the cookies to a rack or a plate to finish cooling.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The centers should feel soft and a little moist, not raw. You should smell oats, cinnamon, and toasted sugar.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The edges should look golden. The centers should look set but not dark.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The edges should look golden. The centers should look set but not dark.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. It contains eggs and raw flour. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the raisins are very dry, soak them in warm water 10 minutes, then drain and pat them dry. Drain means pour off the liquid. For oatmeal chocolate chip cookies, swap the raisins for chips.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
