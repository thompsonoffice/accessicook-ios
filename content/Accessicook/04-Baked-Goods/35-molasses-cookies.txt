Molasses Cookies
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Soft, spiced cookies made with molasses, which is a thick, dark syrup. They are rolled in sugar before baking, which gives a slight crunch on the outside and a chewy center.

SERVINGS
About 24 cookies

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes, plus a short chill if the dough is soft

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 1/4 cups (two and a quarter cups) all-purpose flour
- 2 teaspoons baking soda
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- 1 teaspoon ground ginger
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) cloves or allspice, optional
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) unsalted butter, softened
- 1 cup packed brown sugar
- 1 large egg
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) molasses
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) granulated sugar, for rolling

TACTILE TIPS
Molasses feels thick and sticky. A little oil in the measuring cup can help it slide. Softened butter should give like cool clay. The dough should feel soft but shapeable. If it feels sticky, chill it 20 to 30 minutes. Baked cookies should feel set at the edges and slightly soft in the centers.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.

3. Stir the dry ingredients in a bowl until they are combined.

4. In a second bowl, beat the butter and sugar until the mix feels fluffy and even. This is called creaming.

5. Add the eggs and vanilla, if listed.

6. Beat until combined.

7. Add the dry mix.

8. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour.

9. Stir in the molasses with the eggs and vanilla if listed, then proceed, and roll the balls in the granulated sugar before they go on the sheet.

10. Roll the dough into 1 inch balls.

11. Place them on the sheet with space between them.

12. Bake 10 to 12 minutes.

13. Check at the early time. The edges should feel set and the centers should feel slightly soft. The tops may look crackled.

14. Cool on the pan 2 to 3 minutes, then move the cookies to a rack or a plate to finish cooling.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The centers should feel soft, not wet with raw dough. You should smell ginger, molasses, and toasted sugar, not raw flour or burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cookies should look crackled on top and slightly darker at the edges. The centers may look a little puffy and will flatten as they cool.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cookies should look crackled on top and slightly darker at the edges. The centers may look a little puffy and will flatten as they cool.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. It contains egg and raw flour. Use mitts. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a crisper cookie, bake 1 to 2 minutes longer. These keep well in a closed container for several days.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
