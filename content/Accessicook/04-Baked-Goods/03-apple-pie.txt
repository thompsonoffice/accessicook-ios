ACCESSICOOK
Apple Pie
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Sliced apples baked in a double crust until the fruit is tender and the juices are bubbling. Use a store-bought crust if you want to skip making dough, or see Pie Crust in this chapter. Toss the apples with sugar and spice, then bake until you can hear or see the filling bubble, and a knife slides easily into an apple slice.

SERVINGS
8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 30 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 pie crusts, store-bought or homemade, for a 9 inch pie
- 6 cups peeled, sliced apples, about 2 to 2 1/2 pounds (two to two and a half pounds)
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) sugar
- 2 tablespoons flour, or 1 tablespoon cornstarch
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- 1 tablespoon butter, cut into small pieces
- 1 tablespoon milk, for brushing the top, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Apple slices should be about 1/4 inch (a quarter inch) thick, and even, so they cook at the same rate. After baking, a knife or skewer should slide into the apples easily, and the juices should feel thick, not watery, once the pie cools. The crust should feel golden and set, not pale and soft.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Place a baking sheet on a lower rack to catch drips.

3. Toss the apples with the sugar, flour or cornstarch, cinnamon, salt, and lemon juice.

4. Fit one crust into a 9 inch pie pan.

5. Add the apples and dot them with the butter.

6. Set the second crust on top.

7. Trim the edges, pinch them together, and cut 3 or 4 slits in the top so steam can escape. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

8. Brush the top with milk if you like.

9. Bake 20 minutes, then reduce the oven to 375 degrees F and bake 30 to 40 minutes more, until the crust feels golden and set, and the filling is bubbling through the slits. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens.

10. If the edges brown too fast, cover them with a ring of foil.

11. Cool at least 2 hours before you slice, so the juices thicken.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The crust should feel golden and set. You should hear or sense bubbling under the top crust near the end of baking. A knife should slide into the apples easily. You should smell cooked apple, cinnamon, and toasted crust, not raw dough or burning sugar.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The crust should look golden brown, not pale. Filling should bubble visibly through the slits near the end. After cooling, a slice should hold together, with tender, not raw, apples.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The crust should look golden brown, not pale. Filling should bubble visibly through the slits near the end. After cooling, a slice should hold together, with tender, not raw, apples.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a cut-resistant glove if you peel and slice many apples. Use mitts. The filling is extremely hot, like molten sugar. Cool before you slice. Set the pie on a sheet pan to catch drips, and keep foil away from the oven wall. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A mix of tart and sweet apples tastes better than one kind alone. If the bottom crust stays pale, you can start the pie on the lower rack. Leftovers keep, covered, at room temperature 1 day, or refrigerated 3 to 4 days. Reheat slices until the filling is steaming hot if you want them warm.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
