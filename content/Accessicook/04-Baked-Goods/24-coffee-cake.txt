ACCESSICOOK
Coffee Cake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A tender breakfast cake with a cinnamon crumb topping, meant to be eaten with coffee, not made from coffee. The topping is a mix of butter, sugar, flour, and cinnamon that bakes into a crunchy layer.

SERVINGS
9 to 12 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 55 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) baking soda
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) unsalted butter, softened
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 cup sour cream or plain yogurt
- For the topping: 1/2 cup (half a cup) packed brown sugar, 1/2 cup (half a cup) flour, 1 teaspoon cinnamon, and 4 tablespoons cold butter, cut into cubes

TACTILE TIPS
The batter should feel thick. The topping should feel like damp crumbs, with some pea-size bits of butter. A toothpick in the cake should come out with a few crumbs, not wet batter. The topping should feel crisp.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease a 9 inch square pan, or a similar 9 inch pan.

3. Mix the topping: brown sugar, flour, and cinnamon, then cut in the cold butter until the mix feels like damp crumbs. Cut in means rub or mash the butter into the dry mix.

4. Set this aside.

5. Mix the 2 cups flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt in a bowl.

6. Beat the softened butter and sugar until fluffy.

7. Add the eggs and vanilla, then the sour cream, and mix until even.

8. Add the flour mix and stir just until you no longer feel dry flour.

9. Spread the batter in the pan.

10. Sprinkle the topping evenly over it.

11. Bake 30 to 40 minutes, until a toothpick in the center comes out with a few crumbs, the cake feels set, and the topping feels crisp.

12. Cool at least 15 minutes before you cut.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The cake should feel set and springy under the topping. The topping should feel crisp, not wet and sandy. You should smell cinnamon, butter, and toasted sugar, not raw flour.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The topping should look golden and crisp. A toothpick should come out with a few crumbs, not wet batter. The cake should look risen to the topping, not sunken.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The topping should look golden and crisp. A toothpick should come out with a few crumbs, not wet batter. The cake should look risen to the topping, not sunken.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. The sugar topping is very hot when the cake first comes out. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Chopped pecans can go in the topping if no one has a nut allergy. This is good slightly warm or the next morning, covered once cool.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
