Blueberry Scones
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Cream scones with blueberries folded in. Frozen berries work if you add them still frozen, so they do not bleed as much. Fold means turn the dough over itself gently so the berries stay whole.

SERVINGS
8 scones

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for the board
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) sugar
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 6 tablespoons cold unsalted butter, cut into cubes
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) heavy cream, plus 1 tablespoon for brushing
- 1 cup blueberries, fresh or frozen

TACTILE TIPS
The dough should feel soft and a little dry, just holding together. Frozen berries will make it colder and patchier, which is all right. Baked scones should feel set at the edges, and the berries should feel hot and soft.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment.

3. Mix the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt.

4. Cut in the cold butter until the mix feels like coarse crumbs. Cut in means rub or mash the butter into the flour.

5. Add the cream and stir just until a dough starts to form.

6. Gently fold in the blueberries.

7. Pat the dough into a circle about 3/4 inch (three-quarters of an inch) thick on a floured board.

8. Cut into 8 wedges.

9. Set them on the sheet.

10. Brush the tops with cream.

11. Bake 16 to 20 minutes, until the edges feel set and lightly crisp, and the bottoms feel golden.

12. Cool 10 minutes. The berries will be very hot at first.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set and lightly crisp. You should smell berries, butter, and toasted sugar, not raw dough. The centers should feel cooked. Berries will be very hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The scones should look light golden, with a few darkened berry spots. The insides should look cooked, not wet, even if berry juice has stained some of the crumb purple or blue.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The scones should look light golden, with a few darkened berry spots. The insides should look cooked, not wet, even if berry juice has stained some of the crumb purple or blue.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. Use mitts. Hot berry pockets can burn your mouth, so cool at least 10 minutes. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A simple glaze of powdered sugar and a little milk or lemon juice can be spooned on after the scones cool. Do not thaw frozen berries first.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
