ACCESSICOOK
Simple Sandwich Bread
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A basic loaf of yeast bread for sandwiches and toast. Yeast is a living ingredient that makes dough rise, meaning the dough becomes puffy as the yeast produces gas. Knead means press, fold, and turn the dough to develop gluten, which is the structure that lets bread stretch and hold its shape. Fold means gently turn the mixture over itself so you do not knock out the air. This takes time, but the steps are simple.

SERVINGS
1 loaf

TOTAL TIME
About 3 hours, including rising time

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 3 cups all-purpose or bread flour, plus more for kneading
- 1 packet (2 1/4 teaspoons, or two and a quarter teaspoons) instant or active dry yeast
- 1 1/2 teaspoons (one and a half teaspoons) salt
- 1 tablespoon sugar or honey
- 1 cup warm water, about 105 to 110 degrees F, plus 2 tablespoons more if the dough is dry
- 2 tablespoons oil or melted butter, plus more for the bowl and pan

TACTILE TIPS
The water should feel warm, like a comfortable bath, not hot enough to hurt your finger. Dough that is too hot can harm the yeast. After kneading, the dough should feel smooth and elastic, slightly tacky but not wet. After rising, it should feel puffy and hold a dent that springs back slowly. The baked loaf should feel firm, and a thermometer in the center should read about 190 to 200 degrees F, or the loaf should sound hollow when you tap the bottom with a knuckle, using a mitt.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the flour, yeast, salt, and sugar in a bowl.

2. If you are using active dry yeast and the package asks you to dissolve it in water first, follow that, then add it with the water.

3. Add the warm water and the oil or butter.

4. Stir until a shaggy dough forms.

5. Knead on a lightly floured board 8 to 10 minutes, or in a mixer with a dough hook, until the dough feels smooth and elastic.

6. Add flour only a tablespoon at a time if it is too sticky to handle.

7. Place the dough in a lightly oiled bowl, and turn it so it is coated.

8. Cover the bowl.

9. Let it rise in a warm, draft-free place until it feels doubled and puffy, about 1 hour.

10. Press the air out gently.

11. Shape a loaf and set it in a greased loaf pan.

12. Cover and let it rise again until the dough feels puffy and crowns slightly over the pan, 30 to 45 minutes.

13. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F during the last 15 minutes.

14. Bake 30 to 40 minutes, until the crust feels firm and golden, and the center is about 190 to 200 degrees F, or the loaf sounds hollow when tapped on the bottom with a mitt on your hand.

15. Cool in the pan 10 minutes, then turn the loaf out and cool completely before you slice, or the crumb can compress.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The crust should feel firm. The center should read about 190 to 200 degrees F, or the loaf should sound hollow when the bottom is tapped. You should smell toasted bread, not raw dough or burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The loaf should look evenly golden, not pale and not burnt. The interior should look even and cooked, not doughy in the center.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The loaf should look evenly golden, not pale and not burnt. The interior should look even and cooked, not doughy in the center.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. Use mitts. The pan and loaf are very hot. Cool completely before you slice with a serrated knife, using a slow sawing motion. Water for the yeast should not be hotter than about 120 degrees F, or the yeast can die. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A thermometer takes the guesswork out of doneness and also helps you check the water temperature. If the loaf browns too fast, tent it with foil, meaning rest a sheet of foil lightly on top. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat. Store cooled bread wrapped at room temperature 2 to 3 days, or freeze slices.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
