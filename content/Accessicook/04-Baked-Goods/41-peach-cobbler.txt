Peach Cobbler
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Baked peaches with a biscuit-like topping. Cobbler means fruit baked with dollops of dough that bake up like cobblestones, not a rolled pie crust. Fresh or frozen peaches both work. If you use frozen fruit, do not thaw it fully, and add a few extra minutes.

SERVINGS
6 to 8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 55 minutes, plus a short cooling time

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 6 cups sliced peaches, fresh or frozen, or two large cans of peaches, drained
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) sugar, for the fruit, plus 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- 1 tablespoon cornstarch or flour
- 1 cup all-purpose flour, for the topping
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) sugar, for the topping
- 1 1/2 teaspoons (one and a half teaspoons) baking powder
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 4 tablespoons cold unsalted butter, cut into cubes
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) milk

TACTILE TIPS
The topping dough should feel soft and droppable, like a thick biscuit dough. After baking, the peaches should feel tender, the juices should be bubbling, and the topping should feel set and golden, not wet and raw in the center of a biscuit.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease a 2 quart baking dish.

3. Toss the peaches with 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) sugar, the lemon juice, and the cornstarch or flour.

4. Spread them in the dish.

5. Mix the flour, 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) sugar, baking powder, and salt.

6. Cut in the butter until the mix feels like coarse crumbs. Cut in means rub or mash the butter into the flour.

7. Add the milk and stir just until a soft dough forms.

8. Drop spoonfuls of dough over the fruit.

9. It does not need to cover every gap.

10. Bake 35 to 45 minutes, until the topping feels set and golden, and the fruit is bubbling at the edges.

11. Cool at least 15 minutes before you serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The topping should feel set and cooked in the center of a biscuit, not doughy. The fruit should feel tender. You should smell peaches, butter, and toasted dough, and you should hear bubbling.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The topping should look golden, and the juices should look bubbly and slightly thick at the edges. The peaches should look soft, not stiff.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The topping should look golden, and the juices should look bubbly and slightly thick at the edges. The peaches should look soft, not stiff.

SAFETY
Use mitts. The fruit filling is extremely hot, like molten sugar. Cool at least 15 minutes. Do not taste raw dough. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A sprinkle of cinnamon in the fruit is a good extra. Serve with ice cream. Other stone fruit, such as nectarines or plums, can be used the same way.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
