Shortbread Cookies
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple butter cookie with only a few ingredients. Shortbread is meant to be tender and sandy, not puffy.

SERVINGS
About 24 cookies

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes, plus brief chilling if the dough is soft

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 cup unsalted butter, softened

TACTILE TIPS
The dough should feel like damp sand that holds together when you squeeze it.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 325 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.

3. Mix the flour, sugar, and salt.

4. Cut in the soft butter until the dough feels like damp crumbs and holds together when pressed. Cut in means rub or mash the butter into the flour.

5. Press or roll the dough as the tips describe, then cut or shape the cookies.

6. Shape the dough into a log.

7. Wrap it and chill it if the dough feels soft.

8. Cut the log into slices about 1/4 inch (a quarter inch) thick.

9. Lay them on the sheet.

10. Bake 18 to 22 minutes.

11. Check at the early time. The edges should feel dry and just barely firm.

12. Shortbread stays pale.

13. Cool on the pan 2 to 3 minutes, then move the cookies to a rack or a plate to finish cooling.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set and dry. The cookies should smell like butter and toast, not raw flour.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The edges should look faintly golden. The tops should stay pale.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The edges should look faintly golden. The tops should stay pale.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. It contains raw flour. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Prick each cookie with a fork before baking if you like. Store in a tin so they stay crisp.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
