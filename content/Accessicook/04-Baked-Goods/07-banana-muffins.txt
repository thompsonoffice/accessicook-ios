Banana Muffins
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Ripe bananas make these muffins moist and naturally sweet. Use bananas that feel very soft and smell fragrant. This is a good use for bananas that are too soft to eat out of hand.

SERVINGS
12 muffins

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) all-purpose flour
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) sugar
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) cinnamon, optional
- 3 very ripe bananas, mashed, about 1 1/4 cups (one and a quarter cups)
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) oil or melted butter
- 1 large egg
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

TACTILE TIPS
Ripe bananas should feel very soft and may have brown spots on the peel. The mashed fruit should feel almost like a thick puree. The baked muffins should feel springy on top.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease a muffin pan, or line it with paper cups.

3. Mix the dry ingredients in a bowl.

4. Mix the wet ingredients in a second bowl.

5. Pour the wet mix into the dry mix.

6. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour. A few lumps are fine.

7. Spoon the batter into the cups, filling each about two-thirds full.

8. Bake 18 to 22 minutes.

9. A toothpick in the center of a muffin should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The tops should feel set and springy.

10. Cool in the pan 5 minutes, then move the muffins out so they do not steam and get soggy.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tops should feel set and springy. You should smell banana and toasted sugar, not raw flour. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tops should look risen and golden. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tops should look risen and golden. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
One cup of chocolate chips or chopped walnuts can be folded in with the batter. If your bananas are not very ripe, the muffins will taste less sweet and a little starchy.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
