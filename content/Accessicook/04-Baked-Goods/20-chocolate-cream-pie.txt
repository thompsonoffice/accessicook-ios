Chocolate Cream Pie
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A pie with a cooked chocolate pudding filling in a prebaked crust, finished with whipped cream. The pudding must come to a bubble and thicken on the stove so the eggs cook and the filling sets when it chills.

SERVINGS
8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes, plus several hours of chilling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner to intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pie crust, baked and cooled, or a graham-cracker crust
- 2/3 cup (two-thirds of a cup) sugar
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) cornstarch
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) unsweetened cocoa powder, or 4 ounces chopped semisweet chocolate added at the end
- 2 1/2 cups (two and a half cups) milk
- 3 large egg yolks
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- Whipped cream, for topping, from the Misc chapter or store-bought

TACTILE TIPS
The pudding should feel smooth and thick enough to coat a spoon, and it should bubble slowly as you stir. After a full chill, a slice should feel set and creamy, not runny. Lumps can often be stirred or strained out while the filling is still hot.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the sugar, cornstarch, salt, and cocoa in a saucepan.

2. If you are using chopped chocolate instead of cocoa, mix only the sugar, cornstarch, and salt now.

3. Whisk in the milk and egg yolks until the mix feels smooth. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk so there are no lumps.

4. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until the mix thickens and large, slow bubbles break the surface.

5. Let it bubble 1 minute while you stir.

6. Take the pan off the heat.

7. Stir in the butter, vanilla, and chopped chocolate if you are using it, until the filling feels smooth.

8. Pour the filling into the cooled crust.

9. Press plastic wrap or a piece of buttered parchment onto the surface if you want to reduce a skin. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens.

10. Cool, then refrigerate at least 4 hours, until a slice holds its shape.

11. Top with whipped cream just before serving.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The filling should feel thick, glossy, and smooth, and you should see or feel slow bubbles as it finishes. You should smell chocolate and cooked milk, not scorched milk or raw egg. After chilling, a slice should feel set.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The filling should look thick, glossy, and smooth in the pan, and a chilled slice should hold a clean edge, not puddle.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The filling should look thick, glossy, and smooth in the pan, and a chilled slice should hold a clean edge, not puddle.

SAFETY
Do not taste the filling until it has boiled and then cooled. Stir constantly so it does not scorch. The pan and filling are very hot. Cool, then refrigerate within 2 hours. Keep the pie refrigerated. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A graham crust is easier than a pastry crust here. If you get lumps, pour the hot filling through a strainer. Leftover pie keeps 3 days in the refrigerator.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
