ACCESSICOOK
Pound Cake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A dense, buttery loaf cake. The name comes from an older formula of a pound each of butter, sugar, eggs, and flour. This is a slightly smaller home version. It keeps well and is good plain, toasted, or with fruit and whipped cream.

SERVINGS
1 loaf, about 10 slices

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 30 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
- 1 cup sugar
- 4 large eggs
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract

TACTILE TIPS
Softened butter should give like cool clay, not feel melted. The batter will feel thick. A toothpick in the center of the baked cake should come out clean or with a few crumbs, and the top should feel set and slightly firm.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 325 degrees F.

2. Grease a loaf pan and line it with parchment if you have it.

3. Mix the flour, baking powder, and salt.

4. Beat the butter and sugar until the mix feels very fluffy and pale. This is called creaming, and it matters in this cake.

5. Add the eggs one at a time, beating after each, then add the vanilla.

6. Add the flour mix and stir just until you no longer feel dry flour.

7. Spread the batter in the pan and smooth the top.

8. Bake 60 to 75 minutes, until a toothpick in the center comes out clean or with a few crumbs, and the top feels set.

9. If the top browns too fast, tent it with foil, meaning rest a sheet of foil lightly on top. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

10. Cool in the pan 15 minutes, then turn out and cool completely before you slice.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel set. A toothpick in the center should come out clean or with a few crumbs. You should smell butter and vanilla, not raw batter. The cake should feel firm enough to slice after it cools.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look golden and may have a lengthwise crack, which is common. A toothpick should come out clean or with a few crumbs. The crumb should look tight and even, not wet.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look golden and may have a lengthwise crack, which is common. A toothpick should come out clean or with a few crumbs. The crumb should look tight and even, not wet.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Cool before you slice, or the crumb can compress and feel gummy. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little lemon zest in the batter is a good change. Zest is the thin, colorful outer peel of a lemon or other citrus fruit, not the bitter white layer under it. This cake tastes even better the next day. Toast leftover slices lightly.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
