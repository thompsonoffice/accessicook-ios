Lemon Loaf
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A tender lemon loaf, sometimes finished with a simple lemon glaze, meaning a thin icing of sugar and lemon juice that sets as it cools. Zest is the fragrant yellow outer skin of the lemon, not the bitter white pith underneath.

SERVINGS
1 loaf, about 10 slices

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) unsalted butter, softened
- 1 cup sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) milk
- 2 tablespoons lemon zest, from about 2 lemons
- 3 tablespoons lemon juice, divided, plus 1/2 cup (half a cup) powdered sugar for an optional glaze

TACTILE TIPS
Zest should feel like fine, dry, fragrant shreds, not wet white pith. Softened butter should give like cool clay. The baked loaf should feel springy, and a toothpick in the center should come out with a few crumbs.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease a loaf pan, or line it with parchment.

3. Mix the dry ingredients in a bowl.

4. Mix the wet ingredients in a second bowl, including any mashed fruit.

5. Add the wet mix to the dry mix.

6. Stir just until combined.

7. Stir in the lemon zest and 2 tablespoons of the lemon juice.

8. Spread the batter in the pan.

9. Bake 45 to 55 minutes.

10. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few crumbs, not wet batter. The top should feel set and springy.

11. Cool in the pan 10 minutes, then turn the loaf out to finish cooling.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel set. You should smell bright lemon and butter, not raw flour. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few crumbs.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look light golden, not dark. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few crumbs. The glaze, if used, should look thin and set, not wet and runny.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look light golden, not dark. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few crumbs. The glaze, if used, should look thin and set, not wet and runny.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. A Microplane or zester is sharp. Work slowly and stop when you feel the pith, which is paler and bitter. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a glaze, mix 1/2 cup (half a cup) powdered sugar with 1 to 2 tablespoons lemon juice until it feels thick but pourable, then spoon it over the cooled loaf. This loaf is even better the next day.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
