Chocolate Cake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A moist chocolate cake made with cocoa powder. The batter is often a little thinner than yellow cake batter. Cool the cake completely before you frost it. See Chocolate Frosting in the Misc chapter.

SERVINGS
One 9 inch layer cake, or a 9 by 13 inch sheet, about 12 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 3/4 cups (one and three-quarters cups) all-purpose flour
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) unsweetened cocoa powder
- 1 1/2 teaspoons (one and a half teaspoons) baking soda
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) baking powder
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 2 cups sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 1 cup milk
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) oil
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
- 1 cup hot water or hot coffee

TACTILE TIPS
Cocoa powder feels dry and fine and can puff into the air, so add it slowly. The batter will feel thinner after you add the hot liquid. A toothpick in the baked cake should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease two 9 inch pans or one 9 by 13 inch pan.

3. Mix the flour, cocoa, baking soda, baking powder, salt, and sugar in a bowl.

4. Add the eggs, milk, oil, and vanilla.

5. Beat until the mix feels even.

6. Add the hot water or coffee carefully, and stir until the batter feels smooth and thin.

7. Pour the batter into the pans.

8. Bake 30 to 38 minutes, until a toothpick in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs, and the top feels springy.

9. Cool in the pans 10 minutes, then turn out and cool completely before frosting.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel springy. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs. You should smell chocolate and toasted sugar, not raw flour or burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tops should look set and slightly pulled from the sides. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The crumb should look even and moist, not wet and sunken.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tops should look set and slightly pulled from the sides. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The crumb should look even and moist, not wet and sunken.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Hot water or coffee can splash. Add it slowly. Cool completely before frosting. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Coffee deepens the chocolate flavor and will not make the cake taste like coffee. This cake stays moist for a few days if it is covered.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
