ACCESSICOOK
Monkey Bread
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Pull-apart pieces of dough coated in butter, sugar, and cinnamon, baked in a bundt or tube pan. This version uses refrigerated biscuit dough to keep the method short. The pieces should be cooked in the center, not doughy, and the caramel-like coating will be extremely hot at first.

SERVINGS
8 to 10 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour with refrigerated biscuit dough

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cans refrigerated biscuit dough, 16 ounces each, or the equivalent
- 1 cup packed brown sugar
- 2 teaspoons cinnamon
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) unsalted butter, melted
- Optional glaze: 1 cup powdered sugar mixed with 2 tablespoons milk

TACTILE TIPS
Each biscuit piece should feel coated, not dry. After baking, a center piece should feel cooked and light, not doughy and wet. The coating should feel sticky and set, not liquid, after a short cool.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease a bundt pan or a tube pan very well, including the tube.

3. Mix the brown sugar and cinnamon in a bowl.

4. Cut each biscuit into 4 pieces.

5. Dip the pieces in the melted butter, then toss them in the cinnamon sugar, and layer them in the pan.

6. Drizzle any leftover butter and sugar over the top.

7. Bake 30 to 40 minutes, until the top feels set and golden, and a center piece feels cooked, not doughy.

8. If the top browns too fast, tent it with foil, meaning rest a sheet of foil lightly on top. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

9. Cool in the pan 10 minutes, then turn the bread out onto a plate.

10. Cool 10 minutes more before you pull it apart.

11. Add glaze if you like, after it is not piping hot.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel set. A center piece should feel cooked, not doughy. You should smell cinnamon, butter, and toasted sugar, not raw dough or burning sugar. The coating will be extremely hot at first.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The bread should look deeply golden, with a sticky, caramel-colored coating. A center piece should look cooked and fluffy, not raw and pale.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The bread should look deeply golden, with a sticky, caramel-colored coating. A center piece should look cooked and fluffy, not raw and pale.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. Use mitts. The sugar coating is like molten caramel and can cause a serious burn. Cool at least 10 minutes in the pan, then 10 minutes more after turning out, before you pull pieces apart. A bundt pan is heavy and awkward. Use a stable landing zone. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If a piece in the very center is still doughy, return the bread to the pan and bake 5 to 10 minutes more. Leftovers reheat at 300 degrees F until warm.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
