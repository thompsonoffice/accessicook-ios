Baked Cheesecake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A creamy baked cheesecake on a graham-cracker crust. The filling is mostly cream cheese, sugar, and eggs, and it must reach a set, not-liquid center. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F, but a cheesecake is also judged by a slight jiggle in the very center, which finishes setting as it chills. Chill several hours before you slice it.

SERVINGS
12 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 30 minutes, plus several hours of chilling

DIFFICULTY
Intermediate

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) graham-cracker crumbs
- 3 tablespoons sugar, for the crust
- 5 tablespoons melted butter
- 24 ounces cream cheese, softened
- 1 cup sugar, for the filling
- 3 large eggs
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sour cream, optional, for a slightly tangier filling

TACTILE TIPS
Softened cream cheese should feel smooth and spreadable, not cold and lumpy. After baking, the edges should feel set, and the very center should jiggle slightly like firm gelatin, not like liquid. After a full chill, a slice should feel creamy and hold its shape.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 325 degrees F.

2. Mix the crumbs, 3 tablespoons sugar, and melted butter until the mix feels like damp sand.

3. Press it firmly into the bottom of a 9 inch springform pan, or a deep 9 inch cake pan lined with parchment.

4. Bake the crust 8 to 10 minutes, then cool slightly.

5. Beat the cream cheese and 1 cup sugar until the mix feels smooth, with no lumps.

6. Add the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla and optional sour cream, and mix just until even.

7. Too much beating can add air and cause cracks.

8. Pour the filling over the crust.

9. Bake 45 to 60 minutes, until the edges feel set and the center still jiggles slightly. The surface should not look wet and liquid.

10. If you use a thermometer, the center should be near 150 to 160 degrees F.

11. Turn off the oven.

12. Crack the door and let the cake sit 30 minutes, then cool on a rack.

13. Refrigerate at least 4 hours, or overnight, before you release the pan and slice.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The center should jiggle slightly, not slosh. You should smell baked dairy, vanilla, and toasted crust, not raw egg or burning. After chilling, a slice should feel creamy and firm enough to cut.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look mostly set, maybe with a small pale center, and not browned like a cake. A little crack can happen and is all right. After chilling, a slice should look creamy and hold its shape, not slump into a puddle.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look mostly set, maybe with a small pale center, and not browned like a cake. A little crack can happen and is all right. After chilling, a slice should look creamy and hold its shape, not slump into a puddle.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw filling. It contains raw eggs. Use mitts. A springform pan can leak, so set it on a sheet pan. Cool, then refrigerate within 2 hours. Keep the cake refrigerated, and do not leave slices out more than 2 hours. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Run a thin knife around the edge after baking to reduce cracking as it cools. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens. A pan of hot water on a lower rack can help the cake bake more gently. Serve plain or with fruit.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
