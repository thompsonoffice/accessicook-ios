Cinnamon Rolls
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Soft cinnamon rolls made with store-bought dough, such as pizza dough or a refrigerated roll dough, so you can skip yeast work on a first try. If your package includes its own directions, follow those times, then use the doneness cues here. A filling of butter, sugar, and cinnamon is spread on the rolled dough, then the dough is rolled up and sliced.

SERVINGS
8 to 12 rolls

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes with store-bought dough, or longer if the package includes a rise

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pound pizza dough or 1 can refrigerated roll dough, at room temperature if it was chilled, unless the package says otherwise
- 3 tablespoons unsalted butter, softened, plus more for the pan
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) packed brown sugar
- 1 1/2 tablespoons (one and a half tablespoons) cinnamon
- Optional glaze: 1 cup powdered sugar mixed with 2 tablespoons milk and 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) vanilla extract

TACTILE TIPS
Softened butter should spread easily. The dough should feel elastic and not ice-cold. The baked rolls should feel set in the center, not doughy, and the filling should feel hot and melted, not wet with raw dough underneath.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F, or to the temperature on the dough package if it is different.

2. Grease a baking pan.

3. Mix the brown sugar and cinnamon in a small bowl.

4. On a lightly floured board, roll or pat the dough into a rectangle about 1/4 inch (a quarter inch) thick.

5. Spread the softened butter over the dough.

6. Sprinkle the cinnamon sugar evenly on top.

7. Roll the dough up from a long side into a log.

8. Pinch the seam shut. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

9. Cut the log into 8 to 12 even slices.

10. Set them cut-side up in the pan, close but not tightly jammed.

11. Bake 18 to 25 minutes, until the tops feel set and golden, and the center roll feels cooked, not doughy.

12. Cool 10 minutes.

13. If you want a glaze, stir the powdered sugar, milk, and vanilla until smooth, then spoon it over the warm rolls.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tops should feel set. The center of a middle roll should feel cooked and hot, not doughy. You should smell cinnamon, butter, and toasted dough, not raw flour. The pan and filling will be very hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The rolls should look puffed and golden, with melted cinnamon sugar at the edges. The center of a middle roll should look cooked, not raw and pale.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The rolls should look puffed and golden, with melted cinnamon sugar at the edges. The center of a middle roll should look cooked, not raw and pale.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. Use mitts. A knife for slicing the log should be used slowly on a stable board. Sugar filling is extremely hot when the rolls first come out. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you use a yeast dough that needs to rise, let the shaped rolls rise until they feel puffy before you bake, following the package. Cream cheese frosting can replace the simple glaze. Leftovers reheat at 300 degrees F until warm, or briefly in the microwave, then eat at once.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
