ACCESSICOOK
Brownies
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A pan of dense chocolate brownies. This version is fudgy in the center, not cakey. Cool them before you cut so the squares stay neat.

SERVINGS
16 brownies

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) unsalted butter
- 8 ounces semisweet chocolate, chopped, or 1 1/3 cups chips
- 1 cup sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) all-purpose flour
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt

TACTILE TIPS
Melted chocolate feels smooth and slightly thick. The batter should feel glossy and heavy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease the pan, or line it with parchment, leaving a little overhang as a handle.

3. Melt the butter and chocolate in a pot over low heat, or in a microwave-safe bowl in short bursts, stirring until smooth.

4. Let it cool 5 minutes.

5. Stir in the sugar, then the eggs and vanilla.

6. Stir in the flour and salt just until combined.

7. Spread the batter in a greased 8 inch square pan.

8. Spread the batter or dough evenly in the pan.

9. Bake 22 to 28 minutes.

10. A toothpick in the center should come out with moist crumbs, not wet batter.

11. Cool in the pan before you cut, unless the recipe tips say otherwise.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The center should feel soft but not liquid when you press it lightly. A toothpick should come out with moist crumbs, not wet batter. You should smell deep chocolate, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look dry, shiny, and slightly crackled. The edges should look set and a little pulled from the pan.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look dry, shiny, and slightly crackled. The edges should look set and a little pulled from the pan.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Melted chocolate and the pan are hot. Cool before cutting. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For nut brownies, stir in 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) chopped walnuts. For cakier brownies, add an extra 2 tablespoons flour.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
