Peanut Butter Cookies
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Simple peanut butter cookies with the classic fork crisscross on top. The pattern is not just decoration. It helps these dense cookies bake evenly.

SERVINGS
About 24 cookies

TOTAL TIME
About 25 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup creamy peanut butter
- 1 cup sugar
- 1 large egg
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- A pinch of salt, if the peanut butter is unsalted

TACTILE TIPS
The dough should feel soft and slightly oily, and it should hold a ball shape.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.

3. Mix the peanut butter, sugar, egg, and vanilla until even.

4. Roll the dough into 1 inch balls.

5. Place them on the sheet with space between them.

6. Press a fork on each ball to make a crisscross pattern.

7. Bake 9 to 11 minutes.

8. Check at the early time. The edges should feel set. The centers should still feel soft.

9. Cool on the pan 2 to 3 minutes, then move the cookies to a rack or a plate to finish cooling.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The centers should feel soft and will firm as they cool. You should smell roasted peanuts and sugar, not burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cookies should look dry on top, with a pale golden edge, and the fork marks should still be visible.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cookies should look dry on top, with a pale golden edge, and the fork marks should still be visible.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. It contains egg. If you cook for someone with a peanut allergy, skip this recipe entirely and keep it away from other baking. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a slightly thicker cookie, add 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) flour. Crunchy peanut butter works too and adds texture you can feel.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
