ACCESSICOOK
Cornbread
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A skillet or pan cornbread with a tender crumb and a slightly crisp edge. This version is lightly sweet. Serve with chili, soup, or a pat of butter. See also Corn Muffins in this chapter.

SERVINGS
9 squares

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup all-purpose flour
- 1 cup yellow cornmeal
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) sugar
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 cup milk
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) melted butter or oil
- 1 large egg

TACTILE TIPS
Cornmeal feels sandy. The batter should feel pourable, thicker than pancake batter but looser than muffin batter. The baked bread should feel set in the center, and a toothpick should come out clean or with a few crumbs.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Grease an 8 inch square pan, or a 9 inch skillet that can go in the oven.

3. Mix the flour, cornmeal, sugar, baking powder, and salt in a bowl.

4. Mix the milk, melted butter or oil, and egg in a second bowl.

5. Pour the wet mix into the dry mix.

6. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour. A few lumps are fine.

7. Spread the batter in the pan.

8. Bake 18 to 24 minutes, until a toothpick in the center comes out clean or with a few crumbs, and the top feels set.

9. Cool 10 minutes, then cut into squares.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel set and springy. You should smell toasted corn and butter, not raw meal. A toothpick in the center should come out clean or with a few crumbs.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look golden, and the edges should look slightly darker and pulled from the pan a little. A toothpick should come out clean or with a few crumbs.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look golden, and the edges should look slightly darker and pulled from the pan a little. A toothpick should come out clean or with a few crumbs.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. If you use a skillet, the handle will be extremely hot. Use a handle cover or a mitt every time you touch it. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a crispier edge, heat the greased skillet in the oven for 5 minutes, then pour in the batter. It will sizzle. For a less sweet bread, cut the sugar to 1 tablespoon. Leftovers toast well the next day.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
