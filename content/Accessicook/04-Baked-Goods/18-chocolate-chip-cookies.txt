ACCESSICOOK
Chocolate Chip Cookies
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A classic drop cookie with brown sugar and chocolate chips. The edges set first, and the centers stay a little soft, then firm as they cool.

SERVINGS
About 24 cookies

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 1/4 cups (two and a quarter cups) all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) granulated sugar
- 3/4 cup packed brown sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
- 2 cups chocolate chips

TACTILE TIPS
Softened butter should give easily when pressed, like cool clay, not like a hard block. Packed brown sugar should hold the shape of the cup.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.

3. Stir the dry ingredients in a bowl until they are combined.

4. In a second bowl, beat the butter and sugar until the mix feels fluffy and even. This is called creaming.

5. Add the eggs and vanilla, if listed.

6. Beat until combined.

7. Add the dry mix.

8. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour.

9. Stir in the chocolate chips.

10. Drop rounded tablespoons of dough onto the sheet, leaving about 2 inches of space.

11. Bake 10 to 12 minutes.

12. Check at the early time. The edges should feel set and the centers should feel soft.

13. They will firm as they cool.

14. Cool on the pan 2 to 3 minutes, then move the cookies to a rack or a plate to finish cooling.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The centers should feel soft, not wet with raw dough. You should smell toasted sugar and chocolate, not raw flour or burning.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The edges should look light golden. The centers may still look pale and slightly puffy. That is all right.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The edges should look light golden. The centers may still look pale and slightly puffy. That is all right.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. It contains eggs and raw flour. Use mitts on the hot pan. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For thicker cookies, chill the dough 30 minutes. For crisper cookies, bake 1 to 2 minutes longer. Leave space. They spread.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
