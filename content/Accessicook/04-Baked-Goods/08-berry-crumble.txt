Berry Crumble
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Mixed berries baked under a simple crumb topping. Frozen berries work well and do not need to be thawed. A little cornstarch helps the juices thicken.

SERVINGS
6 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 45 minutes, plus a short cooling time

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 5 cups mixed berries, fresh or frozen
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) sugar, plus more to taste if the berries are very tart
- 2 tablespoons cornstarch
- 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) packed brown sugar
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) rolled oats, optional
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) cold unsalted butter, cut into cubes
- A pinch of salt

TACTILE TIPS
The topping should feel like damp crumbs. After baking, the berries should feel very soft, the juices should feel slightly thick, not watery, once the crumble cools a little, and the topping should feel crisp.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease an 8 inch square pan or a similar baking dish.

3. Toss the berries with the sugar, cornstarch, and lemon juice.

4. Spread them in the dish.

5. Mix the flour, brown sugar, optional oats, and salt.

6. Cut in the butter until the mix feels like damp crumbs. Cut in means rub or mash the butter into the dry mix.

7. Sprinkle the topping over the berries.

8. Bake 30 to 40 minutes, until the topping feels crisp and the juices are bubbling thickly at the edges.

9. Cool at least 15 minutes before you serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The topping should feel crisp, not wet and raw. You should hear bubbling. You should smell cooked berries and toasted topping, not raw flour. The fruit will be extremely hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The topping should look golden. The juices should look bubbly and slightly glossy, not thin and watery. The berries should look collapsed.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The topping should look golden. The juices should look bubbly and slightly glossy, not thin and watery. The berries should look collapsed.

SAFETY
Use mitts. Berry filling is like molten sugar and can burn your mouth. Cool at least 15 minutes. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Serve with ice cream or Whipped Cream from the Misc chapter. If the berries are very juicy, add an extra teaspoon of cornstarch.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
