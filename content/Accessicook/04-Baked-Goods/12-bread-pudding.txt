ACCESSICOOK
Bread Pudding
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Stale or toasted bread soaked in a sweet egg custard and baked until the center is set. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. This is a good way to use leftover sandwich bread or the Simple Sandwich Bread in this chapter.

SERVINGS
8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 15 minutes, plus a short rest

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 6 cups cubed stale or lightly toasted bread, about 3/4 inch (three-quarters of an inch) pieces
- 4 large eggs
- 2 cups milk
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sugar
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 2 tablespoons butter, for the pan, plus a little more, cut into small pieces, for the top
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) raisins, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The bread should feel dry enough to soak up custard, not already soggy. After a brief soak, every cube should feel moist. After baking, the center should feel set, like a firm custard, not liquid, and a thermometer should read 160 degrees F.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Butter an 8 or 9 inch baking dish.

3. Spread the bread cubes in the dish, and scatter the raisins if you are using them.

4. Mix the eggs, milk, sugar, vanilla, cinnamon, and salt until even.

5. Pour the custard over the bread.

6. Press the bread down gently so it soaks.

7. Let it sit 10 to 15 minutes.

8. Dot the top with the remaining butter.

9. Bake 40 to 50 minutes, until the center feels set and a thermometer in the middle reads 160 degrees F. The top should feel golden and slightly crisp in spots.

10. Cool at least 15 minutes before you serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The center should feel set, 160 degrees F, not liquid. You should smell custard, cinnamon, and toasted bread, not raw egg or burning sugar. A knife near the center should come out mostly clean.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look golden, with some darker toasted edges of bread. The center should look set, not wet and liquid when you nudge the dish.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look golden, with some darker toasted edges of bread. The center should look set, not wet and liquid when you nudge the dish.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw custard. It contains eggs. Use mitts. The dish and the custard are very hot. Cool 15 minutes. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, and reheat to 165 degrees F. Cook egg dishes to 160 degrees F. There should be no liquid raw egg left.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A splash of cream or a simple vanilla sauce is a good topping. Croissants or challah make a richer pudding. Leftovers reheat well, covered, until the center is 165 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
