Baking Powder Biscuits
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Tender, flaky biscuits for breakfast, supper, or strawberry shortcake. Cut in means rub or mash cold butter into the flour until the mix feels like coarse crumbs with some pea-size pieces. Those pieces of butter make steam and flakes in the oven. Handle the dough as little as you can.

SERVINGS
8 to 10 biscuits

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for the board
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 teaspoon sugar, optional
- 6 tablespoons cold unsalted butter, cut into small cubes
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) cold milk, plus 1 or 2 tablespoons more if needed

TACTILE TIPS
The butter should feel cold and firm, not greasy and soft. After you cut it in, you should still feel small pieces of butter. The dough should feel soft and a little shaggy, not smooth like bread dough. Baked biscuits should feel light, with a set, slightly crisp top and a tender interior.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment.

3. Mix the flour, baking powder, salt, and optional sugar.

4. Cut in the cold butter with a pastry cutter, two knives, or your fingertips, until the mix feels like coarse crumbs with some pea-size bits.

5. Add the milk.

6. Stir just until a soft dough forms.

7. Turn the dough onto a lightly floured board.

8. Fold it over itself 3 or 4 times, then pat it to about 3/4 inch (three-quarters of an inch) thick. Fold means gently turn the mixture over itself so you do not knock out the air.

9. Cut biscuits with a cutter or the rim of a glass, pressing straight down without twisting.

10. Twisting can seal the edges and reduce the rise. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens.

11. Set the rounds on the sheet, close together for softer sides, or spaced apart for crisper sides.

12. Bake 12 to 16 minutes, until the tops feel set and lightly crisp, and the biscuits have risen.

13. Serve warm, or cool on a rack.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tops should feel set and lightly crisp. You should smell butter and toasted flour, not raw dough. The center of a biscuit should feel cooked and fluffy, not wet.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tops should look golden, and the sides should look risen in layers, not squat and pale. The insides should look fluffy, not wet and doughy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tops should look golden, and the sides should look risen in layers, not squat and pale. The insides should look fluffy, not wet and doughy.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. Use mitts. A cutter has a sharp edge. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you do not want to cut rounds, pat the dough into a square and cut 8 to 9 squares with a knife. Leftover biscuits reheat well at 300 degrees F for 5 to 8 minutes, or split and toasted. See Biscuits and Sausage Gravy in the Breakfast chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
