Strawberry Shortcake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Split biscuits or shortcake rounds topped with sugared strawberries and whipped cream. Macerate means toss the fruit with sugar and let it sit so the berries soften and release juice. You can use the Baking Powder Biscuits in this chapter, or store-bought biscuits in a pinch. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

SERVINGS
6 to 8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes, including the biscuits

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 recipe Baking Powder Biscuits, or 8 store-bought biscuits, warmed
- 1 pound strawberries, hulled and sliced
- 3 tablespoons sugar, or to taste
- 1 teaspoon lemon juice, optional
- 1 recipe Whipped Cream from the Misc chapter, or store-bought whipped cream

TACTILE TIPS
Hull means remove the green cap and the pale core of the strawberry. After sitting with sugar, the berries should feel slightly softened, and you should feel juice in the bowl. The biscuits should feel warm and tender. The cream should feel soft and fluffy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Toss the sliced strawberries with the sugar and optional lemon juice.

2. Let them sit 15 to 30 minutes, stirring once, until you feel juice in the bowl.

3. Bake the biscuits as in the Baking Powder Biscuits recipe, or warm store-bought biscuits at 300 degrees F until they feel hot, about 8 minutes.

4. Split the biscuits.

5. Spoon berries and juice over the bottoms.

6. Add whipped cream, then the biscuit tops, and more berries and cream if you like.

7. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The berries should feel slightly soft and juicy, and they should smell sweet and fresh, not fermented or sour. The biscuits should feel warm and tender. The cream should feel soft and fluffy.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The berries should look glossy and slightly softened, sitting in a little red juice. The cream should look soft and fluffy, not melted into a puddle if you serve at once.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The berries should look glossy and slightly softened, sitting in a little red juice. The cream should look soft and fluffy, not melted into a puddle if you serve at once.

SAFETY
Wash the berries. Use a stable board and a small knife to hull and slice them. Use mitts if you bake or reheat the biscuits. Keep whipped cream cold until you assemble. Leftover assembled shortcakes get soggy, so store the parts separately. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Other berries can replace some or all of the strawberries. If the berries are very ripe and sweet, use less sugar. Pound Cake from this chapter can replace the biscuits.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
