ACCESSICOOK
Apple Crisp
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Baked apples under a crumbly topping of oats, flour, sugar, and butter. This is easier than apple pie because there is no crust to roll. The fruit should be tender, and the topping should feel crisp, not wet and sandy.

SERVINGS
6 to 8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 55 minutes, plus a short cooling time

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 6 cups peeled, sliced apples
- 2 tablespoons sugar, for the fruit, plus 1 tablespoon lemon juice
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) packed brown sugar, for the topping
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) all-purpose flour
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) rolled oats, not instant
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) cold unsalted butter, cut into cubes

TACTILE TIPS
Apple slices should be even, about 1/4 inch (a quarter inch) thick. The topping should feel like damp crumbs with some pea-size bits of butter. After baking, a knife should slide into the apples easily, and the topping should feel crisp.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease a 2 quart baking dish, or an 8 or 9 inch square pan.

3. Toss the apples with 2 tablespoons sugar, the lemon juice, and the cinnamon.

4. Spread them in the dish.

5. Mix the brown sugar, flour, oats, and salt.

6. Cut in the butter until the mix feels like damp crumbs. Cut in means rub or mash the butter into the dry mix.

7. Sprinkle the topping evenly over the apples.

8. Bake 35 to 45 minutes, until the topping feels crisp and golden, and a knife slides easily into the apples, and the juices are bubbling at the edges.

9. Cool at least 15 minutes before you serve. The filling is very hot at first.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The topping should feel crisp, not wet and raw. A knife should slide into the apples easily. You should smell cooked apple, cinnamon, and toasted oats, not raw flour or burning sugar. You may hear bubbling at the edges.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The topping should look golden and crisp, and the juices should look bubbly at the edges. The apples should look collapsed and tender, not pale and stiff.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The topping should look golden and crisp, and the juices should look bubbly at the edges. The apples should look collapsed and tender, not pale and stiff.

SAFETY
Use a stable board if you peel and slice the apples. Use mitts. The fruit filling is extremely hot. Cool at least 15 minutes. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Serve with ice cream or Whipped Cream from the Misc chapter. Peaches or mixed berries can replace some of the apples. Leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator and reheat well until steaming hot.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
