ACCESSICOOK
Blondies
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Chewy bar cookies that taste like a brown-sugar cookie in a pan. They are the vanilla cousin of brownies. Do not overbake, or they will be dry instead of chewy.

SERVINGS
16 squares

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup unsalted butter, melted
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) packed brown sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 cup chocolate chips or chopped white chocolate, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The batter will feel thick. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter, and not completely dry. The edges should feel set, and the center should feel slightly soft. It will firm as it cools.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease the pan, or line it with parchment, leaving a little overhang as a handle.

3. Mix the melted butter and brown sugar until even.

4. Add the eggs and vanilla, and mix well.

5. Add the flour, baking powder, and salt, and stir just until you no longer feel dry flour.

6. Stir in the chocolate chips, if using.

7. Spread the batter or dough evenly in the pan.

8. Bake 22 to 28 minutes.

9. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The edges should feel set, and the center should feel slightly soft.

10. Cool in the pan before you cut, unless the recipe tips say otherwise.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The center should feel slightly soft, not wet with raw batter. You should smell brown sugar and vanilla, not raw flour or burning. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look shiny and light golden, and the edges should look slightly darker and pulled from the pan. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look shiny and light golden, and the edges should look slightly darker and pulled from the pan. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Cool before you cut, or the squares will smear. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Walnuts or pecans can be added if no one has a nut allergy. Line the pan with parchment so you can lift the slab out to cut.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
