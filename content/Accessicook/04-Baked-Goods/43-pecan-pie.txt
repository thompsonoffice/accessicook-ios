ACCESSICOOK
Pecan Pie
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A sweet pie with a gooey filling and a layer of pecans. The filling contains eggs and must set. Because of the nuts, do not serve this to anyone with a pecan or tree-nut allergy. Cool the pie before you slice it so the filling firms.

SERVINGS
8 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 15 minutes, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 pie crust, fitted into a 9 inch pie pan
- 1 cup packed brown sugar
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) light corn syrup or maple syrup
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
- 3 large eggs
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) pecan halves

TACTILE TIPS
The filling should feel pourable and even. After baking, the center should feel set but still slightly soft, with a gentle jiggle, not liquid. The pecans should feel toasted, not burnt. After cooling, a slice should hold together.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Mix the brown sugar, corn syrup or maple syrup, melted butter, eggs, vanilla, and salt until even.

3. Spread the pecans in the crust.

4. Pour the filling over them.

5. Bake 45 to 55 minutes, until the center is set but still slightly jiggly, and the nuts smell toasted, not burnt.

6. If the crust edge browns too fast, cover it with foil.

7. Cool at least 2 hours before you slice.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The center should feel set with a slight jiggle, not slosh. You should smell toasted pecans, caramel, and baked crust, not raw egg or burning nuts. After cooling, a slice should feel firm enough to cut and still a little gooey.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The filling should look set and slightly puffed, with toasted pecans on top, not a liquid center. After cooling, a slice should hold its shape, with a glossy, set filling.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The filling should look set and slightly puffed, with toasted pecans on top, not a liquid center. After cooling, a slice should hold its shape, with a glossy, set filling.

SAFETY
Do not serve this to anyone with a tree-nut allergy. Do not taste raw filling. Use mitts. The filling is extremely hot, like molten sugar. Cool before you slice. Refrigerate after 2 hours if you are keeping the pie. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Toast the pecans lightly in a dry skillet first if you want a deeper flavor, and cool them before they go in the pie. Store leftovers in the refrigerator.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
