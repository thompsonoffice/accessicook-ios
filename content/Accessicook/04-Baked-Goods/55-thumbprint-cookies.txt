ACCESSICOOK
Thumbprint Cookies
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Butter cookies with a small jam well in the center, made by pressing your thumb into each ball of dough. Fill the well with jam before the cookies go in the oven.

SERVINGS
About 24 cookies

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sugar
- 1 large egg yolk
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) jam

TACTILE TIPS
Softened butter should give easily when pressed, like cool clay. The well should feel like a small, even dent. The jam should feel thick enough to stay in the well, not watery.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.

3. Beat the butter and sugar until the mix feels fluffy and even. This is called creaming.

4. Add the egg yolk and vanilla.

5. Beat until combined.

6. Add the flour and salt.

7. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour.

8. Roll the dough into 1 inch balls.

9. Place them on the sheet with space between them.

10. Press your thumb into each ball to make a small well about 1/2 inch (half an inch) across.

11. Spoon a little jam into each well. Do not overfill, or the jam will spill as it bubbles.

12. Bake 12 to 14 minutes.

13. Check at the early time. The edges should feel set, and the jam should feel hot and tacky, not loose and runny.

14. Cool on the pan 2 to 3 minutes, then move the cookies to a rack or a plate to finish cooling. The jam is very hot at first.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The jam should feel tacky and hot, not loose and runny. You should smell butter and cooked fruit, not raw flour or burning sugar.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cookies should look pale gold at the edges. The jam should look set and shiny, not watery.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cookies should look pale gold at the edges. The jam should look set and shiny, not watery.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. It contains egg yolk and raw flour. Use mitts. The jam is very hot when the cookies come out. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the well puffs shut as they bake, press it again with the back of a spoon right after the pan comes out, then add a little more jam if the well is empty. Apricot, raspberry, and strawberry jam all work well.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
