Chocolate Chip Muffins
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple vanilla muffin with chocolate chips. These are less sweet than cookies and make a good breakfast or snack with fruit.

SERVINGS
12 muffins

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sugar
- 2 teaspoons baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 cup milk
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) oil or melted butter
- 1 large egg
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 cup chocolate chips

TACTILE TIPS
The batter should feel thick, with a few lumps. The chips should be mixed through, not all in one spot. Baked muffins should feel springy, not wet in the center.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease a muffin pan, or line it with paper cups.

3. Mix the dry ingredients in a bowl.

4. Mix the wet ingredients in a second bowl.

5. Pour the wet mix into the dry mix.

6. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour. A few lumps are fine.

7. Gently fold in the chocolate chips. Fold means turn the batter over itself gently.

8. Spoon the batter into the cups, filling each about two-thirds full.

9. Bake 18 to 22 minutes.

10. A toothpick in the center, away from a large chip, should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

11. Cool in the pan 5 minutes, then move the muffins out so they do not steam and get soggy.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tops should feel set and springy. You should smell vanilla, chocolate, and toasted sugar, not raw flour. A toothpick in a part without a large chip should come out with a few moist crumbs.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tops should look golden, with melted chips on the surface. A toothpick should come out with a few crumbs, not wet batter. Avoid a pocket of melted chocolate when you test, or it can look wet when the muffin is actually done.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tops should look golden, with melted chips on the surface. A toothpick should come out with a few crumbs, not wet batter. Avoid a pocket of melted chocolate when you test, or it can look wet when the muffin is actually done.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Melted chips are very hot. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Mini chips spread more evenly than large ones. A pinch of cinnamon is a good extra. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). Cool completely before you store them.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
