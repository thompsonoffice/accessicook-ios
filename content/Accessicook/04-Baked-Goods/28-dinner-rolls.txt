ACCESSICOOK
Dinner Rolls
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Soft dinner rolls from store-bought dough, baked until the tops are golden and the centers are cooked. This is an easy way to put bread on a supper table without mixing yeast dough from scratch.

SERVINGS
12 rolls

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes with store-bought dough, plus any rise the package requires

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 1/2 pounds (one and a half pounds) pizza dough or frozen dinner-roll dough, thawed if frozen, following the package
- 2 tablespoons melted butter, plus more for the pan
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, if the dough is unsalted

TACTILE TIPS
The dough should feel soft and elastic, not icy and not sticky enough to be unworkable. Baked rolls should feel set and lightly golden on top, and the center of a roll should feel dry and cooked, not gummy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F, or to the temperature on the package.

2. Grease a baking pan.

3. Divide the dough into 12 pieces.

4. Shape each piece into a ball by pulling the edges under and pinching them at the bottom.

5. Set the balls in the pan, close but not crushed together.

6. Brush the tops with melted butter.

7. If the package asks for a rise, cover the pan and let the rolls sit until they feel puffy.

8. Bake 15 to 22 minutes, until the tops feel set and golden, and the center roll feels cooked, not gummy.

9. Brush with a little more butter if you like.

10. Cool 5 minutes and serve warm.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tops should feel set and lightly soft-crisp. You should smell toasted bread and butter, not raw dough. The center of a middle roll should feel cooked and hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tops should look golden, not pale and not dark brown. The insides should look fluffy and dry, not doughy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tops should look golden, not pale and not dark brown. The insides should look fluffy and dry, not doughy.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. Use mitts. If you bake from frozen dough, follow the package for thawing in the refrigerator, not on a hot counter. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A sprinkle of coarse salt or sesame seeds can go on after the butter and before the bake. Leftovers make good sandwich rolls the next day, or they can be warmed at 300 degrees F.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
