Zucchini Bread
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A moist quick bread that uses grated zucchini. The zucchini almost disappears into the loaf and keeps it from drying out. Squeeze out some of the liquid so the bread is not soggy.

SERVINGS
1 loaf, about 10 slices

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- 2 large eggs
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) oil
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) sugar
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) grated zucchini, lightly squeezed

TACTILE TIPS
Grated zucchini feels damp and grassy. After a light squeeze in a clean towel, it should feel moist but not dripping. The baked loaf should feel springy, not wet in the center.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease a loaf pan, or line it with parchment.

3. Mix the dry ingredients in a bowl.

4. Mix the wet ingredients in a second bowl, including any mashed fruit.

5. Add the wet mix to the dry mix.

6. Stir just until combined.

7. Spread the batter in the pan.

8. Bake 50 to 65 minutes.

9. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The top should feel set, and the center should not feel wet.

10. Cool in the pan 10 minutes, then turn the loaf out to finish cooling.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel set. You should smell cinnamon and toasted sugar, not raw flour or a grassy, underbaked smell. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look golden brown and may have a slight crack. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look golden brown and may have a slight crack. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

SAFETY
Use a box grater on a stable board, and watch your knuckles. Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Cool before you slice. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A light squeeze is enough. If you wring the zucchini completely dry, the loaf can be less moist. Chocolate chips or walnuts are common additions.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
