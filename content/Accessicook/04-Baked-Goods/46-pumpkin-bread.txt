Pumpkin Bread
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A spiced pumpkin loaf that uses canned pumpkin puree, not pie filling. It is moist, like banana bread, and keeps well for a few days.

SERVINGS
1 loaf, about 10 slices

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 3/4 cups (one and three-quarters cups) all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 2 teaspoons pumpkin pie spice, or 1 1/2 teaspoons (one and a half teaspoons) cinnamon plus 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) ginger
- 1 cup sugar
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) oil
- 2 large eggs
- 1 cup canned pumpkin puree
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) water or milk

TACTILE TIPS
The batter will feel thick and heavy. The baked loaf should feel springy, and a toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease a loaf pan, or line it with parchment.

3. Mix the dry ingredients in a bowl.

4. Mix the wet ingredients in a second bowl, including any mashed fruit.

5. Add the wet mix to the dry mix.

6. Stir just until combined.

7. Spread the batter in the pan.

8. Bake 55 to 70 minutes.

9. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The top should feel set.

10. Cool in the pan 10 minutes, then turn the loaf out to finish cooling.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel set. You should smell pumpkin and warm spice, not raw flour. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The top should look dark golden and may crack slightly. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The top should look dark golden and may crack slightly. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Cool before you slice. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Chocolate chips or pepitas, which are pumpkin seeds, can be added. Tent with foil if the top browns too fast. Wrap when cool.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
