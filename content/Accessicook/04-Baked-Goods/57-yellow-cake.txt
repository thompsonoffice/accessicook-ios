Yellow Cake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple butter cake with a tender, yellow crumb from eggs and butter. Bake it as two layers or as a sheet cake, then frost it when it is fully cool. See Vanilla Frosting or Chocolate Frosting in the Misc chapter.

SERVINGS
One 9 inch layer cake, or a 9 by 13 inch sheet, about 12 servings

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 1/4 cups (two and a quarter cups) all-purpose flour
- 2 1/2 teaspoons (two and a half teaspoons) baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) unsalted butter, softened
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) sugar
- 3 large eggs
- 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
- 1 cup milk

TACTILE TIPS
Softened butter should give like cool clay. The batter should feel smooth and pourable. A toothpick in the center of the baked cake should come out clean or with a few crumbs, not wet batter. The top should feel springy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease two 9 inch round pans, or one 9 by 13 inch pan, and line the bottoms with parchment if you have it.

3. Mix the flour, baking powder, and salt in a bowl.

4. In a second bowl, beat the butter and sugar until the mix feels fluffy and even. This is called creaming.

5. Add the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla, and beat until combined.

6. Add the flour mix in three parts, alternating with the milk, and stir just until you no longer feel dry flour.

7. Spread the batter in the pans.

8. Bake 25 to 35 minutes for layers, or 30 to 38 minutes for a sheet, until a toothpick in the center comes out clean or with a few crumbs, and the top feels springy.

9. Cool in the pans 10 minutes, then turn the cakes out and cool completely before you frost them.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tops should feel springy. A toothpick in the center should come out clean or with a few crumbs. You should smell butter, vanilla, and toasted sugar, not raw flour.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tops should look golden and slightly pulled from the sides of the pan. A toothpick should come out clean or with a few crumbs. The crumb, when cool, should look even and yellow, not wet in the center.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tops should look golden and slightly pulled from the sides of the pan. A toothpick should come out clean or with a few crumbs. The crumb, when cool, should look even and yellow, not wet in the center.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Cool completely before frosting, or the frosting will melt and slide. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If the layers dome a lot, you can trim the tops with a long knife once they are cool, using a slow sawing motion. This cake freezes well, wrapped, without frosting.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
