Cream Scones
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Tender scones made with cream, cut into wedges, and baked until the edges are lightly crisp. They are a little richer and more crumbly than biscuits. Handle the dough gently.

SERVINGS
8 scones

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for the board
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) sugar
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 6 tablespoons cold unsalted butter, cut into cubes
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) heavy cream, plus 1 tablespoon for brushing
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The dough should feel soft and a little dry, just holding together, not wet and sticky. You should still feel small bits of butter. Baked scones should feel set and lightly crisp at the edges, and tender inside, not wet in the center.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment.

3. Mix the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt.

4. Cut in the cold butter until the mix feels like coarse crumbs with some pea-size bits. Cut in means rub or mash the butter into the flour.

5. Add the cream and optional vanilla.

6. Stir just until a dough forms.

7. Turn the dough onto a lightly floured board.

8. Pat it into a circle about 3/4 inch (three-quarters of an inch) thick.

9. Cut the circle into 8 wedges, like a pie.

10. Set them on the sheet with a little space.

11. Brush the tops with the remaining cream.

12. Bake 14 to 18 minutes, until the edges feel set and lightly crisp, and the bottoms feel golden, not doughy.

13. Cool 10 minutes.

14. Serve warm or at room temperature.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set and lightly crisp. You should smell butter and toasted sugar, not raw flour. The centers should feel cooked, not wet or pasty.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The scones should look risen and light golden, especially at the edges and on the bottoms. The insides should look dry and fluffy, not wet.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The scones should look risen and light golden, especially at the edges and on the bottoms. The insides should look dry and fluffy, not wet.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. Use mitts. A knife for the wedges should be used with a stable board. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Dried fruit or chocolate chips can be stirred in with the cream. For blueberry scones, see the next recipe. Leftovers toast well the next morning.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
