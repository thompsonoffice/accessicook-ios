ACCESSICOOK
Sugar Cookies
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple vanilla sugar cookie you can drop from a spoon or roll and cut. This version is a drop cookie, which is easier than cutting shapes.

SERVINGS
About 24 cookies

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 3/4 cups (two and three-quarters cups) all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) baking powder
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) sugar, plus extra for pressing
- 1 large egg
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

TACTILE TIPS
After you drop the dough, you can press each cookie with a sugared glass bottom. The sugar feels gritty on the glass.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.

3. Stir the dry ingredients in a bowl until they are combined.

4. In a second bowl, beat the butter and sugar until the mix feels fluffy and even. This is called creaming.

5. Add the eggs and vanilla, if listed.

6. Beat until combined.

7. Add the dry mix.

8. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour.

9. Drop rounded tablespoons of dough onto the sheet, leaving about 2 inches of space.

10. Bake 8 to 10 minutes.

11. Check at the early time. The edges should feel set and look only faintly golden.

12. Cool on the pan 2 to 3 minutes, then move the cookies to a rack or a plate to finish cooling.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The centers should feel soft. You should smell vanilla and toasting sugar.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The edges should look just barely golden. The tops should look pale and dry, not browned all over.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The edges should look just barely golden. The tops should look pale and dry, not browned all over.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. It contains egg and raw flour. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For cut-out cookies, chill the dough 1 hour, roll it about 1/4 inch (a quarter inch) thick, and cut shapes. Bake 8 to 10 minutes. Frost them after they cool. See Buttercream Frosting in the Misc chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
