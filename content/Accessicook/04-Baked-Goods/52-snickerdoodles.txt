ACCESSICOOK
Snickerdoodles
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Soft cinnamon-sugar cookies with a little cream of tartar, which gives them a mild tang and a tender bite. You roll each ball in cinnamon sugar before baking.

SERVINGS
About 24 cookies

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 3/4 cups (two and three-quarters cups) all-purpose flour
- 2 teaspoons cream of tartar
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) sugar, plus 3 tablespoons for rolling
- 2 large eggs
- 3 tablespoons sugar mixed with 1 tablespoon cinnamon, for rolling

TACTILE TIPS
Roll each ball in the cinnamon sugar so it feels evenly sandy on the outside before it goes on the pan.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.

3. Stir the dry ingredients in a bowl until they are combined.

4. In a second bowl, beat the butter and sugar until the mix feels fluffy and even. This is called creaming.

5. Add the eggs and vanilla, if listed.

6. Beat until combined.

7. Add the dry mix.

8. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour.

9. Roll the dough into 1 inch balls.

10. Place them on the sheet with space between them.

11. Bake 8 to 10 minutes.

12. Check at the early time. The edges should feel set and the tops may look slightly crackled.

13. Cool on the pan 2 to 3 minutes, then move the cookies to a rack or a plate to finish cooling.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The edges should feel set. The centers should feel soft. You should smell cinnamon and toasted sugar.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cookies should look crackled on top and faintly golden at the edges. The centers should still look pale.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cookies should look crackled on top and faintly golden at the edges. The centers should still look pale.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. It contains eggs and raw flour. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cream of tartar is a dry, sour-tasting powder in the baking aisle. If you do not have it, the cookie will still work but will taste more like a plain sugar cookie.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
