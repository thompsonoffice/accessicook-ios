Blueberry Muffins
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A classic muffin with fresh or frozen blueberries. Stir the batter only until the flour disappears, or the muffins can turn tough. Frozen berries can go in without thawing.

SERVINGS
12 muffins

TOTAL TIME
About 35 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sugar
- 2 teaspoons baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 cup milk
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) oil or melted butter
- 1 large egg
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) blueberries, fresh or frozen

TACTILE TIPS
The batter should feel thick and slightly lumpy, not silky-smooth. Frozen berries will make it colder and thicker. A toothpick in the center of a baked muffin should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease a muffin pan, or line it with paper cups.

3. Mix the dry ingredients in a bowl.

4. Mix the wet ingredients in a second bowl.

5. Pour the wet mix into the dry mix.

6. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour. A few lumps are fine.

7. Gently fold in the blueberries. Fold means turn the batter over itself gently.

8. Spoon the batter into the cups, filling each about two-thirds full.

9. Bake 18 to 22 minutes.

10. A toothpick in the center of a muffin should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The tops should feel set and springy.

11. Cool in the pan 5 minutes, then move the muffins out so they do not steam and get soggy.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tops should feel set and springy. You should smell baked berries and vanilla, not raw flour or burning sugar. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tops should look risen and lightly golden, with a few berries showing. A toothpick should come out without wet batter.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tops should look risen and lightly golden, with a few berries showing. A toothpick should come out without wet batter.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. It contains egg and raw flour. Use mitts. The pan and berries will be very hot. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
If you use frozen berries, do not thaw them, or the batter can turn blue and wet. A little sugar sprinkled on top before baking gives a light crust. Cool before you store them, then keep in a closed container for 2 days, or freeze.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
