Irish Soda Bread
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A rustic round loaf that rises with baking soda and buttermilk, not yeast. There is no kneading beyond a brief, light mix. Knead means press and fold dough to develop gluten, which you mostly avoid here so the bread stays tender. Fold means gently turn the mixture over itself so you do not knock out the air.

SERVINGS
1 round loaf, about 12 slices

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 4 cups all-purpose flour, plus a little more for the board
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 tablespoon sugar, optional
- 1 3/4 cups (one and three-quarters cups) buttermilk, plus 1 or 2 tablespoons more if the dough is dry

TACTILE TIPS
The dough should feel shaggy and slightly sticky, but it should hold a round loaf. If it feels like a tight bread dough, you have worked it too much. The baked loaf should feel crusty outside and moist inside, and a toothpick or skewer in the center should come out clean.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment, or grease a cake pan.

3. Mix the flour, baking soda, salt, and optional sugar in a bowl.

4. Add the buttermilk.

5. Stir just until a shaggy dough forms.

6. If dry flour remains, add buttermilk 1 tablespoon at a time.

7. Turn the dough onto a lightly floured board.

8. Fold it over itself a few times, just until it holds together.

9. Shape a round loaf about 2 inches tall.

10. Set it on the pan.

11. Cut a shallow X in the top with a knife, about 1/2 inch (half an inch) deep. This helps the center bake.

12. Bake 35 to 45 minutes, until the crust feels firm and a skewer in the center comes out clean. The loaf should sound hollow if you tap the bottom with a knuckle, once you can handle it safely with mitts.

13. Cool at least 20 minutes before you slice.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The crust should feel firm. A skewer in the center should come out clean. You should smell toasted wheat, not raw flour or burning. The loaf may sound hollow when the bottom is tapped.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The loaf should look rustic and golden to light brown, with the X opened a little. The inside should look moist and even, not raw and doughy in the center.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The loaf should look rustic and golden to light brown, with the X opened a little. The inside should look moist and even, not raw and doughy in the center.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. It contains raw flour. Use mitts. The loaf is hot. Cool before you slice, and use a serrated knife with a slow sawing motion. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Raisins or caraway seeds are traditional extras. If you do not have buttermilk, mix 1 3/4 cups (one and three-quarters cups) milk with 1 1/2 tablespoons (one and a half tablespoons) lemon juice or vinegar, and let it stand 5 minutes. Eat this bread the same day if you can. It dries faster than yeast bread. Toast leftovers.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
