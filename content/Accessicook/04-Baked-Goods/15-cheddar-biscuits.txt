Cheddar Biscuits
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Drop biscuits with cheddar cheese, meaning you scoop the dough onto the pan instead of rolling and cutting it. They are more rustic than cut biscuits, and they are faster.

SERVINGS
10 to 12 biscuits

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) garlic powder, optional
- 6 tablespoons cold unsalted butter, cut into cubes
- 1 cup shredded sharp cheddar cheese
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) cold milk, plus a little more if the dough is very dry

TACTILE TIPS
The dough should feel thick and scoopable, stickier than cut-biscuit dough. You should still feel bits of butter and shreds of cheese. Baked biscuits should feel set and lightly crisp on the outside, and tender inside.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 425 degrees F.

2. Line a baking sheet with parchment.

3. Mix the flour, baking powder, salt, and optional garlic powder.

4. Cut in the cold butter until the mix feels like coarse crumbs. Cut in means rub or mash the butter into the flour.

5. Stir in the cheese, then the milk, just until a thick dough forms.

6. Drop 10 to 12 mounds onto the sheet, leaving a little space.

7. Bake 12 to 16 minutes, until the tops feel set and lightly crisp, and the cheese smells toasted, not burnt.

8. Cool a few minutes.

9. Serve warm.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The outsides should feel set and lightly crisp. You should smell toasted cheese and butter, not raw dough or burning cheese. The centers should feel cooked and hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The biscuits should look golden, with melted, slightly browned cheese on top. The insides should look fluffy, not wet.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The biscuits should look golden, with melted, slightly browned cheese on top. The insides should look fluffy, not wet.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw dough. Use mitts. Melted cheese is very hot. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A brush of melted butter on the hot biscuits is a good finish. These are rich, so smaller mounds bake more evenly. Reheat at 300 degrees F until warm.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
