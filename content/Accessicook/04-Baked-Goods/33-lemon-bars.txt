ACCESSICOOK
Lemon Bars
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A buttery shortbread crust with a tart lemon filling that sets as it bakes. The filling contains eggs. Cool the bars completely, then dust them with powdered sugar if you like, and cut with a clean knife.

SERVINGS
16 squares

TOTAL TIME
About 1 hour, plus cooling

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) powdered sugar, plus more for dusting
- 2 cups all-purpose flour, divided
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) salt
- 4 large eggs
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) granulated sugar
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) lemon juice, plus 1 tablespoon lemon zest

TACTILE TIPS
Zest is the fragrant yellow outer skin of the lemon, not the bitter white pith. The crust dough should feel like damp crumbs that hold when pressed. After baking, the filling should feel set, not liquid, and a slight jiggle is all right. After cooling, a bar should cut cleanly.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 350 degrees F.

2. Grease a 9 by 13 inch pan, or line it with parchment.

3. Mix the butter, powdered sugar, 1 3/4 cups (one and three-quarters cups) of the flour, and the salt until a soft dough forms.

4. Press the dough evenly into the pan.

5. Bake 15 to 18 minutes, until the crust feels set and just barely golden.

6. Mix the eggs, granulated sugar, remaining 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) flour, lemon juice, and zest until even.

7. Pour the filling over the hot crust.

8. Bake 18 to 22 minutes more, until the filling feels set and only slightly jiggly in the center, not liquid.

9. Cool completely.

10. Dust with powdered sugar if you like, then cut into squares.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The crust should feel set after the first bake. After the second bake, the filling should feel set, with only a slight jiggle. You should smell lemon and butter, not raw egg. After cooling, a bar should feel firm enough to cut and still slightly creamy.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The filling should look pale and set, not browned and not wet in the center. A cooled bar should show a clean yellow layer over a pale crust.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The filling should look pale and set, not browned and not wet in the center. A cooled bar should show a clean yellow layer over a pale crust.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw filling. It contains eggs. A zester is sharp. Use mitts. Cool completely before you cut. Powdered sugar can puff into the air, so dust it slowly. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A clean, slightly wet knife helps you cut neat squares. Keep bars refrigerated after the first day. They freeze well.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
