Apple Cinnamon Muffins
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Muffins with small pieces of apple and cinnamon. Cut the apple small so the pieces cook through in the same time as the batter.

SERVINGS
12 muffins

TOTAL TIME
About 40 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) sugar
- 2 teaspoons baking powder
- 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 cup milk
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) oil or melted butter
- 1 large egg
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) peeled, diced apple

TACTILE TIPS
Dice means cut into small, even cubes, about 1/4 inch (a quarter inch) to 1/2 inch (half an inch). The apple pieces should feel firm going in and tender in the baked muffin.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Grease a muffin pan, or line it with paper cups.

3. Mix the dry ingredients in a bowl.

4. Mix the wet ingredients in a second bowl.

5. Pour the wet mix into the dry mix.

6. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour. A few lumps are fine.

7. Gently fold in the diced apple. Dice means cut into small, even cubes. Fold means turn the batter over itself gently.

8. Spoon the batter into the cups, filling each about two-thirds full.

9. Bake 18 to 24 minutes.

10. A toothpick in the center should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter. The apple pieces should feel tender.

11. Cool in the pan 5 minutes, then move the muffins out so they do not steam and get soggy.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tops should feel set and springy. The apple pieces should feel tender, not hard and raw. You should smell apple and cinnamon, not raw flour.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tops should look risen and golden, with a little darker color from the cinnamon. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tops should look risen and golden, with a little darker color from the cinnamon. A toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.

SAFETY
Use a stable cutting board and a cut-resistant glove if you peel and dice the apple. Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A tart apple such as Granny Smith holds its shape. A pinch of extra cinnamon sugar on top before baking is a good crust. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). Chopped walnuts are a common addition if no one has a nut allergy.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
