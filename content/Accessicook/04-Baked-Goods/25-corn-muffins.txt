Corn Muffins
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A slightly sweet corn muffin that sits between a dinner cornbread and a breakfast muffin. Serve with chili, soup, or butter and honey.

SERVINGS
12 muffins

TOTAL TIME
About 30 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup all-purpose flour
- 1 cup yellow cornmeal
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) sugar
- 2 teaspoons baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt
- 1 cup milk
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) oil or melted butter
- 1 large egg

TACTILE TIPS
Cornmeal feels sandy and dry, coarser than flour. The batter should feel thick but pourable. Baked muffins should feel set and a little crumbly, not wet in the center.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 400 degrees F.

2. Grease a muffin pan, or line it with paper cups.

3. Mix the dry ingredients in a bowl.

4. Mix the wet ingredients in a second bowl.

5. Pour the wet mix into the dry mix.

6. Stir just until you no longer feel dry flour. A few lumps are fine.

7. Spoon the batter into the cups, filling each about two-thirds full.

8. Bake 15 to 18 minutes.

9. A toothpick in the center should come out clean or with a few crumbs. The tops should feel set.

10. Cool in the pan 5 minutes, then move the muffins out so they do not steam and get soggy.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tops should feel set. You should smell toasted corn and butter, not raw meal. A toothpick in the center should come out clean or with a few crumbs.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tops should look golden, with a slightly coarser surface than a wheat muffin. A toothpick should come out clean or with a few crumbs.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tops should look golden, with a slightly coarser surface than a wheat muffin. A toothpick should come out clean or with a few crumbs.

SAFETY
Do not taste raw batter. Use mitts. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a less sweet muffin to serve with chili, reduce the sugar to 2 tablespoons. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens. A handful of corn kernels can be folded in. See also Cornbread in this chapter, which is baked in a pan instead of a muffin tin.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
