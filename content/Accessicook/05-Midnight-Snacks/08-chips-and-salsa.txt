ACCESSICOOK
Chips and Salsa
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Tortilla chips with salsa for dipping. This is one of the fastest snacks in the book. For homemade salsa, see the Misc chapter. A small bowl for the salsa keeps drips off the table.

SERVINGS
2 as a snack

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes with store-bought salsa, or 15 minutes if you make the Salsa in the Misc chapter

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 4 cups tortilla chips
- 1 cup salsa, store-bought or homemade

TACTILE TIPS
Chips should feel crisp and dry, not stale and leathery. Salsa should feel scoopable, with a fresh, tomato-and-onion or tomato-and-chili smell, not a fermented or off smell. If a jarred salsa fizzes in an unexpected way or the jar lid was bulging, throw it out.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Pour the salsa into a small bowl.

2. Set the chips in a basket or on a plate beside it.

3. Serve at once.

4. If you are using leftover homemade salsa, keep it cold until serving, and do not leave it out more than 2 hours.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The chips should feel crisp and should smell like toasted corn, not stale oil. The salsa should smell fresh and tomatoey or chili-bright, not fermented or musty.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The salsa should look moist and scoopable, not dried at the edges, and the chips should look whole and crisp.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The salsa should look moist and scoopable, not dried at the edges, and the chips should look whole and crisp.

SAFETY
Check the salsa date and the jar. Refrigerate opened salsa. If you make homemade salsa with raw onion and tomato, treat it as a perishable dip and keep it cold. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Queso or Guacamole from the Misc chapter can join the salsa, or replace it. See also Queso and Chips in this chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
