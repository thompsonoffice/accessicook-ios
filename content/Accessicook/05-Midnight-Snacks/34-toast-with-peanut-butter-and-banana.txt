Toast with Peanut Butter and Banana
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Toasted bread spread with peanut butter and topped with banana slices. Toast the bread first, then add the toppings, because a filled sandwich or topped bread does not belong in a toaster. If anyone has a peanut allergy, skip the peanut butter.

SERVINGS
1 snack

TOTAL TIME
About 8 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 or 2 slices bread
- 1 to 2 tablespoons peanut butter
- 1 banana, sliced
- A pinch of cinnamon, optional

TACTILE TIPS
The toast should feel dry and crisp, not soft and pale, and not burnt and brittle. The banana should feel ripe, with a little give, not rock-hard and not collapsing into mush.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Toast the bread in a toaster or in a skillet until it feels crisp and you smell toasted bread, not burning.

2. Let the toast sit 30 seconds so you can handle it.

3. Spread the peanut butter evenly on the toast.

4. Slice the banana on a stable board, then lay the slices on the peanut butter.

5. Add a pinch of cinnamon if you like. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

6. Serve at once, because the toast will soften.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The toast should feel crisp and should smell toasted, not burnt. The banana should smell sweet and ripe, not fermented. The peanut butter should smell fresh, not rancid.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The toast should look evenly golden, not pale and not dark brown. The banana slices should look even and not already brown and wet.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The toast should look evenly golden, not pale and not dark brown. The banana slices should look even and not already brown and wet.

SAFETY
Do not put peanut butter or banana in a toaster. If anyone has a peanut allergy, skip this recipe. Use a stable board for the banana. The toaster and toast can be hot. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Honey is a good drizzle if you want more sweetness. Almond butter or sunflower seed butter can replace peanut butter if they are safe for the people eating.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
