Leftover Fried Rice
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Cold leftover rice fried in a skillet with an egg, a few vegetables, and a splash of soy sauce, until everything is hot, 165 degrees F. Day-old rice works better than fresh rice, because it is drier and less likely to turn mushy. Cook the egg until it is firm, 160 degrees F if it is scrambled into the rice.

SERVINGS
1 or 2 as a late meal

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups cold leftover cooked rice, refrigerated
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 large egg, beaten
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) frozen peas and carrots, or chopped leftover vegetables
- 1 or 2 green onions, sliced, optional
- 1 to 2 tablespoons soy sauce
- Leftover cooked chicken, shrimp, or tofu, optional, already cooked

TACTILE TIPS
Cold rice should feel separate and a little firm, not one wet clump. Break up clumps with wet fingers or a spoon before they hit the pan. The finished rice should feel hot all the way through, 165 degrees F, and the egg should feel firm, not liquid.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high heat.

2. Add the beaten egg.

3. Cook, stirring, until it is firm and in small pieces, then move it to a plate, or push it to the side of the pan.

4. Add the vegetables and cook 2 to 3 minutes, until they feel hot and any frozen vegetables are no longer icy.

5. Add the rice.

6. Stir and press to break up clumps, 3 to 5 minutes, until the rice feels hot, 165 degrees F, and you hear a sizzle.

7. Add the soy sauce, the egg, optional leftover cooked protein, and optional green onion.

8. Stir until everything is 165 degrees F and the egg is firm.

9. Taste a cooled bite, and add soy sauce if it needs it.

10. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The rice should feel hot all the way through, 165 degrees F. The egg should feel firm. You should hear a sizzle, and you should smell toasted rice and soy, not burning, and not a sour leftover smell. If leftover rice smells sour or off, throw it out and do not cook it.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The rice should look separated and slightly toasted in spots, not wet and clumped. The egg should look cooked and yellow, not raw and glossy. Vegetables should look hot and bright.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The rice should look separated and slightly toasted in spots, not wet and clumped. The egg should look cooked and yellow, not raw and glossy. Vegetables should look hot and bright.

SAFETY
Leftover rice should have been refrigerated within 2 hours of cooking. Reheat to 165 degrees F. Cook the egg until firm. Wash the board and your hands after handling the raw egg. The pan is very hot and can spatter. Do not leave cooked rice out on the counter overnight and then fry it. Cook casseroles to 165 degrees F in the center.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is one of the best uses for leftover takeout rice. A little sesame oil at the end is a good finish. If you do not have leftover rice, see Rice in the Misc chapter, cook it, cool it quickly in a shallow container in the refrigerator, then use it later.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
