Garlic Cheese Toast
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Bread topped with butter, garlic, and cheese, then toasted until the cheese melts and the edges are crisp. This is a smaller, cheesier cousin of the Garlic Bread in the Misc chapter, meant as a late-night snack.

SERVINGS
2 to 4 slices

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 4 slices bread, or a split roll
- 2 tablespoons butter, softened
- 1 garlic clove, minced, or 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) garlic powder
- 3/4 cup (three-quarters of a cup) shredded mozzarella or a mix of mozzarella and cheddar
- A pinch of salt

TACTILE TIPS
Softened butter should spread without tearing the bread. Mince means cut the garlic into very small pieces. The finished toast should feel crisp at the edges, and the cheese should feel fully melted.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven or toaster oven to 400 degrees F, or use a skillet over medium-low heat.

2. Mix the butter, garlic, and a pinch of salt. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

3. Spread it on the bread.

4. Set the bread on a pan.

5. Sprinkle the cheese on top.

6. Bake 6 to 10 minutes, until the cheese is fully melted and bubbling, and the edges feel crisp.

7. If you use a skillet, start butter-side down for a minute, then turn, add cheese, and cover until the cheese melts.

8. Cool 1 minute, then serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The cheese should feel fully melted. The edges should feel crisp. You should smell garlic, toasted bread, and melted cheese, not burning garlic or burning bread.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cheese should look melted and bubbling, with a few light brown spots, and the edges of the bread should look golden, not burnt.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cheese should look melted and bubbling, with a few light brown spots, and the edges of the bread should look golden, not burnt.

SAFETY
Use mitts. The pan and melted cheese are very hot. If you use a broiler, do not walk away. Mince garlic on a stable board. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of red pepper flakes or dried oregano is a good extra. This is best eaten at once.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
