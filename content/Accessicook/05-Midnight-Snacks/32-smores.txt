ACCESSICOOK
Smores
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
The campfire snack of graham crackers, chocolate, and a toasted marshmallow, made in an oven, toaster oven, or skillet instead of over an open fire. The marshmallow should be hot and soft, and the chocolate should be slightly melted. The filling will be extremely hot at first.

SERVINGS
2 to 4 snacks

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 4 graham cracker sheets, broken into 8 squares
- 2 milk-chocolate bars, broken into 4 pieces, or 4 large pieces of chocolate
- 4 large marshmallows

TACTILE TIPS
A toasted marshmallow should feel puffed and very soft, with a slightly set or crisp outside if you browned it, not raw and firm, and not burnt and bitter. The chocolate should feel slightly softened once the hot marshmallow sits on it. The assembled snack should be cooled briefly before you eat, because the center can burn your mouth.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven or toaster oven to 400 degrees F, or set a skillet over medium-low heat.

2. Set 4 graham squares on a pan.

3. Add a piece of chocolate to each.

4. For the oven: set the marshmallows on the other 4 graham squares, or on a lined pan, and heat 3 to 6 minutes, until the marshmallows feel puffed and soft, and the chocolate is slightly melted.

5. Watch closely, because marshmallows can go from toasted to burnt quickly.

6. For a skillet: set the marshmallows in a dry skillet and turn them often, 2 to 4 minutes, until they feel puffed and soft and you smell toasted sugar, not burning.

7. Press a hot marshmallow onto each chocolate-topped cracker, then cap with the remaining graham squares.

8. Cool 1 minute, then eat.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The marshmallows should feel puffed and very soft, and they should smell like toasted sugar, not burning. The chocolate should feel slightly melted under the marshmallow. The filling will be extremely hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The marshmallows should look puffed and lightly golden, not white and raw, and not black. The chocolate should look slightly softened. The crackers should still look intact, not scorched.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The marshmallows should look puffed and lightly golden, not white and raw, and not black. The chocolate should look slightly softened. The crackers should still look intact, not scorched.

SAFETY
Use mitts. Melted sugar and marshmallow can cause a serious burn. Cool 1 minute before you eat. If you use a broiler, do not walk away, and keep the pan a safe distance from the element. Do not toast marshmallows on a bare stovetop gas flame in this recipe. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A peanut butter smear on the cracker is a good extra if no one has a peanut allergy. These are best eaten at once.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
