Peanut Butter and Jelly Sandwich
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
The familiar peanut butter and jelly sandwich, written out so you can make it by feel. If you cook for someone with a peanut or nut allergy, skip this recipe entirely and see the cheese or hummus snacks in this chapter instead.

SERVINGS
1 sandwich

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 2 slices sandwich bread
- 2 tablespoons peanut butter
- 1 to 2 tablespoons jelly or jam

TACTILE TIPS
Soft bread can tear if the peanut butter is very thick and cold. Let a stiff jar sit at room temperature a few minutes, or stir it. The finished sandwich should feel evenly filled, not dry in a corner and not dripping.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Lay the two slices of bread on a clean plate or board.

2. Spread the peanut butter evenly on one slice, all the way toward the edges.

3. Spread the jelly on the other slice.

4. If you put both on the same slice, the jelly can slide.

5. Press the slices together, filling sides in.

6. Cut the sandwich if you like, using a gentle sawing motion so the filling does not squeeze out.

7. Serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The sandwich should feel evenly filled. You should smell peanut butter and fruit. The bread should feel intact, not torn into holes.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The filling should look even when you cut the sandwich, with no large dry corners and no big drip of jelly.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The filling should look even when you cut the sandwich, with no large dry corners and no big drip of jelly.

SAFETY
Do not serve this to anyone with a peanut or nut allergy, and keep it away from other food if you are cooking for someone who cannot eat peanuts. Use a clean knife. If you rinse the knife between spreads, dry it so the bread does not get wet. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Crunchy or creamy peanut butter both work. A ripe banana, sliced, can go on top of the peanut butter for a more filling snack. For a toasted version, toast the bread first, then add the spreads, because a filled sandwich is messy in a toaster.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
