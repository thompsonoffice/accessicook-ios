Microwave Baked Potato
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A russet or similar potato cooked in the microwave until the center is soft, then topped like a baked potato. You must pierce the skin before cooking, or steam can build up and the potato can burst. For oven-baked potatoes, see Baked Potatoes in the Misc chapter.

SERVINGS
1 potato

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A microwave-safe mug or dish
- A microwave-safe cover or plate, vented
- A dry mitt or thick cloth for the hot dish

INGREDIENTS
- 1 medium russet potato, or another baking potato, scrubbed
- 1 teaspoon oil or butter, plus more for serving
- A pinch of salt
- Optional toppings: shredded cheese, sour cream, cooked bacon crumbles, or sliced green onion

TACTILE TIPS
The potato should feel firm and heavy for its size, with no soft or wet spots. After cooking, it should yield easily when you squeeze it gently with a mitt on your hand, and a fork should slide into the center with almost no resistance. The potato will be hotter inside than the skin suggests.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Scrub the potato and dry it.

2. Pierce the skin 5 or 6 times with a fork, all around, so steam can escape.

3. Rub the potato with a little oil or butter and a pinch of salt if you like. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

4. Set it on a microwave-safe plate.

5. Microwave 4 minutes.

6. Turn the potato.

7. Microwave 3 to 5 minutes more, until a fork slides into the center easily and the potato feels soft when squeezed with a mitt.

8. If the center is still firm, add 1 minute at a time.

9. Let the potato sit 2 minutes.

10. Cut it open carefully.

11. Add butter, salt, and any toppings.

12. If you add leftover meat or fully cooked bacon that was chilled, the toppings should be heated until 165 degrees F, or added very hot.

13. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: A fork should slide into the center easily. The potato should feel soft when squeezed with a mitt. You should smell cooked potato, not burning. The plate and potato will be very hot. Let it sit 2 minutes so the center finishes cooking.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The skin should look slightly slack, not tight and raw, and not scorched. The inside should look fluffy and dry, not wet and hard in the center.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The skin should look slightly slack, not tight and raw, and not scorched. The inside should look fluffy and dry, not wet and hard in the center.

SAFETY
Pierce the skin before cooking. Use a microwave-safe plate, never metal. Use a mitt to turn and to squeeze-test the potato. Steam escapes when you cut it open, so cut slowly and keep your face back. The inside is extremely hot. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A large potato can take 10 minutes or more. Two potatoes will take longer than one. For a crispier skin, finish the microwaved potato in a 425 degree F oven for 10 minutes if you want to take that extra step.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
