Frozen Burrito Reheat
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A method for heating a store-bought frozen burrito until the center is hot, 165 degrees F. Follow the package when it gives a time, then confirm with a thermometer or a careful check of the center. Frozen burritos can be cold in the middle when the tortilla is already hot.

SERVINGS
1 burrito

TOTAL TIME
About 5 to 20 minutes, depending on the package and method

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A microwave-safe mug or dish
- A microwave-safe cover or plate, vented
- A dry mitt or thick cloth for the hot dish

INGREDIENTS
- 1 frozen burrito

TACTILE TIPS
The package will tell you whether to use a microwave, oven, or both. After heating, the center filling should feel steaming hot, 165 degrees F, not icy or only lukewarm. Let the burrito sit 1 or 2 minutes so the heat evens out.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Read the package for the recommended method and time, and for whether to wrap the burrito in a paper towel or foil. Do not put foil in the microwave.

2. For a microwave: set the burrito on a microwave-safe plate, wrap it in a paper towel if the package says to, and heat for the package time, usually 1 1/2 to 3 minutes, turning halfway if you can.

3. For an oven: heat the oven to the package temperature, often 350 or 375 degrees F, and bake for the package time, often 15 to 25 minutes, turning once if directed.

4. Check the center with a thermometer. It should read 165 degrees F.

5. If it does not, add short bursts of heat, 20 seconds in the microwave or 5 minutes in the oven, and check again.

6. Let the burrito sit 1 or 2 minutes, then unwrap carefully and eat.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The center should be 165 degrees F and feel steaming hot, not icy. You should smell the filling, such as beans, cheese, or meat, not burning tortilla. Let it sit 1 or 2 minutes, because the filling holds heat.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tortilla should look hot and slightly steamy, not scorched. A little filling may bubble at the ends. That can be a sign the center is getting hot, but the thermometer is still the test.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tortilla should look hot and slightly steamy, not scorched. A little filling may bubble at the ends. That can be a sign the center is getting hot, but the thermometer is still the test.

SAFETY
Reheat to 165 degrees F. Use mitts. Steam will escape when you unwrap, so keep your face back. Never put foil in the microwave. The filling can be hotter than the tortilla. Do not eat from a burrito that is still cold in the center. Cook casseroles to 165 degrees F in the center.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A microwave can leave a soft tortilla. An oven gives a firmer wrap but takes longer. A combination, microwave first, then a few minutes in a skillet or oven, is a good middle path if the package allows it.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
