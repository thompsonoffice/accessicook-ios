ACCESSICOOK
Grilled Cheese
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A toasted cheese sandwich cooked in a skillet until the bread is crisp and the cheese is melted. Low to medium heat gives the cheese time to melt before the bread burns. This is a classic late-night meal, and it also works for lunch.

SERVINGS
1 sandwich

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 2 slices sandwich bread
- 1 to 2 teaspoons butter or oil, for the bread or the pan
- 2 slices cheese, or 1/2 cup (half a cup) shredded cheese, such as cheddar or American

TACTILE TIPS
Soft butter spreads more evenly than cold butter and is less likely to tear the bread. The finished sandwich should feel crisp on the outside and fully melted and stretchy inside, not cool and still sliced in the middle.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Spread a thin layer of butter on the outside of each slice of bread, or heat a little butter or oil in a skillet over medium-low to medium heat.

2. Place one slice of bread in the skillet, buttered side down if you buttered the bread.

3. Add the cheese, then the second slice, buttered side up.

4. Cook 3 to 5 minutes, until the bottom feels crisp and golden, not soft and pale, and not burnt. You should hear a gentle sizzle, not a harsh scorch.

5. Turn the sandwich with a spatula.

6. Cook 3 to 5 minutes more, until the second side feels crisp and the cheese feels fully melted.

7. If the bread is brown before the cheese melts, lower the heat and cover the pan for a minute.

8. Move the sandwich to a plate.

9. Cool 1 minute, then cut if you like, and serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The bread should feel crisp. The cheese should feel fully melted and stretchy. You should smell toasted bread and melted cheese, not burning butter. The sizzle should stay gentle.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: Both sides should look even golden brown, not pale and not dark brown or black. When you cut or pull the sandwich, the cheese should look melted and stretchy, not still in a solid slice.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. Both sides should look even golden brown, not pale and not dark brown or black. When you cut or pull the sandwich, the cheese should look melted and stretchy, not still in a solid slice.

SAFETY
The pan is hot. Use a spatula, not your fingers, to turn the sandwich. Melted cheese can burn your mouth, so wait 1 minute. If you use a gas flame, keep the bread and paper towels away from the burner. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A lid helps the cheese melt. Tomato soup from the Lunch chapter is a classic partner. Add a slice of tomato or a little leftover ham, and heat until the center is hot, 165 degrees F if the filling was leftover.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
