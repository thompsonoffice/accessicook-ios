ACCESSICOOK
Cereal and Milk
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A bowl of cereal with cold milk, written out as a late-night snack so it is easy to make in a quiet kitchen. Use a bowl with a wide, stable base, and pour the milk slowly.

SERVINGS
1 bowl

TOTAL TIME
About 2 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 to 1 1/2 cups (one to one and a half cups) dry cereal
- 1/2 to 3/4 cup (half to three-quarters of a cup) cold milk, or enough to come partway up the cereal

TACTILE TIPS
The bowl should feel stable on the counter before you pour. Start with less milk. You can always add more. The cereal should feel wet at the bottom and still slightly crisp at the top if you like contrast, or fully soaked if you prefer it soft.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Set a bowl on a clear counter.

2. Pour the dry cereal into the bowl.

3. Pour the milk slowly, aiming for the center, until you can feel or hear that the cereal is partly covered.

4. Eat at once, because most cereals soften as they sit.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The milk should smell fresh, not sour. The cereal should smell as you expect, not stale or cardboard-like. The bowl should feel comfortably full, not so full that it sloshes.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The milk should look high enough to moisten the cereal without flooding the bowl and making it hard to carry.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The milk should look high enough to moisten the cereal without flooding the bowl and making it hard to carry.

SAFETY
Use a stable bowl. Wipe drips so the bowl is not slippery. Keep milk refrigerated, and do not leave the carton out. If the milk smells sour or has curdled, throw it out. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A sliced banana or a handful of berries can go on top. If you want a dairy-free bowl, use a milk alternative you know is fresh.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
