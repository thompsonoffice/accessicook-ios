Root Beer Float
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A tall glass of root beer with a scoop of vanilla ice cream. The soda will foam, so pour slowly and use a glass with room at the top. This also works with cola or other sodas.

SERVINGS
1 glass

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 scoop vanilla ice cream, about 1/2 cup (half a cup)
- 3/4 to 1 cup cold root beer

TACTILE TIPS
The glass should feel tall and stable. After you add the soda, the foam will rise quickly. Pause if it nears the rim. The ice cream should feel firm when it goes in, then slightly softened as you eat and drink.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Put the scoop of ice cream in a tall glass.

2. Pour the root beer slowly down the side of the glass, or over the back of a spoon, until the glass is comfortably full, leaving room for foam.

3. Pause if the foam rises too fast, then add more soda.

4. Add a straw or a long spoon, and serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: You should hear a strong fizz as the soda hits the ice cream. The drink should smell like root beer and vanilla, and it should feel very cold. If the foam threatens to spill, wait, then pour more slowly.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The foam should look tall and creamy, and the ice cream should still be visible as a scoop, not instantly melted.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The foam should look tall and creamy, and the ice cream should still be visible as a scoop, not instantly melted.

SAFETY
Pour slowly so the glass does not overflow and make the outside slippery. Use a stable glass on a clear counter. Caffeine and sugar can be a lot late at night if that matters for you. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Chocolate ice cream is a good change. A smaller scoop in a smaller glass is easier to manage.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
