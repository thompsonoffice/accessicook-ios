ACCESSICOOK
Popcorn
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
This recipe was on the original request list for the book.

Popcorn is a late-night staple, and you can make it on the stove or in the microwave. Both methods use sound as the main doneness cue: the pops start slow, get rapid, then slow again. When the gap between pops is about 2 seconds, it is time to stop. Stovetop popcorn uses hot oil, so stay with the pot.

SERVINGS
2 to 4, about 8 to 10 cups popped

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes for stovetop or microwave

DIFFICULTY
Beginner, with extra care around hot oil and steam

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large, heavy pot with a tight lid, for the stovetop method
- A large bowl
- Oven mitts
- A microwave, for the bag or bowl method
- A microwave-safe bowl and a microwave-safe plate, for microwave kernels

INGREDIENTS
- Stovetop method: 2 tablespoons vegetable oil, and 1/2 cup (half a cup) popcorn kernels
- Microwave kernel method: 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) popcorn kernels, and 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) oil, optional
- Or 1 bag microwave popcorn, any plain or lightly salted flavor you like
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) salt, or to taste, or 2 tablespoons melted butter, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Kernels feel small, hard, and pointed at one end. They should be dry. Oil is slick and liquid. Popped corn is light, irregular, and fragile. Unpopped kernels in the bowl are still hard and should be left in the bottom when you serve, so no one bites one.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Stovetop method: Put the oil and 3 test kernels in a large, heavy pot.

2. Cover the pot.

3. Set it over medium heat.

4. When those 3 kernels pop, add the rest of the 1/2 cup of kernels. Rest means let cooked meat sit so the juices settle back into the meat.

5. Shake the pot so they sit in one layer.

6. Cover again, leaving the lid just slightly ajar if your pot lid allows a little steam to escape, but keep it mostly closed.

7. Shake the pot now and then as the popping gets going.

8. Use mitts. The pot and lid will be very hot.

9. Listen for the popping to slow to about 2 seconds between pops, then turn off the heat.

10. Let the pot sit 30 seconds so late kernels can finish.

11. Pour the popcorn into a large bowl.

12. Season with salt or melted butter, tossing to coat.

13. Microwave bag method: Place the bag in the microwave with the correct side up, following the bag if you or a helper can check the arrows.

14. Cook on high.

15. Listen, and stop the oven when pops are about 2 seconds apart, even if the package time is not up.

16. Let the bag sit 15 seconds.

17. Open it away from your face, because steam will rush out.

18. Microwave bowl method: Put 1/3 cup kernels in a large microwave-safe bowl.

19. Cover with a microwave-safe plate.

20. Cook on high 2 to 4 minutes, listening and stopping when pops are about 2 seconds apart.

21. Let it stand 30 seconds.

22. Uncover away from your face.

23. Season and serve.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The key cue is sound. Popping starts with a few pops, becomes rapid, then slows. Stop when you can count about 2 seconds between pops. You should smell toasted corn, not burnt oil or burnt corn. The pot, bag, or bowl will be very hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: Most kernels should be open, white, and fluffy. A few unpopped kernels at the bottom are normal. The popcorn should not look scorched or dark brown. A microwave bag may puff up and then settle.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. Most kernels should be open, white, and fluffy. A few unpopped kernels at the bottom are normal. The popcorn should not look scorched or dark brown. A microwave bag may puff up and then settle.

SAFETY
Never leave popcorn unattended. Hot oil, steam, and a hot lid can burn you. Use mitts. Open lids and bags away from your face. Do not use an empty pot on high heat. If the popcorn smells burnt, turn off the heat and leave the lid on until it cools. Do not put metal in the microwave. Stop a microwave bag early rather than letting it scorch. Unpopped kernels are a tooth hazard. Leave them in the bowl. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little more salt or butter can be added at the table. For cinnamon sugar popcorn, toss with a teaspoon of sugar and a pinch of cinnamon while it is warm. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). If many kernels stay unpopped, the heat may have been too low, or the kernels may be old and dry.

LEFTOVERS
Popcorn is best fresh. Store cooled leftovers in a loosely closed bag and eat the same day. It will go stale and does not reheat well. Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
