Ants on a Log
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Celery sticks filled with peanut butter and topped with raisins, which are the "ants." This is a no-cook snack. If anyone has a peanut allergy, use a safe seed butter or cream cheese instead.

SERVINGS
8 to 12 pieces

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer

INGREDIENTS
- 4 celery stalks
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) peanut butter, or cream cheese
- 1/4 cup (a quarter cup) raisins or chocolate chips

TACTILE TIPS
Celery should feel firm and crisp, not limp and rubbery. After you wash it, dry it so the filling stays put. The groove in the celery is the place to spread the peanut butter.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Wash the celery and pat it dry.

2. Trim the ends on a stable board.

3. Cut each stalk into 2 or 3 shorter logs.

4. Spread peanut butter or cream cheese into the groove of each piece.

5. Press raisins or chips onto the filling in a loose line.

6. Serve at once, or cover and refrigerate up to 1 day.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The celery should feel crisp and should smell fresh and green, not sour. The filling should feel even and thick enough to hold the raisins. Raisins should feel soft and sticky, not rock-hard, though harder raisins still work.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The filling should look even in the groove, and the raisins should look pressed on, not rolling off a dry, thin smear.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The filling should look even in the groove, and the raisins should look pressed on, not rolling off a dry, thin smear.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a sharp knife, slowly. Do not serve peanut butter to anyone with a peanut allergy. Wash the celery well, including the groove, where dirt can hide. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cream cheese and dried cranberries are a good variation. Cut the celery into shorter pieces if you want an easier bite.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
