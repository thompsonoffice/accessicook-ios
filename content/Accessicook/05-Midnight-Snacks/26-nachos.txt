ACCESSICOOK
Nachos
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Tortilla chips topped with melted cheese and simple toppings, baked just until the cheese melts. This is a shareable late-night plate. If you add leftover meat, reheat the finished nachos until the toppings are 165 degrees F.

SERVINGS
4 as a snack, or 2 as a late meal

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans

INGREDIENTS
- 8 ounces tortilla chips, about half of a large bag
- 2 cups shredded cheddar or Mexican-blend cheese
- Optional toppings: sliced jalapenos, drained canned black beans, leftover cooked meat, or diced tomato
- Optional finishes: sour cream, salsa, or guacamole, added after baking

TACTILE TIPS
Use sturdy chips so they do not collapse under the cheese. The cheese should feel fully melted and stretchy, not just warm and still shredded. If you add leftover meat or beans, the center of a topped chip should feel steaming hot.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to 375 degrees F.

2. Line a sheet pan with parchment, or use a broiler-safe platter.

3. Spread the chips in a loose, even layer.

4. Avoid a deep pile, or the bottom chips will stay cold and the top will overbrown.

5. Sprinkle the cheese evenly, including some in the middle, not only on the corners.

6. Add any oven-safe toppings, such as jalapenos, beans, or leftover cooked meat.

7. Bake 8 to 12 minutes, until the cheese is fully melted and bubbling, and any leftover meat is 165 degrees F.

8. Add cold finishes such as sour cream or salsa after you take the pan out.

9. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The cheese should feel fully melted and stretchy. You should smell toasted corn and melted cheese, not burning chips. Any leftover meat or beans should feel steaming hot, 165 degrees F.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cheese should look melted and bubbling, with a few light brown spots, not still shredded and not burnt. The chips at the edges may look slightly darker. That is all right if they are not black.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cheese should look melted and bubbling, with a few light brown spots, not still shredded and not burnt. The chips at the edges may look slightly darker. That is all right if they are not black.

SAFETY
Use mitts. The pan and cheese are very hot. Melted cheese can burn your mouth, so let the plate sit 1 or 2 minutes. Reheat leftover meat to 165 degrees F. If you use pickled jalapenos, drain them so the chips do not get soggy. Drain means pour off the liquid. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a smaller snack, use a microwave-safe plate and see Microwave Nachos in this chapter. Leftover nachos get soggy, so make only what you will eat.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
