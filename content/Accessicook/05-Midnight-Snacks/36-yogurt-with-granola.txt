Yogurt with Granola
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple bowl of yogurt with granola and optional fruit. This is close to the Yogurt Parfait in the Breakfast chapter, written here as a smaller late-night snack. Add the granola just before you eat so it stays crisp.

SERVINGS
1 bowl

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup plain or vanilla yogurt
- 1/3 cup (a third of a cup) granola, from the Breakfast chapter or store-bought
- Optional: 1/2 cup (half a cup) berries or sliced banana
- Optional: a drizzle of honey

TACTILE TIPS
The yogurt should feel cool and smooth, not watery and not lumpy in a way that suggests it has spoiled. Granola should feel crisp and dry. Fruit should feel fresh.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Spoon the yogurt into a bowl.

2. Add the fruit if you are using it.

3. Sprinkle the granola on top, and drizzle with honey if you like.

4. Eat at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The yogurt should smell fresh and tangy, not sharply sour or yeasty in an off way, and it should not have a moldy smell. If the yogurt smells or looks wrong, throw it out. Granola should smell toasted, not rancid. Fruit should smell fresh.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The granola should look crisp on top, not already soaked and gray. The fruit should look fresh.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The granola should look crisp on top, not already soaked and gray. The fruit should look fresh.

SAFETY
Keep yogurt refrigerated, and do not leave the carton out. If anyone has a nut allergy, check the granola ingredients, because many granolas contain nuts. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Greek yogurt is thicker and more filling. See Granola in the Breakfast chapter if you want to make your own.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
