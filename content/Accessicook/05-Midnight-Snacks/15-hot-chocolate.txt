Hot Chocolate
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A simple stovetop cocoa made with milk, cocoa powder, and sugar. Heat the milk gently so it does not scorch or boil over. This is also listed here because it is a late-night drink as often as it is a winter afternoon one.

SERVINGS
2 mugs

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups milk
- 2 tablespoons unsweetened cocoa powder
- 2 tablespoons sugar, or to taste
- A pinch of salt
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) vanilla extract, optional
- Optional toppings: whipped cream or marshmallows

TACTILE TIPS
The cocoa should feel smooth, not dusty and not gritty with dry cocoa lumps. The drink should feel steaming hot, but not so hot that it burns your mouth, and it should coat the mug lightly, like thin chocolate milk that is a little richer.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Whisk the cocoa, sugar, and salt with 2 tablespoons of the milk in a small pot until the mix feels smooth, like a paste. Whisk means beat with a fork or a whisk so there are no lumps.

2. Add the remaining milk.

3. Stir until combined.

4. Heat over medium-low heat, stirring often, until the cocoa is steaming hot and you see small bubbles at the edge, not a rolling boil.

5. Take the pot off the heat.

6. Stir in the optional vanilla.

7. Pour into mugs.

8. Add toppings if you like.

9. Let it sit 1 minute, then drink.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The cocoa should feel smooth and steaming hot, not gritty. You should smell chocolate and milk, not scorched milk. If you smell scorching, take the pot off the heat at once and do not scrape the bottom into the mugs.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cocoa should look smooth and even, not dusty with floating dry cocoa, and not covered in a thick skin if you serve it at once. You may see a little steam.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cocoa should look smooth and even, not dusty with floating dry cocoa, and not covered in a thick skin if you serve it at once. You may see a little steam.

SAFETY
Stir often so the milk does not scorch. The pot, steam, and mugs will be very hot. Pour slowly. Let the drink sit 1 minute so you do not burn your mouth. Refrigerate leftover cocoa within 2 hours, and reheat until steaming hot, stirring, without boiling it hard. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A little cinnamon or a square of chopped chocolate, stirred in at the end, makes a richer mug. See also the drinks in the Misc chapter for lemonade, iced tea, and cider.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
