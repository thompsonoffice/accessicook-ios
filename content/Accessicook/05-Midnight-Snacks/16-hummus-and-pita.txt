Hummus and Pita
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Store-bought or homemade hummus with warm pita or crackers and optional raw vegetables. For homemade hummus, see the Misc chapter. Warming the pita makes it more flexible and less likely to crack.

SERVINGS
2 as a snack

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes, or 5 minutes if the pita is already soft

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup hummus, store-bought or homemade
- 2 pita breads, or a handful of crackers or cut vegetables, such as carrots or cucumber
- Optional: a drizzle of olive oil and a pinch of paprika, for the top of the hummus

TACTILE TIPS
Hummus should feel smooth and thick, like a dip that holds a soft peak on a spoon, not watery and not pasty-dry. Pita should feel warm and flexible after heating, not brittle and not wet.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Spoon the hummus into a small bowl.

2. Add optional oil and paprika on top.

3. Warm the pita in a dry skillet 30 to 60 seconds per side, or in a 350 degree F oven for 3 to 5 minutes, until it feels warm and flexible.

4. Crackers or raw vegetables can be used as they are.

5. Cut the pita into wedges if you like, on a stable board.

6. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The hummus should smell like sesame, lemon, and chickpea, not sour or fermented. The pita should feel warm and flexible and should smell toasted, not burning. Vegetables, if used, should feel crisp and fresh.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The hummus should look smooth, and the pita should look soft and slightly puffed, not scorched.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The hummus should look smooth, and the pita should look soft and slightly puffed, not scorched.

SAFETY
If you warm pita in the oven, use mitts. Keep opened hummus refrigerated, and do not leave it out more than 2 hours. If homemade or opened hummus smells sour or looks moldy, throw it out. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
This is a quieter snack than nachos and can be a lighter late-night plate. See also the Hummus Veggie Wrap in the Lunch chapter if you want a fuller meal.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
