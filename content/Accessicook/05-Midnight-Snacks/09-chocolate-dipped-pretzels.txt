Chocolate-Dipped Pretzels
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Pretzels dipped in melted chocolate and left to set. This is a no-bake sweet snack. Melt the chocolate gently so it stays smooth and does not scorch or seize, which means it turns stiff and grainy.

SERVINGS
About 20 pretzel pieces

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes, plus setting time

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 4 ounces chocolate chips or chopped chocolate
- 20 pretzel twists or rods, more or less, depending on how heavily you dip
- Optional: a pinch of coarse salt, or sprinkles, for the top

TACTILE TIPS
Melted chocolate should feel smooth and fluid, like a thin sauce, not thick and pasty, and not burnt and dry. After setting, the coating should feel firm and slightly snap, not wet and not tacky if the room is reasonably cool.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Line a pan with parchment.

2. Melt the chocolate in a microwave-safe bowl in 20 to 30 second bursts, stirring after each burst, until it is almost melted, then stir until smooth.

3. Or set the bowl over a pot of barely simmering water, without letting the bowl touch the water, and stir until smooth. Simmering means small, gentle bubbles.

4. Dip each pretzel partway into the chocolate.

5. Let the extra drip off.

6. Set the pretzel on the parchment.

7. Add a pinch of salt or sprinkles before the chocolate sets, if you like. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

8. Let the pretzels sit until the chocolate is firm, about 20 to 40 minutes at a cool room temperature, or 10 minutes in the refrigerator.

9. Store in a closed container, in a cool place, for up to a week.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The melted chocolate should feel smooth and smell like chocolate, not scorched. After setting, the coating should feel firm. The pretzels should feel crisp, not stale.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The chocolate should look smooth and even, not dull and streaky from seizing. The pretzels should look half-coated and set.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The chocolate should look smooth and even, not dull and streaky from seizing. The pretzels should look half-coated and set.

SAFETY
Chocolate and any bowl set over steam will be hot. Wipe moisture out of the bowl and off utensils, because a little water can make chocolate seize. If you use the microwave, use short bursts and stir, because chocolate can burn even when it does not look different at first. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
White chocolate can be used the same way, still melted gently. If the chocolate is too thick for dipping, stir in 1 teaspoon of neutral oil.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
