Chili Cheese Fries
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Oven fries or leftover fries topped with hot chili and melted cheese. Make or reheat the chili until it is 165 degrees F, then add cheese so it melts. This is a heavy, late-night plate. See Chili in the Supper chapter, and Oven Fries in this chapter.

SERVINGS
2 to 3 as a late meal

TOTAL TIME
About 20 minutes with already-cooked fries and chili, or longer if you start from scratch

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 recipe Oven Fries, or 4 cups cooked fries, leftover or frozen fries cooked until crisp
- 1 1/2 cups (one and a half cups) chili, leftover or canned, reheated to 165 degrees F
- 1 cup shredded cheddar cheese
- Optional: sliced green onion or a spoon of sour cream, added at the end

TACTILE TIPS
The fries should feel crisp before you add the chili, because they will soften under the toppings. The chili should feel steaming hot, 165 degrees F. The cheese should feel melted.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Cook or reheat the fries until they feel crisp.

2. Set them on an oven-safe platter or a sheet pan.

3. Reheat the chili in a pot or in the microwave until it is 165 degrees F and bubbling.

4. Spoon the hot chili over the fries.

5. Sprinkle the cheese on top.

6. Heat in a 400 degree F oven 3 to 5 minutes, until the cheese is melted, or cover the platter for a minute so the residual heat melts the cheese.

7. Add any cold toppings.

8. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The chili should be steaming hot, 165 degrees F. The cheese should feel melted. You should smell chili, toasted potato, and cheese, not burning. The platter will be very hot.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cheese should look melted, and the chili should look bubbling in spots. The fries will look softer under the chili, with crisper pieces at the edges.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cheese should look melted, and the chili should look bubbling in spots. The fries will look softer under the chili, with crisper pieces at the edges.

SAFETY
Reheat leftover chili and leftover fries to 165 degrees F. Use mitts. The plate, chili, and cheese are extremely hot. Let the dish sit 1 or 2 minutes before you eat. Cook casseroles to 165 degrees F in the center.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Vegetarian chili works the same way. This does not keep well once assembled, so top only what you will eat.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
