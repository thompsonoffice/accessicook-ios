Ice Cream Sundae
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Scoops of ice cream with simple toppings, assembled just before you eat so the ice cream does not melt. Keep the ice cream in the freezer until the toppings are ready.

SERVINGS
1 sundae

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 2 scoops ice cream, about 1 cup
- 2 tablespoons chocolate sauce, caramel sauce, or fruit sauce
- Optional toppings: whipped cream, sliced banana, cherries, or sprinkles
- Optional crunch: 1 tablespoon chopped nuts, only if no one has a nut allergy, or crushed cookies

TACTILE TIPS
Ice cream that is too hard to scoop can sit on the counter 2 or 3 minutes, not longer. A warmed scoop or spoon, dipped in warm water and dried, helps. The sundae should be eaten while the ice cream still feels firm, not soupy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Set out a cold bowl and your toppings.

2. Scoop the ice cream into the bowl.

3. Spoon the sauce over the ice cream.

4. Add any optional toppings.

5. Serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The ice cream should feel firm and very cold, and it should smell clean and creamy, not freezer-burned or off. Sauce should feel pourable. Nuts, if used, should smell fresh, not rancid.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The scoops should look rounded and still firm, with sauce on top, not already melted into a puddle.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The scoops should look rounded and still firm, with sauce on top, not already melted into a puddle.

SAFETY
A scoop can slip. Work over the bowl or the carton. Do not serve nuts to anyone with a nut allergy. Ice cream is perishable. Do not leave the carton out. Return it to the freezer at once. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A pinch of salt on caramel sauce makes it taste rounder. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon). See also Milkshake and Root Beer Float in this chapter if you want a drinking version.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
