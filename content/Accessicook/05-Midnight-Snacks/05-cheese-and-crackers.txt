Cheese and Crackers
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A no-cook plate of cheese, crackers, and simple extras. This is useful when you do not want to turn on the stove. Keep the cheese cold until you plate it, then serve soon.

SERVINGS
1 or 2 as a snack

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 2 ounces cheese, such as cheddar, Swiss, or a mix, sliced or cubed
- A handful of crackers, about 8 to 12
- Optional extras: apple slices, grapes, olives, or a few nuts if no one has a nut allergy

TACTILE TIPS
Cheese straight from the refrigerator is firm and easier to slice. After a few minutes on the plate it smells stronger and spreads more easily on a cracker. Feel for a clean, even slice, and keep fruit dry so the crackers do not get soggy.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Slice or cube the cheese on a clean board, using a cut-resistant glove on the hand that holds the cheese if you need it.

2. Arrange the cheese, crackers, and any extras on a plate, with a little space so you can find each item by touch.

3. Serve at once, or cover and refrigerate if you will eat in the next hour, then bring it out just before you eat.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The cheese should feel cool and clean-cut, not slimy. You should smell the cheese you expect, not sourness or ammonia. Crackers should feel crisp and dry. Fruit should feel fresh, not fermented.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cheese should look evenly sliced or cubed, and the plate should look uncrowded so each item is easy to pick up.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cheese should look evenly sliced or cubed, and the plate should look uncrowded so each item is easy to pick up.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a sharp knife, slowly. Keep perishable cheese refrigerated until serving, and do not leave the plate out more than 2 hours, or more than 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Wash the knife and board after use. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Softer cheeses can be served in a small bowl with a knife for spreading. See also Fruit and Cheese Plate in the Misc chapter for a larger version.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
