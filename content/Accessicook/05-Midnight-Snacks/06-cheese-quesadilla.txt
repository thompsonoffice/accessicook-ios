Cheese Quesadilla
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A tortilla folded over cheese and cooked in a skillet until the tortilla is crisp in spots and the cheese is melted. This is a smaller, later-night version of the Quesadillas in the Lunch chapter. Keep fillings light so the quesadilla turns easily.

SERVINGS
1 quesadilla

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large skillet or saute pan
- A heat-safe spatula or wooden spoon
- A splatter screen

INGREDIENTS
- 1 large flour tortilla, or 2 small tortillas
- 1/2 to 3/4 cup (half to three-quarters of a cup) shredded cheese
- 1 teaspoon oil or butter, for the pan
- Optional: a little salsa or leftover cooked vegetables, kept dry

TACTILE TIPS
The tortilla should feel flexible, not stale and cracked. After cooking, it should feel crisp in spots, and the cheese should feel fully melted and stretchy, not still shredded in the center.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oil or butter in a skillet over medium heat.

2. Lay the tortilla in the pan.

3. Sprinkle the cheese over half of it, leaving a small border.

4. Add any optional dry fillings.

5. Fold the bare half over the filling. Fold means gently turn the mixture over itself so you do not knock out the air.

6. Press gently with a spatula.

7. Cook 2 to 3 minutes, until the bottom feels crisp in spots and you smell toasted tortilla, not burning.

8. Turn and cook 2 to 3 minutes more, until the second side feels crisp in spots and the cheese is fully melted.

9. Move to a plate.

10. Cool 1 minute, then cut into wedges and serve.

11. Salsa or sour cream can go on the side.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The tortilla should feel crisp in spots. The cheese should feel fully melted. You should smell toasted flour and melted cheese, not burning. The sizzle should stay gentle.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The tortilla should look lightly golden in spots, not pale and not black. When you cut it, the cheese should look melted and stretchy.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The tortilla should look lightly golden in spots, not pale and not black. When you cut it, the cheese should look melted and stretchy.

SAFETY
The pan is hot. Use a spatula to turn the quesadilla. Melted cheese can burn your mouth, so wait 1 minute. If you add leftover meat, heat the center to 165 degrees F. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A lid helps the cheese melt. Do not overfill, or the filling will spill when you turn it.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
