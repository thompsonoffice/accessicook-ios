ACCESSICOOK
Trail Mix
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A dry mix of nuts, seeds, dried fruit, and an optional sweet piece such as chocolate chips. This is a no-cook snack you can portion into small containers. If anyone has a nut allergy, skip the nuts and use seeds, pretzels, and dried fruit only, or skip this recipe.

SERVINGS
About 10 snack servings

TOTAL TIME
About 10 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 1 cup roasted nuts, such as peanuts, almonds, or cashews, or a mix
- 1 cup seeds or extra nuts, such as pumpkin seeds or sunflower seeds
- 1 cup dried fruit, such as raisins, cranberries, or chopped dried apricots
- 1 cup crunchy extras, such as pretzel pieces or cereal squares
- 1/2 cup (half a cup) chocolate chips or chocolate candies, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Everything should feel dry. If the nuts smell like old oil or paint, they are rancid. Throw them out. Dried fruit should feel slightly leathery and sticky, not wet and not rock-hard unless you like it that way.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Combine all the ingredients in a large bowl.

2. Toss with your hands or a spoon until the mix feels even, with no one ingredient all at the bottom.

3. Store in a closed container at room temperature for up to 2 weeks, or portion into small bags or cups.

4. Serve in small handfuls. This is rich, so a little goes a long way.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The mix should smell toasted and sweet, not rancid or stale. Everything should feel dry. Chocolate, if used, should feel firm, not melted, unless the room is very warm, in which case leave it out or store the mix in a cooler place.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The mix should look even, with visible nuts, fruit, and crunchy pieces, not a pile of the heaviest items at the bottom.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The mix should look even, with visible nuts, fruit, and crunchy pieces, not a pile of the heaviest items at the bottom.

SAFETY
Do not serve nuts to anyone with a nut allergy, and check labels for cross-contact if that matters for your household. Choking is a risk with whole nuts, especially if you serve this to anyone who needs softer food. Keep chocolate mixes out of a hot car or a very warm room. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Salted nuts make a sweeter-salty mix. Unsalted nuts let the dried fruit stand out. Change the ratios to suit the people eating.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
