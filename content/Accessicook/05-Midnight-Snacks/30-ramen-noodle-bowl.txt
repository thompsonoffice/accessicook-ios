ACCESSICOOK
Ramen Noodle Bowl
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Instant ramen cooked in a small pot, then improved with an egg, frozen vegetables, or leftover cooked meat. The noodles are done when they feel tender but still slightly firm, not mushy. If you add an egg, cook it until the white and yolk are firm, remembering that egg dishes should reach 160 degrees F if they are stirred in.

SERVINGS
1 bowl

TOTAL TIME
About 15 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A large pot with a lid
- A wooden spoon or ladle

INGREDIENTS
- 1 package instant ramen noodles, with or without the seasoning packet
- 2 cups water, or as the package directs
- Optional: 1 large egg
- Optional: 1/2 cup (half a cup) frozen peas or mixed vegetables
- Optional: leftover cooked chicken or tofu, for stirring in at the end
- Optional extras: a splash of soy sauce, a little sesame oil, or sliced green onion

TACTILE TIPS
The noodles should feel tender but still slightly springy, not hard in the center and not falling apart. Frozen vegetables should feel hot all the way through. Leftover meat should feel steaming hot. An egg added to the pot should feel set, not liquid and raw.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Bring the water to a boil in a small pot, meaning large, rolling bubbles.

2. Add the noodles and the optional frozen vegetables.

3. Cook 2 to 3 minutes, or as the package directs, stirring so the noodles separate.

4. Reduce the heat to a simmer, meaning small, gentle bubbles. Reduce means boil gently until some of the liquid evaporates and the sauce thickens. Simmer means cook just below a boil, with small, gentle bubbles.

5. Stir in the seasoning packet, or use soy sauce instead if you want less of the packet.

6. If you are adding an egg, crack it into a small cup first, then slide it into the simmering broth, or beat it and stir it in.

7. Cook until the egg is firm, or the white is set and the yolk is as firm as you want, at least 160 degrees F for a stirred-in egg dish.

8. Add leftover cooked meat or tofu, and heat until it is 165 degrees F if it was leftover.

9. Pour into a bowl.

10. Add any optional extras.

11. Let it sit 1 minute, then eat.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The noodles should feel tender and slightly springy. The broth should be steaming hot. You should smell savory broth and noodles, not a sour or stale seasoning smell. Leftover meat should feel steaming hot, 165 degrees F.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The noodles should look separated and tender, not a tight brick. Vegetables should look bright and hot. An egg should look set, not raw and glossy if you wanted it firm.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The noodles should look separated and tender, not a tight brick. Vegetables should look bright and hot. An egg should look set, not raw and glossy if you wanted it firm.

SAFETY
Boiling water can burn you. Pour slowly. The bowl will be very hot. If you add leftover meat, reheat it to 165 degrees F. Cook eggs until they are firm, or to 160 degrees F for a stirred egg. Instant packets are high in sodium, so you can use only part of the packet. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
A handful of spinach can wilt in the hot broth at the end. This is a good use for a leftover baked chicken breast, sliced and reheated in the soup.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
