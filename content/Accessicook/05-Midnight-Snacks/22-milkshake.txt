ACCESSICOOK
Milkshake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Ice cream and milk blended until thick and smooth. A blender works best. A large jar and a strong spoon can work if you let the ice cream soften slightly, but the texture will be lumpier.

SERVINGS
1 large glass

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A mixing bowl
- A whisk or fork

INGREDIENTS
- 2 cups ice cream
- 1/2 to 3/4 cup (half to three-quarters of a cup) cold milk
- 1/2 teaspoon (half a teaspoon) vanilla extract, optional, especially with vanilla ice cream
- Optional: 2 tablespoons chocolate sauce, or a banana, or a spoon of peanut butter if no one has a peanut allergy

TACTILE TIPS
Start with less milk. The shake should feel thick enough to drink through a straw with a little resistance, not thin like milk, and not so stiff that it will not pour.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Add the ice cream, 1/2 cup (half a cup) of the milk, and any optional flavorings to a blender.

2. Blend until the mix feels smooth, starting on a low speed so the blender does not splash.

3. If it is too thick to pour, add a little more milk and blend again.

4. Pour into a glass and serve at once.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The shake should feel thick, cold, and smooth. You should hear the blender change from a choppy sound to a smoother one as lumps disappear. You should smell ice cream and any mix-ins, not a burnt-motor smell from a blender that is stuck. If the blender stalls, stop and add a little milk, or stir, then start again.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The shake should look smooth and thick, with no large ice cream lumps, and it should mound slightly as it pours, not run like milk.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The shake should look smooth and thick, with no large ice cream lumps, and it should mound slightly as it pours, not run like milk.

SAFETY
Seat the blender lid firmly. Start on low. Never put a spoon in a running blender. Do not serve peanut butter shakes to anyone with a peanut allergy. Wash the blender soon, because dried shake is hard to clean. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a chocolate shake, use chocolate ice cream or chocolate sauce. For a malted shake, add 1 tablespoon malted milk powder if you have it.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
