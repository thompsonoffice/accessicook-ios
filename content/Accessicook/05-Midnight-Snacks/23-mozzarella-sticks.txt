ACCESSICOOK
Mozzarella Sticks
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Frozen mozzarella sticks baked or air-fried until the coating is crisp and the cheese is hot and melted, but not all leaked out. Follow the package time, then use the cues here. These are messy and very hot in the center at first.

SERVINGS
2 to 4 as a snack, about 8 to 12 sticks

TOTAL TIME
About 15 to 25 minutes from frozen

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A stable cutting board with a damp towel underneath
- A sharp chef's knife
- A cut-resistant glove for the hand that holds the food
- A rimmed baking sheet or the baking dish named in the ingredients
- Parchment paper or foil, when useful
- Long oven mitts
- A reserved landing space for hot pans
- A talking or instant-read food thermometer

INGREDIENTS
- 8 to 12 frozen mozzarella sticks
- Marinara sauce, warmed, for dipping, optional

TACTILE TIPS
Do not thaw the sticks first, or the cheese can leak before the coating crisps. The coating should feel crisp, and the cheese should feel melted and stretchy inside. If cheese is flowing out of every stick, they may have gone a little long, but they can still be eaten if the coating is not burnt.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Heat the oven to the package temperature, often 400 or 425 degrees F, or heat an air fryer to the package temperature, often 360 to 400 degrees F.

2. Arrange the frozen sticks in a single layer on a pan or in the air-fryer basket, with a little space between them.

3. Bake or air-fry for the package time, often 6 to 12 minutes, turning once if directed.

4. Check a stick: the coating should feel crisp, and the cheese should be melted.

5. If the cheese is still firm, add 1 or 2 minutes.

6. Cool 2 to 3 minutes.

7. Serve with warm marinara if you like.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The coating should feel crisp and should smell toasted, not burning. The cheese should feel melted and very hot. Let the sticks sit 2 to 3 minutes, because the cheese can burn your mouth.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The coating should look golden, not pale and not dark brown. A little cheese may leak at the ends. The inside of a test stick should look melted and stretchy, not still solid and cold.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The coating should look golden, not pale and not dark brown. A little cheese may leak at the ends. The inside of a test stick should look melted and stretchy, not still solid and cold.

SAFETY
Use mitts. The pan, basket, and cheese are extremely hot. Do not eat one the second they come out. Follow the package, and do not overcrowd, or the coating can stay soggy. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, though they are best the first time. Serve cream and cheese sauces hot. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F, or until a sauce or gravy returns to a boil.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
The frozen supermarket version is the late-night shortcut this page is for.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
