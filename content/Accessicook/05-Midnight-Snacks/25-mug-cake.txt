Mug Cake
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A small vanilla cake mixed and cooked in a microwave-safe mug. Microwave cakes cook very fast and can go from done to dry in under a minute, so start with the shorter time. Do not taste the raw batter, because it contains flour and may contain egg.

SERVINGS
1 mug

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer
- A microwave-safe mug or dish
- A microwave-safe cover or plate, vented
- A dry mitt or thick cloth for the hot dish

INGREDIENTS
- 4 tablespoons all-purpose flour
- 3 tablespoons sugar
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) baking powder
- A pinch of salt
- 3 tablespoons milk
- 2 tablespoons oil or melted butter
- 1/4 teaspoon (a quarter teaspoon) vanilla extract
- Optional: 1 tablespoon chocolate chips or a few berries

TACTILE TIPS
Use a microwave-safe mug that is at least 12 ounces, so the batter has room to rise. The batter should feel smooth and pourable, like a thin pancake batter. The cooked cake should feel set and springy on top, not wet in the center, and not dry and rubbery.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Mix the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt in the mug until even.

2. Add the milk, oil or butter, and vanilla.

3. Stir until you no longer feel dry flour, including in the corners of the mug.

4. Stir in the optional chips or berries.

5. Microwave 60 to 90 seconds, starting with 60 seconds, until the top feels set and springy, and the center is not wet batter.

6. If the center is still wet, add 10 to 15 seconds at a time.

7. Stop as soon as it is set.

8. Let the mug sit 1 minute. The cake will finish cooking and the mug will be very hot.

9. Eat from the mug, or loosen the cake with a spoon and turn it out.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The top should feel set and springy, not wet. You should smell vanilla and cooked cake, not raw flour or burning. The mug will feel very hot. Let it sit 1 minute.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The cake should look risen and set on top, not wet and shiny in the center, and it should not spill over the rim if the mug was large enough. A few moist crumbs on a toothpick are all right.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The cake should look risen and set on top, not wet and shiny in the center, and it should not spill over the rim if the mug was large enough. A few moist crumbs on a toothpick are all right.

SAFETY
Use a microwave-safe mug, never metal. Do not taste raw batter. The mug and cake will be very hot. Use a pot holder to take the mug out. Let it sit 1 minute so you do not burn your mouth. Do not taste raw dough or batter that contains eggs or raw flour. Bake until the doneness cues in the steps are met. Reheat leftovers until they are steaming hot.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
For a chocolate mug cake, replace 1 tablespoon of the flour with cocoa powder. This is best eaten at once. See also Mug Brownie in this chapter.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
