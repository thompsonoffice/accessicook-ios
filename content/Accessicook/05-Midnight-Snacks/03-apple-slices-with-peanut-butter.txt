Apple Slices with Peanut Butter
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
Fresh apple slices with peanut butter for dipping or spreading. This is a fast, filling snack with no cooking. If anyone has a peanut allergy, use a seed butter that is safe for them, or skip the spread and use cheese instead.

SERVINGS
1 snack

TOTAL TIME
About 5 minutes

DIFFICULTY
Beginner

EQUIPMENT
- Measuring cups and spoons with tactile markings
- A liquid measuring cup and a liquid level indicator when you are measuring liquids
- A tactile or talking timer

INGREDIENTS
- 1 apple
- 2 tablespoons peanut butter, or another nut or seed butter that is safe for the people eating
- A pinch of cinnamon, optional

TACTILE TIPS
A ripe apple should feel firm, not mushy, and should smell fresh, not fermented. Slices should be even enough to dip. If the peanut butter is very stiff, stir it first.

BEFORE YOU START
Read the whole recipe once before you turn on the heat. Set out the equipment and ingredients. Clear a landing space for hot pans. If you will be cutting, put a damp towel under the cutting board. If you are cooking with a helper, decide who will handle knives, the stove, and the thermometer before you start.

STEPS
1. Wash the apple.

2. Set it on a stable board.

3. Cut the apple into quarters, then cut out the core from each piece, then slice.

4. Use a cut-resistant glove on the hand that holds the apple if you need it.

5. Spoon the peanut butter into a small bowl, or spread it on the slices.

6. Add a pinch of cinnamon if you like. A pinch is a small amount you can hold between two fingers, about 1/8 teaspoon (an eighth of a teaspoon).

7. Serve at once, because cut apples brown as they sit.

8. A little lemon juice on the slices can slow the browning if you need to wait a few minutes.

HOW TO TELL WHEN IT IS DONE
Non-visual cues: The apple should feel crisp and juicy, and it should smell fresh. The peanut butter should feel smooth or evenly crunchy, depending on the jar, not oily and separated without a stir, and not rancid. Rancid peanut butter smells like old oil or paint.

Visual cues for a sighted helper: The slices should look pale and fresh, not brown and dry, and the peanut butter should look even and scoopable.

FOR THE SIGHTED HELPER
Say what you see out loud, especially color and browning. Share any thermometer reading with your partner. Do not skip the temperature checks in the steps. Visual cues help, but they do not replace a thermometer for meat, poultry, egg dishes, or leftovers. The slices should look pale and fresh, not brown and dry, and the peanut butter should look even and scoopable.

SAFETY
Use a stable board and a sharp knife, slowly. Do not serve peanut butter to anyone with a peanut allergy. Wash the knife after spreading if you will use it on other food. This dish has no raw meat. Still keep perishable ingredients cold. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F.

TIPS AND VARIATIONS
Cheddar cheese sticks are a good substitute if peanut butter is not an option. A firm apple such as Honeycrisp or Granny Smith holds its shape for dipping.

LEFTOVERS
Refrigerate perishable leftovers within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the room is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use shallow containers so the food cools faster. Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days. Reheat leftovers to 165 degrees F. Bring leftover soups, sauces, and gravies back to a boil.
