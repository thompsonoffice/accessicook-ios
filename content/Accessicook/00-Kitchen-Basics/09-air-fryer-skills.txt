Air-Fryer Skills
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
An air fryer is a small, intense convection oven. It can crisp leftover pizza, cook frozen fries, roast vegetables, and finish chicken without heating a full oven. It also runs very hot, the basket is a burn hazard, and crowding the basket gives you steamed, soft food instead of crisp food.

LEARN YOUR MACHINE
Air fryers vary. A time that works in one basket will be too long in another. The first time you cook a food, start at the low end of the time range, shake or turn once, and check early. Keep a short braille or text note of the times that work in your machine.

Read, or have a helper read, your model's booklet for maximum fill level, preheat instructions, and whether the basket and crisper plate are dishwasher safe.

PREHEAT, LOAD, AND SHAKE
Many recipes in this book tell you whether to preheat. If they do, preheat with the basket in, using a timer. If you oil the food, a thin brush of oil is often safer for nonstick coatings than some aerosol sprays. Check your manual.

Load food in a single layer with a little space between pieces whenever you want crispness. Cook in batches rather than piling. Pull the basket out by the handle and set it on a reserved heat-safe landing zone. Shake the basket or turn the food with tongs, then return it. Never reach into the well of the air fryer with a bare hand.

DONENESS AND SAFETY
Use the same USDA temperatures you use in the oven: 165 degrees F for poultry and leftovers, 160 for ground beef, 145 plus a 3-minute rest for whole-muscle beef, pork, and lamb, and 145 for fish. Check more than one piece, especially pieces from the center of the basket.

The air fryer housing, the basket, and the steam that puffs out when you open it can all burn you. Keep the machine back from the wall, on a stable, heat-safe surface, with space around it. Do not set it under cabinets that can be damaged by hot exhaust. Unplug it when you are finished and let it cool before you wash the basket.

FAT AND SMOKE
Fatty foods such as bacon can smoke. Check your manual; some makers recommend a little water in the drawer, or a lower temperature. Stay in the kitchen the first time you cook a fatty food. If you smell heavy smoke, stop, unplug, and let the machine cool, then clean out grease.

CLEANING
Grease left in the drawer will smoke the next time. Wash the basket and plate after they are cool. Wipe the interior only when the machine is unplugged and cool.
