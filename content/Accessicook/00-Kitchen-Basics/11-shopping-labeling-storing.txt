ACCESSICOOK
Shopping, Labeling, and Storing Food
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A cookbook is only useful if you can find the ingredients again tomorrow. This chapter covers shopping, getting things home safely, labeling them so you can identify them, and storing them so they stay safe to eat.

SHOPPING
If you shop in a store, a consistent route helps: produce, pantry, then cold and frozen foods last so they spend less time warming up. A helper, a store associate, or a sighted-assistance app can confirm package sizes, expiration dates, and whether fruit is ripe or bruised. If you shop online or with a delivery service, save a usual list and check the substitutions before they arrive.

For meat and poultry, feel that packages are cold and not leaking. Put them in a separate bag so juices cannot drip onto bread or produce. Frozen food should feel solid, not slushy. Dairy should feel cold.

GETTING FOOD HOME
Go home promptly, especially in hot weather. Refrigerate or freeze perishable food within 2 hours, or within 1 hour if it is hotter than 90 degrees F. Use an insulated bag if the trip is long.

LABELING AT HOME
When groceries come out of the bag, label anything you will not know by shape, size, or smell. Talking labelers, braille labels, bump dots, rubber bands (for example, one band for mild salsa and two for hot), and large-print tape all work. Label leftovers with the name and the date. A day-of-the-week bump system is enough if you do not use a print date.

Keep the system simple enough that you will actually use it. A helper can apply labels as you put food away, and you can check that you can identify each item yourself.

STORING
- Refrigerator: 40 degrees F or below. Store raw meat and poultry on a low shelf in a pan or on a plate so they cannot drip onto other food.
- Freezer: 0 degrees F or below. Wrap food well and label it. Frozen leftovers are safest for quality if you use them within 3 to 4 months, though they stay safe longer if kept frozen solid.
- Pantry: cool, dry, and consistent. Once you open sauces, salsas, and many condiments, read or have a helper read the label; some must be refrigerated after opening.
- Eggs: keep them in the refrigerator, not on the counter.
- Leftovers: 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator, then freeze or discard.

If you are not sure how long something has been in the refrigerator, and it is a perishable leftover older than 4 days, throw it out. When in doubt, throw it out.

CANS, JARS, AND DAMAGED PACKAGES
Do not use cans that are bulging, leaking, or badly dented at a seam. Do not use jars with a popped lid or a broken seal. If a package of raw meat is leaking, you can still cook the meat if it was kept cold and smells normal for raw meat, but clean any juices from the refrigerator right away.
