ACCESSICOOK
A First-Week Practice Plan
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
If you are returning to cooking, or teaching a helper how you like to work, it helps to practice a few small skills before you take on a long braise or a sheet of cookies. This chapter is a five-day practice plan. Each day has a small task and an optional meal from this book. Skip a day or change the order if you need to. The point is confidence, not speed.

DAY 1: THE KITCHEN AND A PIECE OF TOAST
Walk the kitchen and name, out loud, the home of the board, the knife, the thermometer, the timer, the salt, the oil, and the mitts. Add bump dots to one stove setting and one oven setting if you have not already. Then make toast the way you prefer: toaster, toaster oven, or skillet.

Cues: the kitchen smells toasted, not burnt. The bread feels firmer and drier. A helper can look for an even golden color. Optional meal: avocado toast, cinnamon toast, or a peanut butter and jelly sandwich from the snacks chapter, if you already have the ingredients.

DAY 2: BOILING WATER AND A SIMPLE SIDE
Practice filling a pot no more than two-thirds full, finding the burner, and bringing water to a boil. Listen for the change from quiet to a rolling bubble, and feel the steam from a safe height, not by putting your hand over the pot. Cook corn on the cob, steamed broccoli, or stovetop rice from the sides chapter, or just boil the water, turn it off, and let it cool if you only want the skill.

Practice turning the heat off before you move the pot.

DAY 3: EGGS AND A THERMOMETER
Make scrambled eggs or the omelette from the breakfast chapter. Practice cracking eggs into a small bowl first, beating them, and cooking until they are set and, for the omelette, 160 degrees F. This is also a good day to practice the claw grip by cutting a piece of butter or a slice of bread, not a pile of onions.

Optional add-on: the bacon recipe, or a simple cheese toast, if you want a fuller breakfast.

DAY 4: PASTA AND A SAUCE
Use the pasta primer in the pasta chapter. Practice a full pot of boiling water, stirring the pasta so it does not clump, saving a cup of pasta water, draining safely, and dressing the pasta with butter and cheese or with marinara. If you have a helper, have them watch the pot for boil-overs while you handle the timer and the doneness bite test.

Optional full meal: spaghetti with meat sauce, if you are ready to brown ground beef and use the 160 degree F check, or fettuccine Alfredo if you want a cream sauce instead.

DAY 5: SOMETHING IN THE OVEN
Bake a sheet of cookies, a pan of cornbread, baked chicken breasts, or oven bacon. Practice setting the rack while the oven is cold, preheating, using long mitts, pulling the rack out, and using a timer. For chicken, practice the 165 degree F thermometer check in more than one piece. For baked goods, practice the toothpick or spring-back test and the smell of a baked, not raw, center.

WHERE TO GO NEXT
After this week, pick meals you actually want to eat. Good early projects from this book include grilled cheese, pancakes, chili, baked potatoes, chicken nuggets, and a fruit crisp. Save deep frying, candy, and large holiday roasts until the everyday habits feel automatic.

If a recipe goes poorly, that is data, not a verdict. Note whether the heat was too high, the pieces were uneven, or a step was skipped, and try the same recipe once more before you decide it is not for you.
