ACCESSICOOK
Microwave Skills
============================================================

INTRODUCTION
A microwave is one of the most useful tools in an accessible kitchen. It reheats leftovers, steams vegetables, makes mug cakes, and cooks a baked potato. It also heats unevenly, and the containers get hotter than people expect. This chapter covers safe containers, even heating, and how to check that food is actually hot enough.

CONTAINERS AND COVERS
Use microwave-safe glass, ceramic, or labeled microwave-safe plastic. Do not put metal, including gold-rimmed dishes and twisted metal ties, in the microwave. If you are not sure a container is safe, choose glass.

Cover food so it does not spatter, but leave a vent for steam. A microwave-safe lid, a plate, or a slightly loose wrapper all work. The cover and the dish will be hot. Use a dry mitt or a thick cloth, and lift the cover away from your face.

STIRRING, ROTATING, AND STANDING TIME
Microwaves have cold spots. Stir soups, sauces, and leftovers halfway through. If your microwave has no turntable, rotate the dish once or twice. After cooking, let the food stand for a minute or two; cooking continues during that time, especially in dense foods.

CHECKING TEMPERATURE
Leftovers, casseroles, and anything with meat or poultry should be reheated to 165 degrees F. Check in more than one spot with a food thermometer, including the center. If one area is cooler, stir and heat longer. Sauces, soups, and gravies should return to a boil.

Mug cakes and mug eggs are done by the cues in those recipes, usually a set center. If a mug feels only warm at the bottom and molten in the middle, it is not done.

SPLATTER, STEAM, AND SUPERHEATED WATER
Plain water can occasionally become superheated in a microwave and then boil over when you move the cup. Use a microwave-safe container, do not overheat plain water, and consider placing a wooden stir stick in the cup when you heat water for tea. Stand back when you uncover any liquid.

Clean splatters when the oven is cool. A dirty microwave heats unevenly and smells like the last sauce that exploded.

LABELS AND BUTTONS
If the keypad is flat, add bump dots to Start, Stop, Add 30 seconds, and the buttons you use most. Some microwaves have an audible confirmation; learn yours. A talking assistant can also time a manual cook if the microwave itself is hard to program.
